# Research Brief: Will Anthropic be trading on a US exchange by 4:00 PM ET, Friday Nov 20, 2026?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Anthropic shares must be trading on Nasdaq or another US exchange at the 4:00 PM ET close on Friday, Nov 20, 2026. The market closes the same day, 21:00 UTC. In practice this means the first trading day must be **Nov 20 or earlier**. A debut on Monday Nov 23 or in Thanksgiving week (Thanksgiving is Thursday Nov 26) resolves **NO**.
- **What does not count:** a priced deal that has not yet opened, when-issued or grey-market trading, tokenized pre-IPO products, and secondary platforms such as Forge. A first trade on the afternoon of Nov 20 should still count. Mega-IPOs often open around midday; SpaceX opened intraday on its first day (IPOScoop, Jun 12 2026).
- **Who decides:** The market creator, based on whether there is a listed ticker. The planned ticker is "ANT" on the Nasdaq Global Select Market (Anthropic S-1, Oct 26 2026).
- **Edge cases:**
  - A direct listing or a listing on another US exchange would also count.
  - A trading halt right after the debut is ambiguous, but "is trading" would probably still be read as YES once the stock is listed.
  - The rules don't say how a cancelled or withdrawn offering is treated beyond NO.
- **Regulatory timing:** Anthropic submitted a confidential draft S-1 on Jun 1, 2026. For confidentially submitted filings, the public S-1 must be on file at least 15 days before the roadshow starts. With a public filing on Oct 26, the earliest roadshow start was about **Nov 10** (Leverage Shares; Nasdaq/Fool on the same rule for SpaceX). Before launch, Anthropic must file an S-1/A with a price range. After that come the bookbuild, the SEC declaring the registration effective, pricing, and the first trade (usually the next day).

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Recent mega-IPOs that publicly targeted a launch window**
- **SpaceX:** Public S-1 on May 20, 2026; roadshow about Jun 4; priced Jun 11 at $135 (about $75B raised); traded Jun 12. That is **23 days from public filing to first trade, exactly on the reported schedule** (Fool/Nasdaq, May 2026; IPOScoop, Jun 12 2026).
- **Arm (2023):** Public filing on Aug 21 to debut on Sep 14, about 24 days, on schedule. This is from background knowledge, not searched.
- **Applying SpaceX's pace to Anthropic:** Oct 26 + 23 days = **Wed Nov 18**. On-schedule execution gives YES with only about 2 trading days of slack.
- **Rough base rate:** About 75–85% of large, well-advised tech IPOs that publicly file and signal a target week list within about a week of that target **when markets are calm**. Examples include Facebook 2012, Alibaba 2014 (slipped a few weeks before filing its range), Uber 2019, Arm 2023 and SpaceX 2026. This is my rough tally from background knowledge, not a systematic dataset.

**B. Pending IPOs hit by a sharp market selloff just before launch**
- **April 2025 tariff selloff:** Klarna, StubHub and Chime postponed IPOs that were about to launch. Most pending deals paused for weeks. (Background knowledge.)
- **Counter-example:** Uber in May 2019 launched during a trade-war selloff week and still priced, at the low end of its range.
- **Other delays and cancellations:** Saudi Aramco postponed in Oct 2019 and listed in Dec 2019. WeWork withdrew in 2019 after investor pushback on valuation. Ant Group was halted by regulators days before its Nov 2020 debut.
- **Rough base rate:** When a sharp selloff hits in the week before a planned launch, about 40–60% of affected deals go ahead roughly on time. The rest slip by weeks or more.

**C. Investor pushback on valuation for headline IPOs**
- When buy-side anchors publicly push for a lower valuation before launch, issuers usually either cut the range and go (Facebook-style or Uber-style) or delay. Cutting the price usually preserves the timetable. Delaying a few weeks is enough to resolve this question NO.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (all require launch by about Nov 10–12):**
1. **Proceed on schedule at a cut valuation.** Anthropic files a range implying about $1.0–1.5T around Nov 9–10, runs a roadshow of about 7–8 days like SpaceX's, prices Nov 18–19, and trades Nov 19–20. This depends on markets stabilizing on Nov 6–9 and on Anthropic accepting a lower valuation.
2. **Compressed roadshow.** Launch slips to about Nov 12–13 but a 4–5 day roadshow still allows pricing on Nov 19. Mega-deals with heavy pre-marketing and testing-the-waters meetings can shorten the roadshow, but this is rarer at a $50–100B size.
3. **A smaller raise** (for example $40–60B) makes the book easier to fill quickly, which supports path 1.

**Paths to NO:**
1. **Short slip.** Launch in the week of Nov 16 pushes trading into Nov 23–25, before Thanksgiving. That still matches Bloomberg's "before Thanksgiving" framing but resolves NO. FutureSearch's own forecast reportedly put pricing around Nov 24.
2. **Postponement into December or 2027** while waiting for markets to settle, which is the April 2025 pattern.
3. **Withdrawal or indefinite pause**, or a regulatory or SEC timing problem, or a government dispute (the DoD risk factor).

**Key point:** Even a modest 3–5 day slip resolves NO. The window has almost no slack: the SpaceX-pace estimate is Nov 18, against a Nov 20 deadline.
