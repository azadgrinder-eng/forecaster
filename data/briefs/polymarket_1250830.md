# Research brief: Will the CDC counter show at least 7,500 U.S. measles cases for 2026?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts:** confirmed measles cases in the U.S. for calendar year 2026, as shown on the CDC Measles Cases and Outbreaks counter (cdc.gov/measles/data-research) "at the resolution time." The question closes Dec 31, 2026, 11:59 PM ET. Only the CDC's figure counts. State dashboards, the Johns Hopkins tracker and news tallies do not.
- **YES:** the CDC 2026 total reads 7,500 or more when the market resolves.
- **The CDC count lags behind other sources.** On Nov 13, 2026, Johns Hopkins counted 5,739 cases while the CDC counted 5,412, a gap of about 327 (Reuters via US News, Nov 13, 2026). Wisconsin's health department reported 649 cases in 2026 as of Nov 13, while CDC state data showed about 419 (qz/Today, Nov 2026).
- **Year-end behavior in 2025:**
  - The CDC's Dec 31, 2025 update showed 2,065 cases for 2025. The total later rose to 2,289, about 11% higher, because of late reporting.
  - The CDC also counts cases with rash onset in the last epidemiological week (Dec 28 to Jan 3) toward the earlier year (respiratory-therapy.com; CDC page as quoted, 2026).
  - So the counter's value on Dec 31 is clearly lower than the final 2026 number.
- **Key ambiguity: when the resolver reads the counter.** If it reads the counter strictly on Dec 31, only already-published weekly updates count. If it resolves days or weeks later, catch-up reporting would add cases.
- **Update schedule (uncertain).** In 2026 the CDC appears to publish weekly, with data "as of" a Wednesday or Thursday, posted about a day later. Example: data as of Nov 12, page updated Nov 13, Reuters story Friday Nov 13. Possible remaining updates fall near Nov 19/20, Nov 26/27 (Thanksgiving), Dec 3/4, Dec 10/11, Dec 17/18, Dec 24/25 (Christmas) and Dec 31/Jan 1 (New Year). Holidays may cause skipped or shifted updates. An update with data as of Dec 31 might not be published before close. Realistically that leaves about 5–7 weekly increments.

- **Scale:** 2026 is already about 2.4 times 2025's 2,289 cases. It is the highest count since elimination in 2000 and the worst year since 1991–92 (Contagion Live, Nov 13, 2026; Gazette).

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)
- **Nov 18, 2026:** Pennsylvania reports 1,589 cases in 2026, up 69 in the past week, and an eighth death. Lancaster County, the epicenter, has 545 cases. On Nov 13 officials said growth "appears to have slowed" (Philadelphia Inquirer, Nov 18; newsfromthestates, Nov 2026).
- **Nov 17, 2026:** New York has 281 cases this year (273 in the state outside New York City, 8 in the city). The state health department responded to the U.S. losing elimination status. State health departments report 10 deaths nationally (7 PA, 2 WI, 1 NY); the CDC lists 6 (NYSDOH, Nov 17; Today, Nov 2026).
- **Nov 13–17, 2026:** The Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) commission determined the U.S. has lost measles elimination status after 26 years (US News/HealthDay, Nov 16).
- **Nov 13, 2026:** Wisconsin reports 649 cases in 20 counties since August, up from 431 on Nov 3. That is about 218 cases in 10 days, the fastest-growing state outbreak, and a second pediatric death. The CDC's Wisconsin figure (about 419) lags far behind the state's (Wisconsin DHS, Nov 13 and Nov 5).
- **Nov 12, 2026 (latest CDC figure found):** 5,412 confirmed cases, up 230 (+4.4%) in a week. 96% (5,216) are outbreak-associated. There were 43 new outbreaks in 2026, cases in 47 jurisdictions, 591 hospitalizations and 6 CDC-confirmed deaths (Contagion Live, Nov 13; Reuters/US News, Nov 13).
- **Nov 5, 2026:** CDC total 5,182 (+330). Minnesota has 139 cases, with a record 59 in one week, more than 80% in Todd County (KAXE, Nov 10; Fox9).
- **Other states:**
  - South Carolina: about 670 cases in CDC 2026 data. Its 2025–26 outbreak was declared over on Apr 26, 2026.
  - Utah: about 536 cases, with 0 in the latest 4 weeks on one tracker.
  - California: 60 cases as of Nov 9 (qz; viruswatcher; CDPH).
- **Scheduled before close:** weekly CDC updates (around Nov 19/20, then weekly). Thanksgiving (Nov 26) and Christmas (Dec 25) may delay updates. Minnesota updates Thursdays; Pennsylvania and Wisconsin give weekly-ish updates.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES:**
1. **Faster spread.** Wisconsin (about 150–200/week by state count) and Minnesota (about 60/week, record high) keep growing. Added to Pennsylvania (about 50–70/week) and smaller outbreaks, the national rate rises to 330 or more per week and stays there through December.
2. **Backlog catch-up.** The CDC absorbs the Wisconsin gap (about 230) and other pending state cases in one or two big weekly jumps of 400 or more.
3. **Late resolution.** The resolver checks the counter in January, after late-reported and late-December cases are added. This could add 5–10% based on the 2025 pattern.
4. **New large outbreaks** start in under-vaccinated communities during holiday travel.

**Paths to NO:**
1. **Steady pace.** About 230–280/week continues, ending near 6,900–7,350.
2. **Holiday slowdown.** Thanksgiving and Christmas reporting slowdowns or skipped CDC updates cut the effective number of weeks to 5 or 6.
3. **Outbreaks fade.** Pennsylvania is already slowing, and South Carolina and Utah are quiet. If Wisconsin and Minnesota peak, the weekly rate falls toward about 200.
4. **Strict Dec 31 reading.** The counter's last published update before close carries data only through about Dec 17–24.

## 5. Signposts
- **Next CDC updates (around Nov 19/20 and Dec 3/4):**
  - Gains of 350 or more per week would make YES plausible.
  - Gains of 250 or less per week would make YES very unlikely. The total would need to be at least about 5,700 by Nov 19 and 6,400–6,500 by Dec 10 to stay on track.
- **Wisconsin's CDC number vs. the state count:** whether the CDC catches up to the state's 649+.
- **Minnesota weekly counts** (Thursdays): whether they stay at 50+ per week.
- **Pennsylvania's weekly additions:** below 50 means the outbreak is fading.
- **CDC holiday schedule:** whether updates are posted around Nov 26/27, Dec 24/25 and Dec 31.
- **Resolver behavior:** any clarification of whether the counter is read exactly on Dec 31 or later.
- **News of new large outbreaks** (any state with 50+ new cases per week).
