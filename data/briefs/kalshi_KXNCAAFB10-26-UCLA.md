# Research Brief: Will UCLA win the 2026 Big Ten Championship Game?

*Note on method: direct page fetches were blocked by the network proxy, so the facts below come from search-result summaries and headlines. Where sources disagree, I say so. Verify the key numbers before relying on them.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** UCLA wins the 2026 Big Ten Football Championship Game. It is on **Saturday, December 5, 2026, at 8:00 p.m. ET**, at Lucas Oil Stadium in Indianapolis, on FOX (Big Ten "2026 Discover Big Ten Football Championship Game" page; Indiana Sports Corp listing).
- **How teams qualify:** The Big Ten has no divisions. The two teams with the best conference records play, and ties are broken by the league's tiebreaker rules. Those rules include record against common opponents and, as a late step, a computer-rankings tiebreaker (AP, "A 6-way race for the Big Ten title…", Nov 24, 2026).
- **Two things must happen:** UCLA must (a) qualify and (b) win the game. If UCLA doesn't qualify, NO is effectively settled once the Nov 27–28 games are final. Otherwise it is settled on the night of Dec 5.
- **Who decides:** Kalshi, based on the official game result. Overtime counts.
- **Edge cases:** A postponement past the market's close (Jan 3, 2027, 04:30 UTC) is very unlikely. The question is about the conference title game only. College Football Playoff results don't matter. I found no other ambiguity.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Teams in a conference title game:** Before any information about the teams, a participant wins 50% of the time. The Big Ten game has often gone against the favorite or the "better" team:
   - 2012: Wisconsin, a 7-5 team, routed Nebraska.
   - 2013: Michigan State beat unbeaten Ohio State.
   - 2025: Indiana beat Ohio State 13-10.
   Betting favorites in Power-conference title games win roughly two-thirds of the time. That figure is my rough historical estimate, not something I verified this session.
2. **Rematches of a regular-season game:** UCLA beat Oregon 17-15 *at Oregon* on Oct 10, 2026 (ESPN box score, Oct 10, 2026). The team that won the first meeting wins a conference-title rematch somewhat more often than not, but the effect is small. Market-style ratings, which already price in the first result, matter more.
3. **A team losing its starting QB late in the season:** UCLA has already lost starter Nico Iamaleava for the season (October). Its replacement, Ty Dieffenbach, is questionable with a hip injury. In point-spread terms, a downgrade to a true-freshman QB is usually worth several points. Here the reported line on the USC game moved about 10 points, as noted below.
4. **Surprise turnaround teams:** UCLA went 3-9 in 2025 and is 10-1 in 2026 under first-year coach Bob Chesney. Teams that outperform their preseason ratings this much tend to have underlying ratings well below their record. UCLA's SP+ profile fits that pattern (Section 3).

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

Chesney calls him "better than we thought" and says the decision will come late in the week (ESPN, ~Nov 24). True freshman Madden Iamaleava is the projected starter if Dieffenbach can't go.

  - One scenario sends the second spot to a computer-rankings tiebreaker between UCLA and Iowa: UCLA and Oregon lose, while Indiana and Iowa win, along with other results (AP, Nov 24).

Pro Football Network put UCLA's chance of reaching the game at 47.5% after the Michigan loss, behind Oregon and Indiana (PFN, ~Nov 22).

  - UCLA also beat Minnesota and Michigan State, along with others.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**P(YES) = P(UCLA qualifies) × P(UCLA wins the title game).**

**Ways for UCLA to qualify:**
- **A. UCLA beats USC → clinches.** The main path. The reported line implies roughly a 30–35% UCLA win chance with the freshman starting. If Dieffenbach plays and is effective, that rises to around 45–50%.
- **B. UCLA loses but at least two of Indiana, Iowa and Oregon also lose.** Indiana is normally a big favorite over Purdue. Iowa–Nebraska and Oregon–Washington are more competitive. I'd put this path at roughly 10–25% of the loss branch, plus a small extra chance from the computer-rankings tiebreaker.
- **My rough combined estimate for qualifying:** about 40–55%. Published models range from 47.5% (PFN) to 61.4% (cfbroot).

**Winning the title game (Dec 5, neutral site), given UCLA qualifies:**
- **Opponent:** most likely Oregon, otherwise Indiana or Iowa.
- **In UCLA's favor:**
  - It already beat Oregon, on the road.
  - Its defense ranks near the top nationally and forces many turnovers.
  - Dieffenbach should be healthier a week later.
- **Against UCLA:**
  - Its offense is middling (SP+ about 32nd).
  - Oregon and Indiana rate higher in efficiency ratings.
  - Several of UCLA's wins were close.
  - The QB room has been thinned by injuries.
- **Plausible range:** 35–50% against Oregon or Indiana, and closer to 50% against Iowa.

**Ways to NO:**
- UCLA loses to USC and fewer than two of the three challengers lose.
- Or UCLA qualifies and then loses in Indianapolis.

## 5. Signposts

- **Friday, Nov 27:** Iowa vs. Nebraska and Wisconsin vs. Minnesota. An Iowa loss shrinks the field and helps UCLA if it loses to USC.
- **Late this week:** Dieffenbach's status. Moves toward YES if he is confirmed as the starter, toward NO if Madden Iamaleava starts. Watch the official availability report and Chesney's remarks.
- **Saturday, Nov 28:** The UCLA–USC result decides most of the question. A UCLA win lifts it to roughly P(title-game win), about 40–50%. If UCLA loses, it all depends on the Indiana/Purdue, Iowa/Nebraska and Oregon/Washington results, and the question could be settled as NO that night.
- **Nov 29–Dec 1:** Title-game matchup confirmed, plus updated SP+/FPI ratings and Dieffenbach's health for Dec 5. Signs that he is fully healthy push toward YES.
- **Any computer-rankings tiebreaker:** Only matters in the narrow scenario where UCLA and Iowa are tied for the second spot.
