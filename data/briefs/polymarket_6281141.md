# Research Brief: Will Iran initiate a general closure of its airspace by Nov 30, 2026?

**Note on method:** WebFetch was blocked in this environment, so I could not open any page directly. Everything below comes from search-result summaries. Where those summaries conflict, I say so.

## 1. Resolution mechanics

**What counts as YES:**
- Iran itself must start a general closure of the Tehran FIR (Iran's flight information region) or all of Iranian airspace.
- It must happen between market creation and **Nov 30, 2026, 11:59 PM ET**. The market closes Dec 1, 04:59 UTC.
- The closure must suspend essentially all commercial flights crossing Iranian airspace.
- Exceptions for flights the Iranian civil aviation authority has pre-approved are allowed. The rules cite the Jan 14, 2026 closure (all flights except international flights with express permission) as qualifying.
- There is no minimum duration. The Jan 14–15 closure lasted only about 4–5 hours and still counts.

**What does not count:**
- Regional or partial closures, such as the western-airspace closure of April 2024 or the Hormuz drill area of Jan 27, 2026.
- Suspensions of VFR or general aviation only.
- Restrictions imposed by airlines or other countries, including the EASA, FAA and French notices.
- Closures caused only by weather, earthquakes or volcanic eruptions.

**Who decides:** Official Iranian aviation authority statements or NOTAMs, plus a consensus of credible reporting.

**Edge cases and ambiguities:**
1. **Western closure plus domestic cancellations.** On June 8, 2026, Iran issued a NOTAM closing its western airspace and reportedly cancelled all flights nationwide until further notice (Türkiye Today, Jun 8, 2026). Cancelling all domestic flights is not obviously the same as closing the FIR to transit traffic. If a similar event happens, resolution is a judgment call.
2. **"Initiates" during an existing closure.** One tracker claims Iranian airspace was "closed again by Iranian declaration" as of Nov 9, 2026 (iranwarlive.com, via search summary). Other sources say the airspace is formally open:
   - Safe Airspace: "Oct 2026: open to overflights."
   - SaferRoutes: "not closed on paper" in October.
   - Iran's civil aviation authority denied issuing a nationwide closure NOTAM on Sep 1, 2026 (Iran International, Sep 1, 2026).

   If a closure that began before market creation is still running, it is unclear whether it counts as "initiated" in the window. Forecasters should treat the current formal status as **unverified**.
3. **Creation date unknown.** I do not know when the market was created. The listed examples run through Apr 25, 2026, which suggests creation after late April.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Iranian general closures since Oct 2024.** I count about 5 qualifying or near-qualifying episodes in about 25 months:

| Date | Trigger | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Oct 1–2, 2024 | Iran's missile salvo on Israel | All flights cancelled overnight |
| Oct 26, 2024 | Israeli strikes on Iran | All flights suspended until morning |
| Jun 13, 2025 | Israeli strikes | Full closure; phased reopening, fully restored about Jul 17 |
| Jan 14–15, 2026 | Anticipated US strike (no attack yet) | Precautionary closure for a few hours |
| Feb 28, 2026 | US/Israeli war | Full closure; partial reopening Apr 18, international flights at Imam Khomeini airport from Apr 25 |
| Jun 8, 2026 | Missile exchange with Israel | Ambiguous: western airspace plus nationwide flight cancellations |

The two 2024 dates come from my background knowledge, not from searches in this session.

- That is roughly 0.2 episodes per month. Treated as a Poisson process over the remaining ~16.5 days, the naive probability is about **10%**.
- The episodes are strongly clustered around kinetic escalations, so an unconditional rate is a weak guide.

**B. Conditional on a large strike wave on Iran's interior or a large Iranian salvo.**
- All three large Israeli or US strike campaigns on Iran's interior (Oct 2024, Jun 2025, Feb 2026) produced a general closure within hours. That is **3 of 3**.
- Large Iranian missile salvos on Israel produced general closures in Oct 2024. In April 2024 and June 2026 they produced only western or partial closures, so roughly **1 of 3** clearly qualified.

**C. Conditional on limited US strikes on southern and coastal Iran.**
- US strikes resumed around Aug 30, 2026, and continued in September, on Nov 2 and on Nov 6 (Qeshm, Larak, Bandar Abbas, Jask, Sirik).
- None produced a confirmed general closure. Iran's civil aviation authority explicitly denied a closure NOTAM on Sep 1.
- That is about **0 of 4 or more** limited strike episodes.

**D. Precautionary closures on threat alone.** The Jan 14, 2026 closure happened with no attack, under heavy US threat. It is the only clear case. Several other high-threat periods (late Oct to Nov 2026, the April 2024 build-up) did not produce one.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES:**
1. **Israeli or joint US–Israeli strike on Iran's interior before Nov 30.** This path is the most likely route to YES.
   - Historically this leads to a general closure almost every time (3 of 3).
   - It depends on Israel's Nov 15 cabinet decisions, Netanyahu's caretaker politics, US consent, and whether Qatar talks collapse.
   - The Firebrand Project substack, an independent analyst, estimates about 50% (range 40–60%) for renewed major fighting by Nov 30. Treat that as a single non-expert estimate.
2. **Precautionary closure** if Iran sees an attack as imminent, as on Jan 14. A short closure of a few hours would still qualify.
3. **Major Iranian salvo** on Israel or US bases, with a closure to clear its own airspace. This pattern was mixed in the past.

**Paths to NO:**
1. **Status quo continues:** limited US strikes in the south, harassment from Iran, and no large strike wave. Iran's demonstrated behavior in this pattern since Aug 30 is no general closure.
2. **Diplomatic progress through Qatar,** with Trump holding off strikes.
3. **Israeli domestic constraints** under a caretaker government, plus military-leadership reluctance, delaying action past Nov 30.
4. **Strikes happen, but Iran closes only western or southern sectors,** as in April 2024 and June 2026.
5. **Resolution technicality:** airspace is judged already closed, so no new initiation counts.

## 5. Signposts

**Toward YES (large moves):**
- Outcomes of the Israeli security cabinet meeting on Nov 15.
- US carrier or bomber surges, or embassy drawdowns in the Gulf.
- An Iranian NOTAM closing the Tehran FIR, or Flightradar24 showing the FIR emptied.
- An explicit Trump statement that talks have failed.
- A large Iranian salvo.

**Toward YES (smaller moves):** Gulf states such as Iraq, Kuwait or Qatar closing their airspace.

**Toward NO:**
- Announcement of a Qatar-brokered deal or a sanctions or blockade deal.
- Restraint from the Israeli cabinet.
- Continued tit-for-tat in the Hormuz area only.
- Iran's civil aviation authority repeating that no closure NOTAM exists.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **Iran's current formal airspace status is unresolved.** The iranwarlive tracker says it is "closed by declaration" as of Nov 9. Safe Airspace and SaferRoutes say open in October, and Iran denied a closure on Sep 1. This matters for the "initiates" question.
- **Market creation date unknown.**
- **How the June 8, 2026 event was classified** is unknown. That would set the precedent for western closure plus domestic cancellations.
- **No primary NOTAM text could be read,** because WebFetch was blocked. Counts for Oct 2024 come from memory and should be verified.
- **The probability of an Israeli or US strike before Nov 30** is highly uncertain. Signals point in both directions: Trump says "unlikely," while Israel is preparing.
