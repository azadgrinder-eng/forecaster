# Research Brief: Will the 10-Year Treasury par yield reach 5.38% or higher on any date from Nov 3 to Nov 30, 2026?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts for YES:** The "10 Yr" column of Treasury's Daily Treasury Par Yield Curve Rates must print **5.38% or higher** for any date from Nov 3 through Nov 30, 2026.
- **Which number counts:** The question uses Treasury's end-of-day par curve value, not intraday highs or CNBC, Bloomberg or Investing.com quotes. Intraday spikes don't count, and other sources often differ by 2–5 bp. For example, one outlet reported an Oct 7 high of "5.36%", but the par-curve closing high for October appears to be 5.31% on Oct 5.
- **Dates left:** Today is Mon Nov 23. Only five possible par-curve dates remain: **Nov 23, 24, 25, 27 and 30**. Thanksgiving (Nov 26) has no print. Nov 27 should be an early bond-market close (about 2:00 pm ET per one preview), but Treasury normally still publishes a curve that day.
- **When it resolves:** YES resolves as soon as a qualifying value prints. Otherwise it resolves NO once the Nov 30 print is published. If no Nov 30 data appears by Dec 14, it also resolves NO.
- **Edge cases:** Treasury sometimes revises par curve data, which is a small risk. The figure is given to two decimals, so exactly 5.38 counts as YES.
- **Nov 3–20 so far:** I found nothing suggesting any print came close. The November par-curve high appears to be about **5.15% (Nov 13)**.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Size of the move needed.** The last confirmed par-curve value is **4.92% (Nov 20)**. Reaching 5.38% takes **+46 bp within at most five trading days**, which would also be above the 2026 high.
- Typical 10-year daily volatility is about 5–8 bp. In 2026's volatile market, weekly moves have been about 20–28 bp: +28 bp in the CPI week to about 5.14%, then −23 bp in the week ending Nov 20.
- Rough calculation, assuming weekly σ of about 20 bp and a random walk, using the reflection principle for the maximum: P(max over 5 days ≥ +46 bp) ≈ 2 × (1 − Φ(2.3)) ≈ **2%**.
- With σ = 25 bp, it is about 2 × (1 − Φ(1.84)) ≈ **6–7%**.
- Fat tails push this up somewhat. The pullback since mid-month and the risk-off mood push it down.

**B. Historical 5-day 10-year rises of 45 bp or more.** These are rare outside crises. This is my approximate recollection, not a computed count.
- Since 1990, only a handful of episodes come to mind: early 1994, the Dec 2008/Jan 2009 rebound, March 2020 (about +60 bp in 7 days), and a few 2022 weeks that came close (about 35–45 bp).
- That is likely **under 1% of rolling 5-day windows**.

**C. New multi-decade highs.** 5.38% would be the highest 10-year close since about 2001–02. It is above the 2007 peak (about 5.26%) and the October 2026 high (about 5.31%).
- In October 2026, a strong upswing driven by oil, supply and inflation got the yield to about 5.31% and then it reversed.
- Even the November CPI shock (+28 bp in a week) peaked at about 5.15%.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (all need about +46 bp in five sessions or fewer):**
1. A very hot PCE print on Nov 25 (for example core 0.4% or more month-on-month), plus hawkish Fed talk pricing a 50 bp December hike and more hikes after. Even the hot CPI shock earlier this month produced only +28 bp in a week, from a lower starting point.
2. A sharp oil or geopolitical shock (Iran conflict escalation) adding to inflation fears.
3. A fiscal, supply or term-premium shock: a failed or very weak auction (2- to 7-year auctions typically fall in Thanksgiving week), a ratings or fiscal event, or foreign selling. Combined with a quick reversal of the equity rout, this could release the safe-haven bid.

All of these would have to happen together and fast. A move this size usually means a disorderly bond-market event.

**Paths to NO (the default):**
- The yield stays roughly in its 4.8–5.15% range.
- The equity selloff keeps the flight-to-quality bid in place and raises doubt about Fed hikes.
- Holiday-thinned trading lowers the odds of sustained selling, though thin liquidity can also make moves bigger.
- Even a hawkish PCE surprise probably lifts yields by 10–20 bp, not 46.

## 5. Signposts

- **Monday Nov 23 and Tuesday Nov 24 par prints.** If the 10-year is still at or below about 5.0% by Nov 24, YES needs more than 38 bp in three sessions, which would be extreme. A jump to 5.10% or above on Monday would raise the odds somewhat.
- **Nov 25 PCE and GDP.** A core month-on-month print of 0.4% or more, or a big upside revision, would push toward YES. In-line or soft data effectively ends the question.
- **Fed communication** before the blackout period (which starts about Nov 28 for the Dec 8–9 meeting): open endorsement of 50 bp from several officials would lift yields.
- **Equities and AI selloff.** A sharp rebound in risk appetite would remove the safe-haven bid and push yields up. A continued rout pushes them down.
- **Oil and Iran headlines; Treasury auction results this week.**
