# Research Brief: Will *The Angry Birds Movie 3* have a Tomatometer above 80 on Dec 28, 2026?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** The film's Rotten Tomatoes Tomatometer must be **strictly above 80**, meaning 81% or higher, when checked on **Monday Dec 28, 2026 at 10:00 AM ET**. A score of exactly 80% is NO. The rules also say the market settles "the Monday after wide release." With the current US release date of Wednesday Dec 23, 2026 (Box Office Mojo / The Numbers listings; IndieWire holiday preview), that Monday is Dec 28. The two definitions agree.
- **Who decides:** Kalshi, reading the public Tomatometer. It uses the score as displayed, not the "Fresh" or "Certified Fresh" labels.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Date change:** The film has already moved once, from Jan 29, 2027 to Dec 23, 2026. If it moved again, the fixed Dec 28 timestamp and the "Monday after wide release" rule would disagree. A further move is unlikely with about 6 weeks to go.
  - **Some markets open earlier:** One listing gives a Jan 1, 2027 date for India, and The Numbers shows Dec 22 for Poland. This does not affect the US wide release.
  - **When the score is read:** Resolution uses the score about 5 days after release, not the final one. Scores on comparable family films often drift down after the first wave of reviews (see section 2).
  - **Rounding:** Rotten Tomatoes shows whole percentages. Being near 80% is the main risk, because 80% resolves NO.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. The franchise itself**
- *The Angry Birds Movie* (2016): about 43–44% (from memory; I did not re-verify this).
- *The Angry Birds Movie 2* (2019):
  - About **81–82% before release**, on roughly 28 reviews (Toonado, CBR, ScreenCrush, Aug 2019).
  - About 73% a little later (Shortlist, 2019).
  - **72% final** on 108 reviews: 78 fresh, 30 rotten (Apple TV / RT listing).
  - I could not find the exact score on the Monday after release. Release-week coverage still described it as "highest-rated video game movie" in the high 70s to low 80s, so it was probably near the 80 line at the equivalent checkpoint.
- **Takeaway:** 0 of 2 films ended above 80. The better-reviewed sequel was briefly above 80 early on. Movie 3 has the same writer as Movie 2 (Thurop Van Orman). Its director, John Rice, co-directed Movie 2.

**B. Recent mid-tier studio animated sequels and IP films (2024–2026), final or near-final scores**
- **At or above 81 (6):** Transformers One (~89%, from memory), Sonic 3 (~86%, from memory), Bad Guys 2 (87%; 88% on 42 reviews at release), Minions & Monsters (89%), Dog Man (~81–82%, and the source did not label whether that was the critic or audience score), Paramount's *SpongeBob: Search for SquarePants* (Dec 2025; 84% early on 25 reviews).
  - SpongeBob's final is 78% on 73 reviews, but its early score was above 80, which is the relevant window here.
- **Below 80 (7):** Despicable Me 4 (~56%), Kung Fu Panda 4 (~71%), Garfield Movie (~36%), Smurfs 2025 (21%), Super Mario Galaxy Movie (42%), Moana 2 (~61%), Mufasa (~57%). Scores marked "~" are from memory.
- **Rough rate:** About 40–45% of this group scored above 80. For video-game adaptations specifically, the rate is lower: Mario Galaxy 42%, Angry Birds 2 72%, with Sonic 3 the exception.

**C. Early versus final drift for family animation**
- Two directly relevant cases show a 5–9 point drop from early score to final:
  - AB2: 81–82% falling to 72%.
  - SpongeBob Search: 84% falling to 78%.
- Bad Guys 2 held steady (88% to 87%).
- **Implication:** A film whose final score lands around 75–78 can still read 81+ at the Monday checkpoint. A Monday-after score of 81+ seems to need a "true" final quality of roughly 75 or better.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES (81+ on Dec 28):**
- The creative team carries over AB2's warmth and gag-heavy tone, and early family-friendly critics give it a high-80s start. A modest drop still leaves it at 81+.
- Paramount screens it early (an early embargo signals confidence). By Monday it has about 60–90 reviews and the score is stable in the 80s.
- Comparable cases: Bad Guys 2, SpongeBob Search at release, Sonic 3.

**Paths to NO:**
- Reviews come in mid-70s or lower, like AB2's final 72% or Paramount's SpongeBob Search final of 78%.
- The studio change brings weaker visuals or comedy (the Creative Bloq concern). The "parenting sequel" premise reads as formulaic.
- A late embargo (day before or day of release) usually means mediocre reviews.
- Even if it starts above 80, the score drifts down by Monday as more reviews arrive. In the near-miss case it sits at exactly 80 or 79–80.

## 5. Signposts
- **Review embargo timing:**
  - An early lift, about 1 week or more before Dec 23, points toward YES.
  - A lift on Dec 22–23, or no press screenings at all, points strongly toward NO.
- **First social reactions after press screenings:** Effusive "best of the franchise" reactions are mildly positive. Critics' social reactions are a weak signal.
- **The opening Tomatometer and review count:** This is the single strongest signal. Rough guide from the drift data:
  - 90%+ on 30+ reviews gives a high chance of staying above 80.
  - 82–86% on 25–40 reviews is a toss-up because of downward drift.
  - 80% or below early is likely NO.
- **Metacritic opening:** Below 55 suggests a final Tomatometer in the 60s–70s. Above 62 is consistent with 80+.
- **Release-date change or a quiet marketing push:** Would be a negative signal.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not find AB2's exact Tomatometer on the Monday after its release, which is the closest calibration point.
- No information yet on the review embargo, premiere, runtime or test-screening buzz.
- I could not judge how DNEG's handoff from Sony Pictures Animation affects quality. Trailer reactions are anecdotal.
- The reference class for B is small and hand-picked, so the 40–45% rate is rough.
