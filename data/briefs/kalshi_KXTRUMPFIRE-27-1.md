# Research Brief: Will Trump fire exactly 1 non-acting Cabinet member before Jan 1, 2027?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Trump himself must announce that he fired, or asked for the resignation of, **exactly 1** non-acting Cabinet member before Jan 1, 2027. The announcement can come from his social media accounts, his own video or audio, a direct quote in written reporting, or something released directly by him. Whether the person actually leaves does not matter, and a later reversal does not cancel a firing.
- **Who is in the Cabinet:** The heads of the 15 executive departments as of Issuance, plus the EPA Administrator, White House Chief of Staff, DNI, OMB Director, CIA Director, USTR, UN Ambassador, CEA Chair, SBA Administrator and OSTP Director. The National Security Adviser is not on the list. Acting officials are excluded.
- **Who decides:** Kalshi. The ticker appears to be KXTRUMPFIRE-27. I could not get the official rules page, the issuance date or any settlement notes, because kalshi.com and the mirror sites were blocked.
- **Critical ambiguity 1: how many events have already counted?** The question asks for "exactly 1." If Kalshi already counts two or more past exits as firings, YES is impossible. If it counts zero, YES needs exactly one new firing in about 40 days. If it counts exactly one, YES needs no further firings. The three Trump-announced exits in 2026 were all worded softly:
  - **Kristi Noem (DHS), Mar 5, 2026.** Trump said on Truth Social that she "served us well" and "will be moving to be Special Envoy for The Shield of the Americas," with Sen. Markwayne Mullin replacing her. Reporting noted that he "did not explicitly say he was firing Noem" (Yahoo/Global News, Mar 5, 2026). The press widely called it an ouster or firing.
  - **Pam Bondi (Attorney General), Apr 2, 2026.** Trump's Truth Social post called her a "loyal friend" and said she would move to a private-sector job, with Todd Blanche as acting AG. He gave no reason. Media, including PolitiFact, called it a firing. Fox reported that Bondi was told the night before (Mediaite, Apr 2, 2026; KATU/Sinclair, Apr 2, 2026).
  - **Susie Wiles (Chief of Staff), Nov 6, 2026.** Trump posted that Stephen Miller "will become the Chief of Staff starting Monday, November 9, 2026. Susie Wiles, who has done an outstanding job, will be moving over to the Trump Political Operation." A White House official said Wiles offered her resignation the night before (WAGM/AP, Nov 6, 2026; Washington Post, Nov 7, 2026). The Daily Beast headlined it "Trump Fires Susie Wiles."
  - **Lori Chavez-DeRemer (Labor), Apr 20, 2026.** Reported as a resignation while she was under investigation. MS NOW quoted a White House official saying she was told to "resign or be fired." Trump made no firing announcement. A Manifold market ruled that her exit did not count as a firing.
  - **Tulsi Gabbard (DNI), May 22, 2026.** She resigned, citing her husband's cancer. Trump said she was "unfortunately" leaving. Axios reported that she had narrowly avoided being fired in April (Axios and CNBC, May 22, 2026). It is unlikely to count.
- **Plausible counts so far:**
  - **0** under a strict reading that requires Trump to say he fired someone or asked for a resignation.
  - **1** if only Bondi counts.
  - **2** if Noem and Bondi count.
  - **3** if Wiles counts as well.

  I found no Trump quote using the words "I fired" about Noem, Bondi or Wiles.
- **Critical ambiguity 2: the issuance date.** It is unknown. A Kalshi blog snippet describes a market on how many Cabinet members Trump "will say he fired this year," which suggests the window is calendar 2026. If the market opened after April 2026, Noem and Bondi might fall outside it. "As of Issuance" also fixes which 15 department heads count.
- **Smaller points:** Trump's later sole nominee to replace a fired official is not an "acting" member once confirmed. Blanche is described as "Attorney General" by November coverage, so he may now be confirmed. The deadline is Dec 31, 2026; the market close is listed as Jan 1, 2027 at 15:00 UTC.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Trump second term, 2026.** There were three Trump-announced removals or reassignments of Cabinet-list officials (Noem, Bondi, Wiles) in about 10.7 months, roughly 0.28 per month. There were none in 2025: commentators noted he avoided firing Cabinet members in his first year back (Fox News, Apr 2026). For the remaining ~40 days (about 1.33 months), a Poisson model gives P(at least 1 new Trump-announced ouster) ≈ 1 − e^(−0.37) ≈ **31%**. Counting only exits Trump framed as explicit firings, the rate is close to 0.
2. **Trump first term (2017–2021), Cabinet-list officials Trump announced or forced out.** Examples: Priebus (by tweet, Jul 2017), Price (forced resignation, Sep 2017), Tillerson and Shulkin (fired by tweet, Mar 2018), Sessions (resignation requested, Nov 7, 2018), Kelly (announced by Trump, Dec 2018), Coats (by tweet, Jul 2019), Mulvaney (by tweet, Mar 2020). That is about 7–8 in 48 months, or 0.15–0.17 per month. Over 40 days this implies about **18–20%** for at least one.
3. **Post-midterm windows.** Turnover rises after midterm losses. After the November 2018 midterms, Sessions was pushed out the next day, Kelly's exit was announced Dec 8, Zinke left under pressure Dec 15, and Mattis resigned and was pushed out early. That is about 4 Cabinet-level exits within 7 weeks, about 2 of them clearly Trump-initiated and announced. This history argues for a rate above the term average for Nov–Dec 2026. The Wiles exit on Nov 6 fits the pattern.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

  - In a *60 Minutes* interview, Trump said he likes his Cabinet but expects "maybe a few changes." Asked whether he expects to fire anyone, he answered "I do."

  - He said he has confidence in War Secretary Hegseth, and the White House says it backs him (Benzinga, Nov 2026; Newsweek, Nov 2026; TheWrap, Nov 2026).

Possible triggers are the new Democratic Congress's oversight plans before it is seated in January, year-end reshuffles, and any confirmation fights. Reporting describes a "Cabinet shakeup rumor mill" (Rachael Bade Substack, Nov 2026), but I could not read its names.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (which path applies depends on how Kalshi counts past exits):**
- **A. Count is currently 0** under a strict reading. YES needs exactly one explicit, Trump-announced firing or resignation request in the next ~40 days. The likeliest targets are Hegseth (under heavy pressure, though Trump publicly backs him), Lutnick or others in the rumor mill, and possibly Blanche if he becomes a political liability. Trump's habit of softening ousters as "moving to" new roles would make even a real firing ambiguous.
- **B. Count is currently 1** (for example, only Bondi). YES needs no further countable firing through Dec 31. Trump's "very little" departures comment supports this; his "I do" answer about firing someone cuts against it.

**Paths to NO:**
- **C. Count is already 2 or more** (Noem and Bondi, possibly Wiles). NO is then locked in. This is the most natural reading if Kalshi treats any Trump-announced ouster as a firing, as most media did.
- **D. Count is 0 and stays at 0.** No explicit firing occurs, or any departures are framed as resignations or reassignments.
- **E. Count is 0 or 1 and two or more firings follow** in a post-midterm purge.

## 5. Signposts

- **Kalshi settlement notes, clarifications or rules text** showing which past exits counted. This is the single biggest factor. Evidence that Noem or Bondi counted moves the probability sharply toward NO, or toward 0% if both counted. Evidence that none counted makes YES depend on one new explicit firing.
- **The market's issuance date.** If it opened after April 2026, Noem and Bondi may not count.
- **A new Trump post or quote** using "fired," "terminated" or "asked to resign" about any Cabinet-list official, especially Hegseth, Blanche, Lutnick or Mullin. Its effect depends on the current count: it moves toward YES from 0 and toward NO from 1.
- **Trump later describing Bondi or Noem as "fired"** in an interview, which would make retroactive counting more likely.
- **Departures framed as voluntary resignations**, which likely do not count.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not read Kalshi's official rules, the issuance date or any settlement history. All Kalshi and mirror pages were blocked.
- I could not verify the full text of the Noem, Bondi and Wiles Truth Social posts beyond the quoted excerpts, or whether Trump ever said "I fired" about any of them.
- I could not identify the names in the post-midterm rumor mill, or confirm whether Blanche was formally confirmed as AG or is still acting.
- The Wikipedia list dates Noem's exit to Mar 31, 2026 (when the change took effect) rather than Mar 5 (the announcement). That could matter if issuance fell between those dates.
