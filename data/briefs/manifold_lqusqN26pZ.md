# Research brief: Will the first BLS print for November 2026 nonfarm payrolls be +150,000 or higher?

*Prepared 2026-11-17. My page fetches were blocked, including bls.gov, so every figure below comes from search-result summaries of secondary reporting. Where outlets disagreed, I say so.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **YES condition:** The first BLS Employment Situation release for November 2026 must show a seasonally adjusted change in total nonfarm payrolls (establishment survey) of +150,000 or more. +149,000 or lower resolves NO.
- **Release date:** Scheduled for Friday, December 4, 2026, at 8:30 AM ET (BLS schedule, per the question; Next Jobs Report: Dec 4, 2026, fedratecalc). The market closes December 4 at 13:25 UTC, which is 8:25 AM ET, five minutes before the release.
- **What counts:** Only the first print. Revisions in the January 2027 release are ignored unless BLS formally corrects the first November release before resolution. Revisions to September and October published in the same December 4 release don't matter.
- **Delay and N/A risk:** If no BLS release contains the November headline by December 11, 2026, the market resolves N/A. Congress passed a stopgap funding bill (CR) that runs to December 11, 2026, so a lapse in funding should not affect the December 4 release (Trump signs government funding bill, Fox News, fall 2026). For context, the shutdown from October 1 to November 12, 2025 delayed the November 2025 report to December 16, 2025 (Jobs report November 2025, CNBC, Dec 16 2025). The delay risk this time looks low.
- **Edge cases:**
  - Exactly +150,000 resolves YES.
  - BLS rounds to the thousand.
  - A strike or a large federal-employment change in the reference week would count in the headline as published.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. First prints in 2026 so far (10 months).** I compiled these from secondary reports:

| Month | First print |
|---|---|
| Jan | +130k |
| Feb | −92k |
| Mar | +178k |
| Apr | +115k |
| May | +172k |
| Jun | +57k |
| Jul | −23k |
| Aug | +162k |
| Sep | +29k |
| Oct | +165k |

- **4 of 10 (40%)** were ≥150k: March, May, August and October. March was partly a bounce-back from a February strike and bad weather.
- **Sawtooth pattern:** Every first print ≥150k in 2026 was followed by a weaker month whose first print was below 150k: Mar→Apr 115k, May→Jun 57k, Aug→Sep 29k. That is 0 of 3. The sample is tiny, but it may reflect leftover seasonal noise or sampling noise.

**B. Trend after revisions.** Approximate latest values:

| Month | Latest value |
|---|---|
| Jun | ~+31k |
| Jul | ~−10k (sources conflict; one gives +21k) |
| Aug | +186k |
| Sep | +60k |
| Oct | +165k (first print) |

- The August–October average is about **137k**.
- The May–October average is about **100k**.
- CNBC's search summary says the "average monthly gain over the prior 12 months" was **48k**.
- **How far the first print can stray from trend:** BLS's 90% confidence interval for a monthly change is roughly ±130k. Consensus misses with a standard deviation of about 75–100k are common, and October beat consensus by about 100–110k.
- **Rough implication:** If the underlying trend is 100–130k, getting ≥150k needs a positive surprise of about 20–50k. That works out to roughly 30–40% under a normal error model. This is my own calculation, not a sourced estimate.

**C. November first prints historically.**
- Before 2025, November first prints were often strong: 2016 +178k, 2017 +228k, 2018 +155k, 2019 +266k, 2021 +210k, 2022 +263k, 2023 +199k, 2024 +227k. These came from a much stronger trend, so they are of limited use.
- November 2025 was +64k (CNBC, Dec 16 2025).
- First prints for November tend to be revised up later. Collection is short because of Thanksgiving, and response rates are low (64% in 2025 per the BLS release). That suggests the first print may slightly understate true November strength (KPMG / Calculated Risk commentary).
- **Thanksgiving timing:** Thanksgiving falls on November 26, 2026, which is fairly late. In 2024, when it fell on November 28, retail lost 28k in November because seasonal adjustment penalized delayed holiday hiring, and KPMG expected hiring to shift into December (EPI and KPMG on November 2024). This is a modest downside risk for retail.

  - Breakeven payroll growth estimates range from about 0 to 87k/month because of very low net immigration. The St. Louis Fed (Mar 2026) gives 15–87k. The Fed Board's FEDS Note (Apr 2 2026) gives under 10k. Brookings (Jan 2026) says it could be negative.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (≥150k):**
1. **Momentum is real.** The August–October average is about 137k after revisions, layoffs are very low (claims around 200k), and the economy is adding demand for health care, data-center construction and logistics. A print in the 150–200k range is plausible.
2. **Positive sampling noise.** The first-print error is roughly ±130k at 90%. Even with a 100–120k trend, a meaningful share of outcomes lands above 150k.
3. **Health care keeps adding 40k+ a month**, along with transport/warehousing holiday hiring. Big-box and logistics hiring for the holidays tends to show up in October–November.

**Paths to NO (≤149k):**
1. **The sawtooth repeats.** Every ≥150k first print in 2026 was followed by a weak one. October's large beat may have pulled some strength forward or reflect a high sampling draw.
2. **Late Thanksgiving (Nov 26)** pushes seasonal retail and courier hiring into December, and seasonal adjustment then subtracts from November.
3. **Low November response rates** have historically biased the first print down, with upward revisions coming later.
4. **The trend is slower than October suggests.** On a 6- or 12-month view (about 100k and about 48k), the trend is well below 150k. Low labor-force growth caps sustainable hiring.
5. **One-off drags:** a strike in the reference week, continued federal attrition, or a hit to rate-sensitive sectors if the Fed turns hawkish or oil prices rise.
6. **Expectations are low.** The October consensus was about 60k. Even a consensus that rises for November would likely sit well below 150k (my inference; I found no published November consensus).

## 6. Uncertainties

- **No primary source.** I could not open bls.gov or other full pages. Revised values for June, July, August and September conflict across outlets (July is −10k or +21k; the exact August/September split also varies). The 2026 first prints come from news summaries.
- **No November consensus or preview found.** As of November 17, no November 2026 consensus or bank preview was available.
- **Breakeven is very uncertain** (0–87k), so it's hard to tell whether October reflects real reacceleration or noise.
- **Unverified Fed framing.** The talk of a "December Fed rate hike" and earlier mentions of an Iran war/oil shock come from low-quality outlets. I could not confirm the macro or policy backdrop for November.
- **Strikes and weather.** I found no information on strikes or weather in the November 8–14 reference week.
- **Seasonal residuals.** I can't say whether the sawtooth reflects true residual seasonality or chance.
