# Research brief: Carbon Arc Coca-Cola Point of Sale Growth index above 100 for November 2026 (Kalshi)

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** Carbon Arc's Coca-Cola Point of Sale Growth index for November 2026 must be **strictly above 100**. The index compares spend with November 2025, so 100 means equal spend. A reading of exactly 100.0 resolves NO. I could not find how many decimal places Carbon Arc reports, so a value close to 100 could be decided by rounding.
- **When it is measured:** The question settles on the "finalized" Carbon Arc value recorded at **11:59 p.m. ET on Dec 2, 2026**. The market closes Dec 3 at 04:59 UTC, which is the same moment. The panel updates daily as receipts arrive, so the reading can drift until Dec 2. A Coinbase listing of the September contract (ticker KXCOCACOLAPOS-26OCT03) says the first value Carbon Arc reports is used and later revisions are ignored (Coinbase Predictions, Sept 2026). That matches the "recorded on the 2nd" wording here.
- **Data source:** Carbon Arc's receipt panel, covering in-store and online purchases captured by receipt-scanning and rewards apps. It is a nominal-dollar spend measure, so price increases count as growth.
- **Ambiguities:**
  - The rules don't say whether "Coca-Cola" means only Trademark Coke (Coke, Diet Coke, Coke Zero Sugar) or the company's whole portfolio. Sprite has its own market, which suggests the trademark only.
  - The month has only a two-day lag, so late receipts from the last days of November (including the Black Friday and Cyber weekend of Nov 27–30) may be under-counted.
  - Changes in who is in the panel can move the index regardless of real sales.
  - Kalshi decides the outcome using Carbon Arc's data.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **Coca-Cola North America (NA) price/mix and revenue.** Price/mix (the effect of price changes plus the mix of products and channels) has been positive in essentially every quarter since 2021, at roughly 8–12% in 2022–2023 and fading to low single digits by 2026. Q3 2026 NA price/mix was **+1%**, NA net revenue **+3%** and unit case volume flat. Trademark Coca-Cola grew while water, sports drinks, coffee and tea declined (Coca-Cola Q3 2026 results via search summaries, Oct 27, 2026). This is my own tally of company releases. On this measure, NA dollar growth was positive in about 95% or more of quarters since 2021, which points to YES. But the margin in 2026 is thin, about 1–3%, against monthly noise in a receipt panel.
2. **US scanner data for Trademark Coke (Nielsen/Circana).** Trademark Coke's US retail dollar sales grew year over year in a large majority of 4-week periods in 2023–2025, mostly because of pricing. I'd estimate 75–85% of periods. That is from memory of industry coverage, not verified this session.
3. **Brand-level receipt-panel indices like this one.** These are noisier than company totals. A ±2–4 point monthly swing is plausible. The September 2026 Coca-Cola contract was set with a threshold of 99, which hints that recent readings sat near 100 rather than well above it. That is my inference from how the contract was set up, not a published value. I could not find any settled monthly values for this index.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)
- **Nov 26, 2026 (today):** Thanksgiving. In 2025 Thanksgiving fell on Nov 27, so both years have their full Thanksgiving week inside November.
- **Calendar mix:** November 2026 has five Sundays and five Mondays. November 2025 had five Saturdays and five Sundays. Swapping a Saturday, typically a heavy grocery day, for a Monday is a small headwind, perhaps −0.5 to −1 point. This is my estimate.
- **Nov 23, 2026:** KO stock was up about 40% over the 12 months to Nov 20, 2026 (Trefis, Nov 23, 2026), consistent with solid fundamentals.
- **Oct 27, 2026, Q3 call:** CEO Henrique Braun said NA volume improved month to month through the quarter. He said pricing was roughly in line with inflation and the company gained value share. He also noted weakness at restaurants, which matters less for a retail receipt panel. The company raised its 2026 outlook (Q3 2026 call coverage, Oct 27, 2026; WABE).
- **Oct 1, 2026:** Ohio's SNAP ban on sugar-sweetened beverages took effect. Waivers have been approved in about 22–23 states. Courts vacated some, including Colorado, Iowa, Nebraska, Tennessee and West Virginia, leaving roughly 18. Ten states had them in effect by April 2026, starting with five on Jan 1, 2026 (USDA FNS; NACS; ABC7, Dec 31, 2025).
- **Effect of SNAP restrictions:** Studies find SNAP households cut purchases of restricted soda by **12–13%** (Allcott et al. NBER WP 35659, Aug 2026; Frisvold and Lozano Rojas NBER WP 35613, Aug 2026). Numerator estimates about **$430M** of soda sales at risk in 2026 (Numerator, May 2026). Against total US soda sales of more than $50B, that is a small drag of perhaps 0.5–1 point on Trademark Coke, larger in affected states.
- **The November 2025 comparison month:** The federal shutdown ran Oct 1–Nov 12, 2025, and SNAP benefits lapsed or were only partly paid in early November 2025. Numerator found SNAP households' weekly grocery spend fell about 10% in late October 2025. Some convenience stores reported sales down about 10% right after Nov 1 (Numerator; Fortune, Nov 12, 2025). A weak base month makes November 2026 easier to beat. Partly offsetting this, some SNAP households in waiver states can no longer buy soda with benefits at all.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES**
- Low-single-digit price increases on flat-to-slightly-positive Trademark Coke volume.
- The weak November 2025 base caused by the SNAP lapse.
- Continued strength in Coke Zero Sugar.
- A strong Thanksgiving week in both years, so no holiday shift distorts the comparison.

**Paths to NO**
- SNAP restrictions in about 18 states cutting soda spend by SNAP households.
- Weak lower-income consumers.
- Private-label substitution or more deep promotions, which lower dollars per unit.
- The calendar mix that loses a Saturday.
- Panel-composition noise or under-counted late-month receipts at the Dec 2 snapshot.
- Rounding to exactly 100, which resolves NO.

**What decides it:** Whether the "true" underlying trend is around +1–3% (likely YES) or around −1–0%. The noise in the panel is comparable in size to that trend.

## 5. Signposts
- **Daily Carbon Arc readings for November** before Dec 2 (if the forecaster can see them): the strongest signal by far.
- **Settled October or September 2026 Coca-Cola index values:** they show the level and trend just before November.
- **Circana/Nielsen 4-week CSD (carbonated soft drink) or Trademark Coke dollar data through mid-November:** positive numbers point to YES.
- **Coca-Cola Consolidated or other bottler commentary at December conferences:** comments on pricing or volume.
- **Readings of the Pepsi, Sprite or soda-basket indices for November:** a broad soda decline would point to NO.

## 6. Uncertainties
- No historical values of this index were found, so I can't tell its typical level or how much it swings.
- The exact brand scope, the rounding precision, and how much the index moves between early and finalized readings are unknown.
- The share of the panel made up of SNAP or lower-income households is unknown. Receipt-rewards apps may over-represent price-sensitive shoppers, which would amplify both the SNAP-restriction drag and the easy comparison against November 2025.
