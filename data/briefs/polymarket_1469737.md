# Research brief: Will Mojtaba Khamenei be Iran's head of state on December 31, 2026?

*Note on method: every page fetch was blocked by the network proxy. The facts below come from search-result summaries of the listed articles, not from reading the full articles. Dates come from the outlets' headlines or URLs.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What is being decided.** This is a multi-outcome market. The "Mojtaba Khamenei" outcome resolves YES if he is the person who "de facto holds and exercises the powers of the head of state" of the Islamic Republic of Iran on **December 31, 2026 at 12:00 PM ET (17:00 UTC)**. That is about 34 days from today (Nov 27, 2026).
- **The test is control, not title.** The rules ask who exercises "primary governing authority … including effective control over the armed forces, national institutions, and core executive decision-making, regardless of formal title." Symbolic status and "nomination without effective authority" do not count. If nobody governs, the market resolves to "No Head of State."
- **Who decides.** "A consensus of credible reporting." No single named authority makes the call.
- **Key ambiguity: the de facto clause.** Under Iran's constitution the Supreme Leader is head of state and commander-in-chief. Mojtaba has held that post since March 8 or 9, 2026. But much analysis says the IRGC holds the real power and that his role is "largely to legitimize decisions made by his generals" (Al Arabiya/Reuters-sourced analysis, Apr 28, 2026; JNS; Gulf International Forum). A strict reader could argue that IRGC commander Ahmad Vahidi, or a security council, is the de facto authority.
  - Counterpoint: no source I found calls Vahidi or anyone else the "de facto ruler" or "head of state." Mainstream outlets still describe Mojtaba as Supreme Leader and report his directives as the leader's (Arab News and CNN, Nov 24–26, 2026).
  - On a "consensus of credible reporting" standard, the resolver will most likely name Mojtaba while he is alive and in office. Still, this is a real tail risk that does not depend on any event happening.
- **Other edge cases:**
  - **Severe incapacity or death that Iran hides.** He has made no live public appearance since Feb 28, 2026. If credible reporting showed he was dead or incapacitated before Dec 31, the market would likely resolve to someone else or to "No Head of State," even without an official announcement.
  - **Formal replacement.** The Assembly of Experts could choose a successor, or an interim leadership council could take over under Article 111 of the constitution.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Iran's Supreme Leaders leaving office, 1979–2026.** There have been two transitions in about 47 years: Khomeini's natural death (June 1989) and Ali Khamenei's assassination (Feb 28, 2026). That is roughly 4% per year, or about 0.4% over a 34-day window. This understates risk today because Iran is at war and its leader is a named target.
2. **Autocrats removed irregularly (coups, assassinations, revolutions).** Political-science datasets such as Archigos put this in the low single digits per leader-year, about 1–3%. Over 34 days that is about 0.1–0.3%.
3. **Leaders openly targeted for killing by Israel or the US during active conflict.** This is a better fit but a small sample:
   - Ali Khamenei was killed about 8 months after the June 2025 war.
   - Hamas leaders Haniyeh and Sinwar were killed roughly 10 and 12 months after Oct 7, 2023.
   - Hezbollah's Nasrallah was killed after about 12 months of escalating conflict. His successor, Naim Qassem, has survived more than a year.

   A rough hazard for "declared target during intermittent hostilities" is about 15–40% per year, or about 1.5–4% per month.
   - Israel's Defense Minister Katz declared the next leader a "target for elimination" in March 2026 and again in July 2026.
   - Mojtaba has survived about 8.5 months, including periods of renewed US strikes in July–August and November 2026. That points to the lower end of the range, since he is apparently well protected.

**What the base rates imply.** Combining these, the chance he leaves office or dies in the window is low single digits. On top of that sits a small, separate risk that the resolver interprets "de facto" to mean someone else.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (he is still the recognized leader on Dec 31):**
- **Status quo continues.** The most likely path. He stays hidden and keeps issuing regular written directives (four or more in November alone). The IRGC-dominated system keeps presenting him as Supreme Leader, and reporting keeps calling him head of state.
- **A deal or calmer conflict.** This lowers the risk of assassination.

**Paths to NO:**
- **Assassination by Israel or the US.** The threats are declared and active, and US strikes resumed in November. This depends on intelligence about his location and on a political decision to escalate, which a decapitation strike would mean. Trump might hold back while a deal is still possible. *Rough size: about 1–3% over the window.*
- **Hidden death or incapacity becomes credible.** His injury severity has never been verified and there has been no live appearance in about 9 months. If strong evidence emerged, such as defectors, an Assembly of Experts session, or officials contradicting each other, resolution could flip. *About 1–2%.*
- **Internal sidelining or formal replacement.** This would mean the IRGC or a leadership council openly displacing him. Nothing currently points this way: his allies Rezaei and Vahidi were consolidated in August, and analysts call him the "IRGC's crown prince." *Under 1%.*
- **Regime collapse or revolution.** The November protests are local and economic. *Under 0.5% in 34 days.*
- **Resolver reading.** The resolver might decide the IRGC or SNSC is the de facto head of state rather than Mojtaba. This depends on how literally the market creator applies the "de facto" language. *Small, perhaps 1–4%, and hard to estimate.*

## 5. Signposts

**Would lower the probability of YES:**
- reports of an Israeli or US strike on leadership sites, or one aimed specifically at Mojtaba
- his written statements stopping for two weeks or more
- the Assembly of Experts convening in an emergency session
- officials giving conflicting accounts of his condition
- credible leaks that he is incapacitated
- a major escalation, such as the US resuming nightly strikes or Israel joining
- prominent outlets (Reuters, NYT, WSJ) starting to describe Vahidi or another figure as "Iran's ruler"
- protests escalating nationwide, with security forces defecting

**Would raise the probability of YES (from an already high starting point):**
- a verified live video or audio appearance
- a US–Iran deal or durable ceasefire
- continued routine directives and appointment decrees in his name
- foreign leaders or envoys dealing with his office

## 6. Uncertainties

- **His physical condition and whereabouts.** These are fully unverified. The latest visual evidence is an undated video released in August, and since then there have been only written statements, which could in principle be issued by his office without him.
- **How the market creator reads "de facto."** I could not confirm whether the resolver will default to the constitutional Supreme Leader or might name an IRGC figure. I also could not confirm how this market's structure, or any similar earlier markets, handled this question.
- **The exact US–Iran military status at the end of November.** It is unclear whether strikes are paused or ongoing; the reports I found conflict on dates.
- **Israel's current intent.** I found no Israeli threat against Mojtaba dated after July 2026.
- I could not read the full articles, because fetches were blocked. Some details rest on search summaries, and some sources are partisan: NCRI, Geller Report, Iran International, and JNS.
