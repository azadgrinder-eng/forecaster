# Research Brief: Will Bangladesh finish 3rd at Eurovision Song Contest Asia 2026?

*Prepared 2026-11-07. Note: my page-fetch tool was blocked by the network proxy, so everything below comes from search-result summaries of the cited pages. I could not read any page in full, and some details could not be checked against the original text.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Bangladesh must be ranked exactly 3rd in the official Grand Final ranking of Eurovision Song Contest Asia 2026. This is one outcome of a multi-outcome market: the market resolves to whichever country places 3rd.
- **Date:** The Grand Final is Saturday 14 November 2026 in Bangkok. Sources disagree on the start time: 19:30 ICT (Eurovoix) or 14:30 CET, which is 20:30 ICT (Eurovision.com). The market closes 2026-11-15 04:59 UTC, after the show. Results should be announced live on the night.
- **Format:** A single show with no semi-finals, so all 11 entries compete in the final (Eurovoix running order, 29 Oct 2026).
- **Tie-breaks in the market rules:** total points, then jury points, then public-vote points, then alphabetical by English country name. A tie that reaches the alphabetical step is very unlikely.
- **"Other" outcome:** The market resolves to "Other" if results are not announced by 31 Jan 2027, 11:59 PM ET.
- **Who decides:** Official Eurovision information (eurovision.com/asia) and live footage. A consensus of credible reporting is also acceptable.
- **Edge cases:**
  - If Bangladesh withdraws, is disqualified, or fails to perform, it cannot be ranked 3rd.
  - Sources disagree on the venue: IdeaLive at BRAVO BKK in earlier reports, One Bangkok Forum in later ones. This does not affect resolution.
  - This is the first edition, so the scoring rules have never been used before. Early reports said some voting details were not finalised. Rules were "confirmed" on 28 Oct 2026 (Eurovoix).

**Voting system as reported:**
- The result is 50% jury and 50% public vote.
- There are 11 national juries, one per participating country.
- The public vote is online only, on the ZOOP platform. It produces 12 sets of points: one per participating country plus a pooled "Rest of the World" set, which counts the same as one country (Eurovision.com "How to Vote"; Eurovoix, 28 Oct 2026).
- Each user gets 8 free votes and can buy up to 10 "booster" votes. Votes must be spread across at least 3 songs.
- Viewers cannot vote for their own country.
- A fan explainer describes a 12-10-8-7…1 points scale. That scale is unofficial and unconfirmed.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Naive prior for an 11-entry field:** 1/11 ≈ 9.1% chance of placing 3rd.
2. **Who finishes 3rd at the main Eurovision Song Contest (finals with 25–26 entries), from my own knowledge rather than a source fetched here:**
   - 2021: Switzerland
   - 2022: Spain
   - 2023: Israel
   - 2024: Ukraine
   - 2025: Estonia

   In 5 of 5 years, the 3rd-place finisher was a widely tipped pre-contest top contender, not an outsider. Entries rated near the bottom by fans and pundits before the contest almost never finish in the top 3. Large surprises do happen, but they are usually mid-table entries rising, not last-ranked entries.
3. **Pundit and fan rankings for this contest:** Aussievision's 16-member team ranked Bangladesh's "Tumi Ele" **last of 11 (34 points)**. Six other countries each received at least one top score of 12 from the team: Malaysia, Bhutan, Thailand, Laos, South Korea and Cambodia (Aussievision team rankings, Oct/Nov 2026). Bangladesh does not appear among the leaders of any fan poll I found. If 3rd place usually comes from the top 3–5 pre-contest picks, an entry ranked last by pundits starts well below the 9% naive prior.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (Bangladesh 3rd):**
- **Strong jury result:** A polished a cappella performance could do well with juries, which reward vocal skill and originality. With only 11 juries, a few high scores can move an entry a long way.
- **Public vote via the Rest-of-the-World pool:** Bangladesh has a very large population and diaspora (Gulf states, UK, US, and Bengali speakers in India, which is not competing). Their votes can only count through the single Rest-of-the-World set, which is 1 of 12 public-vote sets. Strong mobilisation there could give Bangladesh 10–12 points from that set.
- **South Asian neighbours:** Nepal and Bhutan might give Bangladesh some points.
- **Split field:** Because the field is small, the gap between 3rd and 6th place may be narrow if the frontrunners take the top two places and the rest is fragmented.
- **Stand-out staging:** A cappella and beatbox is distinctive in a field mostly made up of pop songs, which can lift an entry above its pre-contest rating.

**Paths to NO:**
- **Weak pre-contest standing:** The team ranking put Bangladesh last.
- **Strong rivals for the top three:** Malaysia, the Philippines and South Korea are most often named as contenders, and Thailand has host appeal.
- **Bloc voting:** Six of the 11 countries are in Southeast Asia, and that bloc's juries and public votes are likely to reward each other.
- **No votes from home:** Bangladesh's large domestic audience cannot vote for its own entry. Other countries' audiences may mobilise more, especially Filipino fans, who are typically very active online.
- **Language and familiarity:** A Bengali-language a cappella song may have limited appeal to voters in East and Southeast Asia.
- **Point spread:** Even if Bangladesh does well, it could easily finish 4th–6th rather than exactly 3rd.

## 5. Signposts

- **Rehearsal reports (around 9–13 Nov):** Fan-press reactions to the staging and vocals are the most useful upcoming signal. Strong praise for Bangladesh in rehearsals would raise the probability, and reports of sound or mixing problems with an unaccompanied vocal group would lower it.
- **Jury show (around 13 Nov):** Jury scores are not public until the final, but reports on how the jury show went could be informative.
- **On-site press-centre polls**, if any are published.
- **Official clarification of the points scale** (for example, whether jury and public points use 12-10-8…1), which affects how spread out the scores will be.
- **Withdrawals, disqualifications or illness**, for Bangladesh or for a top contender. A top contender dropping out would slightly help Bangladesh.
- **Reports of large Bangladeshi or diaspora voting campaigns on ZOOP.**
