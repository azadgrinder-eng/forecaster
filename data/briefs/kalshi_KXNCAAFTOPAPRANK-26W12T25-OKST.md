# Research brief: Will Oklahoma State be in the AP Top 25 Week 12 poll (expected Sunday, Nov 15, 2026)?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** Oklahoma State appears at No. 1–25 in the AP Top 25 poll that the AP officially labels "Week 12" for the 2026 season. "Others receiving votes" does not count. Only the AP's official website decides; other outlets' week labels are irrelevant.
- **Week labeling:** Media called the poll released Sunday, Nov 8, 2026 "Week 11" (CBS Sports, Nov 8, 2026; AJC, Nov 2026). So the Nov 15 release should be Week 12, and it will reflect games played through Saturday, Nov 14. The small risk is that the AP's own label differs from the media's. If it does, the Week 12 poll could be a different release (for example Nov 22). That would change the question to depend on the Arizona State game as well.
- **Only game before the poll:** No. 10 Texas Tech at Oklahoma State, Saturday, Nov 14, Boone Pickens Stadium, 8:30 PM CT, a blackout game (okstate.com, Nov 10, 2026). The poll comes out the next day.
- **Other polls don't count:** Oklahoma State's No. 25 spot in the College Football Playoff (CFP) committee rankings and its place in the US LBM Coaches Poll do not count.
- **Close date:** Dec 8, 2026. That is well after the poll comes out, so timing is not an issue.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **Win probability in the Texas Tech game:** Oklahoma State is a 3.5-point home favorite (BetMGM's listing is −3), with a total of 72.5 (Heartland College Sports, Nov 11, 2026; ClutchPoints, Nov 2026). These are game lines, not prices on this question. Historically a 3–3.5-point college favorite wins roughly 58–62% of the time; this is my estimate from standard spread-to-win-probability conversions, not something I looked up. Oklahoma State is 7-2 against the spread this year; Texas Tech is 4-5 (ESPN preview, Nov 2026).
2. **Unranked teams just outside the poll that beat a top-10 team in November:** I found no compiled frequency. From general knowledge, an unranked team that already gets votes and beats a top-10 opponent in November, finishing with 3 losses, almost always enters the AP poll the next week, usually around Nos. 18–24. **There is a counterexample from this very season.** After Oklahoma State upset No. 6 Oregon 39-31, it stayed unranked and was only 4th among teams receiving votes (Yahoo/SI, Sept 2026). It entered at No. 19 one week later after beating West Virginia (okstate.com, Sept 27, 2026). That early-season reluctance was tied to the opening loss to Tulsa and low preseason expectations. In November, voters reward a win over a top-10 team more.
3. **Teams with 4 losses in mid-November:** If Oklahoma State loses, it falls to 6-4. Four-loss teams rarely sit in the Week 12 AP Top 25. This season Oklahoma State lost its No. 24 spot after its third loss, a 51-49 defeat at Iowa State, and fell to 3 votes, 9th among unranked teams (Pistols Firing, early Nov 2026). On that pattern, the chance of being ranked after a loss is close to zero (my estimate: about 0–3%).

**The rough structure:** P(YES) ≈ P(win) × P(ranked | win) + P(loss) × P(ranked | loss). The second term is nearly zero.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES:**
- **Most likely path (main scenario):** Oklahoma State beats No. 10 Texas Tech and goes to 7-3. Several things favor entering the poll:
  - It is already about No. 28.
  - It is No. 25 in the CFP rankings.
  - The win would be over a top-10 team, it already beat No. 6 Oregon, and voters like its high-powered offense.
  - The two teams just ahead of it (Missouri and Duke) are both road underdogs to top-5 teams.
  - At least one ranked team near the bottom (SMU or Wake Forest) must lose, and LSU and Arizona are underdogs to top-10 teams.
  - A likely landing spot would be around Nos. 18–23. A narrow or ugly win still very likely gets it in.
- **Long shot:** a narrow loss combined with chaos in Nos. 18–25. This is very unlikely given 4 losses.

**Paths to NO:**
- Oklahoma State loses to Texas Tech. This is the main risk, at about 40% on the game line.
- It wins, but voters stay conservative because of the losses to Tulsa, Houston, and Iowa State, and several teams beat it out. This could happen if Missouri or Duke pull upsets, or if ranked teams near the bottom all win. In that case it might sit at about No. 26–27. That is unlikely after a win over a top-10 team, but this season's Oregon example shows it can happen.
- The game is postponed or there is a labeling mismatch (very small risk).

## 5. Signposts
- **The Texas Tech result (Saturday night, Nov 14) decides almost everything.** A win pushes the probability strongly toward YES; a loss pushes it nearly to NO.
- Late changes in the game line, or QB or injury news (Mestemaker's status), before kickoff.
- **Earlier Saturday results:**
  - Missouri at Georgia, Duke at Miami, Wake Forest at SMU, Texas at LSU, and Utah at Arizona. Losses by ranked teams in Nos. 18–25 open spots; upsets by Missouri or Duke add competition.
  - Wins by other teams receiving votes (Liberty, Louisville) matter little because they are far behind.
- Voters publishing their ballots (Yahoo publishes one voter's ballot) and Sunday poll projections from SI or College Football News.
