## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **YES:** Gibbs has the most rushing yards of any player across all NFL games in Week 10. "Pro Football" here means the NFL.
- **Tie:** If several players tie for the top total, the market pays 1/N to each, where N is the number tied. If Cook and Gibbs ended level, YES would pay $0.50.
- **NO:** Gibbs didn't play, or someone else had more yards. Gibbs did play.
- **Timing:** Week 10 of the 2026 season ran from Thursday Nov 12 to Monday Nov 16, 2026. The last game was Monday night, Chargers 23, Ravens 20 (Heavy, Nov 2026). The market closes Dec 1, 2026 at 01:15 UTC, about two weeks after the week ended.
- **Who decides:** Kalshi, using official league statistics. I couldn't check whether this contract's rules count NFL stat corrections made after the games. The NFL usually issues those within a few days of the week ending. This is the main open question in the rules, because a correction is the only remaining path to YES.
- **Data glitch:** ESPN's weekly leaders page lists Gibbs's team as "CAR" (Carolina) instead of Detroit. That is a display error and doesn't change the yardage.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **Stat corrections large enough to flip the result.** Gibbs would need Cook to lose at least 4 yards relative to him to tie, and at least 5 to pass him outright. NFL rushing-yard corrections are uncommon and usually small, typically 1–3 yards, often from a play being reclassified (for example, a lateral, a sack versus a scramble, or an aborted snap). A correction of 4 or more yards to the right player, in the right direction, during the one week that matters is rare. I estimate it well below 5%. That is my own judgment, not a counted frequency; I couldn't find a dataset of correction sizes.
2. **Second place in the weekly leaderboard later becoming first.** Once all games are final, the leader on the day after the week ends is almost always the final leader. Reversals come only from corrections or data errors.
3. **Before the week, for context only.** Gibbs was among the most likely weekly rushing leaders. He has topped 150 rushing yards four times in nine 2026 games (search summary citing the CBS/NBC game write-ups, Nov 15, 2026). Even so, with about 30 starting running backs, any single back usually has well under a 20% chance of leading a given week. This no longer matters much because the games are over.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
- **NO (by far the most likely):** The official totals stay at Cook 157 and Gibbs 153 and the market settles NO. Nothing else needs to happen.
- **Tie, paying 1/2:** A correction makes the totals equal. For example, Cook drops 4 yards, Gibbs gains 4, or some mix. This requires very specific adjustments.
- **YES:** A correction puts Gibbs ahead of Cook by at least 1 yard. That would take a 5-yard or larger net swing, or evidence that one of the reported numbers was wrong. Most sources agree on both numbers, which makes a data error unlikely.
- **Possible NO-side wrinkle:** If Gibbs's true rushing total were lower than 153, as the conflicting Athlon summary hinted, his position only gets worse.

## 5. Signposts
- **NFL/Elias stat corrections for Week 10 (expected around Nov 18–20):** Any change to Cook's or Gibbs's rushing yards is the only thing that could move this question much.
- **Kalshi's settlement notice or posted rules:** These would confirm whether post-game corrections count. If Kalshi settles on the numbers as of the end of the week, YES is close to 0.
- **The official NFL Game Statistics & Information System (GSIS) box score or Pro Football Reference's game log for BUF@NYJ:** Confirming 157 for Cook would lock in NO. A different number would change the picture.
