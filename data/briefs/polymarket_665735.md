# Research brief: Will S&P, Moody's or Fitch downgrade the US before 2027?

**Research limits:** Every attempt to open a full article was refused by the network proxy. Everything below comes from search-result summaries and headlines. I flag dates and figures I could not confirm against the agencies' own releases.

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** S&P, Moody's or Fitch lowers the US long-term sovereign credit letter rating between now and Dec 31, 2026, 11:59pm ET. That is about 40 days from today (Nov 21, 2026).
- **Starting point (believed current):**
  - S&P: AA+, stable outlook. Downgraded from AAA on Aug 5, 2011.
  - Fitch: AA+, stable outlook. Downgraded from AAA on Aug 1, 2023.
  - Moody's: Aa1, stable outlook. Downgraded from Aaa on May 16, 2025.
  - So YES needs at least one move to AA (S&P or Fitch) or Aa2 (Moody's) or lower.
- **What does not count:**
  - A change of outlook to negative, or placement on Rating Watch / CreditWatch negative.
  - Short-term rating changes.
  - Downgrades by other agencies such as Scope (already AA-), DBRS or KBRA.
  - Fitch's sector-wide "deteriorating" outlook for North American sovereigns (published around Nov 16–17, 2026). This is not a rating action on the US.
- **Who decides:** Official releases from S&P, Moody's or Fitch, with "a consensus of credible reporting" as a fallback.
- **Edge cases:**
  - Read literally, "at any point by December 31, 2026" has no start date, so it could be argued to include Moody's May 2025 downgrade. The title ("Another US debt downgrade") and normal practice mean the window should start when the market was created, presumably after May 2025.
  - A downgrade announced late on Dec 31 ET counts.
  - Agencies sometimes publish after US market close. An action published early on Jan 1 UTC that is still Dec 31 ET would count. The close time of 04:59 UTC matches 11:59pm ET.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Unconditional history of US downgrades by the big three**
- There have been 3 downgrade events in total: 2011, 2023 and 2025.
- Since the first one in August 2011 (about 15.3 years), that is roughly 0.2 per year.
- Spread evenly over a 40-day window, that gives about 2%.
- This overstates the risk today, because each past downgrade came after a visible warning stage (see B).

**B. Warning before action (most relevant)**
All three US downgrades followed a formal negative outlook or watch:
- **S&P 2011:** negative outlook on Apr 18, 2011, CreditWatch negative on Jul 14, downgrade on Aug 5. That is about 3.5 months from first signal and 3 weeks from watch.
- **Fitch 2023:** Rating Watch Negative on May 24, 2023, downgrade on Aug 1. About 10 weeks.
- **Moody's 2025:** negative outlook on Nov 10, 2023, downgrade on May 16, 2025. About 18 months.

So 3 of 3 US downgrades came after a negative signal at least 3 weeks earlier. Today all three agencies have the US on stable outlook, with no watch placement. In general, sovereign downgrades straight from stable with no watch are uncommon among investment-grade sovereigns and usually follow a sudden shock.

S&P's sovereign studies give an average annual downgrade rate across all sovereign ratings of about 9.6%; the rate was 5.2% in 2023 (S&P 2023 Annual Sovereign Default and Rating Transition Study, Mar 27, 2024). The rate for AA-category sovereigns, and especially those on stable outlook, is materially lower. My rough estimate is 2–4% per year per agency, but I could not pull the exact matrix row. Scaled to 40 days and three agencies, that is roughly 0.5–1.5%.

**C. Shutdowns and debt-limit fights as triggers**
- Major shutdowns or standoffs in 1995–96, 2013, 2018–19 and Oct 1–Nov 12, 2025 (43 days, the longest on record) led to no big-three downgrade.
- Fitch placed the US on Rating Watch Negative in October 2013 but did not downgrade.
- Debt-limit crises did lead to downgrades in 2 of about 4 serious episodes (S&P 2011, Fitch 2023). Both happened near or after the X-date, after a watch placement.
- The current X-date is projected for late 2027 or early 2028, outside this window.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **~Nov 19–20, 2026:**
  - Moody's reportedly kept the US at Aa1, stable, in its periodic review. It cited stronger-than-expected growth: 3.4% annualized in Q3 and about 2.3% for 2026 (investinglive, ~Nov 2026). I could not confirm the exact date against Moody's own release.
  - The Bipartisan Policy Center says the $41.1T debt limit could be hit in February or March 2027, with an uncertain X-date (Benzinga, Nov 2026).
- **Nov 18, 2026:** Moody's said the US fiscal path is unlikely to change materially after the midterms. It expects Congress to raise or suspend the debt limit, possibly after lengthy negotiations (Newsquawk, Nov 18, 2026).
- **Nov 16–17, 2026:** Fitch's *North American Sovereigns Outlook 2027* switched to "deteriorating." Fitch cited divided government, a higher risk of shutdowns and debt-ceiling brinkmanship. It projects a 2027 US general government deficit of 7.6% of GDP and debt of about 121% of GDP. I found no reported change to Fitch's US rating or outlook (Investment Executive / Newsquawk / Fibre2Fashion, Nov 2026).
- **~Nov 13, 2026:** The National Taxpayers Union projects the debt limit will be hit around February 2027, with the X-date around December 2027 (NTU, Nov 2026). Goldman puts the latest deal date at about October 2027 (Le Private Banker, Nov 4, 2026).
- **Nov 3–4, 2026 midterms:** Democrats won both chambers, including about 228 House seats and about 54 Senate seats (OPB/NPR, Nov 4, 2026). That creates divided government from January 2027. Scope said the result was "credit positive" for checks and balances and kept the US at AA-, stable (Reuters via Investing.com, Nov 4, 2026). Some Republicans floated raising the debt limit in the lame-duck session through reconciliation, and Trump asked for it before year-end. A CIBC analyst expects that effort to fail.
- **Government funding:** The current continuing resolution (H.R. 6500) expires on **Dec 11, 2026**, and none of the 12 FY2027 appropriations bills has been enacted. A funding gap would start Dec 12. Another short CR into 2027 is widely expected, but a December shutdown "cannot be ruled out" (Harbinger Strategies, Oct–Nov 2026; Jones Walker; The Hill, Nov 2026).
- **Oct 3, 2026:** A rating agency warned that US debt is "increasingly at the mercy of the market" as interest costs rise, and that the elections add debt-ceiling risk (Fortune, Oct 3, 2026).
- **~Aug 13, 2026:** Fitch affirmed AA+, stable. It forecast growth of 1.9% in 2026 and 2027, a 2026 deficit of 7.4% of GDP (the highest among AA-rated sovereigns), and inflation of 3.4% (investinglive / Yahoo / Newsquawk, Aug 2026).
- **Aug 18, 2026:** Gross federal debt passed $40T. Interest costs are about $1T a year (Fortune, Aug 19, 2026; PGPF).
- **Jun 26, 2026:** S&P affirmed AA+, stable. It cited a resilient economy and high but stable deficits, and expects net general government debt to approach 100% of GDP. It warned the rating could be lowered **within two years** if deficits rise because lawmakers fail to contain spending or manage revenue effects of tax changes (Fortune/Bloomberg, Jun 27, 2026; The Star, Jun 29, 2026).
- **May 2026:** Fitch analysts warned the US rating could slip because of high debt (Fortune, May 3, 2026; PGPF).
- **Feb 20, 2026:** The Supreme Court ruled 6–3 that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs. About $175B in collected revenue is potentially refundable, and CRFB estimates about $1.7T in lost revenue through 2036 (Foreign Policy, Feb 20, 2026; NPR, Feb 21, 2026; PGPF). Tariff revenue had been central to S&P's August 2025 affirmation. S&P and Fitch nonetheless affirmed stable *after* the ruling, so the agencies appear to have absorbed this shock already.

**Scheduled events before Dec 31:** the Dec 11 funding deadline and possible shutdown from Dec 12; lame-duck attempts to raise the debt limit or pass full-year appropriations; Dec 31 expirations of smaller programs (Ex-Im Bank, Community Health Center Fund). I found no scheduled US sovereign review by S&P or Fitch in this window. The US is not on the EU sovereign rating calendars, so agencies can act at any time.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (all appear low probability):**
1. **Sudden downgrade straight from stable**, triggered by:
   - a severe governance shock, such as moves against Fed independence, refusal to pay obligations, or extreme debt-limit threats ahead of the 2027 fight;
   - a bond-market or interest-cost shock;
   - a large unfunded fiscal package passed in the lame duck.
   This is unprecedented for the US.
2. **Fast escalation:** a long December shutdown combined with explicit debt-limit hostage-taking leads an agency to place the US on watch *and* downgrade within weeks. In 2011 the gap from watch to downgrade was 3 weeks, so this is possible but needs a watch placement almost immediately.
3. **Fitch acting on its new "deteriorating" view.** Fitch is the most hawkish of the three and moved fastest in 2023. However, its 2023 sequence took about 10 weeks from watch to downgrade, and the 2023 downgrade came after the X-date had passed.

**Paths to NO (base case):** Stable outlooks persist through year-end. Congress passes a CR or omnibus around Dec 11, or a short shutdown has no rating effect, as in 2013, 2018–19 and 2025. The agencies wait for the 2027 debt-limit fight. The more likely sequence is a negative outlook or watch in 2027, then any downgrade later. All of that is outside the window.

## 5. Signposts

Signals that would **raise** the probability sharply:
- Any agency moves the US to negative outlook or Rating Watch / CreditWatch negative. Watch in particular can lead to action within weeks.
- An agency publishes an unscheduled US sovereign comment framed around rating pressure.
- A December shutdown beyond about 2 weeks, combined with threats to prioritize or skip payments.
- A disorderly Treasury market: a jump in 10- or 30-year yields, or a failed auction.
- Actions undermining Fed independence.

Signals that would **lower** it: passage of a CR or full-year appropriations by Dec 11; a debt-limit increase in the lame duck; continued strong growth or revenue data; quiet from the agencies into the holidays. Agencies rarely take major sovereign actions in late December.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not open any primary agency release, so all rating and outlook statuses come from secondary summaries. Several sources had internal inconsistencies:
  - One Substack claimed a "Nov 10" Moody's negative outlook. That almost certainly refers to the Nov 10, 2023 action. One source also misdated the Moody's downgrade as 2026.
  - The exact dates of Fitch's August 2026 affirmation and Moody's November 2026 review are unconfirmed.
- I could not confirm whether S&P's June 2026 statement amounted to a formal one-in-three downgrade risk or was the usual language attached to a stable outlook.
- I could not get exact one-year downgrade rates for AA sovereigns on stable outlook, or the share downgraded straight from stable without a watch. The numbers in section 2B are estimates.
- The state of the Dec 11 funding talks as of today is unclear, and so is whether the lame-duck debt-limit effort is still alive.
- I did not determine whether any agency has scheduled US commentary for December.
