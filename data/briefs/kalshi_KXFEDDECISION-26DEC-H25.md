# Research Brief: Will the Fed hike exactly 25 bp at the December 8–9, 2026 FOMC meeting?

*Prepared Nov 14, 2026. I left out fed funds futures and CME FedWatch probabilities on purpose. Many search results quoted them. My direct page fetches were blocked by the network proxy, so everything below comes from search-result summaries of the cited articles. Treat exact quotes as second-hand.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **YES** only if the FOMC raises the federal funds target range by exactly 25 bp at the meeting that ends Dec 9, 2026. From today's 3.75%–4.00%, that means a new range of 4.00%–4.25%.
- **NO** in every other case:
  - a hold
  - a 50 bp or larger hike (the outcomes are mutually exclusive, so a 50 bp move resolves "Hike 25bps" NO)
  - any cut
  - the meeting being cancelled or not held on its scheduled date (that resolves to "maintains rate")
- **Who decides:** the FOMC statement, released around 2:00 pm ET on Dec 9. The market closes at 18:59 UTC (1:59 pm ET), just before the release.
- **Edge cases:**
  - An unscheduled move between meetings is not covered by the wording. If the Fed hiked 25 bp before Dec 9 and then held, a strict reading resolves NO. This is very unlikely.
  - A "technical" change to IORB alone, with no change to the target range, should not count.
  - The question is about the target range, not the effective rate.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **Fed delivers what officials have clearly signalled about 4 weeks ahead.** Since the Fed began announcing decisions in 1994, it has almost never skipped a move that was this clearly signalled this close to the meeting: the official dot plot, the main hawks, and a unanimous Reuters poll all point the same way. I count no clear case of a fully signalled hike being cancelled in the following month. Size surprises do happen, though. In June 2022 the expected 50 bp became 75 bp after a hot CPI print in the final week. This class suggests P(some hike) is above 90% and the main risk to YES is the size.
2. **Hiking cycles that began with 25 bp steps: did the Fed speed up to 50 bp?** (Sizes from my own knowledge of the record.)
   - 1994: 25/25/25, then 50 in May, 50 in Aug, 75 in Nov. Escalated.
   - 1999–2000: five 25 bp hikes, then 50 in May 2000. Escalated once, late.
   - 2004–06: 17 straight 25 bp hikes. Never escalated.
   - 2015–18: all 25 bp. Never escalated.
   - 2022: started at 25, then 50, then 75s. Escalated.
   
   That is roughly 3 of 5 cycles with an escalation at some point, but escalation happened at only about 4 of roughly 45 individual hike decisions. Escalation tends to come when inflation is accelerating and the Fed is seen as behind the curve. That describes 2026 at least partly: headline inflation is around 3.7%, there is an energy shock, and Waller says 25 bp alone puts "credibility at risk".
3. **Committee behaviour under a new chair with a split committee.** Hawkish dissents in October (Hammack, Cook) were for 25 bp, not 50. The FOMC rarely moves by more than the most hawkish dissent asked for at the previous meeting. A Fed that skipped October would usually resume at 25 bp rather than 50 bp. The exception is when data force its hand, as in 2022.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
- **YES (25 bp):**
  - The September dots and the October hawkish dissents both asked for exactly 25 bp.
  - Economists are unanimous on 25 bp.
  - Hammack wants small, frequent steps and Goolsbee won't back 50.
  - Warsh appears less hawkish than the committee (AI-disinflation view) and has a political incentive not to over-tighten; Trump reportedly dislikes hikes (Fortune, Sep 16, 2026).
  - A 25 bp move reached by consensus is the path of least resistance for a new chair.
- **NO via 50 bp:**
  - Core runs at about 0.3% for a third month, which meets Waller's stated trigger.
  - Logan talks of moving "more rapidly this winter".
  - October PCE (Nov 25) and November payrolls (Dec 4) come in hot.
  - Energy prices jump again on Iran strikes.
  - Historical analogues are 1994 and May 2000. This depends on Warsh being persuaded and on hawks building a coalition. Waller's support makes 50 bp a genuine option, not a fringe one.
- **NO via hold:**
  - A sharp equity or AI selloff tightens financial conditions.
  - The November jobs report is weak, or PCE is soft.
  - Oil keeps falling after the midterms.
  - Warsh prefers to wait for y/y CPI data (Dec 10) and the new SEP.
  
  This looks unlikely given the guidance from the dot plot and committee speakers. A hold after an October skip would be a major credibility reversal.
- **NO via cancellation:** negligible.

## 5. Signposts
- **Warsh, Nov 16 and any other pre-blackout remarks:** endorsing the December dots or "gradual" points toward 25 bp. Remarks on financial-stability or AI-productivity worries point toward hold or 25 bp. Echoing the "credibility" language points toward 50 bp.
- **October minutes (about Nov 18):** how many participants discussed larger increments.
- **October PCE (Nov 25):** core PCE at 0.3% or more m/m raises the odds of 50 bp. A print of 0.2% or less firms up 25 bp.
- **November payrolls (Dec 4):** a strong print with unemployment at or below 4.1% raises the 50 bp tail. A negative print or a jump in unemployment raises the chance of a hold.
- **Pre-blackout "Fed whisperer" reporting (WSJ/Nikileaks, Bloomberg, about Nov 26 – Dec 8):** historically the strongest single tell for size, as in June 2022.
- **Oil/Iran news and equity drawdowns:** an oil spike points to 50 bp; a deep equity selloff points to 25 bp or a hold.
- **Comments from Jefferson, Williams or Bowman (the core leadership):** what they say about step size matters a lot.

## 6. Uncertainties
- I could not read primary Fed documents (statement text, dot plot details) directly because fetches were blocked. All figures come from secondary summaries.
- The October dissenters are reported inconsistently: Hammack/Cook in some sources, Hammack/Kashkari/Logan in others.
- Year-over-year core CPI is uncertain. One source implies 2.6% in September, which conflicts with core PCE at 3.1%. Treat it as unreliable.
- I don't know Warsh's own preference on size, or how much weight he gives Waller versus the doves.
- I couldn't confirm the exact dates of the minutes, the blackout start or the Beige Book.
- Current oil prices and the state of the Iran conflict are only partly known.
- Political pressure from the Trump administration on a Trump-appointed chair is hard to measure.
