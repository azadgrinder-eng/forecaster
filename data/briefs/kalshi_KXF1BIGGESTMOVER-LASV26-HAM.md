# Research brief: Will Lewis Hamilton be the Biggest Mover at the 2026 Las Vegas GP? (Kalshi)

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts:** the main race of the 2026 Las Vegas Grand Prix, set for Saturday night local time on Nov 21, 2026 (early Nov 22 UTC). Some outlets list the date as "22 Nov", which is a timezone or listing difference.
- **YES:** Hamilton has the single largest *positive* value of (official FIA starting grid position − official FIA final classified position).
- **Grid:** the official FIA starting grid after all grid penalties. A pit-lane starter counts as one place behind the last car on the grid unless the FIA gives a numeric slot. If the race is red-flagged and restarted, the original grid is used.
- **Finish:** the official classification, including post-race penalties and disqualifications published before expiry. Later appeals are ignored. Retirements that are still classified count. Unclassified DNFs, DNS and DSQ are excluded, and Hamilton himself would resolve NO in those cases. Post-race DSQs of other cars can still change the result. In 2025 both McLarens were disqualified after the race, which moved Antonelli and Hamilton up two places each (Racer, Nov 23 2025).
- **Ties:** split as 1/N. This matters because a shared top gain (for example two drivers on +10) is plausible.
- **Disruption rules:** if the race is red-flagged and never resumed, the classification at the stoppage counts as long as at least 2 green-flag laps were run. Otherwise it settles at a fair price. A reschedule within 48 hours keeps the market open. A move to another circuit, a reschedule of more than 48 hours or a cancellation also settles at a fair price.
- **Structural point:** a driver who starts near the front cannot gain many places. The pole-sitter's best possible differential is 0. To win this market, Hamilton realistically needs to start in the midfield or at the back.
- **Field size:** 22 cars in 2026, because Cadillac joined as an 11th team (LasVegasWonders driver guide, 2026). The deepest possible start is P22, a maximum gain of +21.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Biggest mover at Las Vegas, 2023–2025** (from published grid and result statistics):
- **2023:** Lance Stroll, +14 (started 19th after a grid penalty, finished 5th). Hamilton started 10th and finished 7th (+3). (LightsOut Blog stats, cited in 2024 search results)
- **2024:** Hamilton, +8 (10th to 2nd). He was the biggest mover and Driver of the Day. (LightsOut Blog 2024 stats; F1DA Beehiiv)
- **2025:** Kimi Antonelli, +14 (17th to 3rd after the McLaren DSQs). Hamilton was second-best at +11 (started 19th after Tsunoda's pit-lane start, classified 8th). (The Race, Nov 2025; LightsOut Blog 2025 stats)
- **Hamilton's record at this race:** biggest mover once in three runnings (33%), and second once.
- **Size of the winning gain:** +8 to +14 at Vegas. The track has long straights, overtaking is easy, and qualifying is erratic because of the cold, tyre warm-up problems and occasional rain.

**B. Naive per-driver rate:** 1/22 ≈ 4.5% per race. A driver in a front-running car who usually qualifies in the top 6 should be well below that. Conditions on the YES path:
- In 2026 races where the winner started near the back, the biggest gains were large: Verstappen +15 in Australia (F1 Chaos), Sainz +8 in China (F1 Chaos), and Verstappen +19 in Brazil (21st to 2nd) (NL Times, Nov 8 2026).
- Even starting from P20, Hamilton did **not** win this in Brazil 2026: he went 20th to 9th (+11) against Verstappen's +19 (Crash.net; RacingNews365, Nov 8 2026).

**C. How often Hamilton starts deep in 2026:** I could not find a 2026 average grid position. What I could find:
- He qualifies near the front most weekends. He took pole in Mexico on Oct 31 (GrandPrix.com) and qualified 0.002s off Leclerc at Spa (PlanetF1).
- His back-of-grid starts have come from penalties, such as the full power-unit change in Brazil (Formula1.com / NewKerala, Nov 8 2026).
- Rough prior: he starts outside the top 10 in perhaps 10–20% of races (rain, crashes, penalties). This is an estimate, not a measured figure.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 8, 2026, São Paulo GP:** Hamilton started 20th after a full PU change, treating qualifying "as a test". He reached about 9th on lap 1 and finished 9th, lapped. Norris won and Verstappen was 2nd from 21st (+19). Mercedes clinched the constructors' title. (Manila Times, Nov 9; RacingNews365; Crash.net; NL Times)
  - Because Hamilton has a fresh PU, another PU penalty in Vegas is less likely. A gearbox or other component penalty is still possible.
- **Standings after Brazil:** Antonelli 390, Verstappen 279, Russell 262, Hamilton 261, Leclerc ~249–251, Norris 246. Three rounds remain: Las Vegas, Qatar and Abu Dhabi. (GPFans; SI; Scuderiafans, Nov 8–9 2026)
  - Antonelli clinched the drivers' title in Mexico (Silverstone.co.uk). Hamilton is fighting for P2/P3, so he has an incentive to maximise his result rather than take a tactical penalty.
- **Nov 1, Mexico City GP:** Hamilton started from pole and finished 2nd to Verstappen. There was first-lap drama involving Leclerc; reports conflict on who was to blame. (Ferrari.com; PlanetF1; GrandPrix.com qualifying report)
- **Oct 2026, Russell penalty plans:** after a power-unit failure in Malaysia (penalty taken in Singapore), Mercedes plans an upgraded power-unit element through the FIA's ADUO upgrade scheme. Russell said "maybe Las Vegas", chosen because overtaking is easier there.
  - Reports describe it as probable but not confirmed, and I found no confirmation dated November. (Sky Sports; GrandPrix247; GPBlog; Yahoo, roughly mid–late Oct 2026)
  - **This is the most important competitor factor.** A Mercedes, the title-winning car, starting deep at Vegas would be a strong biggest-mover candidate.
- **June 14, 2026:** Hamilton won the Barcelona GP, his first win for Ferrari (FIA; Racer). Ferrari is second in the constructors' standings and is a regular podium and front-row car.
- **Scheduled before close:** Las Vegas FP1–FP3 and qualifying on Nov 19–21, race on Nov 21 local time. Penalty announcements usually come Thursday to Saturday. Market close is Nov 29.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES:**
1. **Hamilton starts deep (about P12 or lower)** after a qualifying mishap (Vegas is cold, tyres are hard to switch on, and 2025 qualifying was wet), a crash, track limits, a red flag in Q1/Q2, or a component penalty. He then charges in a fast Ferrari.
   - This is the main path. Conditional on it, he needs to beat other deep starters, especially Russell if Russell takes his penalty.
   - Precedent: in 2025 he made +11 from P19 and still lost to Antonelli's +14.
2. **Hamilton starts midfield (P8–P11) and finishes on the podium** while no one else gains much, as in 2024 (+8).
   - This needs an unusually "static" race for everyone else. Vegas often has a Safety Car, strategy splits and tyre graining that can produce this.
3. **Ties:** a shared top gain gives a partial payout.

**Paths to NO:**
- Hamilton qualifies in the top 6, which is his typical 2026 position. His ceiling is then about +5, which rarely wins this market.
- A penalised front-runner starts at the back and charges: Russell is likely; Verstappen managed +19 in Brazil.
- Hamilton retires unclassified, is disqualified, or starts but drops back.
- Hamilton is the deep starter but gets stuck or hit on lap 1. Vegas Turn 1 and Turn 14 have a history of first-lap contact.

## 5. Signposts

- **Russell's penalty:** confirmed size, or a back-of-grid start, would push toward **NO**. If Mercedes defers it to Qatar or Abu Dhabi, that is mildly **YES**.
- **Grid penalties for Hamilton:** gearbox or PU-element penalties announced Thursday or Friday would push strongly toward **YES**. A penalty only matters if the Ferrari still has race pace.
- **Hamilton's qualifying result (Nov 21 local, the decisive signpost):**
  - Top 5: probability collapses to roughly 1–3%.
  - P10–P15: meaningful chance.
  - P16 or lower without Russell also starting deep: he becomes a co-favourite.
- **Other front-runners starting deep** (Verstappen, Antonelli, Norris, Leclerc after a qualifying crash or penalty) would push toward **NO**.
- **Practice long-run pace:** if Ferrari is clearly fastest in race trim, it raises the conditional gain from any midfield start.
- **Weather forecast for Nov 19–21:** rain or very cold conditions mean more qualifying chaos and a higher chance a strong driver starts deep. That helps every strong driver, Hamilton included.

## 6. Uncertainties

- Whether Russell's Vegas penalty is confirmed, and how big it is: 10 places, 5 places, or back of the grid.
- Hamilton's 2026 average grid position and how many times he started outside the top 10. I found no season table.
- Ferrari's expected competitiveness at Vegas in 2026 under the new regulations. I found no 2026 preview.
- Whether Hamilton's Brazil PU change leaves any further allocation issues, such as gearbox or energy-store limits.
