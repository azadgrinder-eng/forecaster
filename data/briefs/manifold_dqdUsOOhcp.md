## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **YES condition:** The official S&P 500 closing value on **Monday, Nov 30, 2026** must be **greater than 0.99 × the all-time high**. The creator clarified on Oct 13, 2025 that the test is close > 0.99 × ATH. A close of exactly 0.99 × ATH resolves at 50%. After-hours trading does not count.
- **Current threshold:** The record close is **7,818.93 (Tue, Oct 6, 2026)** (InvestmentNews, Oct 2026; ifyouspent "S&P 500 record close October 6 2026 explained"). So the YES line is **> 7,740.74**, unless a new record is set before Nov 30.
- **If a new high is set before Nov 30:** the threshold moves up to 0.99 × the new record. The index could therefore rally to a new high and still resolve NO if it then slips more than 1% by Nov 30.
- **Ambiguity (closing vs. intraday high):** The rules say "all-time high" without saying closing or intraday. The intraday high on Oct 6 was **7,844.52**. If a strict resolver used the intraday high, the threshold becomes **7,766.07**, which requires about +2.70% from the Nov 13 close instead of about +2.37%. A closing-basis reading seems most natural, because the rules talk about the "closing value" being within 1% of the "all-time high".
- **Calendar:** There are 10 trading days left after the Nov 13 close: Nov 16–20, Nov 23–25, Nov 27 (half day after Thanksgiving) and Nov 30. Markets are closed on Nov 26. Nov 30 is a normal full session.
- **Edge cases:** If S&P DJI corrects or revises the close, the official close presumably governs. No resolver or source is named; the resolver is presumably the market creator.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Random walk using today's volatility (most direct).**
- Latest close: **7,561.67 (Fri, Nov 13)** (CNBC, Nov 13, 2026). That is 3.29% below the record. Reaching 7,740.74 needs a gain of **+2.37%** (log return 0.0234) in 10 trading days.
- VIX was **19.92 on Nov 9**, down from 25.64 on Nov 6 and 28.18 on Nov 5 (FRED VIXCLS via search, Nov 10, 2026). The Nov 13 reading was not found.
- A VIX near 20 implies about 1.26% daily and about 4.0% over 10 days. Realized volatility usually runs below implied, so 3.3–3.6% is a reasonable range.
- With roughly +0.1% drift, the chance the Nov 30 close is above 7,740.74 is about **z ≈ 0.6–0.7, or roughly 24–29%**:
  - σ = 4.0%: about 29%
  - σ = 3.5%: about 26%
  - σ = 3.0%: about 23%
- Subtract a small amount, perhaps 1–3 points, for the path where the index sets a new high and then closes more than 1% below it.
- Using the intraday-high threshold (+2.70%) lowers the estimate by about 3–4 points.

**B. Unconditional share of days the S&P closes within 1% of its record (from memory, not verified this session).**
- Since 1950, the S&P 500 has closed within 1% of its record on only about 15–25% of days, because long drawdowns (1973–80, 2000–07, 2008–13) weigh heavily.
- In strong bull years (e.g., 2017, 2021, 2024) the share was about 50–60%+.
- Related published figures: since WWII, stocks spent about 40% of the time within 5% of ATH and 54% within 10% (Fortune, Nov 14, 2019). About 5% of trading days since 1928 set new ATHs (same source).
- The current state, 3.3% below the record with volatility just after a shock, fits below the bull-year base rate. That matches reference class A.

**C. Recovery after a sharp drop near a record.**
- Motley Fool (Nov 6, 2026) found only **2 similar cases since 1950** of a 4%+ one-day fall from near a record. That is too few to compute a rate, and the outcomes could not be read.
- Since Nov 5, the index has already recovered from 7,353.73 to 7,561.67 (+2.8%), roughly halfway back to the record.
- Seasonality usually tilts mildly positive: post-midterm Q4 and Thanksgiving week have historically been strong. This is a small effect, perhaps +0.3–0.5% expected drift, and not decisive.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (index needs about +2.4%, to roughly 7,741–7,900+):**
1. **Nvidia beats and gives a clean succession message on Nov 17.** That would relieve the AI complex. The stock is down more than 30% in six weeks, so a relief rally of 8–10% is plausible and would add about 0.5% to the index directly, plus spillover to other semiconductor and AI names.
2. **A strong Anthropic IPO on Nov 19** that confirms AI appetite.
3. **Rotation continues.** The non-tech sectors rose on Nov 13, and broadening could lift the index even if megacap tech is flat.
4. **Yields pull back from about 5%** if data cools or Fed officials push back on 50 bp hike talk.
5. **Seasonal tailwinds** from momentum after the 2.5% weekly gain and the Thanksgiving-week bias.

**Paths to NO:**
1. A **disappointing Nvidia report or guidance**, or more leadership and investigation fallout (the congressional briefing, any national-security angle).
2. **The 10-year holds above 5%** and hike expectations harden, perhaps through hawkish FOMC minutes or Fed speakers.
3. **A weak Anthropic IPO** or heavy AI supply weighs on AI valuations.
4. **Policy fears** after the Democratic midterm sweep, such as AI regulation.
5. **Simple failure to recover:** even a modest rally of +1–2% leaves the index short of the line. This is the most likely NO path.
6. **New high then a pullback** of more than 1% before Nov 30. This is less likely in a 10-day window.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **Nvidia CEO status:** Huang's departure is reported by CNBC and Seeking Alpha (Nov 13–14), but earlier reports said he would stay. A primary Nvidia press release confirming it was not found.
- **Record verification:** The 7,818.93 record (Oct 6) is supported by several sources, including a Nov 12 report measuring the distance from it. A record set between Oct 13 and Nov 4 could not be ruled out with primary data, though nothing suggests one.
- **Missing data:** The Nov 13 VIX reading was not found. The exact peak of the 10-year yield on Nov 13 is unclear: one source says it "hit 5%", while 4.94% is cited for Nov 10.
- **Fed path:** It is unclear whether the Fed has already hiked in 2026. Sources speak of "another" hike, but the policy history this year was not established.
- **Base rates:** The historical "within 1% of ATH" frequencies above are from memory, because the price data could not be downloaded. The 2 historical analogs Motley Fool cited for a 4% drop near a record could not be read.
- **Closing vs. intraday high:** It is unclear whether the resolver would use the closing or the intraday ATH. That shifts the required gain from +2.37% to +2.70%.
- **Earnings data:** The October PCE release date was not established, and shutdown-related data gaps from 2025 complicate year-over-year comparisons.
