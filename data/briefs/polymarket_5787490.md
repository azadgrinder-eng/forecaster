# Research brief: Will Trump name a new White House Press Secretary by Nov 30, 2026?

*Prepared Nov 8, 2026. The fetch tool was blocked by the network proxy, so I could not open any article directly. Every fact below comes from search-result summaries of the cited pages, and the dates come from those summaries. Treat fine details accordingly.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** Trump or his administration announces a new White House Press Secretary to replace Katie Zacharia. The announcement must come between market creation and Nov 30, 2026, 11:59 PM ET, which is Dec 1, 04:59 UTC. That leaves about 22 days from today.
- **What doesn't count:** Someone named as acting or interim press secretary. The person does not need to be sworn in or start work within the window; only the announcement matters.
- **Who decides:** Official administration sources come first, such as Truth Social posts, White House statements and whitehouse.gov. A consensus of credible reporting is the fallback.
- **Edge cases and ambiguities:**
  - **An offer is not an announcement.** On Nov 7 Trump posted that Dana White "has been offered the job of Press Secretary" (CBS News, Nov 7, 2026; Washington Post, Nov 7, 2026). That most likely does not count as naming a replacement, since it is an offer White has not accepted. A strict reading needs a later "X will be/is my Press Secretary" statement. Check whether the market was created before or after Nov 7, and whether the resolver would count the offer post by itself. I could not find a ruling.
  - **Unusual title or setup.** If White takes some hybrid, part-time or ceremonial version of the job (Trump suggested he bring UFC fighters as "assistants"), it may be argued whether he is a real press secretary. A Trump announcement calling him Press Secretary would probably count.
  - **Promoting a deputy, such as Principal Deputy Press Secretary Anna Kelly.** This counts if she is named press secretary. It does not count if she is called acting.
  - **A joke announcement.** One aide reportedly said Trump was joking about the White offer (Yahoo/Sports reporting, Nov 7, 2026). A clearly unserious post could create resolution disputes.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **How fast Trump has named press secretaries after a departure (first term).** By my count, the gap from departure announcement to a named successor was 0 to 12 days in 3 of 3 cases:
   - Spicer to Sanders, July 21, 2017: 0 days.
   - Sanders to Grisham: departure announced June 13, 2019; Grisham named June 25, 2019; about 12 days.
   - Grisham to McEnany, April 7, 2020: 0 days.
   
   These dates are from my background knowledge; I did not re-check them in this session.
2. **The same job earlier in the second term.** This is the closest comparison.
   - Leavitt's exit was announced Aug 12, 2026, and she left Aug 27 (CNBC, Aug 12, 2026; local reporting on Zacharia, Oct 9, 2026).
   - Zacharia was named Oct 9 (ITV, Oct 9, 2026; Boston Globe, Oct 9, 2026). That is 58 days after the announcement and about 43 days after the job became empty.
   - During that search the White House reportedly "struggled with higher-profile names," several of whom had criticized Trump (local reporting on Zacharia, Oct 2026). It also went about 40 days without any briefing (Mediaite, early Sept 2026).
   - Zacharia was ousted Oct 16 (Axios, Oct 16, 2026; NBC News, Oct 16, 2026). If this vacancy runs as long as the last one (about 43 days), a successor would be named around **Nov 28**, which is right at the deadline. By Nov 30 this vacancy will be 45 days old.
3. **Other Trump senior communications vacancies.** From background knowledge: after Hope Hicks left as communications director (March 2018), Bill Shine was not named until about July 2018, roughly 4 months. Trump has sometimes let communications jobs stay empty for months, with others covering. That suggests the second-term pattern of long gaps is plausible.

**Rough picture:** In the first term, successors were named fast (well under 22 days). In the second term the process has been slow and chaotic, and the vacancy is already 23 days old. Under a constant-hazard view based on the Leavitt gap, the chance a name lands in any given 3-week stretch is moderate, roughly 35–50%. The live White situation could shift that a lot in either direction.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES**
- **White accepts, and Trump announces it after the Nov 9 meeting.** This depends on whether White will leave or scale back his UFC/TKO CEO role, and on how serious Trump is. The "next chapter" podium post leans yes. CNN's source and the aide's "joking" remark lean no.
- **White declines, and Trump names someone else quickly to end the embarrassing vacancy.** Candidates include Anna Kelly, another current communications staffer, or a loyalist media figure such as Loomer or a Fox/Newsmax personality. Trump has repeatedly made staff picks by Truth Social on short notice. Miller's arrival could bring a broader communications reshuffle, possibly with a press secretary named as part of it.
- **Political pressure.** The midterm losses and coverage of the White House as dysfunctional give Trump a reason to install a spokesperson before the new Congress.

**Paths to NO**
- White declines or the offer fizzles, and the search drags on, as the 43-day gap after Leavitt did. Candidates are wary after the Zacharia fiasco, and the reported trouble recruiting high-profile names makes that worse.
- Trump is comfortable doing his own press, as he did during the Leavitt vacancy, where briefings stopped for about 40 days. Someone may brief informally or be named acting, which does not count.
- Post-midterm turmoil (the new chief of staff, Cabinet departures) pushes the press job down the priority list. The Thanksgiving week cuts into the effective window.
- White's role ends up framed as advisory, or as something other than press secretary.

## 5. Signposts
- **The Nov 9 Trump–White meeting.** This matters most. A joint statement or a Truth Social post saying White "will be" press secretary would move the probability to near-certain YES. A clear decline from White or his camp would push it down, though not to zero, because a fast pivot is possible.
- **Any Truth Social post naming a press secretary,** or saying "will be announced shortly." Trump often previews picks.
- **Signs Miller is restructuring communications,** such as Cheung's status changing or new deputies being named. That would mean a press secretary pick is probably coming.
- **The White House naming an acting or interim spokesperson or briefer.** That leans NO: it reduces urgency and does not count.
- **Reports from Axios, Politico Playbook, CNN or NYT of a shortlist or finalist interviews.** That leans YES.
- **UFC/TKO corporate statements about White's role.** For example, a succession plan would lean YES, and "remains CEO" would lean NO.

## 6. Uncertainties
- Whether White actually declined (CNN's source) or is still considering (TMZ and his own statements). This was unresolved as of Nov 8.
- How serious Trump's offer was. An aide called it a joke, but Trump posted it publicly.
- When the market was created, and whether the Nov 7 "offered the job" post could count by itself. I believe it does not, but the resolver's view is unknown.
- Who is briefing now, and whether anyone has been formally named acting. I found no reporting on this.
- Whether Miller as chief of staff will speed up or slow down communications hires.
- Some search summaries contained clearly mixed-up details. For example, one attributed a Pentagon public-affairs and Heritage background to Dana White, which appears to belong to Alyssa Farah Griffin. I discarded those, but small factual errors may remain because I could not open any primary article.
