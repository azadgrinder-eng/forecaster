# Research brief: Will Iran's government attempt to evacuate Tehran? (window closes 2 Dec 2026)

**Main finding:** On 28 February 2026, Iran's government publicly told Tehran residents to leave the city. Several outlets report it. The Supreme National Security Council (SNSC), Iran's top security body, put out a statement, and authorities sent mass text messages. If that counts as "attempting an evacuation," the question may already be YES. Whether it resolves YES depends mostly on how the rules are read, not on what happens next.

---

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** "Iran's government attempt[s] an evacuation of Tehran in the next 12 months." Only the *Iranian government* counts. Israeli or US evacuation warnings for Tehran (June 2025, 2 March 2026) do not. Trump's June 2025 post "Everyone should immediately evacuate Tehran!" also does not count.
- **Time window:** The close date is 2 Dec 2026 22:59 UTC. A 12-month window suggests the market opened around early December 2025. That was shortly after President Pezeshkian's November 2025 warning that Tehran might have to be evacuated because of drought. If so, the window runs from about Dec 2025 to 2 Dec 2026 and **includes 28 Feb 2026**. I could not confirm the exact creation date. If the market was created after 28 Feb 2026, the February event would fall outside the window. That seems unlikely given the close date.
- **Who decides:** Presumably the market creator or the platform's resolution process. No named resolution source was given.
- **Main ambiguity: what is "attempt an evacuation"?**
  - The 28 Feb 2026 action was an SNSC public appeal plus government text messages to Tehran's roughly 10 million residents. They urged people to leave for other cities "if they were able," to stay calm, and to avoid malls. Schools and universities were closed. (Al Arabiya, 28 Feb 2026; Al Jazeera, 28 Feb 2026; Voice of Emirates, 28 Feb 2026)
  - Careful outlets call it advice or an appeal. Some aggregators headlined it as "Iranian Govt Orders All Citizens to Leave Capital Tehran" (ProPakistani, 28 Feb 2026). Times of Israel's liveblog headline reads "Iran tells residents to evacuate Tehran."
  - **Arguments for YES:** the national government officially and directly told the whole city's population to relocate. That is arguably an attempted evacuation.
  - **Arguments for NO:** it was voluntary and conditional ("if you can"). There was no organized transport, no compulsion, and no announced evacuation plan, so a strict reader could say it was not an "evacuation attempt."
- **Other edge cases:**
  - Partial evacuations of specific districts, for example over water.
  - Moving government bodies or "relocating the capital" (the long-discussed Makran coast plan). That is probably not an evacuation.
  - Official statements that evacuation "may be necessary," like Pezeshkian's in November 2025. These are probably not an attempt.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Iranian government response when Tehran is under heavy air attack.**
   - Iran–Iraq "War of the Cities" (1985–88): there was a large spontaneous exodus from Tehran in 1988, but I could not confirm a formal government order.
   - June 2025 Twelve-Day War: Israel and Trump issued evacuation warnings. The Iranian government did not tell people to leave, and hundreds of thousands left on their own.
   - Feb–Apr 2026 war: the SNSC told residents to leave on day one. Reporting says the aim was to avoid a disorderly June-2025-style exodus, when convoys were hit. (Al Arabiya/Yahoo summaries, 28 Feb 2026)
   - **Result:** of the last two major US/Israeli campaigns on Tehran, one (2026) brought a government call to leave. The most recent precedent shows the government now *does* issue such calls when large-scale strikes start.
2. **Megacities evacuated over drought or water shortage.** Cape Town "Day Zero" 2018, Chennai 2019, São Paulo 2014–15, Mexico City's recurring crises, Tehran 2025: **0 of about 5** led to an evacuation. Experts called a water-driven evacuation of Tehran "impossible" (Foreign Policy, 11 Nov 2025).
3. **Leader talk of evacuation versus actual action.** Pezeshkian's conditional warning in November 2025 ("if it still doesn't rain, we'll have to evacuate Tehran") did not lead to a water-driven evacuation in the year since. Other officials played it down. (NPR, 13 Nov 2025; RFE/RL, Nov 2025)

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **About 5–7 Nov 2026:**
  - Trump's national security team is debating whether to resume full-scale strikes on Iran after the 3 Nov midterms. Some advisers want action before a Democratic-controlled Congress takes office in January. (Fox News live, 5 Nov 2026; CBS live, Nov 2026)
  - US "defensive" strikes hit around the Strait of Hormuz, including Qeshm Island, after an IRGC attack on a US aircraft. The Pentagon says this is not a return to full-scale war.
  - Tankers have been hitting mines near Hormuz.
  - Iran is pushing diplomacy (Russia, the UAE, a proposal through Qatar), but says it will retaliate more widely if the war resumes. (Al Jazeera, 6 Nov 2026; CBS)

That is up from about 18% in May 2026 and about 11% in Nov 2025. Rationing and night-time pressure cuts continue, mainly in southern districts. (Voice of Emirates, 27 Aug and early Nov 2026; IranWire)
- **8 Jul 2026:** the US–Iran ceasefire collapsed after Iranian attacks on shipping in Hormuz. Trump called the ceasefire "over" but kept talks open. Low-level strikes have been traded since. (Wikipedia, 2026 Iran war; US News timeline, 8 Jul 2026)
- **17 Jun 2026:** the US and Iran signed a 14-point memorandum of understanding. (Wikipedia/CFR)
- **7 May 2026:** US strikes on sites in southern Iran and in Tehran during the ceasefire. (Wikipedia)
- **7–8 Apr 2026:** Pakistan-brokered ceasefire, extended indefinitely on 21 April. (House of Commons Library; CFR)
- **Mar 2026:**
  - Tehran was described as a "ghost town" (France24, 3 Mar 2026).
  - Roughly 100,000+ people fled in the first two days, by one refugee-agency estimate. Many could not afford to leave, and travel times and costs to cities like Rasht rose sharply. (The New Humanitarian, 12 Mar 2026)
  - Israel issued Tehran evacuation warnings on 2 March.
- **28 Feb 2026:**
  - US and Israeli strikes began and killed Supreme Leader Khamenei. His son Mojtaba was named successor and is reportedly in hiding.
  - **The SNSC and government text messages urged Tehran residents to leave for other cities.**
- **Nov–Dec 2025:** Pezeshkian warned that Tehran could need evacuating if rains failed. Dams were at about 11% and storage was about half the previous year's (Iran International, 5 Dec 2025).
- **Scheduled before 2 Dec 2026:** no fixed events. Key items are the White House decision on resuming major strikes (possible any day) and autumn rains in November.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES**
- **A. The February 2026 event counts (main path).** The resolver treats the SNSC statement and mass texts as an attempted evacuation inside the window. This depends on how the resolver reads "attempt an evacuation" and on the creation date.
- **B. A new call before 2 Dec.** The US (or the US and Israel) resumes full-scale strikes on Tehran, and the SNSC repeats its February 2026 call. The February precedent makes a repeat likely if heavy strikes on Tehran resume. This depends on Trump's decision in the post-midterm window and on whether strikes reach Tehran rather than just the Hormuz area.
- **C. A water-driven evacuation.** Very unlikely in 25 days. Dams are higher than a year ago and the rainy season is starting.

**Paths to NO**
- The resolver decides the voluntary February advisory is not an "evacuation attempt," and there is no full-scale war on Tehran before 2 Dec.
- The market was created after 28 Feb 2026, so the February event is out of the window.
- Diplomacy (the Qatar channel) or a Trump decision against escalation keeps strikes limited to the Gulf and Hormuz area.

## 5. Signposts

- **Biggest factor:** any clarification from the resolver or in market comments on whether the 28 Feb 2026 SNSC call counts. A "yes" points strongly to YES. A "no" makes the outcome mostly depend on whether the war resumes.
- A Trump announcement of resumed major operations, or US/Israeli strikes on Tehran itself, would push toward YES under path B.
- New SNSC or civil-defence messages, school closures, or fuel and bread queues in Tehran would push toward YES.
- A US–Iran deal, a new ceasefire, or Qatar-mediated talks making progress would push toward NO for new events.
- Heavy November rain in Tehran and rising dam levels would make the water path even less likely.

## 6. Uncertainties
