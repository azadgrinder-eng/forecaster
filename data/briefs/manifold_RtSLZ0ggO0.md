# Research brief: Will this user's #1 artist on Spotify Wrapped 2026 be a metal band?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** the #1 artist in the user's own Spotify Wrapped 2026 has to be "categorized primarily under the Metal genre or any of its subgenres." NO covers any other genre, and also an artist whose "primary musical identity is not fundamentally recognized as Metal."
- **Who decides:** the person who made the market, who is also the listener. The rules don't name a genre authority such as Spotify's genre tags, Wikipedia or RateYourMusic. For crossover artists, the creator's own judgement will settle it.
- **Timing problem:** the market closes on Nov 23, 2026, but Wrapped 2026 hasn't come out yet. Spotify had not announced a date as of Nov 12, 2026. Most outlets expect Wednesday, Dec 2, 2026, the Wednesday after US Thanksgiving (Official Charts / Her Campus / spotifystats.com, Nov 2026). Past launch dates were Dec 3 2025, Dec 4 2024, Nov 29 2023, Nov 30 2022 and Dec 1 2021 (Jamwise; Techloy). So the result will very likely be known only after trading closes. On Nov 5, 2026 Spotify posted in its newsroom that "Wrapped season is almost here" (Hypebot, Nov 2026). A launch before Nov 23 would break the pattern of the last five years.
- **How Wrapped ranks artists:** by a weighted count of streams, not by minutes. The main artist on a track gets full credit and featured artists get partial credit. A play counts once it passes 30 seconds, and Private Mode or plays excluded from the taste profile don't count (Spotify Newsroom methodology post, Dec 5, 2025, via TechRadar). Counting starts Jan 1 and runs until "a couple of weeks" before launch, so probably mid-to-late November (Spotify's Molly Holder, cited in 2025 coverage). About one to two weeks of counting are left.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Limp Bizkit:** most sources call them nu metal, with rap metal or rap rock as secondary labels. Apple Music lists *Significant Other* as "Hard Rock" (Wikipedia-derived pages; Apple Music). Nu metal is normally treated as a metal subgenre, so Limp Bizkit at #1 probably resolves YES, but a purist could argue "rap rock."
  - **Linkin Park:** labels include alternative rock, nu metal, alternative metal and rap rock. Their 2024 *From Zero* era leans alt-rock, so this one could go either way. That is my judgement; I didn't find a source on it.
  - **Gojira:** clearly metal (progressive, groove or death metal).
  - **Clearly not metal:** Pink Floyd (progressive rock), Green Day (punk), The Smiths (indie), MF DOOM (hip-hop), Superheaven (grunge and emo-leaning alt rock; I didn't find a source for this), and Lumi Athena (phonk or electronic; also from my own knowledge).

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **This listener's own #1, 2024–2025:** neither #1 was metal (Lumi Athena in 2024, Pink Floyd in 2025). The #1 changed both years, and the 2025 #1 (Pink Floyd) wasn't in the 2024 top 5 at all. So the top spot moves around a lot and a new artist can take it.
2. **Share of metal-ish artists in the listener's top 5:**
   - 2024: Limp Bizkit, plus Linkin Park if counted, so 1–2 of 5.
   - 2025: Limp Bizkit and Gojira, so 2 of 5.
   - Across the 10 slots, that's 3–4 metal-ish (30–40%). If the #1 were drawn at random from this taste profile, the chance it is metal would be roughly 30–40%.
   - Across the 4 top-2 slots, 2 were metal-ish (Limp Bizkit both times), or 50%.
3. **The steady runner-up:** Limp Bizkit was #2 in both 2024 and 2025. It is the only artist in both top-5 lists. Gaps between top artists are usually small: MIDiA found the average person spends only about 4.2% of listening time on their #1 artist, and most staff fell between 3% and 6% (MIDiA Research, 2025 and 2024 posts). That suggests a steady #2 is often close to #1, and a one-off obsession like Pink Floyd or Lumi Athena can easily take the top spot. I found no published figure for how often a listener's top artist repeats or how often a #2 moves up.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES:**
- **Limp Bizkit moves from #2 to #1.** This is the most likely YES path: the band has steady, lasting appeal for this user and was active in 2026 (tour and new songs). It depends on there being no one-off obsession this year like Pink Floyd in 2025, and on the resolver counting nu metal as metal. That second condition is likely.
- **Gojira climbs from #5, or another metal band becomes the user's 2026 obsession.** Taste has drifted from Lumi Athena and Linkin Park toward Pink Floyd, Gojira and Superheaven, which may point to heavier or more "serious" rock. A possible Gojira album would help.
- **Linkin Park returns at #1.** That probably resolves YES only if the creator counts them as nu metal.
- **The question itself is evidence.** The creator can see their own listening (Spotify's in-app stats, third-party trackers) and chose to ask specifically about metal. That may mean a metal band is in contention. It could also just be a fun question, so it's weak evidence either way.

**Paths to NO:**
- **A new non-metal obsession wins**, as happened in both observed years (an electronic or phonk artist, then a prog-rock band).
- **Pink Floyd repeats**, helped by the 2026 reissues.
- **A non-metal regular rises**, such as Green Day, The Smiths, Superheaven or MF DOOM.
- **Limp Bizkit wins but the resolver calls them "rap rock."** This is a small risk.

## 5. Signposts

- **Strongly toward YES:** the creator posting screenshots or comments from in-year stats or third-party trackers showing a metal artist on top; news of a Gojira album or single in autumn 2026; a new Limp Bizkit single or album release.
- **Toward NO:** the creator mentioning a new favourite outside metal; more Pink Floyd activity driving catalogue plays.
- **Wrapped launch:** an official date, almost certainly in early December and after the market closes, settles the outcome but not trading. A launch before Nov 23 would be unusual.
- **Clarification on Limp Bizkit or Linkin Park:** if the creator says nu metal counts or doesn't count, the chance of YES moves a lot.

- The genre judgement is the creator's: there's no official source, and Spotify's algorithmic tags couldn't be checked.
- I couldn't find an empirical base rate for how often a listener's Wrapped #1 repeats or how often their #2 moves up.
