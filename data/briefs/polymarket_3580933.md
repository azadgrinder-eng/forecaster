# Research brief: Will Puma Shen (沈伯洋, DPP) win the November 28, 2026 Taipei mayoral election?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Puma Shen wins the Taipei mayoral race in the regular election on Saturday, November 28, 2026, and becomes the next mayor because of that result. Interim or acting mayors do not count. Taiwan elects mayors in a single round, and whoever gets the most votes wins. There is no runoff and no 50% threshold, so Shen does not need a majority.
- **Who decides:** The market follows a consensus of credible reporting. If that is ambiguous, it uses the official Central Election Commission results (db.cec.gov.tw). Counting normally finishes on election night, and the commission certifies about a week later. The new term begins on December 25, 2026.
- **"Other" outcome:** If no result is known by June 30, 2027, the market resolves "Other." This would most likely require a postponement, for example from a disaster or a candidate's death, which under Taiwanese law can trigger a postponement.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Recount:** Taiwanese law lets a candidate request a court-ordered recount when the margin is under 0.3% of valid votes. The 2018 Taipei race was decided by about 3,254 votes (0.24%), and its recount fight dragged on for months. A similar case could delay final certification, though probably not past June 2027.
  - **Election lawsuits:** A court could later annul a result, but that would not change who takes office.
  - **Late-November weather:** A typhoon already postponed the October 23 ballot-number draw. Late-November typhoons are rare.
- **The field:** Six candidates are on the ballot, numbered in an October 27 draw (CNA, Oct 27, 2026; TVBS, Oct 27, 2026):
  1. Guo Xi (郭璽)
  2. Lin Chih-cheng (林志成)
  3. Puma Shen (沈伯洋), DPP
  4. Hsiao Wen-chien (蕭文乾)
  5. Chiang Wan-an (蔣萬安), KMT incumbent
  6. Tang Hsin-min (唐新民)

  The race is effectively Shen against Chiang. The TPP did not run a candidate, under a KMT–TPP cooperation agreement for the local elections (East Asia Forum, Aug 19, 2026).
- **Poll blackout:** Taiwanese law bars publishing polls in the last 10 days before the vote, so the last public polls will appear around November 17–18.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

All historical figures in this section are from my own knowledge. I did not re-verify them in this session.

**A. Party performance in Taipei mayoral races since direct elections began in 1994 (8 races)**

- A DPP nominee won outright once: Chen Shui-bian in 1994, with 43.7%, when the KMT vote split with the New Party.
- A DPP-backed independent won twice: Ko Wen-je in 2014 and 2018. In 2018 Ko ran without DPP support, and the DPP's own candidate got about 17%.
- The KMT won in 1998, 2002, 2006, 2010 and 2022.
- Base rate for a DPP-party nominee winning: 1 of 8 (~12%). Counting DPP-aligned candidates: 2–3 of 8.
- Results in head-to-head pan-green vs pan-blue races in Taipei:
  - 1998: Ma 51% vs Chen 46%
  - 2006: Hau 54%
  - 2010: Hau 56% vs Su 44%
  - 2014: Ko 57% vs Lien 41%, the one green-aligned win
- Taipei's partisan baseline leans blue by roughly 5–10 points in a normal year.

**B. Taipei incumbents seeking re-election**

- 1998: Chen Shui-bian lost, despite high approval ratings.
- 2002: Ma won.
- 2010: Hau won.
- 2018: Ko won by 0.24 points.
- Incumbents won 3 of 4, or 75%.

**C. Incumbent mayors of Taiwan's six big municipalities seeking re-election, 2010–2022**

This tally is approximate:
- 2010: 2 of 2 won (Hau, Hu).
- 2014: 3 of 5 won. Hu Chih-chiang (Taichung) and Wu Chih-yang (Taoyuan) lost in the anti-KMT wave.
- 2018: 2 of 3 won. Lin Chia-lung (Taichung) lost in the anti-DPP wave.
- 2022: 5 of 5 won.

Incumbents won about 12 of 15 (~80%). The losses came in wave years against the incumbent's party.

**D. Polling accuracy in Taipei**

- 2014 and 2022: final polls picked the right winner.
- 2018: most polls showed Ko comfortably ahead, but Ting Shou-chung nearly won after a late surge.
- Taiwanese polls have historically differed by pollster in ways that track media partisanship: blue-leaning outlets such as TVBS and UDN, green-leaning outlets such as SETN, Liberty Times-affiliated pollsters and some associations.
- Leads of about 5 points or more in the last pre-blackout polling averages have usually held, but 2018 shows that late swings of 5–10 points can happen in Taipei.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 6, 2026:** A second debate is still not scheduled. The two camps are blaming each other. KMT legislator Lo Chih-chiang attacked Shen's campaign chief Wu Szu-yao (China Times, Nov 6; Nov 4). Chiang said on Nov 3 that he was "inclined to attend" if the format could be agreed (World Journal). Six outlets have jointly invited both candidates: Liberty Times, CTV, TTV, FTV, CTS and Mnews (Focus Taiwan, Oct 31).
- **Nov 5:** A New Bloom analysis says it is unclear whether the second debate will happen and calls the Chiang camp reluctant (New Bloom, Nov 5). Shen says the race is about turnout: "if it's high enough, we win" (Business Today, Nov 5).
- **Nov 4:** ETtoday poll, fieldwork Nov 1–3: Chiang 50.7%, Shen 41.9%, a lead of 8.8 points. Chiang is down 1.3 points from ETtoday's previous poll (UDN, Nov 4; CTI News).
- **Nov 2–3:** Strategy and Public Research Institute of Taiwan (台灣韜略策進學會), post-debate poll (Taipei Times, Nov 3; Newtalk, Nov 2):
  - Shen 52.6%, Chiang 35.3%, margin of error ±2.9.
  - Shen leads in 11 of 12 districts; only Nangang favors Chiang.
  - Chiang's job performance: 48.3% dissatisfied, 38.4% satisfied.
  - More respondents now expect Shen to win than Chiang, the first time this pollster has found that.
- **~Nov 2:** ETtoday post-debate poll: Chiang 52.0%, Shen 40.7%. On who voters expect to win: Chiang 55.9%, Shen 39.0% (China Times, Nov 2; World Journal).
- **Nov 2:** Shen complains that Chiang read prepared material during the debate and asks that questions not be shared in advance. TVBS disputes his account (Taiwan News, Nov 2).
- **Oct 31:** The first and so far only televised debate, hosted by TVBS and SETN. TVBS same-day landline snap poll (1,747 responses):
  - Vote: Chiang 46%, Shen 42%.
  - Who won the debate: Shen 47%, Chiang 40% (Taiwan Tang Substack; Focus Taiwan, Oct 31).
  - TVBS's series shows Chiang's lead shrinking: 28 points (late May), 8 (early October), 4 (post-debate).
- **Oct 30:** Shen makes civil defense his top priority and pledges to serve the full four-year term and turn down any Cabinet post (Taiwan News, Oct 30).
- **Oct 28:** UDN poll, fieldwork Oct 20–22 and 25, n=1,115, ±3.0 (UDN; Taiwan News; ETtoday, Oct 28):
  - Vote: Shen 45%, Chiang 41%, 13% undecided.
  - Who voters expect to win: Chiang 48%, Shen 35%.
  - Chiang's job satisfaction: 50% satisfied, 37% dissatisfied.
  - TPP supporters: 75% Chiang, 16% Shen.
  - Independents: Shen 40%.
  - UDN is a blue-leaning paper, so a Shen lead in its poll stands out. Some commentators called it a KMT "get-out-the-vote" poll (SETN, Oct 28).
- **Oct 27:** Ballot numbers drawn: Shen #3, Chiang #5. ETtoday poll published the same day: Chiang 48.6%, Shen 41.1% (China Times, Oct 28).
- **~Oct 20:** Taipei Trust in Democracy Association (信民協會) poll: Shen 50.3%, Chiang 33.3%. Its September poll had Chiang 41.7%, Shen 40.5% (Taipei Times, Oct 20; Taipei Times, Sep 22).
- **Oct 27:** A DPP insider describes Shen's campaign as turning into a "movement" (Taipei Times, Oct 27).
- **Oct 3–7:** TVBS poll, n=1,120: Chiang 49%, Shen 41% (UDN; Wikipedia summary).
- **August:** Taiwan Public Opinion Research Center poll: Chiang 48.4%, Shen 33%.
- **June:** Z.Media poll: Chiang 55.2%, Shen 27.5%.
- **May 27:** TVBS poll: Chiang 58%, Shen 30%.
- **May 13, 2026:** The DPP nominates Shen. President Lai attends (Focus Taiwan, May 13).
- **Background from my own knowledge, before 2026:**
  - Shen is a legislator-at-large, a criminologist and founder of Doublethink Lab.
  - In 2025 China put him on sanctions and wanted lists.
  - The DPP-driven mass recall of KMT legislators failed completely in July–August 2025, a setback for the DPP.
  - Chiang won in 2022 with 42.3% in a three-way race. DPP candidate Chen Shih-chung got 31.9% and Huang Shan-shan 25.1%.

**Rough poll picture:**
- Every poll since mid-October lies somewhere between Chiang +11 and Shen +17.
- Blue-leaning or neutral pollsters have Chiang ahead by 4–11: TVBS, ETtoday.
- The exception is UDN, which has Shen +4.
- Green-leaning pollsters have Shen ahead by 17: SPRIT (韜略) and 信民協會.
- Every pollster's trend line has moved toward Shen since May.
- In every poll that asked, more voters expect Chiang to win than Shen, with the one exception of SPRIT.

**Scheduled before the close:**

- The last polls before the blackout, around Nov 17–18.
- Final-weekend rallies.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (Shen wins):**
- **Momentum continues:** Shen's rise from about 30% to about 42–45% keeps going, and undecided voters (about 12–24%) break toward him. This depends on his edge with independents and women, his debate advantage, and young voters turning out. Young turnout tends to be lower in local elections, and Shen himself has made turnout the deciding factor.
- **Dislike of Chiang:** If the SPRIT and 信民協會 polls are right, Chiang's net approval is negative. That conflicts with UDN, which found him 50–37 satisfied.
- **Debate avoidance backfires:** If Chiang is seen as dodging a second debate, it could hurt him. If a second debate happens, Shen, who is generally seen as the better debater, could gain from it.
- **Polls understate Shen:** Blue-leaning landline polls may miss younger, mobile-only voters.

**Paths to NO (Chiang wins):**
- **Taipei's built-in KMT lean:** The city's structural blue tilt plus the advantages of incumbency. Incumbents won about 75–80% of the time in reference classes B and C.
- **Consolidated opposition vote:** TPP voters back Chiang 75–16, and with no TPP candidate the anti-DPP vote is united, unlike in 2018 and 2022.
- **Voters expect Chiang:** Most polls show voters expect him to win, and this measure has often predicted Taiwanese results better than stated vote intention.
- **Midterm penalty for the ruling party:** Local elections tend to punish the party holding the presidency. The DPP lost badly in 2018 and 2022.
- **Late consolidation:** Blue voters who are "silent" in polls, as the KMT claims, come home in the final week, as Ting's voters did in 2018.
- **Shen's China profile cuts both ways:** It rallies his base but may put off some swing voters who are wary of tension with Beijing.

## 5. Signposts

- **Last polls before the Nov 18 blackout:**
  - The strongest signal toward Shen would be TVBS or ETtoday showing him tied or ahead.
  - The strongest signal toward Chiang would be UDN reverting to a Chiang lead, or green-leaning polls narrowing to single digits.
  - The "who will win" expectation numbers crossing over in blue-leaning polls would be a strong sign for Shen.
- **Second debate:** Whether it is held, and snap reactions to it. If Chiang is seen as refusing, that probably helps Shen a little.
- **Late developments:** Scandals or legal issues for either candidate; Chinese military drills or sanctions-related news; how heavily Ko Wen-je and the TPP campaign for Chiang.
- **Turnout signals:** Absentee-voting debates, how many young people return home to vote, and the size of the final-weekend rallies.
- **Taipei City Council party-list or council polling,** as a check on partisan baselines.
- **On election night,** early precinct returns from Da'an, Wenshan and Neihu, which have been blue strongholds.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **I could not open any article pages directly; the fetch requests were refused.** Everything above comes from search-result summaries. Exact field dates, sample sizes and methods (landline, mobile or online share) could not be checked for several polls, especially SPRIT, 信民協會 and ETtoday.
- **The house effects are large: about a 25-point spread between pollsters.** That makes any average sensitive to which pollsters you trust. I found no independent aggregator or track record for the 2026 pollsters.
- **I could not find President Lai's current approval rating, the national party ID numbers, or any Ko Wen-je/TPP endorsement activity in this race.** I also found nothing on the four minor candidates. Their combined vote share is unknown, but each is probably under 2%.
- **Whether a second debate will take place is unresolved.**
- **The historical base rates in Section 2 are from my own knowledge and were not re-verified here.**
