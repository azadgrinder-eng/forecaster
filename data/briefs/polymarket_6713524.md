# Research brief: Will Israel carry out a qualifying strike on Lebanon on Saturday, 28 November 2026 (Jerusalem time)?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** at least one strike started by Israel that hits Lebanese land or internal waters at any point from 00:00 to 23:59 Jerusalem time on 28 Nov 2026. The strike must be one of these:
  - an air strike: bombs, air-to-surface missiles, or air-launched drones; or
  - a surface-to-surface strike: one-way attack drones, or cruise or ballistic missiles.
- **What does not count:** artillery, mortars, MLRS rocket artillery, naval gunfire, small-arms or machine-gun fire, ground incursions, demolitions or "explosions" of buildings, FPV drones, short-range loitering munitions, ATGMs, intercepted munitions, and threats.
  - This matters a lot here. Lebanon's National News Agency (NNA) daily reports usually mix "airstrikes, artillery shelling, explosions [demolitions], machine-gun fire" (Anadolu, 13 Nov 2026). Only the airstrike part qualifies.
- **Who decides:** a consensus of credible reporting plus official Israeli and Lebanese government and military statements. Normally this means NNA, Lebanon's Health Ministry, IDF statements, and wire services. If reports conflict, the market can stay open up to 3 extra calendar days.
- **Edge cases:**
  1. **Strikes inside the Israeli-held "security zone."** Israel has occupied parts of south Lebanon since the 2026 war, and Katz says this ground will become a permanent security zone (Oct 2026). The rules count disputed territory only if it was "claimed by and under the de facto control of Lebanon." A strict reading could exclude strikes inside the zone. Most forecasters will probably treat the zone as Lebanese land, because the border itself isn't disputed. Still, strikes *outside* the zone, like Qabrikha on 4 Nov, are the clean case.
  2. **Small drones.** When IDF quadcopters drop grenades or stun grenades, it's unclear whether they count as "air-launched drones" or as excluded "minor" strikes.
  3. **Timing near midnight.** Jerusalem time is UTC+2 in November, so strikes reported late on 27 Nov or early on 29 Nov need careful timestamping.
  4. **Day of week.** 28 Nov 2026 is a Saturday (Shabbat). The IDF has historically struck Lebanon on Saturdays, so this is a minor factor.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **The November 2024 to February 2026 ceasefire.** Israeli air and drone strikes happened on most days. UNIFIL counted more than 10,000 Israeli violations, about one every 53 minutes (Arab News, cited 2026). Monthly summaries from ALMA, a pro-Israel research group, recorded strikes almost every week (ALMA monthly summaries, Dec 2025 to Feb 2026). My rough estimate is that 70–90% of days had at least one qualifying strike. I did not count this day by day.
2. **The March to June 2026 war and the "ceasefire" that began 17 April.** Strikes were effectively daily. ACLED counted at least 565 Israeli air and drone strikes between 17 April and 14 May, about 20 a day (ACLED, May 2026). ALMA counted 328 strike waves in 19–26 May and 281 in 8–14 June. Daily rate: about 100%.
3. **The lull after the 19–20 June understandings / framework agreement, roughly late June to early November 2026.** ALMA reported "since 20 June, the IDF has not conducted any airstrikes in Lebanon" (ALMA, 15–21 June report). There were sporadic incidents: retaliatory strikes killed four people in early September (NBC, about Sept 2026), and UNIFIL counted 455 projectiles over a weekend in August (UN News, Aug 2026). But Times of Israel called the 4 Nov strike "the first southern Lebanon IDF airstrike in weeks." In this regime, probably fewer than 20–30% of days had a qualifying strike.
4. **The most recent window, 4–13 Nov 2026.** Air strikes were reported on 4, 5, 6, 10, 12 and 13 Nov: at least 6 of 10 days (CFR tracker; Anadolu, 12–13 Nov). Coverage of the other days may simply be missing from my searches, so this is a lower bound.

**What this means:** the answer depends heavily on which regime holds on 28 Nov. If the November pace continues, a given day lands at about 60–90%. If Israel returns to the June–October lull, it is much lower.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)
- **13 Nov (Fri):** Israeli warplanes carried out two strikes near Khiam (Marjayoun district). There was also artillery, demolitions and machine-gun fire in four districts, drones over Beirut, and no reported casualties (Anadolu, 13 Nov 2026).
- **12 Nov:** Airstrikes hit Haddatha (Bint Jbeil district) and Al-Mansouri, plus Kfar Kila after an earlier explosion, along with artillery fire (Anadolu, 12 Nov; CFR tracker).
- **11 Nov:**
  - US envoy Mike Waltz said he would visit Lebanon "next week," around 16–20 Nov, to push changes to UNIFIL and called the force a "human shield" for terrorists (Israel Hayom, 11 Nov 2026).
  - The US opposes renewing UNIFIL, whose mandate ends 31 Dec 2026 (L'Orient Today).
  - A lower-quality Substack post says Washington now wants Hezbollah disarmed across all of Lebanon (11 Nov).
- **10 Nov:** The CFR tracker lists Israeli air strikes and shelling.
- **Early to mid Nov:** Lebanon's Health Ministry put the death toll from Israeli attacks since 2 March at 4,390. It was above 4,300 in early September, which implies only about 90 deaths in roughly two months and points to a low-intensity period (Anadolu, Nov 2026; NBC, Sept 2026).
- **~6 Nov:** Al Jazeera reported Lebanese stocking up on supplies amid fears of a new Israeli escalation. The CFR tracker lists more strikes.
- **5 Nov:** An Israeli air strike on a car between Aynata and Aitaroun killed three girls aged 10–14 and their grandmother, causing outrage across Lebanon (CFR tracker).
- **4 Nov:** A drone strike on a motorcycle in Qabrikha, outside the buffer zone, killed one person. The IDF said the targets were "observing IDF troops." This was the "first airstrike in weeks" (Times of Israel; 24NewsHD, 4 Nov 2026). The IDF said it "remains committed to the agreement between Israel and Lebanon" (ZeroHedge/SGT Report, Nov 2026).
- **Post-election period, since 27 Oct:** Israel held elections on 27 Oct 2026, and coalition talks are ongoing (Times of Israel live blog, 12 Nov). Defence Minister Katz says the government doesn't need the opposition's permission to launch operations in Lebanon during this period. The Jerusalem Post judged that many of Katz's election-season threats were political posturing.
- **October:**
  - **5 Oct:** Netanyahu said Israel will keep striking Hezbollah.
  - **6 Oct:** Hezbollah leader Qassem said "resistance" continues while the occupation continues.
  - **~7 Oct:** The IDF shifted to a "forward-defense" posture and is preparing winter positions; an Israeli source said troops will stay for years (Long War Journal, Oct 2026).
  - **~20 Oct:** Katz threatened strikes on Beirut and a deeper advance (Shafaq).
- **Background:**
  - The war restarted on 2 March 2026, when Hezbollah fired on Israel in support of Iran.
  - A truce began on 17 April. US-mediated Israel–Lebanon talks led to the June framework / 19 June understandings, which tie Israeli withdrawal to Hezbollah's disarmament. Hezbollah is not part of the talks, and the talks are deadlocked (PBS; House of Commons Library; Ynet).
  - The US–Iran conflict has flared repeatedly through 2026; the latest dated Iranian strike on Bahrain I found was 1 Sept (Shafaq).
- **Scheduled before 28 Nov:**
  - Waltz's Lebanon visit (week of 16 Nov).
  - Israeli coalition formation, which may produce a new government.
  - US-mediated military-track talks, timing unclear.
  - 27 Nov: second anniversary of the November 2024 ceasefire.
  - Ongoing debate over UNIFIL's replacement ahead of 31 Dec.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
- **YES paths:**
  1. **The November pace simply continues.** Routine "threat removal" drone strikes on people near IDF positions, plus strikes on suspected Hezbollah infrastructure. This needs no new escalation, only the status quo of an Israeli army deployed in south Lebanon, Hezbollah probing the zone, and a permissive Israeli government.
  2. **Escalation.** A Hezbollah attack kills IDF soldiers (the June precedent: 5 soldiers killed, followed by a surge of strikes), or Israel acts on its "expand operations" or Beirut threats, or the Iran front flares again.
  3. **Pressure tactics.** Israel steps up strikes to pressure Lebanon over full disarmament or the post-UNIFIL arrangements.
- **NO paths:**
  1. **A return to the June–October lull.** Examples: US pressure during Waltz's visit, a diplomatic breakthrough, or the Israeli military restricting itself to ground and artillery fire.
  2. **The day falls in a gap.** Even at a 60–80% daily rate, any single day can be quiet. Quiet days are more likely if activity is limited to demolitions, artillery and machine-gun fire, which don't qualify.
  3. **Resolution technicalities.** Strikes occur only inside the occupied zone and get excluded under the de facto-control clause, or they are small-drone grenade drops that don't count.

## 5. Signposts
- **Toward YES:**
  - Daily NNA and Anadolu reports keep listing "warplanes" or "drone strikes" between 15 and 27 Nov.
  - Hezbollah attacks causing IDF casualties.
  - Katz or a new government announces an expanded operation.
  - Waltz's visit ends with no progress, or the US backs Israeli pressure.
  - IDF statements about "removing threats."
  - Iran-front escalation.
- **Toward NO:**
  - Several consecutive days with no reported air or drone strikes.
  - A US-announced de-escalation, or a monitoring mechanism agreed during Waltz's visit.
  - Progress in the Israel–Lebanon talks.
  - Explicit Israeli restraint, for example to avoid upsetting the US over the post-UNIFIL arrangements.
- **Resolution-relevant:** how reporters describe strike locations (inside or outside the buffer zone) and munition types (warplane, drone, artillery).

## 6. Uncertainties
- I could not get a day-by-day count of qualifying strikes for October or November 2026. The 6-of-10 figure for early November is a lower bound built from fragmentary search results; I couldn't open full articles because fetching was blocked by the proxy.
- I couldn't establish how long the "weeks" without airstrikes before 4 Nov lasted, or whether occasional drone strikes during the lull were simply underreported.
- I don't know the state of Israeli coalition formation, whether a new defence minister or policy is coming, or what Waltz's visit produced.
- The status of the US–Iran front in November 2026 is unclear; the latest firm data point is September.
- It's unclear how the market will treat strikes inside the Israeli-occupied zone, and small-drone munitions.
- Some claims come from secondary or aggregator sources: the CFR tracker excerpts, a Substack post, ZeroHedge/SGT Report, and ALMA, which is a pro-Israel advocacy group.
