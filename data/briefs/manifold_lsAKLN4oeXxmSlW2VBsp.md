## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Direct-to-consumer biohacking or "cosmetic" health products sold without FDA trials, and whether major outlets later call them scams.** Mainstream coverage of Lumina (Scientific American 2024; Undark/ASBMB April–June 2024; SF Standard Feb 2025; McGill OSS) was skeptical: it raised safety and efficacy concerns and said the company avoided the FDA. None of the coverage I found used fraud or "scam" framing. Products like this usually fade quietly (pauses, pivots, shutdowns) rather than being exposed as fraud. That makes a new major-outlet "scam" story in a 20-day window rare. My rough estimate is a single-digit percent chance per month, absent a trigger event.
2. **Small, angel-funded biotech startups with a failed first product.** Many of these wind down within one to three years. But they rarely announce it in a specific three-week window. Lantern's stated plan was about six months of re-engineering followed by a volunteer colonization test (LessWrong). That would be a self-run test, not an institutionally overseen trial.
3. **Subjective Manifold markets with no clear trigger at close.** Creators often resolve on their own reading of the evidence, or extend the close. I could not get hard frequencies. This market has stayed open through both the website outage and the sales-pause news, which suggests the creator is not reading those as YES.

**NO paths:** No FDA approval, IRB-overseen trial, or published human efficacy data has been reported. The company has said it would not publish its data, to protect its intellectual property (earlier coverage via search summaries). The chance of a NO trigger in 20 days looks very low.
