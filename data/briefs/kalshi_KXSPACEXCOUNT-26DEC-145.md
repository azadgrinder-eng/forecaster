# Research brief: Will SpaceX have more than 145 launches in 2026?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** SpaceX must have more than 145 launches in calendar 2026, so at least 146. Launches through 31 Dec 2026 count. The market closes 1 Jan 2027 at 15:00 UTC.
- **Missing rules:** The question text does not name a resolution source. It also does not say which vehicles count (Falcon 9, Falcon Heavy, Starship) or whether failed or suborbital launches count. This is the most important ambiguity.
  - **If all SpaceX vehicles count**, as with NextSpaceflight's "SpaceX overall" tally or the Spaceflight Now launch log, Starship adds about 4–5 launches this year.
  - **If only Falcon launches count**, reaching 146 is close to arithmetically impossible (see section 3).
  - **For comparison:** a similar forecasting question (RAND Forecasting Initiative) counted every SpaceX orbital launch attempt (Falcon 9, Falcon Heavy and Starship) regardless of outcome, using Spaceflight Now's launch log. Starship flights 12–14 may have been suborbital. If the market counts only orbital launches, its count could come in 1–3 lower.
- **Date edge cases:** UTC versus local date matters for late-December launches near midnight. Trackers also disagree by 1–2 launches on how they treat Falcon Heavy and on milestone dates.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **SpaceX's start-of-year guidance versus what it actually flew.** SpaceX usually ends a few percent below its January targets.
   - 2024: target about 144 Falcon launches; actual 134 Falcon (138 including Starship) (Space.com, Aug 2026).
   - 2025: target about 170–180; actual 165 Falcon (Space.com, Jan 2026).
   - 2026: Gwynne Shotwell's guidance was "140 to 145-ish" Falcon 9 launches (Cryptobriefing/NewSpaceEconomy, Apr 2026). Hitting the top of that range and adding about 5 Starship flights would give roughly 150 in total. If the usual 2–7% shortfall repeats, the Falcon 9 count would be about 134–141.
2. **Late-year launch counts.** December 2025 had 13 SpaceX launches (SpaceNexus summary). That came near the peak of the 2025 cadence of about 14 per month. In 2026 the monthly cadence is lower (see section 3).
3. **Extrapolating the 2026 pace.**
   - At the full-year average (123 Falcon launches in 316 days, about 0.39 per day), the remaining 40 days would add about 15–16 Falcon launches.
   - At the recent pace (Falcon 9 #100 on about 22 Aug to #120 on about 11–12 Nov: 20 launches in 81 days, about 0.25 per day), they would add only about 10.
   - The recent pace is the better guide because of the structural slowdown described below.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)
- **Nov 19–23:** Several Vandenberg Falcon 9 government missions were scheduled: USSF-317 (dates listed variously as 17–21 Nov), NROL-136 (21 Nov), USSF-266 (23 Nov) and USSF-380 (27–28 Nov). One preview said a launch that week would be the 122nd Falcon 9 flight of 2026 (NSF launch preview, week of 16 Nov 2026). I could not confirm which of these had flown by 21 Nov.
- **Nov 16:** One tracker counted 127 SpaceX launches in 2026, out of 269 orbital launch attempts worldwide (search summary of trackers, about 16 Nov).
- **Nov 13–14:** Starship Flight 15 deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites, and the ship landed from orbit for the first time (Space.com live blog, 14 Nov 2026). That is Starship's 4th flight of 2026 (flights 12–15).
- **Nov 11–12:** USSF-627 flew. NextSpaceflight lists it as the 120th Falcon 9 mission of 2026 and the 126th SpaceX launch overall. Wikipedia counts 123 Falcon-family launches as of 12 Nov (120 Falcon 9 plus 3 Falcon Heavy).
- **Nov 6:** USSF-432 Falcon 9 flew from Vandenberg after repeated delays from 1 Nov (Spaceflight Now, 6 Nov 2026). Only 2 Falcon launches flew in 1–12 Nov.
- **Rest of the manifest:**
  - Cygnus NG-25 on Falcon 9 from SLC-40, 29 Nov.
  - December listings as of 16–17 Nov: only 4 SpaceX launches, none with a firm date. They include Globalstar-2R mission 2, CHORUS, SDA Tranche 2 Transport Layer D (Florida) and Layer E (Vandenberg). USSF-31, Falcon Heavy USSF-75 and NROL-195 are also mentioned as possible.
  - Starlink missions usually appear on trackers only a few weeks ahead, so December is probably under-listed.
- **Starship Flight 16:** FAA/CADENA planning material shows NET 10 Dec 2026, flying from LC-39A as Starlink Group 30-1 (tech-insider, about 27 Oct 2026). NSF analysis suggests it may slip to late January 2027. Kiko Dontchev said on 21 Oct that the pad was effectively complete and SpaceX hoped to launch from Florida before year end.
- **Oct 24–31:** KeepTrack counted 118 Falcon 9 flights from January through October. The shortfall against 2025 is mostly fewer Starlink launches.
- **Florida Starlink pause:** Since 25 Aug, Starlink launches have moved entirely to California. The next Florida Starlink launch is planned for Starship, and there was a gap of at least 6 weeks with no Florida Falcon launches (SpaceLaunchSchedule; KeepTrack, 24 Oct 2026). A Substack analysis projected 143 Falcon 9 launches for the year and described the slowdown as a deliberate shift toward Starship. A June 2026 SpaceX filing reportedly expects Falcon 9 launches to decline.
- **Feb 2026:** Falcon 9 was briefly grounded after an upper-stage anomaly on a Starlink mission. Details are thin (KeepTrack, 4 Feb 2026).

**My estimate for 21 Nov:** about 122–125 Falcon launches plus 4 Starship, so **about 127–130 in total**. Reaching 146 needs **about 16–19 more launches in about 40 days**, an average of 12–14 per month. Since August SpaceX has managed about 7–9 per month.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES (all-vehicle count):**
- Vandenberg runs at its maximum cadence on SLC-4E (and SLC-6, if it is active), about every 3–4 days, adding 10–12 launches.
- Florida Falcon launches pick up: NG-25 plus 3–5 December customer missions.
- Starship Flight 16 launches before 31 Dec.
- SpaceX pushes late-year launches to hit its guidance or for IPO optics. There were reports of a SpaceX stock event around 19 Nov.
- All of these together give roughly 146–150. YES requires almost everything to go right.

**Paths to NO:**
- The current pace of about 0.25 Falcon launches per day continues, adding about 10–11, plus Starship 0–1, for a year-end total of about 139–143.
- Any Falcon 9 anomaly or grounding, weather on the Pacific range, or slips of government missions (USSF and NRO missions often slip) would lower the count further.
- If the market counts Falcon only, the likely year-end is about 135–141, which is NO with near certainty.

## 5. Signposts
- **Exact year-to-date count on 21 Nov:** whether 0, 1, 2 or 3 of the Nov 16–21 Vandenberg missions flew. Each launch changes the required pace.
- **Late-November count:** if the total stays below about 133 by 30 Nov, YES becomes very unlikely. A total of 135 or more would make it live.
- **Florida Starlink:** whether Falcon 9 Starlink launches from Florida restart in December. That is the main source of upside.
- **Starship Flight 16:** a firm date before 31 Dec, such as an FAA licence modification or a static fire of Ship 43, would add 1 launch.
- **Anomalies:** any Falcon anomaly or FAA mishap investigation would push strongly toward NO.
- **Resolution source:** clarification of which vehicles count and which source is used, especially whether Starship counts.

## 6. Uncertainties
- I could not open primary trackers (Wikipedia, NextSpaceflight, NSF, KeepTrack). The connection was refused, so all counts come from search snippets and conflict by 1–3 launches.
- I could not confirm which launches flew between 16 and 21 Nov.
- The market's resolution source is unknown, as is whether it counts Starship and whether Starship flights 12–14 were orbital.
- December Starlink plans from Vandenberg are not public.
- The 143 Falcon 9 projection's date and method are unclear, and it conflicts with the 118-through-October count.
