# Research brief: "Will this market cause anyone to attend Metagame 2026?"

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** At least one person attends Metagame 2026 for at least one "full" day, by a reasonable reading of "full", and attends because of this market. The creator must receive "strong evidence" that both things happened.
- **Who decides:** Only the market creator. There is no objective data source. It is a self-referential, at-the-creator's-discretion question. "Strong evidence" is not defined. It could mean a credible public statement, a ticket bought with a discount code that appeared only in this market, or the creator's own knowledge.
- **The creator is probably an organizer, but this is unconfirmed.** The description is written in the first person ("We have…") and offers discount codes. Ricki Heicklen created earlier Metagame markets: "Which users will attend Metagame 2025?", "How many people will attend Metagame 2025?" and "Will the Metagame Conference take place Sept 12–14, 2025?" She is also named as a Metagame contact (Manifold Markets, 2025; Astral Codex Ten Open Thread 390). If the creator is an organizer, they can check ticket records, such as discount-code use, which makes strong evidence easier to get.
- **Timing:**
  - Metagame 2026 ran from 2:00pm Friday, November 6 to 9:00pm Sunday, November 8, 2026, at Lighthaven, 2740 Telegraph Ave, Berkeley (metagame.games, accessed via search Nov 2026).
  - Today is November 9, so the event is over and whatever attendance happened has already happened. What remains is whether evidence reaches the creator before the market closes on November 17, 2026.
  - Ticket sales appear to have closed October 29, 2026 (metagame.games key dates, via search). The same list gives a refund cutoff of October 6, which doesn't fit with the other dates.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Causation:** How much does the market need to have mattered? Would one of several reasons count? Would a discount code that swung a marginal buyer count?
  - **Organizers, staff and volunteers:** It's unclear whether people who were already going count.
  - **Free Night Market only:** On Nov 7 there was a free public "Metagame 2026 Night Market" (LessWrong events listing). Someone who attended only that probably doesn't meet "one full day". For 2025, a similar Manifold market excluded people who came only to public afterparties.
  - **Partial days:** Friday ran only from 2pm. Whether Friday alone counts as a "full" day is ambiguous.
  - **Trader incentives:** YES holders have a reason to claim causation. That could produce weak claims the creator rejects, or credible ones the creator accepts.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Self-referential "will this market cause X" markets on Manifold**
   - I could not find a clean historical frequency. Searches for "will this market cause/convince anyone to attend Manifest/Metagame" turned up no exact analogues.
   - Related self-attendance markets resolve both ways. "Will I attend Manifest 2023" (dreev) resolved YES; "Will @prismatic attend Manifest" (2026) resolved NO (Manifold, various dates).
   - As a rough prior, markets of the "will this market cause one person to do something cheap" type resolve YES fairly often. The bar is "anyone", one person is enough, and creators tend to read evidence generously. This is my judgment, not a counted rate.

2. **Single promotional post converting a marginal attendee**
   - Metagame 2025 had about 201–300 registered attendees (a resolved Manifold attendance market).
   - The audience overlaps heavily with Manifold users. The 2025 "which users will attend" market had 42 traders and resolved YES for many named Manifold users, including RickiHeicklen, patio11 and EmmettShear.
   - Tickets cost about $325–$425 (eventsforgamers / Pocket Gamer listings). The market offered discount codes; in 2025 a solved puzzle was worth $100 off (2025 promo, via search).
   - A post seen by dozens to hundreds of engaged, Bay Area-heavy users that also offers a discount has a non-trivial chance of converting at least one marginal buyer. But a marginal buyer is not the same as someone whose attendance was caused by the market, and someone still has to report it.

3. **Ricki Heicklen's / organizer-run event markets**
   - Her past Metagame and Manifest markets were factual (head counts, attendee lists) and were resolved.
   - I found no evidence about how she handles soft, causal criteria like this one.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES**
- **(a) Discount code traced to the market.** Someone used a discount code that appeared only in this market, or a puzzle linked from it, and the organizer-creator can see it in ticket sales. This is the strongest and easiest route if such a code existed and was used.
- **(b) Credible testimony.** A Manifold user comments or DMs that they went, or upgraded to full attendance, because of the market. The creator may know these people personally, since the community is small and Bay Area-based.
- **(c) Deliberate self-fulfilment.** A trader holding YES attends partly to make the market resolve YES. Self-referential markets often create this incentive. Creators usually count it, since the market did cause the attendance.

**Paths to NO**
- **(a) No one comes forward** before the creator resolves. Low-traffic markets often have no claimants.
- **(b) Claims fall short.** The claimed attendees were already planning to go, came only to the free Night Market, or attended less than a full day.
- **(c) Creator applies a strict counterfactual standard** and rules any claim not strong enough.
- **(d) Small audience.** Market reach was low: few traders and limited visibility so close to an event with sales already closed.

The outcome depends mainly on four things: how many people saw the market, whether it held a unique and traceable discount code, who the creator is (organizer or not), and how strictly the creator reads "cause".

## 5. Signposts

These are listed roughly from strongest to weakest effect.

- **A comment such as "I bought a ticket because of this market" or "used the code from here",** especially from a known user, would be a strong move toward YES.
- **Creator confirmation of a code redemption.** If the creator is an organizer and says the market's code was redeemed, that would be a strong move toward YES.
- **Creator stating a strict standard,** or rejecting an early claim, would move toward NO.
- **Low engagement.** If the market has few traders or comments and no claims by about Nov 12–15, with the event over, that moves toward NO. Attendees usually post soon after the event.
- **Post-event recaps** (LessWrong, Substack, Discord) that mention the market would move toward YES.
- **Confirmation of the creator's identity.** If the creator is not an organizer, they have to rely on self-reports, which slightly lowers the chance of strong evidence.

  - the creator's identity (Ricki Heicklen is a likely guess, but unconfirmed)

  - whether it contained a unique discount code
