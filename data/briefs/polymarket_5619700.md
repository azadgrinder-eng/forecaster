# Research brief: Will Ed Davey stop being Liberal Democrat leader by 31 December 2026?

*Prepared 26 November 2026. The proxy blocked every page I tried to open in full, so every fact below comes from search-result summaries. Check any single detail that matters before relying on it.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Ed Davey stops being Leader of the Liberal Democrats for any length of time between the market's creation and 31 December 2026, 11:59 PM ET.
- **Announcements count immediately.** If he announces a resignation or removal before the deadline, the market resolves YES even if he stays on until a successor is chosen. For example, "I will step down once a successor is elected in spring" would be YES.
- **Who decides:** official statements from Davey or the Lib Dems, or a consensus of credible reporting.
- **Time left:** about 5 weeks (26 Nov to 31 Dec), which includes the Christmas recess. Parliament usually rises around 18 December.
- **Edge cases:**
  - Death or incapacity would count. A temporary acting leader during illness is unclear but plausibly counts as "ceasing to be Leader for any period."
  - A no-confidence vote that triggers a leadership contest but does not formally remove him is ambiguous. It would probably be read as removal only if he stops being leader or announces he is going.
  - Leadership changes in the Welsh or Scottish parties do not count. David Chadwick became Welsh Lib Dem leader on 16 November 2026, and that is irrelevant here (Wikipedia, David Chadwick, accessed Nov 2026).
  - Hinting at a far-future succession without announcing a departure would presumably not count.
- **How a removal works under party rules:** rebels need a letter signed by a quarter of MPs (18 of 72). A majority of MPs must then vote no confidence to trigger a leadership contest (PoliticsHome / The Romiley Gazette, Oct 2026; Oxford Chronicle, 15 Oct 2026).

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Lib Dem leaders' exit rate, from my own knowledge.** Since 1988 there have been 8 leaders: Ashdown, Kennedy, Campbell, Clegg, Farron, Cable, Swinson and Davey. Seven exits over about 38 leader-years is roughly 0.18 exits a year, or about 1.5% a month.
- Three of those exits followed general elections: Clegg 2015, Farron 2017, Swinson 2019. No general election is expected before 31 December.
- The four exits between elections were Ashdown (1999, retired), Kennedy (Jan 2006, forced out), Campbell (Oct 2007, forced out) and Cable (2019, retired). That is about 0.105 a year.
- For a 5-week window, this gives a baseline of about 1–1.5%.

**B. Lib Dem leaders under open pressure from their MPs.** Kennedy and Campbell both fell fast once MPs moved against them openly.
- Kennedy: open MP revolt in late December 2005, resigned 7 January 2006, about 2–3 weeks later.
- Campbell: resigned 15 October 2007, within days of intense pressure.
- Davey is different. The open move against him (Maynard, 9 October 2026) has already failed, and about 6 weeks have passed without a second attempt.
- Lib Dem coups have tended to succeed quickly or not at all. That suggests the immediate danger has passed, but it does not rule out another attempt.

**C. UK party leaders who survived a challenge.** In a small sample from memory, May (December 2018), Johnson (June 2022) and Corbyn (2016) all survived a challenge.
- Johnson was gone within about 1 month and May within about 5 months. Corbyn stayed on.
- Roughly 1 in 3 fell within 2 months, but each time it took a new trigger: a scandal, ministers resigning en masse, or an election defeat.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (all low probability over 5 weeks):**
1. **A second coup succeeds.** It would need 18 signatures and then a majority of the 72 MPs. The October attempt could not even force a vote, and there is no consensus successor now that Cooper has ruled herself out. This would probably need a new trigger: a very bad poll run (for example Lib Dems at 7–8% and behind the Greens), more frontbench resignations, or a scandal.
2. **Davey goes voluntarily.** He could announce a timetable to step down before the next election, for family reasons (he is a carer for his disabled son) or because of the pressure. A Christmas or New Year announcement is a known pattern; Ashdown announced in January 1999. Because announcements count immediately, a December announcement of a 2027 departure would resolve YES.
3. **Scandal.** A damaging Post Office inquiry finding or a personal scandal.
4. **Health or death.** Very low probability.
5. **Coalition or an early election under Burnham.** Even if Burnham called an election, it would not happen before 31 December. A coalition would keep Davey in place, not remove him.

**Paths to NO (the default):**
- The status quo holds through the holiday period.
- Rebels lost momentum in October, the Cooper alternative is off the table, and MPs are reportedly being disciplined through the scorecards.
- Davey has consistently signalled he intends to fight the next election.
- Lib Dem polling has been stable at around 10–12% in November.

## 5. Signposts

**Would raise the probability of YES (from biggest move to smallest):**
- Another frontbench resignation, or reports that the 18-signature letter is circulating or complete.
- A senior figure such as Cooper, Munira Wilson, Layla Moran or Alistair Carmichael breaking publicly with Davey.
- Davey cancelling engagements, or reports of family or health problems.
- A Post Office inquiry report criticising him personally.
- Several polls with the Lib Dems below the Greens, or at 7–8%.
- Briefings that he is "considering his future" over Christmas.

**Would lower the probability of YES:**
- Quiet news through the Christmas recess.
- Davey's New Year message framing 2027 plans. He issued one for 2026, per libdems.org.uk.
- Rebels being given frontbench roles again.
- YouGov holding at 12% or better.
