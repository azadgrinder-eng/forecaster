# Research Brief: Will Russian diesel be imported into the US by Nov 30, 2026?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

**What counts as YES.** Diesel of Russian production origin must be physically delivered to a US port or onto US land, for commercial or government use in the US. It must arrive between market creation and **Nov 30, 2026, 11:59 PM ET**.

**The import also has to qualify in one of two ways:**
- a consensus of credible reporting says it was made under the Oct 9 Trump–Putin agreement (or an amended or successor arrangement), or
- it was authorized under OFAC General License 135 (or an amended or successor license).

**Who decides.** The market creator, based on a consensus of credible reporting.

**What does not count:**
- fuel a ship carries for its own engines
- cargo held in bond for re-export
- cargo passing through the US in transit to another country

**Edge cases and ambiguities:**
- **Ship arrives but doesn't unload.** A tanker that only anchors offshore or "for orders" near New York should not count. "Physically delivered to a US port" points to a discharge.
- **Ship-to-ship transfers or lightering** inside US waters are ambiguous.
- **Product refined elsewhere from Russian crude** (e.g. India or Turkey) would probably not count, because the rules require "Russian production origin" and a link to the agreement or GL 135.
- **Discreet discharges.** A buyer might unload quietly to avoid legal exposure. Ship-tracking firms (Kpler and others) and the press are watching closely, though, so a real discharge would very likely be reported.
- **US territories.** Whether Puerto Rico or the US Virgin Islands count as "terrestrial territory of the United States" isn't stated. They probably would.
- **Market creation date** isn't given. It is presumably around Oct 9–10.

**Legal background.** GL 135 was issued Oct 9, 2026 and runs to Apr 7, 2027. It authorizes the sale, delivery, offloading and importation of Russian-origin diesel, but only under two OFAC sanctions programs (31 CFR 587 and 589). It does **not** expressly address the 2022 Ending Importation of Russian Oil Act. That law bans US imports of Russian petroleum products (the "Chapter 27" fuels category) and can only be ended by a presidential certification followed by a congressional review period (Clyde & Co, Oct 2026; HSF Kramer, Oct 2026; diplomacyandlaw.com, Oct 2026).

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Russian diesel or petroleum-product imports into the US since the 2022 ban.** There have been **zero** from March/April 2022 to Nov 9, 2026, about 55 months. The first tanker sent in October 2026 was described as "the first Russian diesel shipment to the US since January 2022" (The Insider; National News, Oct 23, 2026). That is a very low base rate, but it applied while no license existed.

2. **US cargoes enabled by an OFAC general license where the legal basis was clear.** Example: the Chevron/Venezuela licenses (GL 41, 2022). Under these, cargoes typically arrived within weeks; I'd put it near 100% within about 6 weeks. This is from memory, not verified in this session.
   - **Key difference:** in those cases no statute contradicted the license. Here, the binding problem is that the license appears to conflict with a statute.

3. **Licensed but legally contested or reputationally toxic flows, where private firms "over-comply".** Examples:
   - Russia-related energy carve-outs that went little used
   - Iran humanitarian channels
   
   Firms facing unclear criminal or civil exposure usually stay out. A rough heuristic, not a measured frequency: the large majority of such channels see little or no uptake in the first 1–2 months.
   - **Counter-example:** in January 2018 a controversial Russian (Yamal) LNG cargo did discharge at Everett, Massachusetts. That import was clearly legal, however.

**Early evidence for the current case.** Of the first 3 cargoes declared for the US, at least 2 had diverted within about 4 weeks (Kpler via FT and AP, Nov 5–6, 2026). Of 8 monitored tankers, 7 had declared the US as their destination (RFE/RL, Nov 3). As of Nov 9, none had confirmed a US discharge.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 7–9, 2026:** No Russian diesel has reached the US. Al Jazeera (Nov 7) reports traders refusing to buy because they fear legal action. CNN (Nov 7) still describes a tanker as "headed for US shores."
  - One report says a third tanker has followed the first two in turning away. It's unclear whether that means the Agioi Fanendes.
  - Vesseltracker reportedly describes the Agioi Fanendes as having "aborted delivery" in New York. I could not verify this.
- **Nov 6, 2026:** France 24 calls it Trump's "stillborn diesel deal."
  - Euromaidan Press: the Agioi Fanendes' last position on free tracking sites was Oct 28, off northern Scotland, heading west.
  - HuffPost: a White House official confirmed the administration's interest in the cargo.
  - Lawmakers warned banks, port authorities and fuel distributors that facilitating imports could bring civil or criminal liability. Rep. Raskin cited up to 20 years in prison (RFE/RL; Texas Politics, Nov 3–6).
- **Nov 5–6, 2026:** Two tankers diverted (AP/PBS; Meduza; FT via Georgia Today):
  - **Talisman** was off Algeria in the Mediterranean on Nov 5.
  - **Leruo** (Sierra Leone-flagged, Hong Kong-owned, EU/UK-sanctioned) switched its destination to Brazil.
  
  Kpler's Matt Smith on the Agioi Fanendes: "not guaranteed to discharge… may yet divert." He also said it "has been slow in the last few days."
- **Nov 5, 2026:** US diesel inventories are below a 20-year low (Ukrainska Pravda).
- **Nov 3, 2026:** Ukrainian monitoring tracks 8 tankers carrying about 300,800 t of Russian diesel, 7 of them declaring the US, with arrivals expected from Nov 7. Four (Leruo, Primorye, Manta, Feliks) are sanctioned by the EU, UK or Ukraine. Advocacy groups have identified no US buyer or terminal (RFE/RL, Nov 3).
- **Nov 3, 2026:** Reps. Doggett and Kaptur asked CBP Commissioner Rodney Scott whether CBP will refuse entry, seize cargo, or refer attempted imports to Homeland Security Investigations and the DOJ. I found no CBP response. US midterm elections were held Nov 3.
- **Nov 2, 2026:** New Jersey Gov. Mikie Sherrill wrote to NJ port and terminal operators. She said importing Russian diesel violates the 2022 Act and that GL 135 cannot take effect without a congressional review process (under CAATSA §216) that has not begun. She warned of civil and criminal penalties.
- **Late Oct 2026:** A bipartisan backlash: four Republican senators joined Democrats, and Bloomberg Government reported a "growing GOP revolt."
  - Bessent says the Graham sanctions law does not bar the purchases and that Treasury will act "with all the legal authorities we have." He also denied this amounts to sanctions relief (The Hill; Hans India).
- **Oct 27, 2026:** The energy secretary told CNBC the administration may let more Russian diesel onto the market.
- **Oct 22–23, 2026:** The Agioi Fanendes (Malta flag, Greek owner, IMO 9798375) loaded about 40,000 t (~300,000 bbl) of ultra-low-sulfur diesel at Primorsk. It signalled New York with an ETA of Nov 7, then switched to "for orders." The owner told the FT: "discharging port is not confirmed yet."
- **Oct 15, 2026:** Lawfare (Scott Anderson) argues GL 135 "seems unlikely to lead to an actual increase in US imports." Criminal exposure could be pardoned, but civil fines (up to the cargo's value) and vessel seizure remain risks. Customs penalties can be pursued for 5 years, which spans into a future administration (OilPrice).
- **Oct 9, 2026:** Trump announced the deal: 300,000 t of diesel "immediately," 500,000 t in November, then 1 million t. Treasury issued GL 135. Who pays, what price, and which companies are involved was never disclosed (NPR; CBS; CFR).
- **Wider context:** There is a severe diesel squeeze following the Iran war that began Feb 28, 2026:
  - US retail diesel was about $6/gal in September.
  - Distillate stocks are at the lowest seasonal level since 1982, and East Coast stocks hit a record low.
  - EIA expects inventories below 5-year lows through most of 2027 (Argus; Anadolu; Bloomberg via BNN, Sep 2026).

**Scheduled events before Nov 30:** none confirmed. Things that could happen:
- arrival or diversion of the Agioi Fanendes and the other declared tankers
- a CBP response to the Doggett/Kaptur letter
- Treasury FAQs or an amended GL 135
- congressional action under CAATSA review
- litigation (none filed as of the latest reports)

**Shipping time:** a Baltic-to-US East Coast voyage takes about 12–16 days. A cargo loaded by about Nov 14 could still arrive in time.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES:**
- **(a) The Agioi Fanendes (or another cargo already at sea) finds a buyer and unloads.** This depends on someone willing to accept the 5-year civil, customs and criminal exposure. A politically aligned or small, closely held trader is the most likely candidate. A terminal outside New Jersey would avoid Sherrill: Gulf Coast states, Philadelphia, or Puerto Rico.
- **(b) A government buyer.** For example, the Defense Logistics Agency, a reserve, or another federal channel takes delivery, so no private firm bears the risk. I found no reporting that this is planned.
- **(c) The administration lowers legal risk.** Possible steps:
  - a DOJ/OLC opinion or CBP entry guidance
  - a presidential certification under the 2022 Act
  - an amended GL 135
  - explicit non-enforcement assurances
  
  Even then, private firms would discount any assurance that a future administration could reverse within the 5-year limitation period.
- **(d) A courtesy discharge.** The Kremlin and the White House push a small symbolic cargo through to "prove" the deal works.

**Paths to NO:**
- **(a) Continued buyer and terminal refusal.** No US buyer or terminal has been identified (Nov 3). Banks have been told to preserve records. State-level pressure is in place, starting with New Jersey/New York.
- **(b) Diversions keep going.** Cargoes head to Brazil, the Mediterranean or West Africa, where diesel is also tight, so sellers don't need the US.
- **(c) Political momentum fades.** The midterms (Nov 3) have passed, which removes part of the pre-election price motive.
- **(d) Enforcement.** CBP or the DOJ blocks or seizes a cargo, or a court injunction does. Litigation is possible, though none has been filed yet.
- **(e) The vessels themselves add risk.** Several are sanctioned by the EU and UK, which complicates insurance and financing.

## 5. Signposts

**Would push strongly toward YES:**
- AIS (ship-tracking) or Kpler data showing a Russian-origin cargo berthed or discharging at a US terminal
- a named US buyer or terminal
- a CBP statement that it will process entries under GL 135
- a DOJ/OLC opinion
- a government purchase announcement
- Trump publicly pressuring for a delivery or naming a destination port

**Would push toward NO:**
- the Agioi Fanendes changing its destination away from the US or selling elsewhere
- the remaining declared tankers dropping US destinations
- CBP saying it will enforce the 2022 Act
- a lawsuit or injunction
- Congress moving a CAATSA §216 joint resolution of disapproval
- administration officials pivoting to "the diesel goes to global markets" framing (which is already partly visible: "supply… to global markets")

**Timing:** if nothing is unloaded by about Nov 20 and no new US-declared loadings appear at Primorsk by about Nov 15, YES becomes very hard to reach given transit times.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **The Agioi Fanendes' exact current position and status (as of Nov 8–9).** One vessel-tracking site says it aborted its New York delivery. Other sources say it is still heading to the US, slowly, "for orders." I could not check AIS directly.
- **Whether the "third tanker diverted" report refers to the Agioi Fanendes** or another ship.
- **Whether any federal government channel is set up to buy.** I found no evidence either way.
- **CBP's actual operating position** on entries. No public answer found.
- **Whether a quiet, small or partial discharge would be reported** and would meet the "consensus" standard.
- **Market creation date,** and whether a US territory port counts.
- **Status of the remaining tankers from the Nov 3 list of 8** (Primorye, Manta, Feliks and others). I couldn't confirm their current destinations.
