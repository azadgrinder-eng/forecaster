# Research Brief: Will Chinese and Philippine military forces clash before 2027? (as of Nov 20, 2026)

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** an "incident involving the use of force" between Chinese military forces and Philippine military forces, between market creation and Dec 31, 2026, 11:59 PM ET. The rules give examples: melee weapons, missile strikes, artillery fire, exchange of gunfire, or other direct military engagement.
- **Ramming:** it counts only if it is intentional and causes significant damage (for example, a hole in the hull) or sinks a *military* ship. Scrapes and dents do not count.
- **What does not count:** warning shots, artillery fire into uninhabited areas, and missiles landing in territorial waters or passing through airspace.
- **The coast guard asymmetry matters most:**
  - The China Coast Guard (CCG) counts as Chinese military.
  - The Philippine Coast Guard (PCG) does not count as Philippine military. By the same logic, the fisheries bureau (BFAR) and civilian fishermen almost certainly do not count either.
  - So the many CCG water-cannon attacks and rammings against PCG and BFAR ships do **not** qualify. The Philippine side has to be the Navy, Marines, Air Force or other armed forces personnel or assets.
- **When the window opens:** the current market's URL slug ends in "20260723214807932", which suggests it was created around **July 23, 2026**. If so, the July 20, 2026 baton incident falls *before* the window. There is also an identically titled earlier market without the timestamp suffix (Polymarket event pages; Lines.com). One aggregator said it was "not yet settled" whether the July 20 incident qualifies. The new market appearing three days after that incident suggests the original was treated as resolved, or was going to resolve, on it, but I could not confirm this. If true, it sets a useful precedent: a CCG baton strike that injured a Philippine Navy sailor and damaged a navy rubber boat was treated as a qualifying "melee weapon" encounter.
- **Who decides:** a "consensus of credible reporting," judged in practice by Polymarket and UMA.
- **Edge cases:**
  - Water cannon is not listed as either qualifying or excluded. Water cannon against a navy-crewed boat that injures personnel is a gray zone.
  - Navy-chartered civilian resupply boats crewed partly by navy personnel (such as the Unaizah May boats in 2024) are ambiguous.
  - Lasers, fire-control radar lock-on and near-collisions are probably not "use of force."
  - The two governments usually give conflicting accounts (China often says Philippine sailors attacked first), which could cloud the "consensus" test.
- **Time left:** about 41 days (Nov 20 to Dec 31, 2026).

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. CCG or PLA physical force directly against Philippine military personnel or assets, with injury or damage (the closest reference class).** These are my counts from the record:
- **Clear-cut:**
  - Jun 17, 2024, Second Thomas Shoal: the CCG boarded navy rigid-hull inflatable boats with blades and axes, and a sailor's thumb was severed.
  - Jul 20, 2026: a CCG officer hit a navy sailor on the head with a wooden baton and a navy rubber boat was damaged (USNI News, Jul 20, 2026; AP/ABC, Jul 20, 2026).
- **Arguable:**
  - Mar 5 and Mar 23, 2024: water cannon against navy-chartered resupply boats injured navy personnel.
  - Dec 10, 2023: ramming and water cannon on resupply boats, with the armed forces chief aboard an escort boat.
  - Aug 2023: water cannon on chartered resupply boats.
- **2025:** I found no qualifying navy-involved use-of-force incident. The 2025 force was aimed at PCG and BFAR ships (Aug 2025 Scarborough chase, Oct 12, 2025 Thitu ramming of a BFAR ship).
- **Implied rate:** about 2 clear-cut events in roughly 3.3 years (mid-2023 to Nov 2026), or about 0.6 per year. Including the arguable cases gives about 1.5 to 2 per year, but those cluster in 2023–24 before the July 2024 provisional resupply arrangement.
- **Rough math:** a Poisson rate of 0.6 to 1.5 per year over 0.11 years gives about **6–15%** for the remaining window. That is before adjusting for the higher tension in late 2026.

**B. Gunfire or kinetic armed clashes between China and any Southeast Asian claimant.** The only fatal naval battles were the Paracels in 1974 (South Vietnam) and Johnson South Reef in 1988 (Vietnam). There have been none in 38 years, and none ever with the Philippines. That base rate is far below 1% per 41 days.

**C. Months with any China–Philippines physical confrontation of any kind, counting PCG and BFAR.** In 2026 so far this happened in most months: March (a near-collision with a warship and a fire-control radar lock), July (three incidents in one week), September (ramming plus a blocked resupply mission), and October–November (more patrols and diplomatic escalation). Confrontations are frequent, but most involve non-qualifying Philippine agencies. Only a small fraction, roughly 1 in 10 or fewer, involve Philippine *military* personnel being physically hit.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES:**
1. **The most likely path:** a resupply mission to Second Thomas Shoal turns physical. CCG personnel board, strike or seize navy boats (as in June 2024 and July 2026). This depends on how many missions happen in the window and whether China keeps up the blocking it resumed in September. Chinese warships were present in September.
2. **A navy ship is rammed hard enough to breach the hull.** This is less likely, because the PLA Navy and CCG usually ram PCG and BFAR ships, not navy ships.
3. **The Philippine Navy escalates or escorts more.** For example, navy ships escort BFAR or PCG missions at Scarborough and get hit by water cannon with injuries. This is a gray zone for resolution.
4. **A loose resolution call:** an ambiguous incident, such as water cannon on a navy-crewed chartered boat, is counted.

**Paths to NO:**
- The status quo continues. Gray-zone pressure stays aimed at the PCG and BFAR, which do not qualify.
- Post-summit diplomacy and the US presence (Trump's visit) dampen Chinese escalation.
- Monsoon weather reduces operations.
- Resupply missions go through under the provisional arrangement without boarding.
- Navy and PLA Navy encounters stay at near-collisions, radar locks and lasers.

## 5. Signposts

- **Toward YES:**
  - A resupply mission reported as blocked or boarded, especially with navy rubber boats near the Sierra Madre.
  - The Philippines announcing that navy ships will escort missions.
  - CCG or PLA statements about "law enforcement boarding."
  - Another ramming that clearly involves a navy ship.
  - Any Philippine injury where the victim is identified as navy or marine.
- **Toward NO:**
  - Reports that a December resupply mission went through calmly.
  - Bilateral consultations or a revived provisional arrangement.
  - No incidents through mid-December.
- **On resolution interpretation:** whether the original market was formally resolved YES on the July 20 baton strike. That would confirm a low bar (any melee strike on navy personnel counts).
