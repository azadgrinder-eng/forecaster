# Research Brief: Will Tennessee win 10 or more regular-season games in 2026?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** Tennessee finishes the 2026 regular season with at least 10 official wins. Ties don't count as wins. The SEC Championship Game, bowl games and College Football Playoff games don't count.
- **Current position:** Tennessee is **7–2 (4–2 SEC)** after beating Kentucky 34–6 on Nov 7. Wikipedia and fbschedules both list 7–2 as of Nov 8, and ESPN's Nov 7 game page confirms the win. Two sources (Yahoo, Legion Report) still show 6–2 and are out of date.
- **What's left:** Three games, so **Tennessee must win all three (3–0)** to reach 10:
  - Nov 14 at Texas A&M. Kickoff time is reported inconsistently: ESPN says 3:00 PM ET, Fox says 8:00 PM.
  - Nov 21 vs LSU in Knoxville (Neyland Stadium)
  - Nov 28 at Vanderbilt. One listing says Nov 27.
- **Schedule check:** This is the first season with nine SEC games, so the schedule is 3 non-conference games plus 9 SEC games, for 12 in total. The bye was Oct 31. Nine games played plus three left makes 12, so there is no extra game that could help (On3/utsports schedule announcement). Wins I could confirm:
  - Furman 56–9 (Sep 5)
  - at Georgia Tech 45–24 (Sep 12)
  - Alabama 26–10 (Oct 17)
  - Kentucky 34–6 (Nov 7)

  Losses: Texas 20–17 (Sep 26, home) and at South Carolina 27–24 (Oct 24).
- **Edge cases:**
  - If a game is canceled or postponed and not made up, Tennessee can't reach 10. The market closes Dec 20, after the regular season ends.
  - FCS opponents (Furman, Kennesaw State) count as official regular-season wins.
  - Early-season schedule listings online are messy. Some older releases show different home/away sites. The current-season sources (ESPN, Fox, Sports-Reference, Wikipedia) agree on the three remaining games above.

   - No spreads are out yet for LSU or Vanderbilt. If LSU in Knoxville is close to a pick'em (45–55%) and Vanderbilt is about 80–88%, the parlay comes to roughly **20–31%**.

  - ESPN's models rated LSU well above what its record suggests. That's why Tennessee was only about 35% at home against them.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
- **Path to YES (needs all three):**
  - Tennessee's defense keeps playing at its Alabama and Kentucky level, which puts weak offenses under pressure. Texas A&M scored 9 points against South Carolina.
  - Texas A&M's season falls apart further, through a QB change, low motivation or a bowl-eligibility crisis.
  - LSU has a hangover after playing Texas or arrives with injuries. Neyland home-field advantage helps.
  - Vanderbilt, now much weaker than its 2025 team, can't repeat last year's upset.
- **Paths to NO (any one loss):**
  - A close road game in College Station. Texas A&M has plenty of talent, and the spread is only about 3 points.
  - LSU is the biggest obstacle. ESPN made LSU the favorite, and its talent level is high.
  - A rivalry upset at Vanderbilt. A Tennessee letdown is possible if the Vols' Playoff hopes have changed by then.
  - Injuries to MacIntyre or key defenders. Brame's and Weathersby's statuses are unknown.
  - Tennessee's offense is limited: 125 passing yards against Kentucky. Two of its three-point-or-less results were losses (20–17 and 27–24).

## 5. Signposts
- **Nov 14 result at Texas A&M:** This is the biggest swing. A loss settles the question as NO. A win roughly doubles the probability, to about the product of the LSU and Vanderbilt chances.
- **LSU vs Texas on Nov 14:** A bad LSU loss or new injuries (Leavitt) helps YES. A strong LSU performance hurts.
- **Updated ESPN FPI/SP+ numbers and the LSU opening line (~Nov 15):** Tennessee being favored by more than 3 would move the estimate up a lot.
- **Injury reports:** MacIntyre's health and the statuses of Brame and Weathersby.
- **CFP rankings:** Tennessee's position affects how motivated it is for Vanderbilt. Vanderbilt's own bowl-eligibility push gives it something to play for, since it needs wins at 4–5.
