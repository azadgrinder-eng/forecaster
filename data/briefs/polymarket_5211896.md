# Research brief: Will Pete Hegseth leave as Secretary of Defense (Secretary of War) by Nov 30, 2026?

*Research date: Nov 10, 2026. Direct page fetches were blocked by the network proxy, so every fact below comes from search-result summaries of the cited articles. Where those summaries conflict or look garbled, I say so.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Hegseth stops being U.S. Secretary of Defense for any length of time between market creation and **Nov 30, 2026, 11:59 PM ET**.
- **An announcement is enough:** If his resignation or removal is announced by the deadline, the market resolves YES at once, even if it takes effect later. Example: Trump announces on Nov 25 that he will nominate a replacement (DeSantis has been floated) to start in January. That counts as YES. Trump musing about it or "asking advisers" does not.
- **Who decides:** Official statements from Hegseth or the U.S. government. A consensus of credible reporting can also be used, so anonymously sourced "Trump has decided to replace Hegseth" stories could create ambiguity before anything is official.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Job title:** The department now also uses the name "Department of War" and he is styled "Secretary of War" (war.gov, Nov 9, 2026). It is the same office, so the name doesn't matter. A move to another post would count as ceasing to be Secretary.
  - **Impeachment alone is not removal.** A House impeachment does not make him leave office. Removal needs a two-thirds Senate conviction, which is practically impossible before Nov 30. Impeachment matters only if it pushes a resignation or a firing.
  - **Temporary absence:** Incapacity or temporary delegation of duties probably does not count unless he actually stops holding the office.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Defense Secretaries after a bad midterm for the President's party, especially when a war is blamed.**
- My count, from memory of the historical record:
  - **2006:** Rumsfeld's resignation was announced Nov 8, the day after a midterm loss blamed on the Iraq war.
  - **2014:** Hagel's resignation was announced Nov 24, three weeks after the midterms.
  - **2018:** Mattis's resignation was announced Dec 20, about six weeks after.
  - **2010 and 2022:** No departure (Gates had only signaled he would leave in 2011; Austin stayed).
- **Result:** 2 of the last 5 midterm cycles saw a Defense Secretary departure announced within about 4 weeks of the election, and 3 of 5 within about 7 weeks.
- 2006 is the closest analog: a war-blamed wave, the President's party losing both chambers, and the Pentagon chief as the lightning rod. In that case the exit came within 24 hours. Hegseth has already lasted a week, which weakens the "immediate sacrifice" version of that path.

**B. Trump firings after the 2018 midterms.**
- Sessions was fired Nov 7, 2018, the day after the election.
- John Kelly's exit was announced Dec 8, Zinke's Dec 15, and Mattis's Dec 20.
- Of about 4 major post-midterm exits, 1 came within roughly 3.5 weeks and 3 came 4–7 weeks after. Trump's pattern in 2018 was a wave of changes concentrated in December, not only in November.

**C. Trump's second-term handling of embattled cabinet members.**
- Noem was fired around Mar 5, 2026, and Bondi on Apr 2, 2026. Both followed months of controversy and repeated public expressions of confidence from Trump (KATV/Sinclair explainer, ~Jul 2026).
- Gabbard and Chavez-DeRemer also left, putting cabinet turnover at about 20%.
- Since the midterms, Wiles has been replaced as chief of staff by Stephen Miller, and Harmeet Dhillon has resigned (The Hill/CNN, Nov 6–7, 2026).
- **Lesson:** Trump's public "I'll keep him" statements have repeatedly come before firings. On their own they are weak evidence that Hegseth will stay.

**D. Cabinet officers facing impeachment.**
- Only two have been impeached: Belknap in 1876, who resigned hours before the House vote, and Mayorkas in 2024, who stayed and whose Senate trial was dismissed.
- That is 1 of 2 resigning, too small a sample to lean on.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 9–10, 2026:**
  - Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY, outgoing after losing his primary) filed **eight articles of impeachment**, focused on Hegseth waging war against Iran without congressional authorization and abusing his office (NPR, CNBC, CBS, Nov 9).
  - The resolution is privileged, so the House must act on it, table it, or refer it to committee **within two legislative days**, meaning a vote is expected this week. Massie has warned leadership against using committee referral to dodge a vote on the substance.
  - The House currently splits **218 R – 214 D**, so a handful of GOP defections would pass it. Speaker Johnson opposes it, Jeffries is a "hard yes," and no Democratic defections are expected (CBS/Fox/The Hill, Nov 9).
  - Axios (Nov 8) reports House GOP leaders are **"bracing for enough GOP defections to impeach."** Rep. Bacon is undecided and Rep. Comer is opposed.
  - Massie says he will drop the articles if Hegseth resigns. He predicts Hegseth won't make it to year-end (The Hill, Mediaite, Nov 9).
  - **As of my searches, no vote result had been reported.**
- **Nov 9:**
  - Trump said he would **"stand by" Hegseth even if he is impeached** and said Hegseth "won two wars" (ABC, Nov 9; CNN live, Nov 9).
  - Hegseth issued 33 directives overhauling professional military education (war.gov; Daily Signal, Nov 9), which looks like business as usual.
  - He also pressed for $350B in military funding and urged Republicans to back a reconciliation bill (Fox, ~Nov 9–10).
- **About Nov 5–8:**
  - In a Thursday interview with ABC's Jonathan Karl, Trump said he intends to keep Hegseth despite "plenty of calls" from unhappy allies. He also **acknowledged it would be easier to confirm a replacement before Democrats take the Senate in January** (Mediaite; Yahoo).
  - Hegseth posted a video calling the impeachment a "sham" led by a "disgruntled, defeated congressman" (CBC).
  - The Pentagon spokesman said Hegseth is "100% committed" to serving through the end of the term.
- **About Nov 6–7:**
  - CBS reported Trump was **privately asking advisers whether Hegseth should stay**. Other sources said no decision had been made.
  - A single CBS source said **Ron DeSantis** is being considered as a replacement: he would be confirmed by the GOP Senate during the lame-duck session and take office after his term as Florida governor ends Jan 5, 2027.
  - On *60 Minutes*, Trump said "I do" when asked whether he would fire anyone and that there would be "maybe a few changes," but he voiced confidence in Hegseth. Hours later he announced Miller would replace Wiles (CBS, Newsweek).
  - Earlier that week, on Fox, Trump had said he had no plans to change his Cabinet (The Hill), so his stance has already shifted once.
- **Nov 6:** A Trump ally declined to back Hegseth when asked whether he should be replaced (Washington Post).
- **From Nov 4:**
  - Republicans "quickly turn against" Hegseth after the rout (CNN, Nov 4).
  - Sen. Tillis (R-NC, retiring) says he has "no confidence," calls his vote to confirm Hegseth his "biggest regret," and urged Hegseth to step down and Trump to ask him to.
  - Reports say up to **six GOP senators** have voiced reservations, and an anonymously sourced claim says Hegseth would not be confirmed if renominated today.
  - Tillis says the strained relations are threatening a $1.5T defense package.
- **Nov 3: midterms.**
  - Democrats won **both chambers**. They flipped about 7 Senate seats (NC, SC, OH, TX, KS, IA, ME) and gained about 19+ House seats (OPB/NPR, AP, Nov 4).
  - Some Republicans blame the Iran war for the losses (Fox; CNN). Exit polls emphasized cost of living, corruption, and opposition to Trump. I could not confirm how big a role Iran played.
- **Background:**
  - **Iran:** A U.S.–Iran war took place in 2026, followed by a fragile ceasefire with clashes in the Strait of Hormuz (May–Aug 2026 reports). I found no November update on its status.
  - **Military firings:** In April 2026, Hegseth fired Army Chief of Staff Gen. Randy George and two other senior officers, which angered senators (The Hill).
  - **Signal chat (2025)** and other controversies did not end his tenure.
- **Scheduled before Nov 30:**
  - House vote on the Massie articles, expected the week of Nov 9–13.
  - Senate lame-duck session and the defense spending/reconciliation fight.
  - Thanksgiving recess (Nov 26).
  - The new Congress is sworn in Jan 3, 2027.
  - **Timing pressure:** A replacement confirmed by the GOP Senate would need to be **nominated or announced roughly by late November or early December** to clear hearings before Jan 3. That pushes any decision toward the market window.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES:**
1. **Pre-emptive resignation before or after the House vote.** Massie explicitly offers a way out (resign and the articles go away), and there is precedent in Belknap. This depends on Hegseth's own appetite for the fight, which looks low so far given his defiant video and business-as-usual activity, and on whether Trump quietly asks him to go.
2. **Trump announces a replacement during the lame duck to use the GOP Senate.** This is the DeSantis path. An announcement in November resolves YES even if the handover happens in January. It depends on Trump deciding he wants a confirmable successor before Democrats control the Senate, a point he has publicly acknowledged.
3. **Post-impeachment fallout.** If the House impeaches with GOP votes, Senate Republicans like Tillis could escalate, the defense bill could stall, and Trump may find Hegseth more trouble than he is worth. This depends on the size of the GOP defection.
4. **A new scandal or Iran escalation** (casualties, a Hormuz incident, an inspector general report) adding to the pressure.

**Paths to NO:**
1. **Trump digs in.** He has repeatedly and publicly backed Hegseth, including "even if impeached." He tends to resist firing people when critics demand it, and he may see Hegseth as a loyalist who "won the war."
2. **The House tables or refers the articles,** which deflates the urgency.
3. **The timing slips to December.** Trump's 2018 pattern put most changes in December (Kelly Dec 8, Zinke Dec 15, Mattis Dec 20). A December announcement still fits the lame-duck confirmation logic, just barely, but it would resolve this market NO.
4. **No suitable replacement is lined up,** or DeSantis declines.

## 5. Signposts

**Toward YES (large moves):**
- The House impeaches Hegseth with double-digit GOP votes. Tabling with few defections points the other way.
- Trump stops volunteering support, or uses "we'll see" or "he's a good man, but" language.
- Credible reports that Trump has offered the job to someone or that DeSantis has accepted.
- Senate Armed Services Chair Wicker, or GOP leaders Thune or Barrasso, publicly calling for a change.
- Hegseth cancels public appearances, or there is a sudden flurry of "considering his options" stories.
- Pentagon deputies being positioned as acting secretary.

**Toward NO:**
- A clean tabling vote.
- Trump holds a joint appearance with Hegseth or makes fresh, specific endorsements.
- The Thanksgiving recess arrives with no nominee named, since the lame-duck window for a confirmed successor shrinks fast.
- The defense spending package moves forward with Hegseth leading the negotiations.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **Vote outcome and timing:** I could not confirm whether the House vote has happened or how it went. Defection estimates are anonymously sourced (Axios).
- **DeSantis report:** It rests on one CBS source. It is unknown whether DeSantis wants the job or whether Senate Republicans would confirm him quickly.
- **Trump's private intent:** CBS reports deliberation, while his public statements are supportive. His "few changes" remark had already been partly fulfilled by the Wiles move.
- **Iran:** The war's current status in November 2026 and how much it actually drove the midterm result are both unclear.
- **Garbled attribution:** One search summary appeared to attribute the "won't make it to year-end" prediction to Trump. Other sources attribute it to Massie, and I treated it as Massie's.
- **House numbers:** Senate GOP opposition is reported as "up to six senators," with names other than Tillis unverified. The current House margin (218–214) is as reported and may reflect vacancies.
