# Research brief: Will Xabi Alonso stop being Chelsea manager before 1 January 2027?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** Alonso stops being Chelsea's manager, for any reason, with an effective date before 1 January 2027. The market closes at 15:00 UTC on 1 January 2027. Sacking, resignation, "mutual consent" and leaving for another club all count.
- **Which date counts:** the date he actually leaves, not the date of the announcement. If Chelsea announced on 31 December that he leaves on a later date, that should resolve NO. If they sacked him on, say, 30 December and announced it on 1 January, it should resolve YES. Chelsea announced Enzo Maresca's exit on 1 January 2026 (Sky Sports / Flashscore coverage of Rosenior's sacking, Apr 2026), so a departure right on this boundary has happened before. Kalshi decides the outcome.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Gardening leave or suspension:** if he is suspended or sidelined while still under contract, it is unclear whether he has "ceased to be manager". Chelsea normally announces sackings with immediate effect, which lowers this risk.
  - **Job title:** Alonso's title is "Manager", not "Head Coach" (Chelsea FC announcement, May 2026). A change of title with Alonso staying on is very unlikely and probably would not count.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
**A. Chelsea managers under the BlueCo owners (May 2022 to now)**
- Five permanent managers have left in about 54 months:
  - Tuchel, sacked September 2022
  - Potter, sacked 2 April 2023 after about 7 months
  - Pochettino, left by mutual consent May 2024 after one season
  - Maresca, left 1 January 2026 after about 18 months
  - Rosenior, sacked 22 April 2026 after less than 4 months (Fox Sports / Sky Sports, 22 Apr 2026)
- That is about 5 / 54 ≈ 9% per month, or roughly 10–11% for the ~5 weeks left, before adjusting for current form.
- **How new hires have done at their first Christmas:**
  - Potter was 10th at the World Cup break in 2022 and was still kept until April.
  - Pochettino was in the bottom half in December 2023 and was still kept.
  - Maresca got through his first Christmas.
  - Only Rosenior went within 4 months, after five straight league defeats without scoring, the club's worst run since 1912 (Gulf News, Apr 2026).
- So for full-season hires BlueCo has kept 3 of 3 through their first 1 January. It has sacked 1 of 2 mid-season hires quickly.

**B. Premier League mid-season sackings in general**
- Typically about 4–7 Premier League managers are sacked before 1 January each season. That suggests about 3–8% per club over a 5-week December window. This is my own estimate and was not checked in this research.
- The risk is concentrated in clubs that are underperforming against expectations and have owners who sack quickly. Chelsea is both right now.

**C. Alonso's own record**
- Real Madrid sacked him in January 2026, about 7 months into the job, while the team was second in La Liga (ESPN, May 2026). Elite clubs have shown they will act quickly on him.
- At Leverkusen (2022–25) he was never under that kind of threat.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES**
- The losing run continues against Forest and Palace, followed by defeats to Liverpool and Man City. Four or five more losses by mid-December, a drop to 10th or lower, and possibly a Carabao Cup exit would echo the Rosenior trigger.
- A dressing-room revolt becomes public.
- BlueCo acts during the Christmas fixtures, as it did with Maresca on 1 January. Note that a 1 January exit like Maresca's would resolve NO.
- Less likely: a falling-out over January transfer control leads to a mutual-consent exit.

**Paths to NO**
- Reported backing from the hierarchy holds.
- BlueCo has stuck with full-season hires through their first winter before (Potter, Pochettino).
- Firing a 4-year marquee hire after 5 months would be costly and would undermine the "manager" project. Unlike Rosenior's contract, which had a break clause, any break clause in Alonso's is unknown.
- The run of games includes beatable sides (Palace, Coventry, Ipswich), and a few wins would ease the pressure.
- January is a natural time to reassess, which pushes any change past 1 January.

## 5. Signposts
- **Forest and Palace results:** two more losses would sharply raise the risk. Two wins would mostly defuse it.
- **Tone of reporting from trusted club sources** (The Athletic, Romano, Matt Law at the Telegraph): watch for a change from "full backing" to "next game crucial", or for news of approaches to replacements. Earlier reports named Iraola and Silva as people Chelsea had contacted (theScore).
- **Reports of player unrest** or public criticism from senior players such as Enzo Fernández.
- **League position:** dropping out of the top 8 or 10 by mid-December would raise the risk. A Carabao Cup exit would add to it.
- **Any statement from the owners** (Eghbali, Boehly) in either direction.
