# Research brief: Will the U.S. invade Iran before 2027?

*Prepared 28 Nov 2026. About 33 days remain before the 31 Dec 2026, 11:59 PM ET deadline. Network limits meant I could not open full articles, so this brief relies on search-result summaries. Exact dates and wording should be treated as approximate where noted.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** By 31 Dec 2026, 11:59 PM ET, the U.S. starts a military offensive *intended to establish control over any portion of Iran*. The offensive only has to start. It doesn't have to succeed or last.
- **Territory baseline:** Land that Iran controlled de facto on 4 Nov 2025 counts as Iranian. That includes the Gulf islands Iran holds and the UAE claims: Abu Musa and the Greater and Lesser Tunbs. A U.S. landing on Abu Musa or the Tunbs would therefore count as invading Iran, even though the UAE claims them.
- **Who decides:** A "consensus of credible sources." There is no named official arbiter.
- **Things that clearly do not count:** air and missile strikes (including the ongoing strikes on Qeshm Island), the naval blockade, disabling or boarding ships at sea, and overland or Caspian interdiction by Task Force Iron Gate (a ~20-person staff in Tampa).
- **Gray zones:**
  - **Special-forces raids.** A raid to seize or destroy enriched uranium at Isfahan would require "a larger ground force securing sites" (NY Post via Kurdistan24, Jul 2026). That arguably means establishing temporary control over territory, so it would probably count, but it is disputable.
  - **Rescue missions.** The April 2026 F-15E pilot rescue put special operations forces on Iranian soil (Wikipedia, "2026 United States F-15E rescue operation in Iran"). It evidently did not resolve this market, so short rescue or extraction raids appear not to count.
  - **Capturing offshore platforms or oil terminals.** This would likely count if they are Iranian-held.
  - **Intent.** It may be disputed whether a quick "hit and leave" raid was meant to establish control.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Hazard rate within this war.** The U.S.–Israel war on Iran began 28 Feb 2026, about 9 months ago. The U.S. publicly and seriously considered seizing Kharg Island in March (Axios, Mar 20, 2026; CNBC, Mar 26, 2026) and a uranium raid in March and July (CNN, Mar 9; NY Post, late Jul 2026). It deployed two Marine Expeditionary Units with about 2,200–2,500 Marines (TIME, Mar 24, 2026). Even so, I found no credible report of any operation to seize territory in 0 of ~9 months.
   - Laplace's rule (1/(n+2)) gives roughly 9% per month, but that is generous given the repeated decisions not to go ahead.
   - A more empirical reading is that serious "ground op under consideration" episodes came about every 3–4 months, and none of 2–3 led to action.
   - Scaled to a 1.1-month window, this points to low single digits to high single digits.
2. **U.S. air/naval coercion campaigns that turned into ground offensives to take territory** (my own tally):
   - Yes: Iraq 1991 (after about 5.5 weeks of air war); Afghanistan 2001 (special forces and allies within weeks).
   - No: Bosnia 1995, Kosovo 1999, Libya 2011, Syria 2014–19 (ground forces were deployed, but against ISIS, not to take territory from the state), Houthis 2024–25, and Iran in June 2025.
   - That is about 2 of 8. In both "yes" cases the ground phase was planned from the start and came within about 2 months. Nine months in, with no ground phase yet, this campaign looks more like the coercive cases that never escalated.
3. **The Trump administration's record on force (2025–26).** It has relied on air power, blockades, and short special-forces actions rather than holding territory. Rubio has said ground troops are not needed to achieve the administration's goals (via Military.com and NPR coverage, Mar 2026). In my background knowledge, the January 2026 Venezuela operation is another example: a short raid, not an occupation. I could not re-verify that this session.

The uranium-raid plan was reviewed but not approved by Trump (NY Post via Kurdistan24/Kyiv Post, late Jul). Foreign Policy judged a large-scale invasion unlikely but limited island seizures possible (Foreign Policy, Jul 24, 2026).

- **8 Apr 2026:** A two-week ceasefire brokered by Pakistan, later extended (PBS). The F-15E rescue happened in April.
- **13 Mar 2026:** U.S. airstrikes on Kharg Island, with seizure "under serious consideration" (Wikipedia "2026 Kharg Island attack"; Axios, Mar 20, 2026).
- **28 Feb 2026:** The U.S. and Israel attacked Iran, reportedly killing Supreme Leader Khamenei (Geo/Tribune timelines).
- **Scheduled before the deadline:** Nothing specific found. The holiday period (Christmas and New Year) usually lowers the odds of starting major new operations, but not with certainty. The new Congress is seated in January, after the deadline.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES:**
- **(a) Seizing Hormuz islands** (Abu Musa, the Tunbs, Larak, parts of Qeshm) to reopen the strait. Mine strikes on escorted tankers in late November strengthen the military case for clearing coastal threats.
  - Depends on: whether Trump concludes the blockade and air strikes are failing; whether Marine Expeditionary Units and amphibious ships are still in the theater (I could not confirm their current posture); and Gulf states' consent.
- **(b) A Kharg Island seizure** to take over or choke off Iran's oil export hub. This is less needed now that the blockade already halts exports.
- **(c) A uranium raid on Isfahan** using a securing force, triggered by intelligence of nuclear or chemical weapons work. Katz and Trump have both raised chemical-weapons claims, and Katz says Iran is renewing nuclear efforts.
- **(d) A broad renewal of the war after the midterms**, under Israeli pressure, with a ground component.

**Paths to NO:**
- Continued blockade, strikes and interdiction, which is the status quo since July.
- A diplomatic deal through Pakistan or Qatar.
- Renewed large-scale air strikes with no ground part. This is the most likely form any escalation would take, and it resolves NO.
- Even if a ground operation is approved, it may not start until after 31 Dec.

## 5. Signposts

**Would move the probability up sharply:**
- Credible reports, especially from AP, Axios, NYT or WSJ, that Trump has approved an island or Kharg seizure or the uranium raid.
- Amphibious ready groups or MEUs, or the 82nd Airborne, moving into or staying in the Gulf.
- U.S. strikes focused on suppressing coastal and island air defenses (the kind of preparation that comes before a landing).
- U.S. casualties from mines or missiles.
- Collapse of talks, or Iranian strikes that kill Americans.

**Would move it down:**
- A new round of talks or a ceasefire announcement.
- The blockade being eased.
- Amphibious groups leaving the theater.
- Trump repeating that the war is "militarily effectively over" (Pakistan Times, ~Nov 2026).
- The holidays passing without any orders.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **Midterm results** and any change in congressional war-powers pressure.
- **Current U.S. ground and amphibious posture in the region.** The latest deployment data I found is from March.
- **Exact dates of the Qeshm strikes** and whether they are a prelude to a landing.
- **How the market resolves gray-zone raids.** The April F-15E rescue is the only precedent.
- **Whether there were small seizures I missed.** I found no credible report of any U.S. seizure of Iranian territory; the only claims (of an Abu Musa landing) came from YouTube videos with no corroboration.
- **Verification depth.** Network limits stopped me opening full articles, so details rest on search summaries. Some timestamps conflict.
