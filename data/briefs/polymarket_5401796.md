# Research Brief: Will a military strike hit Saudi Arabia's East-West (Petroline) pipeline by 30 Nov 2026?

*Prepared 11 Nov 2026. Method note: every page fetch was blocked by the network proxy. All facts below come from search-result summaries of the cited articles, not from reading the full text. Treat specific numbers and quotes as needing checks against the originals.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

**What counts as YES.** An air strike or a surface-to-surface strike must physically hit the pipeline or one of its pumping stations. The window runs from when the market was created to **30 Nov 2026, 23:59 Riyadh time (20:59 UTC)**.
- **Counts:** one-way attack drones, cruise missiles, ballistic missiles, bombs, and drones launched from aircraft.
- **Does not count:**
  - munitions intercepted before impact, and debris from interceptions, whatever damage it causes;
  - artillery and rocket artillery (MLRS);
  - short-range loitering munitions, FPV drones and ATGMs;
  - ground raids, cyberattacks, and threats.

**Who decides.** The sources are official Saudi government statements plus a consensus of credible reporting. If sources conflict, resolution can wait up to 3 full calendar days (Riyadh time) after the first credible report, and that wait can run past 30 Nov. After that, the totality of the evidence decides. No single denial or statement controls the outcome.

**Key ambiguities**
- **When was the market created?** I could not find out. This matters more than anything else:
  - If it was created **before 9 Nov**, the 9 Nov strike in the Medina region may already resolve it YES. The Saudi Energy Ministry said the pipeline was "targeted", a "limited fire" broke out and was put out, and operations were unaffected. The Saudi Press Agency reported it, and Maritime Executive's headline was "Saudi Arabia Confirms Strike." Satellite imagery reportedly showed damage at **Pumping Station No. 8**.
  - If it was created **on 10–11 Nov** (plausibly in response to that strike), a new impact is needed in about 19 days.
- **Saudi wording is often vague.** On 10 Nov, Saudi authorities said they had "intercepted aerial threats and contained a small fire" (Voice of Emirates, 10 Nov 2026). That leaves open whether something hit the pipeline directly or whether interception debris caused the fire, which is exactly what the debris exclusion turns on.
- **Saudi confirmations are sparse.** For the Khurais strike on 4–5 Oct, the only confirmation was an unnamed energy-sector source quoted by AFP. Bloomberg reported that oil kept flowing, and I found no official Saudi confirmation. Expect disputed cases.
- **Houthi claims are not proof.** Claims like those of 26 Oct and 10 Nov do not resolve the market unless Saudi officials or a consensus of reporting confirm an impact.
- **The attacker does not matter.** Houthis, Iraqi militias or Iran all count, as long as the weapon type qualifies. The attacks on 10 Sept were reportedly launched from Iraq.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Credibly reported impacts on the pipeline since the September escalation (10 Sept – 10 Nov 2026, about 61 days)**
- 10–11 Sept: multiple drone strikes in the Riyadh and Madinah regions, confirmed by the Saudi Energy Ministry. The pipeline was shut, and Kpler data showed flows halted from 11 to 22 Sept.
- 4–5 Oct: a pumping station at Khurais was hit, with "big damage" according to the AFP source. Bloomberg disputed that the line was halted.
- 9 Nov: the Medina-region strike, confirmed by the Saudi Press Agency.

That is about 3 episodes in 61 days, roughly one every 20 days. A simple Poisson model (treating strikes as random, independent events) gives about **60–65%** for at least one episode in a 19-day window.

If you count from the start of the near-daily Houthi campaign in early October, there were 2 confirmed episodes in about 37 days (4 Oct and 9 Nov), or roughly one every 18 days. That gives about **65%** over 19 days.

Adding unconfirmed Houthi claims (26 Oct, 10 Nov) roughly doubles the rate. But the claims may not meet the resolution standard.

**B. Whole of 2026 (28 Feb war start – 11 Nov, about 256 days)**
- Roughly 4–5 distinct impact episodes: an early-war pumping-station hit by about April (Malay Mail, 10 Apr 2026, reported output cut by 600k bpd after pipeline strikes), then September, October and November.
- That is about one every 50–60 days, or **about 30%** over 19 days.
- This underweights the current, more intense phase of the Saudi-Houthi war.

**C. The 2015–2022 Saudi-Houthi war (pre-2026)**
- The Houthis fired hundreds of missiles and drones into Saudi Arabia, but confirmed impacts on the East-West pipeline were rare. The clearest is 14 May 2019, when drones hit two pumping stations (GlobalSecurity/SPA, 14 May 2019).
- That is roughly 1–2 confirmed impacts in about 7 years, a monthly rate well under 5%.
- Lesson: hits on this specific asset are much rarer than general attacks, and Saudi Arabia historically under-confirmed them. In 2026, however, the pipeline is a deliberate strategic target because it is the main way around the constrained Strait of Hormuz.

**Background**
- Capacity is about 7m bpd, with about 5m bpd exportable from Yanbu.
- Saudi Arabia has rerouted about 4m bpd through the pipeline because the Strait of Hormuz is constrained. This makes it a top-value target.
- OPEC output is reportedly about 90% of pre-war levels.

**Scheduled events before 30 Nov:** I found nothing specific. UN/Omani mediation and Qatar's US-Iran channel are ongoing, with no dates found.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES**
1. **The Houthi campaign continues at its current pace.** It is near-daily, with explicit targeting of pumping stations claimed on 10 Nov, and the group now has large ballistic-missile and drone stocks. The pipeline runs about 1,200 km with 11-plus pumping stations, which is hard to defend completely. This is the dominant path.
2. **Iraqi militia drones.** These were used in September, and the Medina attack on 9 Nov that the Houthis deny could fit this pattern. Any renewed US-Iran escalation raises this risk.
3. **Direct Iranian strikes** if the US-Iran pause collapses.
4. **Resolution technicality:** if the market was created before 9 Nov, the 9 Nov event probably already qualifies.

**Paths to NO**
1. **A Saudi-Houthi truce or ceasefire** through the $1bn deal or Omani/UN mediation. A ceasefire was described as "not out of the question" in the coming months (Steptoe). Riyadh is currently resisting.
2. **Effective Saudi air defence.** Many attacks are intercepted, and interceptions or debris do not count.
3. **Ambiguous Saudi reporting.** Riyadh tends to downplay damage, and an attack the Houthis claim but Saudi Arabia does not confirm could fail to meet the "consensus" standard.
4. **Houthi targets shift** toward Riyadh airport, the financial district or Abqaiq rather than the pipeline.

## 5. Signposts

**Toward YES**
- A Saudi Energy Ministry or SPA statement saying the pipeline or a pumping station was "targeted" or "hit", or reporting a fire. These have been the decisive confirmations.
- AFP or Reuters energy-source reports of flows stopping.
- Satellite or flaring imagery: the Payne Institute has tracked emergency flaring along the line.
- Houthi statements explicitly naming pipeline targets.
- The US-Iran pause collapsing.
- New launches from Iraq.

**Toward NO**
- An announced Saudi-Houthi truce, prisoner or payment deal, or Houthi pause; or the Qatar track producing a US-Iran deal that includes proxies.
- Several weeks of Houthi strikes concentrated on non-pipeline targets.
- Saudi statements describing interceptions only.

**Resolution-specific**
- Confirmation of the market's creation date relative to 9 Nov.
- How the market treats the 9–10 Nov reports. Saudi wording that mixes "intercepted" and "fire" is a debris-versus-impact edge case.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **Market creation date.** This is the most important unknown. It decides whether the 9 Nov strike counts.
- **Whether 9 Nov was a direct impact or interception debris.** Saudi wording differs between reports.
- **Who carried out the 9 Nov Medina strike.** The Houthis deny it, and Iraqi militias are possible.
- **Whether the claimed attacks on 26 Oct and 10 Nov near Riyadh were real impacts.** Neither is confirmed.
- **The April 2026 pumping-station hit.** It is mentioned only in passing.
- **The status of the US-Iran ceasefire.** Sources disagree on whether a formal ceasefire exists after the July collapse.
- **Saudi-Houthi negotiations.** Progress is unclear, and the $1bn deal is unconfirmed.
- **Whether my sources are complete.** No source gave a full list of strikes. Some search snippets came from low-reliability outlets (houseofsaud.com, naturalnews, RT), and since no full page could be read, their details may be partly wrong.
