# Research brief: Will there be more than 10,000 measles cases in 2026?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** the 2026 measles case count must be above 10,000. The rule ("If the number of measles in 2026 is above 10000") does not say where the cases are counted or who counts them.
- **Most likely reading: US confirmed cases as reported by the CDC.** The CDC publishes a running national count on its measles page, updated weekly on Wednesdays. The count reflects cases reported by noon Thursday of the prior week (CDC measles page, Nov 2026). On that reading this is a question about the US CDC tally, and the forecast depends almost entirely on how far the US count is from 10,000.
- **Big ambiguity: worldwide or non-US counts.** If the market means global cases, or any country's cases, YES is already certain:
  - Mexico alone reports about 13,016 confirmed cases in 2026 (Outbreak News Today, undated, probably Oct–Nov 2026).
  - PAHO counted 47,459 confirmed cases in the Americas through epidemiological week 28 (PAHO epidemiological alert, Aug 7, 2026).
  - WHO provisional data show Bangladesh with 42,174 cases and India with 26,607 over Dec 2025–May 2026 (WHO via CDC global page, ~Jul 2026).
  - Forecasters should check the market's title and description for "US" or "CDC". Most measles-count markets of this kind cite the CDC.
- **Timing:** the market closes Jan 1, 2027 04:59 UTC. The last CDC update before close will probably be about Dec 30, 2026, possibly moved for the holidays. It is unclear whether resolution uses the last pre-close figure or a later, revised full-year figure.
- **Late reports keep adding cases.** For 2025, the CDC showed 2,065 cases on Dec 30, 2025, and the full-year count later reached 2,285–2,289, about 11% higher (ABC/respiratory-therapy.com, Dec 2025–Jan 2026; CDC). Cases with rash onset in the last epidemiological week of the year count toward that year.
- **Confirmed cases only.** The CDC counts confirmed cases, not probable ones. The Johns Hopkins tracker runs somewhat higher: 5,739 on Nov 13 against the CDC's 5,412 on Nov 12, about 6% more (search summary, Nov 2026).

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **Recent US years.** CDC confirmed cases were 2,289 in 2025 and 285 in 2024. In 1991 there were about 9,600 cases; 2026 is already the highest count since then (Al Jazeera, Nov 7, 2026; PBS, 2026). The US has not topped 10,000 cases in a year since 1990, when there were about 27,000. In 0 of 35 years since 1991 has the US exceeded 10,000.
2. **How much a year can grow in its final five weeks.** In 2026 the US count rose by about 200–430 per weekly update in Oct–Nov:
   - Oct 29: 4,852 (+264)
   - Nov 5: 5,182 (+330)
   - Nov 12: 5,412 (+230)
   - Nov 19: 5,838 (+426)
   - Nov 24: 6,039 (+201, a five-day Thanksgiving-shortened interval)
   
   Reaching 10,001 needs about 3,960 more cases, roughly 800 a week across about 5 remaining updates. That is about 2–4 times the recent pace and nearly double the largest weekly jump so far this year. In 2025, December added only about 10% to the year's total.
3. **Revision after year-end.** A gain of about 11% after year-end, as in 2025, applied to a likely Dec 30 count of 7,000–8,000 gives a final count of about 7,800–8,900. That is still under 10,000.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)
- **Nov 25, 2026 (data as of Nov 24):** the CDC reports 6,039 confirmed 2026 cases across 47 jurisdictions, including 18 in international visitors. It counts 9 deaths; tallies of state reports give at least 14. About 671 people (11%) were hospitalized. There were 43 new outbreaks in 2026, and 96% of cases are outbreak-associated (US News/Reuters, Nov 25, 2026).
- **Nov 20, 2026:** Wisconsin DHS reports 832 cases in 2026, up from 649 on Nov 13 and 431 on Nov 3. This is the fastest-growing state outbreak right now (Wisconsin DHS outbreak page; Wisconsin DHS releases, Nov 2026). A CIDRAP headline gives Wisconsin's count as 920 with a third death.
- **Nov 19:** CDC count 5,838, up 426 in one week (US News, Nov 2026).
- **Nov 17:** PAHO declared that the US, Mexico and Bolivia have lost measles elimination status (Al Jazeera, Nov 17, 2026; ABC, Nov 2026).
- **Nov 12–13:** CDC count 5,412 (US News, Nov 13, 2026).
- **Nov 5–7:** CDC count 5,182, the highest in 35 years (Al Jazeera, Nov 7, 2026).
- **Oct 29:** CDC count 4,852 (Contagion Live, Oct 30, 2026).
- **Late Sep:** Pennsylvania's outbreak reached 890 cumulative cases and 4 deaths (PA Dept. of Health, Sep 2026). Reports put Pennsylvania deaths at 9 by late November (US News, Nov 25).
- **Earlier in 2026:** the South Carolina outbreak ended at 997 cases (Oct 2025–Apr 2026). Utah, Arizona and Texas outbreaks drove spring growth. The US passed 2,000 cases by about May and 3,600 by about September (CIDRAP, various 2026 dates).
- **Scheduled before close:** CDC updates around Dec 2, 9, 16, 23 and 30, possibly shifted for the holidays. Thanksgiving and Christmas travel could seed new outbreaks, but measles has a 1–3 week incubation period plus reporting lag, so most holiday-seeded cases would surface in January.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to NO (on the US/CDC reading; dominant):**
- The current pace of about 250–450 a week continues, putting the Dec 30 count at about 7,300–8,300.
- Holiday reporting slowdowns lower the late-December numbers further.

**Paths to YES on the US/CDC reading (all very unlikely):**
- A large batch of backlogged reports, for example from Pennsylvania or Wisconsin, plus explosive new outbreaks pushing weekly increases to 800 or more. This has not happened at any point in 2026.
- Resolution uses a different source with probable cases included, or a much later revised figure. Even then, adding about 10–15% does not reach 10,000.

**Path to YES on a global or non-US reading:** already met. Mexico has about 13,000 cases, the Americas well over 47,000, and the world far more.

## 5. Signposts
- **The Dec 2/3 CDC update.** An increase of 700 or more would be the first sign of acceleration (toward YES). An increase of 300 or less makes NO close to certain.
- **Wisconsin's weekly gains.** These were about 180–200 a week in November. A doubling would matter.
- **Any CDC methodology change**, such as adding probable cases or a large reclassification. This would push toward YES, but would be unlikely to be enough.
- **Clarification of the market's geography or source.** This is the single biggest swing factor.
