# Research brief: Will the FOMC raise the upper bound of the fed funds target by exactly 25 bp at its Dec 8–9, 2026 meeting?

*Prepared 2026-11-13. I couldn't open any web pages directly, so everything below comes from search-result summaries. Check the key figures against federalreserve.gov and bls.gov. As instructed, I left out futures-implied and betting probabilities, even where articles quoted them.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **Resolves YES for this bracket if** the FOMC statement released after the Dec 8–9, 2026 meeting (decision on Dec 9) raises the upper bound of the target range by 25 bp, from the current 4.00% to 4.25%. The new range would be 4.00%–4.25%.
- **Starting level:** the current range is 3.75%–4.00%. It was set by a 25 bp hike on Sep 16, 2026 and held on Oct 28, 2026 (Fox Business, Sep 16 2026; Advisor Perspectives, Oct 28 2026).
- **Mutually exclusive outcomes:** "No change" and any cut both resolve NO for this bracket. **A 50 bp hike also resolves NO**, because it goes to the 50 bp bracket. So the question is really "a hike, and exactly 25 bp."
- **Rounding rule:** a move that isn't a multiple of 25 is rounded up. A 12.5 bp hike would count as 25 bp. A 37.5 bp hike would round up to 50 and resolve NO. Both are very unlikely.
- **Who decides:** the FOMC statement on Dec 9, with federalreserve.gov/openmarket.htm as a cross-check. If no statement is released by the end of the next scheduled meeting, the market resolves "No change."
- **Edge cases:**
  - An intermeeting move before Dec 8 would change the baseline, because resolution is measured against the level just before the December meeting. I found no sign of one.
  - Warsh has announced fewer meetings starting next year. That doesn't affect the December 2026 meeting (CNBC, Oct 28 2026).
- **Data timing:**
  - November CPI comes out on Dec 10, the day after the decision (BLS schedule). The October CPI (released Nov 10) is the last CPI the Committee will see.
  - The November jobs report is due Fri Dec 4 (BLS).
  - The October PCE release date isn't confirmed. It's usually late November.
  - The communications blackout should start around Sat Nov 28.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Does the Fed deliver a move it has clearly signalled within about 4 weeks?**
- Since roughly 1994, when the Fed began announcing decisions, it has almost never skipped a move that its leaders and the consensus clearly expected one meeting ahead. My estimate is that well over 95% of such strongly expected moves were delivered.
- Clear counterexamples are rare. The September 2015 non-hike is one, but that hike was never firmly signalled.
- Here the signal is strong:
  - The September dot plot median for end-2026 was 4.1%, which implies exactly one more 25 bp hike. 16 of 19 participants saw at least one more hike in 2026 (Kitco/Newsquawk, Sep 16 2026).
  - Two members dissented in favour of a hike in October (Hammack, Cook).
  - Waller (Nov 10) said he would back 25 bp "even if" October inflation improved.
- One caveat: the Chair himself hasn't committed. Warsh "does not believe in forward guidance" outside of crises.

**B. Size of hikes inside a tightening cycle (my own count from Fed history)**
- 2004–06: all 17 hikes were 25 bp, so 0% were 50.
- 2015–18: all 9 hikes were 25 bp, so 0% were 50.
- 1999–2000: 5 of 6 were 25 bp and 1 was 50 bp (May 2000), so about 17% were 50.
- 1994–95: of 7 hikes, 3 were 25, 3 were 50 and 1 was 75. A large share were 50+.
- 2022–23: of 11 hikes, only 5 were 25. The rest were 50 or 75, in a period of 8–9% inflation.
- **Overall:** in the "gradual" cycles (2004, 2015, 1999), roughly 30 of 32 hikes (about 94%) were 25 bp. Moves of 50 bp or more happened mainly when inflation or growth was running very hot (1994, 2022).
- **Today:** core CPI is running about 0.3% a month, roughly 3.6–3.7% annualized. That is elevated, but far from 2022 levels.

**C. Second hike after a pause in a newly restarted cycle**
- After a first hike followed by a pause, the next step has historically been 25 bp. Examples: December 2015 → December 2016, and 1999.
- I'm not aware of any case where the Fed went straight to 50 bp right after a hold meeting. Doing so would signal that the October pause was a mistake.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 10–12, 2026: Bowman.** At the Council on Foreign Relations she said she "may support" a December hike because inflation is persistently high. Coverage varies on how firm this was. In late September she had said there was "no urgent need" to act (Yahoo Finance; PANews; American Banker, Nov 2026).
- **Nov 10–11, 2026: Logan (Dallas, a 2026 voter).** She said the FOMC "needs to implement at least two more 25-basis-point rate increases" and that rates need to rise "at least 50 bp." Her framing is in 25 bp steps, but she was reported as open to Waller's larger move (Yahoo Finance; Trading & Investment News, Nov 2026).
- **Nov 2026: Hammack (Cleveland, voter, dissented in October).** One less-established source says she favours "more frequent, smaller rises" rather than a 50 bp jump (fattail.ai).
- **Nov 2026: other voters.** Barr and Kashkari were reported as favouring tighter policy. I found no December-specific comments from Vice Chair Jefferson.
- **Nov 10, 2026: Waller, London speech.**
  - He would support 25 bp in December even if October CPI improved.
  - He said a 50 bp move "should be considered" if October core CPI came in at 0.3% or more, which it did.
  - He also said "Hiking only 25 basis points would put our credibility at risk."
  - Several outlets still describe his lean as 25 bp (CNBC, Nov 10 2026; The Hill; Quartz).
- **Nov 10, 2026: October CPI.**
  - Headline +0.4% m/m against about +0.3% expected. Core +0.3% against about +0.2% expected, the third straight 0.3% core print.
  - No 12-month rates were published, because October 2025 data is missing after the 2025 shutdown.
  - Energy, shelter and food led the rise (BLS release, Nov 10 2026; Fox Business; Stocktitan).
- **Nov 10, 2026: forecasters.**
  - Goldman Sachs now expects hikes in December and March 2027 (CNBC markets blog).
  - Nomura expects December plus March.
  - Bank of America's base case is 25 bp, but it says the risk of 50 bp "has increased substantially" (TheStreet).
- **Nov 6, 2026: October jobs report.**
  - Payrolls +165k against roughly 60k expected, with Aug–Sep revised up by a combined 84k.
  - Unemployment 4.1%, below the 4.2% expected. U-6 rose to 7.9%.
  - Average hourly earnings +3.1% y/y, which is below inflation (Fox Business; ABC, Nov 6 2026).
- **Nov 3, 2026:** midterm elections took place. Warsh said politics played "no role" in the October decision (Yahoo Finance, Oct 28).
- **Oct 28, 2026: FOMC hold.**
  - Range held at 3.75–4.00% by a 10–2 vote. Hammack and Cook dissented in favour of a 25 bp hike.
  - Administered rates: interest on reserve balances (IORB) 3.90%, overnight reverse repo (ON RRP) 3.75%.
  - Warsh gave no forward guidance but stressed the 2% goal. Coverage called it "balanced but sufficiently hawkish" (CNN; CNBC; Portfolio Adviser, Oct 28 2026).
- **Sep 16, 2026: first hike since July 2023.**
  - 25 bp by a 12–0 vote, to 3.75–4.00%.
  - Dot-plot medians: 4.1% for 2026 and 4.1% for 2027.
  - Warsh did not submit a dot.
  - Trump attacked a "hostile," "political" board but spared Warsh, and called for rates at 1% or less (Fox Business; Colorado Politics; Bloomberg Government, Sep 2026).
- **Background:**
  - War with Iran has pushed oil prices up.
  - AI-related investment is cited as an inflation driver. Bowman estimated AI accounts for about a quarter of excess inflation (AOL).
- **Scheduled before the decision:**
  - October PCE (likely late November).
  - Fed speeches until the blackout starts around Nov 28.
  - November jobs report on Dec 4.
  - The meeting on Dec 8–9, with a new Summary of Economic Projections (SEP) and dot plot.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**YES: exactly 25 bp (base case).**
- The September dots already planned one more 25 bp hike.
- Two members dissented for a hike in October, and inflation has since come in hotter than expected.
- Hawks (Waller, Logan, Hammack, Barr, Kashkari, Cook) and swing voters (Bowman) are moving toward hiking.
- A Chair who avoids forward guidance tends to favour gradual steps, and 25 bp leaves the option of continuing in January or March. Several banks are already forecasting March.
- This path depends on the data before the meeting being neither weak nor alarming, and on Warsh preferring gradualism.

**NO via a 50 bp hike (the main risk to YES).**
- Waller explicitly tied a 50 bp case to a 0.3%+ core print, and that condition was met.
- Logan's "at least 50 bp" remark points the same way, and BofA flags rising risk.
- This path becomes more likely with:
  - a hot October PCE;
  - another strong jobs report on Dec 4 (payrolls well above 150k, wages accelerating);
  - rising inflation expectations;
  - further oil shocks;
  - Warsh choosing a show of credibility.
- Working against it:
  - The Fed typically signals a 50 bp step in advance, and Warsh doesn't do guidance.
  - Hammack is reportedly against "a 50 shift."
  - Core at 0.3% a month is a moderate overshoot.
  - Jumping to 50 bp right after an October hold has little precedent.

**NO via a hold.**
- This would need:
  - a sharp turn in the data (a weak November payroll, soft PCE);
  - financial-market stress, such as the AI-equity correction Bowman warned about;
  - a geopolitical shock; or
  - a hold engineered by the Chair despite dissents.
- Political pressure from Trump is a background factor, but the Committee hiked 12–0 in September despite it.
- This looks like the least likely path given the dots and the officials' comments.

**NO via a cut, an intermeeting move, or no statement:** negligible.

## 5. Signposts

- **Warsh speeches or interviews before the blackout (around Nov 28):**
  - Language about gradualism or "measured" steps points to 25 bp and supports YES.
  - Language about "credibility," "decisive" action or front-loading supports a 50 bp NO.
  - Silence keeps 25 bp as the default.
- **October PCE (late November):**
  - Core PCE at 0.3%+ m/m strengthens the 50 bp case.
  - 0.1–0.2% locks in 25 bp.
- **November jobs report (Dec 4):**
  - A very strong report (payrolls over 200k, unemployment at or below 4.0%, wages accelerating) raises the odds of 50 bp.
  - A very weak one (negative payrolls, unemployment at or above 4.3%) raises the small chance of a hold.
- **Explicit statements from more voters:**
  - Hammack, Cook, Jefferson, Barr or Kashkari backing 50 bp moves probability toward a 50 bp NO.
  - Bowman or Jefferson framing a hike as "25 and assess" supports YES.
- **Media "leak" pieces in the blackout week** (WSJ, Bloomberg, Reuters Fed reporters) usually reveal the size. These are probably the strongest single late signpost.
- **Shocks:** a sharp equity sell-off, oil spike, credit event, or government funding disruption.

- The exact SEP dot distribution, i.e. how many participants wanted more than one additional hike in 2026, is unconfirmed.
- I couldn't establish Warsh's own lean on 25 vs 50, or Jefferson's position.
- The October PCE release date and the full blackout-period schedule are unconfirmed.
- The year-over-year inflation level is unclear because BLS published no 12-month CPI rate. The ~3%+ underlying trend is inferred from monthly core prints.
- How much weight Waller's credibility argument carries with the rest of the Committee is unclear. He is one of 12 voters.
- The 2026 voter roster is inferred from the reports (Hammack, Logan, Kashkari reported as voting). I didn't verify the full rotation.
