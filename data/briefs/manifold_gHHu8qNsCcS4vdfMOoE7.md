# Research brief: will the 2026 FIFA World Cup gross more than the 2022 World Cup, adjusted for inflation?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES (plain meaning):** the 2026 World Cup (USA, Canada and Mexico; 48 teams, 104 matches, 11 June – 19 July 2026) has to "gross" more money than Qatar 2022 once both are in the same inflation-adjusted dollars. The question gives no resolution criteria, so who decides is unclear. The market creator will most likely rely on published FIFA figures and press reports.
- **Deadline:** the market closes 14 December 2026. The tournament is over. FIFA says the audited results will appear in its **next annual report, after the audit is finished** (Ministry of Sport / Insider Sport, Oct–Nov 2026). FIFA's 2022 annual report came out before the March 2023 Congress, so the 2026 report will probably appear around March–May 2027, after the close date. Resolution will therefore probably rest on FIFA statements and press reports, not audited accounts.
- **What "gross" could mean.** The answer is YES under every reading I checked:
  - **FIFA total revenue for the four-year cycle.** 2019–2022 was a record **US$7.568bn** (FIFA Annual Report 2022). For 2023–2026, FIFA's revised budget was **$13bn**, raised in May 2025 from $11bn (Inside World Football, May 2025). Infantino reportedly told member associations it would be about **$15bn**, "I think we can top the $15 billion mark" (The Star/Malay Mail citing the Guardian, 19 July 2026; Italpress, July 2026).
  - **Tournament-only revenue.** Before the tournament, FIFA estimated **$8.9bn** from the 2026 World Cup itself (SportsPro, 2026). The common press figure for Qatar is **$7.5bn**, but that is the whole cycle's commercial revenue (Al Jazeera, 20 Nov 2022). Comparing 2026 tournament-only against 2022 whole-cycle is the least favourable comparison for YES, and 2026 still appears to come out slightly ahead (see the arithmetic below).
  - **Tickets and hospitality.** 2022: tickets **$685.9m** plus hospitality **$242.9m**, for **$929m** total (FIFA Annual Report 2022, note 4). 2026: about **$5bn**, according to sources quoted by The Times, against a budget of $3.3bn. FIFA has not confirmed this figure (Insider Sport, 29 Oct 2026). The Times put net ticket revenue alone at about €4.49bn (Zamin.uz, Oct 2026).
- **Inflation adjustment (my arithmetic):**
  - US CPI-U was **336.940** in September 2026 (BLS, released 14 Oct 2026).
  - The 2022 annual average was about 292.7 and December 2022 about 296.8. That gives a multiplier of about **1.135–1.15**.
  - 2022 cycle revenue of $7.57bn becomes about **$8.6–8.7bn** in 2026 dollars.
  - 2022 tickets and hospitality of $929m becomes about **$1.05–1.07bn**.
- **Edge cases:**
  - "Gross" could be read as **economic impact on the host country** instead of FIFA revenue. That reading is hard to settle with comparable data, though 2026 estimates (often $30bn+) also tend to be larger. I did not verify those figures.
  - The ~$15bn is for the **whole cycle**. It includes about $2bn from the 2025 Club World Cup and about $570m from the 2023 Women's World Cup (Ministry of Sport/Insider Sport summaries, Oct–Nov 2026). Taking those out still leaves roughly $12bn+.
  - Using a different currency (EUR or GBP) or a different deflator does not change the ranking.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Each FIFA cycle's revenue against the previous cycle, in real terms.** These are approximate nominal cycle revenues from FIFA reports, recalled from memory and not re-checked here:
   - 2007–10: about $4.2bn
   - 2011–14: about $5.7bn
   - 2015–18: about $6.4bn
   - 2019–22: $7.57bn
   
   Nominal revenue has set a new record in every cycle since at least 2002. In real terms 2014→2018 was roughly +4% and 2018→2022 roughly +1–2%, so real growth was narrow in the last two cycles. Even so, about 5 of the last 5 cycles grew in real terms.
2. **Ticket and hospitality revenue for a host country with large stadiums against a smaller host.** USA 1994 still held the attendance record (3.59m) until 2026. Qatar 2022 had 64 matches in small stadiums, sold 3.18m tickets and earned about $686m from tickets (FIFA AR 2022). 2026 had 104 matches in NFL-size stadiums, a 99.7% average fill and an average of about 65,351 per match, roughly 6.8m in total (AFP via Herald Corp, 20 July 2026). That attendance is about 2x Qatar's, before any effect from higher prices.
3. **Revenue against pre-event budget for FIFA World Cup cycles.** The 2019–22 cycle beat its budget (broadcasting beat its budget by $126m, or 4%) (FIFA AR 2022). For 2023–26, the budget was raised from $11bn to $13bn, and reported actuals (about $15bn) beat even the revised figure. Large shortfalls against budget are historically rare.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Early Nov 2026:** Coverage repeats that FIFA has said 2026 World Cup revenue "has reached $15 billion, exceeding forecasts." FIFA says the financial results will be published in the next annual report once audited (ua.news citing the Guardian; Ministry of Sport, Nov 2026).
- **29 Oct 2026:** The Times reports about **$5bn** in ticket and hospitality revenue, "more than five times" Qatar's, against a $3.3bn budget. The drivers cited are dynamic pricing and FIFA's own resale platform, which takes a fee from both buyer and seller (15% each). FIFA has not confirmed the figure (Insider Sport, 29 Oct 2026; Dailysports, Oct 2026).
- **14 Oct 2026:** US CPI for September is 336.940, up 3.7% year on year (BLS).
- **20–23 July 2026:** The tournament ends with a 99.7% average fill and 65,351 average attendance. Reports say FIFA expects about $15bn of revenue (AFP/Herald Corp, 20 July 2026; Asiae, 23 July 2026).
- **18–19 July 2026:** The Guardian reports that member associations were told revenue is about $15bn, against an expected $11bn (The Star, 19 July 2026).
- **Mid-June 2026:** Single-day and all-time attendance records are broken. The 1994 total is passed partway through the tournament (FIFA/WAM, June–July 2026).
- **May 2025:** FIFA Congress raises the 2023–26 budget to $13bn (Inside World Football, 16 May 2025).
- **Scheduled before close:** No audited release is expected. A FIFA Council meeting or the 2027 budget approval in December might include updated figures.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (dominant):**
- Total revenue: $13–15bn (2023–26) against about $8.6–8.7bn (2022 cycle in 2026 dollars) is roughly 1.5–1.7x.
- Tickets and hospitality: about $5bn against about $1.07bn is roughly 4.7x.
- Even the pre-tournament tournament-only estimate of $8.9bn slightly beats the inflated 2022 cycle total, and actuals reportedly ran above budget.

**Paths to NO (residual):**
- **(a)** The resolver uses an unusual measure, such as profit or net result after the much larger 2026 costs and prize money, or host-country economic impact against Qatar's very large spending.
- **(b)** The resolver compares a narrow 2026 tournament-only figure (if one were reported well below $8.9bn) against the 2022 whole-cycle $7.5bn inflated with a high deflator.
- **(c)** The resolver refuses to resolve without audited figures. That becomes an N/A or timing risk, not NO.

## 5. Signposts

- An official FIFA statement or Council communiqué confirming about $13–15bn would firmly confirm YES.
- FIFA disputing The Times' $5bn ticket figure, or revealing write-downs or refunds, would lower confidence only slightly.
- A resolver clarification defining "gross" as profit or economic impact would be the largest possible downside.
- A FIFA breakdown giving a tournament-only figure: anything above about $8.7bn settles every reading.

## 6. Uncertainties

- None of the 2026 figures are audited. The $15bn comes from reported statements to member associations (Guardian) and later press coverage, and I could not read FIFA's own document.
- I could not find a FIFA figure isolating Qatar 2022 tournament-only revenue, as opposed to the 2019–22 cycle.
- The resolution source and the meaning of "gross" are unspecified.
- The 2026 total attendance is approximate: about 6.8m, derived from the 65,351 average multiplied by 104 matches.
- The historical cycle figures in reference class 1 are from memory, not re-checked here.
- Several full-text pages (Insider Sport, SportsPro, Irish Examiner, Fortune) could not be fetched. Some details come from search summaries and secondary outlets.
