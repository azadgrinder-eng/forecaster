# Research brief: Will Swiss voters approve the popular initiative "For a restriction on fireworks" on 29 November 2026?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What is being voted on.** The federal popular initiative "Für eine Einschränkung von Feuerwerk" (the fireworks initiative) amends the Swiss constitution. It would ban the sale to private persons, and the use, of fireworks that make noise. Quiet fireworks such as "volcanoes" and authorised public displays would still be allowed (SRF "Die Feuerwerksinitiative in Kürze", Oct 2026; Euronews, 27 Oct 2026). It is on the ballot on **29 November 2026** with three other federal items: a VAT increase to fund the AHV pension, a change to the war-materials law, and the "Fairness" initiative on taxing married couples (bluewin/swissinfo, Oct–Nov 2026).
- **What YES requires.** Because it is a constitutional initiative, it needs a **double majority**: a majority of the national popular vote **and** a majority of cantons (at least 12 of 23 cantonal votes, with half-cantons counting as half). The question says "referendum passed." The natural reading is that admin.ch reports the initiative as accepted by both people and cantons.
- **Timing quirk.** The market closes on 28 November 2026 at 22:59 UTC, the day *before* the vote. The result will not be known before the close. Provisional official results appear on admin.ch on the evening of 29 November. In practice the market resolves on those results.
- **Edge cases.**
  - The provisional result on vote day is what everyone treats as decisive. Formal validation by the Federal Council comes weeks later, but reversals are essentially unheard of.
  - A popular majority combined with a cantonal-majority failure would resolve **NO**. This happened with the Responsible Business Initiative in 2020 (50.7% yes, failed the cantons).
  - The question calls it a "referendum." That should not matter: admin.ch will report it as "angenommen" (accepted) or "abgelehnt" (rejected).
  - There is no counter-proposal on the ballot, so there is no tie-break question. Parliament dropped the indirect counter-proposal when the National Council unexpectedly killed it in summer 2026 (Südostschweiz; SRF, 2026).

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. All federal popular initiatives (long-run).**
- About 10–11% of initiatives voted on since 1891 have been accepted, roughly 25 of about 230. This is from my background knowledge (Federal Chancellery statistics), not re-verified in this session.
- This prior is weak here, because it is dominated by initiatives that were polling below 50% from the start.

**B. Initiatives that opened at 60% or more in the first SRG/gfs.bern poll.**
gfs.bern says the yes share of initiatives almost always falls during a campaign. The drop ranges from 2 to 43 points, with an average of about 10 points since 2008 (gfs.bern "Dispositionsansatz" cockpit page).

| Initiative (vote date) | First SRG poll | Result | Accepted? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Abzocker (Mar 2013) | ~65% | 67.9% | Yes |
| Nursing / Pflegeinitiative (Nov 2021) | high 60s | 61.0% | Yes |
| Tobacco advertising for minors (Feb 2022) | low 70s | 56.6% | Yes |
| 13th AHV pension (Mar 2024) | ~61% | 58.2% | Yes |
| Responsible Business (Nov 2020) | 63% | 50.7% | **No** (failed the cantons) |
| Fair-Food (Sep 2018) | ~78% | 38.7% | **No** |
| Food Sovereignty (Sep 2018) | ~75% | 31.6% | **No** |
| Pro Service Public (Jun 2016) | ~58% | 31.6% | **No** (just under the threshold) |

- These figures are from memory. They are approximate and should be checked.
- Counted that way, about 4 to 5 of 7 or 8 such cases passed, roughly 55–65%.
- The big collapses (Fair-Food, Food Sovereignty, Pro Service Public) involved complex economic trade-offs and had low early firmness of opinion.
- The cases that held up (Abzocker, nursing, tobacco, 13th AHV) were simple, emotionally salient issues with firmly held opinions. That profile fits the fireworks initiative better: gfs.bern reports that **74% of likely voters already had a firm voting intention** (gfs.bern 1st SRG trend, 23 Oct 2026).

**C. Applying the average drop to the current polls.**
- First-wave polls are 62–69% yes.
- Applying the average ~10-point fall gives roughly 52–59% yes on vote day.
- Applying a large 15–20-point fall gives roughly 49–54%. That is the zone where the cantonal majority becomes a real risk.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **4 Nov 2026:** Republik reports one survey at 68% yes and a second at 69%. These are most likely Tamedia/LeeWas wave 1 and the SRG poll, not a new wave (Republik, 4 Nov 2026).
- **23 Oct 2026, SRG/gfs.bern wave 1** (fieldwork 5–18 Oct, n=14,874): **69% yes, 29% no**. 74% have firm intentions. gfs.bern calls it a snapshot at the start of the main campaign, not a forecast. Its analyst said the initiative has "very good chances" (gfs.bern; SRF; watson; swissinfo, 23 Oct 2026).
- **Late Oct 2026, YouGov Switzerland, first barometer** (MRP model, 7–19 Oct, n=2,949): **62% yes, 33% no, 6% undecided**.
  - Supporters of the SVP and FDP lean narrowly yes (50% and 53%), even though both parties recommend a no.
  - Women 69% yes, men 54% yes.
  - The regional figures reported (41% German-speaking, 30% French-speaking, 32% Italian-speaking) are inconsistent with the 62% national figure. They are probably "definitely yes" shares and should be checked (YouGov, Oct 2026).
- **~14 Oct 2026, Tamedia/20 Minuten (LeeWas) wave 1** (8–9 Oct, about 11,239 responses): **68% yes, 30% no**, with a majority in every party camp. It was the only one of the four items with a yes majority (20 Minuten; Aargauer Zeitung/schweizheute, Oct 2026).
- **6 Oct 2026, No campaign launched.** The cross-party committee includes the SVP, the Mitte, FDP figures and youth, the Gewerbeverband (small-business association) and the fireworks industry. It is campaigning mainly online because it has limited money, and argues that fireworks are part of Swiss celebration culture, including 1 August (SRF; Südostschweiz/Keystone-SDA; cash.ch, Oct 2026).
- **5 Oct 2026, Yes campaign launched.** It runs on 917 digital screens, with plans to expand to about 3,850. Animal-welfare groups lead it, and a farmer spoke at the launch (Plattform J, 13 and 21–22 Oct 2026).
- **Official recommendation:** the Federal Council (Environment Minister Albert Rösti) and Parliament recommend **NO**. The National Council rejected the initiative 131–50 and the Council of States 40–3 (20 Minuten, Oct 2026). Their argument is that fireworks are already heavily regulated and a ban is disproportionate (admin.ch; euronews, Oct 2026).
- **Background on opinion:**
  - Before the campaign, YouGov found 60% of Swiss oppose private fireworks on 1 August (YouGov, 2025).
  - A separate survey found about half see fireworks as part of the national holiday (swissinfo, 2025).
  - A growing number of municipalities restrict fireworks on 1 August (Blick, 2025–2026). The NZZ wrote on 31 Dec 2025 that "the trend is running against New Year's Eve firecrackers."
- **Still to come before the vote:**
  - The SRG/gfs.bern wave 2, typically published about 11 days before the vote, so around **18 Nov 2026**.
  - Further Tamedia/LeeWas waves; wave 2 had not been found in searches as of 13 Nov. The final wave usually comes about 11 days out.
  - A possible YouGov final barometer.
  - The poll publication blackout starts about 10 days before the vote.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (the likely main scenario):**
- Support stays in the high 50s to mid 60s. The issue is simple and emotional (pets, livestock, wildlife, noise, fine dust), and opinions are firm.
- Majorities in every party camp limit how far the "No" party recommendations can pull support down.
- The No side is underfunded. Animal-welfare donors and the Yes campaign's screen advertising give it visibility.
- At 55% or more nationally, a cantonal majority is very likely, because support is broad and not concentrated in cities.

**Paths to NO:**
1. **A late slide.** The usual initiative decay of about 10 points or more, driven by:
   - "tradition / 1 August / freedom" messaging;
   - fear of enforcement problems and cross-border buying;
   - "already regulated locally" (federalism) arguments.
   A Fair-Food-style collapse seems less likely given the firm opinions, but cannot be ruled out.
2. **A cantonal-majority failure on a narrow popular yes.** Rural German-speaking cantons (central Switzerland, Appenzell, Schwyz, Uri, Obwalden/Nidwalden) and possibly Ticino or Valais could vote no. The Röstigraben (language divide) on this issue is unclear: the YouGov regional figures hint at weaker "definite" support in the French- and Italian-speaking regions.
3. **Turnout composition.** The VAT-for-AHV item may bring out older and more conservative voters. Their effect on this item is ambiguous: older voters are not obviously pro-fireworks, while men and SVP voters are the weakest groups.

## 5. Signposts

- **SRG wave 2 (~18 Nov):**
  - Yes at 60% or more with a small decline → strong move toward YES.
  - Yes at 55% or less, or a decline of 8 points or more, or "No" firming up among SVP/FDP/Mitte voters → meaningful move toward NO and toward cantonal-majority risk.
- **Tamedia/LeeWas wave 2 and 3:** look for cantonal or regional breakdowns. Rural and French/Italian-speaking yes shares below 50% would be the main warning sign.
- **Big late spending by the No side** (fireworks industry, Gewerbeverband) or a viral "tradition" campaign → toward NO.
- **Vote day (29 Nov):** gfs.bern publishes its extrapolation (Hochrechnung) around 12:30–13:30 CET. In practice it settles the outcome within hours.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **No second-wave polls found** as of 13 Nov 2026. I could not confirm whether Tamedia wave 2 has been published, and the trend since mid-October is unknown.
- **Regional and cantonal breakdowns** are unverified. The YouGov regional figures look internally inconsistent, and I found no canton-level estimates.
- **Base-rate examples** in section 2 are from memory and should be checked against admin.ch and gfs.bern archives.
- **Official texts unread.** admin.ch, gfs.bern, SRF, watson and Wikipedia pages could not be opened directly (proxy blocked). Details of the initiative text, including the exact exemptions and what counts as "noise-producing," and the full gfs.bern breakdown come only from search snippets.
