# Research brief: Will PP win the most seats in Spain's 29 November 2026 general election?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **Event:** Spain holds a snap general election for all 350 seats in the Congress of Deputies on Sunday 29 November 2026. Pedro Sánchez announced the dissolution on 5 October 2026, after Congress voted down two housing decree-laws. The dissolution decree was due in the official gazette (BOE) on 6 October (Euronews, 5 Oct 2026; CNN Español, 5 Oct 2026; Diario de Avisos, Oct 2026).
- **What counts as YES:** PP lists must win more seats nationwide than any other party's lists. The joint UPN–PP list in Navarre counts as PP. PSC seats in Catalonia count as PSOE. If two parties tie on seats, the one with more valid votes nationwide wins.
- **Timing:** The market closes 30 Nov 2026 at 04:59 UTC, the night after the vote. Provisional results from the Interior Ministry come in from 20:00 on 29 November and are normally almost complete before midnight (DatosClaros, Nov 2026). The final count and the official proclamation come a few weeks later. Resolution goes by "a consensus of credible reporting", with infoelectoral.interior.gob.es as the tie-breaker if reporting is ambiguous. In practice the election-night provisional count should decide it. The only exception would be a near-tie in seats that the final count could reverse.
- **Fallback:** If no definitive result exists by 30 Nov 2027, the market resolves to "Other". That is very unlikely, because the election is already formally called.
- **Edge cases:**
  - A PP–PSOE tie on seats goes to the national vote total.
  - The rules group Frente Amplio, Podemos and the regional lists separately. That affects the left's vote but not the PP-vs-PSOE contest for first place.
  - Lists were registered by late October. PSOE and Frente Amplio run separately everywhere except some arrangements in the Balearics. Frente Amplio stood down in Teruel, which helps PSOE there (Artículo 14, 27 Oct 2026; Vozpópuli, Oct 2026).
- **Poll blackout:** By Spanish law (LOREG), polls cannot be published in the last 5 days. So the last public polls will come out around 23–24 November.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Does the party leading the polls about 3 weeks out win the most seats?** This tally is from my own knowledge of Spanish elections since 1982; it was not sourced in this search. The party leading private polls a few weeks before the vote won the most seats in about 12 of 14 elections: 1982, 1986, 1989, 1996, 2000, 2008, 2011, 2015, 2016, April 2019, November 2019 and 2023.
   - The misses were 1993, when polls showed PP level with or slightly ahead of PSOE and PSOE won, and 2004, when PP led by about 4–5 points and lost after the 11-M Madrid bombings three days before the vote.
   - The only miss with a lead of 4 points or more followed a major shock in the final week.
2. **How big are recent polling misses on the PP–PSOE gap?** In July 2023 private pollsters overstated PP's lead. IMOP's last poll had PP 32.9% and PSOE 28.2%; the result was 33.1% and 31.7%. That is a 3.5-point underestimate of PSOE and about a 3.3-point overstatement of the gap (Demócrata analysis; El Independiente, 25 Jul 2023; Ara, Jul 2023).
   - Still, every private pollster except the state pollster CIS had PP finishing first, and PP did. The gap error narrowed the race but did not flip the winner.
   - Sociométrica's director put the 2023 error at under 3% and blamed a late surge to the Socialists (Mallorcadiario, 2023).
3. **How votes turn into seats:** In 2023 PP took 33.1% of the vote to PSOE's 31.7%, a 1.4-point lead, and won 137 seats to 121, a 16-seat lead. Spain's many small provinces give the largest party in rural Spain an extra seat bonus, and that has been PP.
   - The 40dB poll of 9 November 2026 is consistent with this: a 2-point vote lead becomes a 10-seat lead (PP 129, PSOE 119).
   - My rough estimate is that PSOE needs to roughly tie or beat PP in votes, perhaps by 0.5–1 point, to win more seats. Vox at about 19% splits the right in small provinces and may weaken PP's bonus somewhat.

- 28 Nov: reflection day, when campaigning stops.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (PP finishes first):**
- **Status quo.** Every private pollster has PP first, with a vote lead of 2–9 points and a seat lead of about 10–40.
- **Seat bonus.** PP's advantage in small provinces adds roughly 5–10 seats on top of its vote lead.
- **Weak PSOE allies.** The left is fragmented (Frente Amplio around 6–7%, Podemos around 2%). That lowers PSOE's coalition prospects, but it does not directly affect who comes first.
- **Government blame for rail safety.** Two deadly crashes in 2026 and a minister's resignation keep the issue alive.

**Paths to NO (PSOE, or in theory Vox, finishes first):**
- **A 2023-style polling miss.** PSOE was underestimated by about 3.5 points then. If that repeats on top of 40dB's 2-point gap, PSOE would lead in votes and could win more seats.
  - Voters on the left may consolidate behind PSOE as Frente Amplio and Podemos fade. The prospect of a PP–Vox majority could mobilise left-wing turnout, as in 2023.
- **Vox draining PP.** Vox polls around 19% and is rising in some surveys, such as DYM and the Valencian poll. If it takes votes from PP, PP falls toward 29–30%.
- **Campaign shocks.** The 16 November debate could backfire for Feijóo. In 2023 his debate win was followed by PSOE gaining ground anyway. A late scandal or other event could also shift votes.
- **Vox first:** essentially implausible, as it polls 10+ points behind PP.

**What each path depends on:**
- How much of 40dB's PSOE momentum is real, as opposed to that firm's house effect.
- Whether the Urda effect on PSOE fades or persists.
- How turnout differs between left-wing and right-wing voters.

## 5. Signposts

- **Toward NO:** Several mainstream pollsters (GAD3, Sigma Dos, DYM, NC Report, Sociométrica) show the gap under 3 points in mid/late November, or 40dB shows a tie or PSOE lead.
- **Toward YES:** The gap stays at 5 points or more in the final pre-blackout wave (about 23 Nov).
- **The debate (16 Nov):** Look at post-debate snap polls and whatever movement follows.
- **Vox above 20% with PP falling below 30%:** this squeezes PP's lead and seat bonus, and slightly favours NO.
- **Late news:** findings of the Urda investigation, further fallout from the rail crashes, or a scandal touching either party.
- **CIS pre-election survey (mid-November):** CIS overstates PSOE, so it carries little signal on its own. Its trend still matters.
- **Election night:** In Spain's provisional count, results from rural provinces come in early and often flatter PP at first. Do not over-read the first 30% of the count.

## 6. Uncertainties

- My web-page fetch tool was blocked by the network, so every figure comes from search summaries of Spanish media and poll aggregators. I could not open the original articles or check individual pollsters' methods.
  - Some figures conflict. For example, El Debate's 25 October average puts Podemos at 2.9%, while others have it at about 2%.
- I could not confirm whether CIS has published a pre-election survey after 14 October.
- How Urda has moved votes is disputed between pollsters, with 40dB and Ateneo del Dato showing PSOE up and DATA10 and IMOP showing it down.
- I have not quantified the break-even point at which PSOE overtakes PP in seats. My estimate is a roughly tied vote to a 1-point PSOE lead.
- I did not verify the historical poll-leader tally in section 2 with a search in this session; it comes from my own knowledge.
- Whether PP and UPN are running a joint list in Navarre in 2026 was not confirmed. It would matter only by about one seat.
- Twenty days remain, which is enough time for a 2023-style late swing.
