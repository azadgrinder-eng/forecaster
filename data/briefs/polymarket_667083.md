## Research brief: Will Zelenskyy and Putin's next meeting be in the US before 2027?

*Prepared 2026-11-27. About 34 days remain. The proxy blocked direct page fetches, so the facts below come from search-result summaries of the cited articles. They were consistent across outlets, but I could not check the full texts myself.*

---

### 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What YES needs:** the next in-person meeting between Zelenskyy and Putin has to happen on US territory by Dec 31, 2026, 11:59 PM ET. This appears to be a multi-outcome market priced by location. Only the first meeting counts, so a meeting elsewhere first (for example Türkiye, the UAE or Moscow) would settle the market on that location even if a US meeting came later.
- **What counts as a meeting:** both leaders are present and interact in person. Video calls and phone calls do not count, and neither does delegation-level talk without both leaders. Meetings in occupied Ukraine, such as Crimea, count as Russia.
- **Who decides:** a consensus of credible reporting.
- **Edge cases and ambiguities:**
  - If both attend the G20 in Miami (Dec 14–15) but never interact, for example they attend different sessions or sit in the same plenary without contact, that probably does not count, because the rules say "interact." A handshake or a short exchange probably does count.
  - UN Headquarters in New York is legally an international zone but would most likely be treated as the US by consensus reporting. No UN leaders' event is scheduled before the close date anyway.
  - A meeting at a US embassy or military base abroad would most likely be counted by the host country's location, not as the US.
- **Default outcome:** if no meeting happens by the deadline, the market resolves "No meeting before 2027," which means NO for the US outcome.

### 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Past Zelenskyy–Putin meetings.** They have met in person exactly once, at the Normandy-format summit in Paris on Dec 9, 2019. There has been no meeting in the roughly 57 months since the full-scale invasion in February 2022, despite many proposals:
   - Istanbul, May 2025: Zelenskyy flew to Türkiye and Putin did not come.
   - After the Alaska summit and the White House multilateral meeting in August 2025: Trump pushed for a bilateral or trilateral meeting and none happened (Wikipedia, Aug 2025 White House meeting).
   - Kyiv/Moscow proposals in January–February 2026, a Miami proposal in September 2026, and a Türkiye proposal in November 2026: all failed.
   - Roughly, at least 6 serious proposals since 2025 led to 0 meetings. Treated as a monthly rate, that is 0 in about 57 months.
2. **Putin attending G20 summits in person.** He last attended in person at Osaka in 2019. He was absent or virtual at Riyadh 2020, Rome 2021, Bali 2022, New Delhi 2023, Rio 2024 and Johannesburg 2025, so 0 of the last 6. He has traveled to the US once in the past decade: Anchorage, Alaska in August 2025 for a bilateral with Trump. His previous US visit was UNGA in 2015.
3. **Wartime leaders meeting before a deal is finalized.** Russia's stated rule is that a summit "makes sense only if the process is fully finalized" (Peskov, May 12, 2026, Eastern Herald). In wars that end in negotiation, heads of state usually meet to sign or ratify an agreement already worked out by envoys, not before. The Abu Dhabi talks are still stuck on territory (Kushner, Ukraine Today, Nov 2026), so this reference class points against a meeting within the next 5 weeks.

- **Nov 12, 2026:** Witkoff and Kushner met Putin in the Kremlin for about 2 hours, then met Zelenskyy near Dublin, where he was attending the European Political Community summit. Witkoff said the next round is agreed. No leaders' meeting was announced (NPR, Nov 12; Kyiv Independent).
- **Nov 10–11, 2026:** US-mediated trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi at envoy/adviser level. They produced a large prisoner exchange but no breakthrough on territory (HNGN, Nov 14; The Conversation).

  - Zelenskyy proposed meeting Putin in Türkiye and asked Erdogan to help arrange it. He rejected a phone call with Putin and asked for a meeting instead (CGTN, Nov 2; Ukrainska Pravda, Nov 3).

- **Nov 1, 2026:** Zelenskyy said there have been "some signals" about a G20 invitation (Ukrainska Pravda, Nov 1). Around Oct 31, Trump said he would invite Zelenskyy, "no problem." Rubio has tied an invitation to talks showing positive results. I found no confirmed formal invitation (UNN; Liga.net).
- **Oct 17, 2026:** Zelenskyy said neither the US nor Russia had confirmed a possible Miami meeting (Ukrainska Pravda).

**Scheduled events before Dec 31:**

- The next trilateral round, probably in Abu Dhabi, expected in December but with no date set.

### 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (meeting in the US):**
- **A. G20 Miami encounter.** This needs three things:
  - Putin decides to attend. Base rate and Bloomberg sourcing suggest this is unlikely, but the Kremlin has kept it open, and Putin did fly to Alaska in 2025.
  - Zelenskyy is formally invited and attends. He has said he would.
  - The two actually interact, through a Trump-brokered trilateral or even a brief handshake. The Kremlin rejected a G20 sidelines meeting in September. If Putin did come to Miami, though, Trump would push hard for a trilateral photo, and a brief interaction would be hard for Putin to avoid in a shared venue.
- **B. A deal-signing summit at the White House or Mar-a-Lago in late December.** This would require the territorial deadlock to break within weeks, and Putin to drop his Moscow-only position for a US venue. Trump's preference for US-hosted ceremonies makes the US the most likely venue if a signing happens, but a finalized deal by Dec 31 looks very unlikely given the Nov 11–24 reporting.

**Paths to NO:**
- **C. No meeting at all (dominant scenario).** Putin skips Miami or sends a deputy such as Lavrov, territory stays deadlocked, and the Kremlin keeps saying "Moscow only, after a deal." Every proposal since 2022 has failed this way.
- **D. A first meeting somewhere else.** Türkiye (Erdogan's offer, Zelenskyy's Nov 2 request), the UAE/Abu Dhabi, Saudi Arabia, or Moscow (which Kyiv rejects). Any of these would also resolve the US outcome NO. This is unlikely within 34 days for the same reasons as C.

**Key dependency:** essentially all of the YES probability depends on Putin attending the Miami G20 in person. Even then, a meeting with Zelenskyy is far from certain given the Kremlin's explicit rejection of that format.

### 5. Signposts

**Would raise the probability of YES a lot:**
- The Kremlin announces Putin will attend Miami in person. This is the single biggest signpost; watch Peskov's briefings and Ushakov's comments, likely in early December.
- The White House formally invites Zelenskyy to the G20 and confirms his attendance.
- Trump, Witkoff or Rubio announce a planned "trilateral" in Miami, especially if Ushakov or Peskov do not immediately deny it.
- A breakthrough on the territorial issue at the December Abu Dhabi round, or reports of a framework agreement ready for leaders to sign.
- Moscow drops its "Moscow only" or "only after finalization" language.

**Would lower the probability of YES:**
- The Kremlin confirms Russia will be represented by Lavrov or someone else, or will take part virtually.
- Congress or the White House withdraws Putin's invitation.
- A major escalation, such as the "massive strike" Ukrainian intelligence warns of, or the talks breaking down.
- Any announced leaders' meeting outside the US, such as in Türkiye or the UAE.
- After Dec 15 with no meeting in Miami, the probability of YES falls to near zero for the remaining 2 weeks.

### 6. Uncertainties

- I could not read the full articles because the proxy refused fetches. All facts come from search-result summaries and may lose nuance, for example the exact wording of Peskov's Nov 26 statement or whether a formal Zelenskyy G20 invitation has been sent.
- I could not establish whether the Kremlin will announce its G20 decision before or only at the last minute. It has said the format will be decided "closer to the event."
- It is unclear whether the September Kremlin rejection of a G20 sidelines meeting still holds after the Nov 19 Trump–Putin Shenzhen meeting, whose Ukraine content was not disclosed.
- The date and venue of the next trilateral round, and whether Europe joins, are not set.
- It is unclear how the resolver would treat an incidental encounter at a summit, such as both leaders in the same plenary room or a group photo without direct exchange.
- One search summary mentioned Kushner citing an "attack on Putin's residence" as a reason Ukraine was delaying. I could not establish its date or details.
