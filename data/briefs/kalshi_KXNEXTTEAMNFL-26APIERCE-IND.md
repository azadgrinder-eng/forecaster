# Research Brief: Alec Pierce's next team, "Stays with Indianapolis or Retires" (resolves Dec 1, 2026)

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** Pierce's "next team" is Indianapolis or he retires, as of the deadline. The question closes 2026-12-01 04:59 UTC, which is 11:59 pm ET on Nov 30. Kalshi decides the outcome.
- **What counts as NO:** In practice, the market resolves to another team if, before the close, Pierce is traded, or signs with another team as a free agent. The filed NextTeam rulebook says that when a free agent signs, the contract resolves to the team he signs with, including his former team (CFTC rulebook filing, 2026, seen only as a search excerpt). A secondary source describes three triggers that pay another team's outcome: a processed trade, a free-agent signing, or the player "joins a new team" (Stokastic summary via search, 2026). A secondary source also says most next-team boards settle Dec 1, 2026.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Release then waiver claim.** Whether this counts as "joining a new team" is not spelled out in what I could see. It would almost certainly count as a new team, but it is very unlikely here.
  - **Retirement** resolves YES.
  - **Being on injured reserve** has no effect. He remains a Colts player.
  - **Why the market is still open is unclear.** Pierce re-signed with Indianapolis on March 9, 2026. Under the free-agent clause that would point to Indianapolis. I could not confirm whether a March version of this market (Robinhood lists an event dated "Mar-08-2026") already settled, or whether this market is a newer one whose "stays" outcome is only confirmed at expiry. Either way, YES appears to need only that Pierce does not join a different team before Dec 1.
  - **Rules not read directly.** Kalshi and the CFTC PDF were blocked from my fetch tool. I relied on search excerpts, so the exact rule text should be checked.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **Players traded in the same season they signed a big free-agent deal.** This is extremely rare. I can't recall a recent case of a top-paid free agent being traded before the deadline of his first season, partly because trading him would speed up the dead money from his signing bonus. My rough estimate is well under 1% per season. This is a qualitative estimate, not a count.
2. **Injured-reserve players traded at the NFL deadline.** Trades of injured players happen but are rare. Teams almost never take on a player with no return timetable, especially one owed $60M+ fully guaranteed. My estimate is under 1%. Same caveat: this is not a count.
3. **Players released or claimed off waivers in the same season after receiving $60M+ fully guaranteed.** This is essentially zero in the NFL.

A general NFL base rate is also relevant. Even during a seller's fire sale, only a few players per team are moved, usually veterans on older or expiring deals. Indianapolis's sales so far fit that pattern: Nelson, Buckner, and possibly Taylor and Downs.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)
- **Nov 10, 2026, 4 pm ET:** the NFL trade deadline (NFL.com 2026–27 important dates; Chargers.com). After it, Pierce can only reach another team by being released and then claimed or signed, or after the season.
- **About Nov 5–6:** Jordan Schultz reported that the Colts would wait until after Sunday's game against Dallas (about Nov 8) before revisiting trade offers. Taylor talks were reported at a "standstill," and Josh Downs has asked for a trade, with the Colts asking about a 3rd-round pick (Yahoo/Heavy, early Nov 2026; NFLTradeRumors).
- **Early Nov:** SI's Colts fire-sale tracker says nobody on the roster is safe except rookie-contract players and some exceptions, explicitly naming **Sauce Gardner, Alec Pierce, and Bernhard Raimann** as exceptions. Covers' trade tracker also calls Pierce "not an automatic trade candidate," given his offseason re-signing and his heel injury.
- **Nov 4, 2026:** Coach Shane Steichen said he "can't put a timetable" on Pierce's return. Pierce has been on IR since about Sept 26 and is rehabbing his heel. The Colts are 2-6 (CBS/RotoWire, about Nov 4).
- **Oct 27, 2026:** The Colts traded Quenton Nelson to Seattle for 2027 3rd- and 2028 5th-round picks, and DeForest Buckner to San Francisco for a 2027 4th (Sports Michiana, Oct 27). GM Chris Ballard had been fired, and Ed Dodds is interim GM. Owner Carlie Irsay-Gordon is reshaping the team (Front Office Sports).
- **About Sept 23–26:** Pierce re-injured his surgically repaired left heel in the Week 2 loss at Kansas City. He was placed on IR, initially for a minimum of four games. The plan is rehab rather than a second surgery, though surgery is still possible (Fox Sports, Sept 23; Newsweek; CBS).
- **Aug 27:** Pierce came off the PUP list, then played 32 snaps in the opener against Baltimore.
- **July 27–28:** He was placed on PUP after offseason ankle/heel surgery. The surgery was more extensive than first reported (FantasyNerds, Aug 12).
- **March 9, 2026:** He re-signed with the Colts for 4 years at $114M ($116M with incentives). $84M is guaranteed, $60M of it fully guaranteed at signing, with a $26M signing bonus. The deal runs through 2029 at about $28.5M per year. It was billed as the largest free-agent receiver contract ever (ESPN/NFL.com/PFR/Spotrac/OTC, Mar 9, 2026).

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES (dominant):**
- No trade by Nov 10. Pierce stays on IR and the Colts' roster through Nov 30. Retirement would also count as YES but is irrelevant here.
- **Why it's likely:**
  - The contract is new.
  - The signing-bonus proration (about $6.5M per year) would mostly speed up into dead money if he were traded.
  - Buyers would be reluctant given the injury and the guarantees.
  - He is the No. 1 receiver the team just committed to.
  - Reporting explicitly calls him one of the untouchables.

**Paths to NO (tail risks):**
1. **A surprise trade by Nov 10.** The new ownership and interim GM are tearing down the roster aggressively, and a team could bet on him for 2027 and beyond. This depends on a buyer accepting an injured receiver owed about $84M guaranteed, and on the Colts accepting the dead money. Very unlikely.
2. **Release and claim before Nov 30.** Effectively nil given the guarantees.
3. **An administrative or rules oddity**, for example the market's treatment of the March re-signing. This is unlikely to resolve against "Stays."

## 5. Signposts
- **Before or at the Nov 10 deadline:** any Schefter, Rapoport, or Schultz report that the Colts are taking calls on Pierce. This would be the main NO driver. Once the deadline passes with Pierce still a Colt, YES becomes close to certain.
- **A Pierce practice return or a reported surgery** would matter little. A surgery would make a trade even less likely.
- **Any official Kalshi clarification** on settlement, given that he re-signed in March.

## 6. Uncertainties
- I could not read the exact Kalshi rule text or confirm why the market remains open after the March re-signing.
- There may be Colts sell-off moves after the Nov 8 Cowboys game that I could not see.
- My base rates are approximate, not systematically counted.
