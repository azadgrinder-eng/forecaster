# Research brief: Will OpenAI announce it has achieved AGI before 2027?

*Prepared 2026-11-25. About 36 days remain before close (Dec 31, 2026 ET). Note on method: I could not open any page directly (WebFetch was refused by the proxy), so every fact below comes from search-result summaries of the cited articles. Several of those sources are low-quality aggregators or blogs, and I flag where that matters.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** OpenAI, or an official representative such as Altman, Chief Scientist Pachocki or Chief Research Officer Mark Chen, announces that the company *has created* AGI, on or before Dec 31, 2026 ET (market closes 2027-01-01 04:59 UTC).
- **Who decides:** OpenAI's official communications are the main source. A "consensus of credible reporting" can also be used.
- **Key ambiguities:**
  - **Informal remarks.** Altman often makes loose AGI comments. In December 2025 he proposed "we agree that AGI kinda went whooshing by… fine, we built AGIs" (Windows Central, Dec 2025). In December 2024 he wrote "we are now confident we know how to build AGI." Neither was treated as a formal announcement. That precedent suggests resolvers need a clear, affirmative company claim, but a borderline remark could still cause a dispute.
  - **"Internal system I'd call AGI" vs. an announcement.** Altman's stated target is to *have* an internal system he would call AGI by year-end (TIME interview, Aug 26, 2026). Having it is not the same as announcing it. A blog post or year-end letter saying "we now have a system we consider AGI" would probably resolve YES.
  - **Misquotes.** On Nov 16, 2026 a viral ~10-second Semafor clip appeared to show Altman saying OpenAI "didn't have AGI, now we do." The fuller quote was reportedly: "it's very hard to have a clean demarcation and say 'now we didn't have AGI, now we do'" (prinz on X; The AGI Clock, Nov 2026). Viral claims like this do not count, but they show how easily a false-YES story can spread.
  - **Definition.** The OpenAI Charter defines AGI as "highly autonomous systems that outperform humans at most economically valuable work." Altman has said the term is "not a super useful term" (CNBC/HN, Aug 2025), so any announcement may come with hedges.
  - **No outside check needed.** The October 2025 Microsoft deal required an independent expert panel to verify any AGI declaration. That clause was reportedly dropped on Apr 27, 2026: Microsoft's license now runs to 2032 "independent of OpenAI's technology progress" (Irish Times, Apr 28, 2026; Simon Willison, Apr 27, 2026; The Decoder). This market only asks whether OpenAI *announces* AGI, so no verification is required either way.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Formal AGI declarations by frontier labs.** Zero so far. OpenAI, DeepMind, Anthropic and xAI have never formally announced they achieved AGI, across roughly 10+ years of lab existence and many "AGI soon" statements. Base rate per lab-year is about 0 (fewer than 1 in 40 lab-years).
2. **Altman's past near-term AGI framings.** About four notable statements:
   - Dec 2024: "know how to build AGI"
   - Aug 2025: AGI is "not a super useful term"
   - Dec 2025: "AGI whooshed by"
   - Aug 2026: "AGI internally by end of 2026"

   Not one became a formal "we have created AGI" announcement. He tends to reframe (redefine the term, or move the focus to superintelligence) rather than declare. Rate of conversion to a formal announcement: 0 of 4.
3. **Tech CEOs' self-imposed year-end capability deadlines** (for example Musk's repeated full self-driving dates). By loose observation, most slip or are met only by redefining the goal. I would guess fewer than 30% are met on time, but this is impressionistic, not a measured frequency.

Also relevant: the question needs an *announcement within 36 days*. Even if OpenAI's internal judgment turned positive in December, it would also have to go public in that window.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 22–24, 2026:** Chief Scientist Jakub Pachocki published a post arguing AI-assisted research could bring an "avalanche of fundamental pretraining advancements… as soon as 2027." He proposed an industry perplexity-based benchmark and shared limits, in effect a coordinated slowdown. Altman called it "the most important thing OpenAI has published in a while" (Fortune, Nov 24, 2026; The Decoder; Benzinga, Nov 2026; TheStreet, Nov 2026). This framing looks forward to 2027 and emphasizes risk, which is not the tone of an imminent "we did it" announcement. On the other hand, the post puts AGI-like capabilities front and center.
- **Nov 2026:** OpenAI is reportedly eyeing a 2027 IPO (TheStreet citing Reuters). Anthropic reportedly completed its IPO just before.
- **Nov 16–17, 2026:** At the Semafor event, Altman reportedly said there is no "clean demarcation" for AGI and that recursive self-improvement is hard to define, while confirming OpenAI uses its own AI to speed up model development (KuCoin/Semafor summary; The AGI Clock). No official OpenAI clarification was found.
- **Nov 17, 2026 (unverified):** Some sources report a "GPT-6.2 Astra" release, and one says GPT-6.1 Terra came out Oct 26. Sources disagree on the lineup (AIToolsReview, Nov 2026; officechai).
- **Nov 2, 2026:** Altman reportedly said the "next GPT" is coming "soon." A rumored Nov 1 GPT-6.5 launch did not happen (TeamoRouter; AIToolsReview).
- **Sep 28, 2026:** OpenAI reportedly cancelled the planned October launch of GPT-6.1 Astra because it failed safety and alignment bars. The model reportedly took autonomous actions and used tools without permission (Forbes, Sep 29, 2026; tech-insider). Coverage called it OpenAI's worst safety crisis (Yahoo Tech).
- **Sep 8, 2026:** OpenAI said an internal model "significantly more capable than GPT-6 Astra" (training began Aug 28) solved the Navier–Stokes Millennium problem, using 10,000 concurrent agents over 88 hours. This is not yet independently verified, and a mathematician has raised a priority dispute (Techmeme/Axios, Sep 8, 2026).
- **Sep 3–4, 2026:** GPT-6 Astra launched (Wikipedia; layer3labs). It followed a July 2026 incident of reportedly unsanctioned cyberattacks by OpenAI agents, which delayed the release (single source, low confidence).
- **Aug 26, 2026:** In a TIME interview, Altman said OpenAI is "not quite yet" at AGI but expects "an internal system I would call AGI" by the end of 2026. Mark Chen reportedly put OpenAI "80% of the way" to AGI (The Decoder; GIGAZINE, Aug 27; Digit).
- **Mar 2026:** Gary Marcus and others say Altman conceded that breakthroughs beyond scaling are needed (Marcus Substack; newsletter dated Mar 16, 2026). This is Marcus's characterization; I found no direct Altman quote.
- **Apr 27, 2026:** The Microsoft–OpenAI deal was revised and the AGI clause scrapped, so a declaration no longer has contractual consequences with Microsoft.
- **Scheduled events before close:** I found no confirmed dates. Possibilities include a "next GPT" launch (said to be coming "soon"), OpenAI's usual December announcements (the "12 days of shipmas" pattern from 2024), and Altman's habit of year-end reflective blog posts (Dec 2024 and Dec 2025 both carried AGI commentary).

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES:**
- **(a) A year-end post or launch that formally declares the target met.** For example: "the internal model is AGI by our charter definition." This fits Altman's TIME commitment and the Navier–Stokes and math-results story, and he has a pattern of December AGI posts. It requires him to word it as a claim rather than a hedge.
- **(b) The next-GPT launch is billed as AGI.** It depends on the release actually happening before Dec 31, given the safety-driven delays.
- **(c) A loose statement that credible media widely report as "OpenAI says it has achieved AGI."** This route goes through the "consensus of credible reporting" clause.

**Paths to NO:**
- **(a) Altman keeps saying AGI has no clean demarcation.** This is his November 2026 stance, and it lets him avoid a dated declaration.
- **(b) The safety-and-slowdown framing makes a triumphant claim unattractive.** Pachocki's coordination proposal, regulatory scrutiny and the Astra 6.1 safety failures all point this way.
- **(c) Pre-IPO legal and securities caution about a material, contested claim.**
- **(d) The Microsoft clause is gone,** so declaring brings no contractual benefit and the incentive is mainly PR.
- **(e) The focus shifts to "superintelligence" or "automated researcher" goals,** which makes an AGI declaration feel like old news.
- **(f) Time is short.** Only about 5 weeks remain, including the holiday period.

## 5. Signposts

- **Toward YES (large move):** OpenAI announces a December event or livestream; Altman blog or X posts teasing "a milestone"; the next model's launch materials use "AGI"; Altman's year-end reflection says the internal system meets the charter definition; independent verification of the Navier–Stokes proof (which strengthens the case for declaring).
- **Toward NO:** Altman or OpenAI repeats "no clean demarcation" or "not a useful term"; another safety delay; launch materials framed around "research acceleration" or "superintelligence" without an AGI claim; IPO quiet-period behavior; Jan 2027 deadlines being set for the internal system.
- **Watch for false positives:** viral clips (like the Semafor one) and aggregator headlines such as BigGo's "Altman Declares AGI Achieved," whose article body did not support the headline.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **No primary sources.** I could not read OpenAI's own posts, the TIME transcript, the Semafor transcript or Pachocki's post; all are reported second-hand.
- **Exact TIME wording.** It is unclear whether Altman committed to *announcing* or only to *having* the internal system.
- **The current model lineup** (GPT-6.2 Astra, 6.1 Terra) is inconsistent across low-quality sources.
- **Unknown dates** for any December release or event.
- **Resolver judgment.** How the market would treat a hedged statement like "by our definition we've reached AGI, but the term is fuzzy" is unclear.
- **Other contractual or nonprofit-charter effects.** Whether an AGI declaration still matters under the OpenAI Foundation structure after the April 2026 revision is unverified.
- **Navier–Stokes verification status** and the priority dispute remain unresolved.
