# Research Brief: Will the Fed go Hike–Pause–Hike at its Sep, Oct and Dec 2026 meetings?

The first two decisions are already done and match. The Fed hiked on Sep 16 and held on Oct 28. **The question now depends only on whether the FOMC raises the upper bound of the target range on December 9, 2026.**

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** The upper bound of the target range must go up at Sep 15–16, stay the same at Oct 27–28, and go up at Dec 8–9. Any increase counts as a hike, so a 50bp move would also resolve YES. A hold or a cut in December resolves to NO/"Other". Any cut anywhere counts as "Other".
- **Status of the first two legs:**
  - **September: hike.** The FOMC raised the range 25bp to 3.75–4.00% in a unanimous 12–0 vote. It was the first hike since July 2023 (Fed press release, Sep 16, 2026; Fox Business, Sep 16, 2026).
  - **October: pause.** The range stayed at 3.75–4.00% in a 10–2 vote (Fed press release, Oct 28, 2026; StockAnalysis, Oct 28, 2026).
  - Both are locked in.
- **What remains:** For YES, the December statement (released Dec 9 at 2:00 pm ET) must show an upper bound above 4.00%.
- **Who decides:** The FOMC statement on federalreserve.gov.
- **Edge cases:**
  - Emergency moves between meetings are excluded. An emergency hike before December would raise the "prior level", which would make a December hike less likely. That scenario is very unlikely.
  - The Fed calendar lists the meeting as December 8–9, with minutes expected Dec 30 (Fed December 2026 calendar). One preview says "December 17–18". That looks like an error.
  - Warsh's plan to reduce the number of meetings applies to the calendar from fall 2027 or 2028 onward. It does not affect December 2026 (Newsquawk; Fortune, Jul 31, 2026).
  - The September and October statements I relied on came from search snippets and secondary reports. The Fed's site blocked direct fetching.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. September Summary of Economic Projections (SEP, the "dot plot") pointing to one more hike in the same year.** I counted these from my own memory of past Fed decisions:

| SEP | What the dots implied | What happened |
|---|---|---|
| Sep 2015 | Liftoff in 2015 | Hiked Dec 2015 |
| Sep 2016 | One hike in 2016 | Hiked Dec 2016 |
| Sep 2017 | One more in 2017 | Hiked Dec 2017 |
| Sep 2018 | One more in 2018 | Hiked Dec 2018 |
| Sep 2023 | One more in 2023 (12 of 19 dots) | Did not hike |

- That is **4 of 5 (about 80%)**.
- In Sep 2026, 16 of 18 participants projected at least one more hike in 2026. The median year-end rate was 4.1%, up from 3.8% in June (Newsquawk; InvestmentNews, Sep 16, 2026). That level of agreement is stronger than in Sep 2023.

**B. Hike–skip–hike in gradual cycles where the Fed hikes only at SEP meetings.**
- The pattern happened repeatedly in 2017–2018: Mar–May–Jun 2017; Mar–May–Jun 2018; Jun–Aug–Sep 2018; Sep–Nov–Dec 2018. It also happened in May–Jun–Jul 2023.
- After a "skip" at a non-SEP meeting within an active cycle, the next SEP meeting brought a hike most of the time.
- Counterexamples where a pause turned out to end the cycle: Aug 2006 (after the June 2006 hike) and Sep 2023.
- My rough count, excluding cycle-ending pauses that were known only in hindsight, is about 7 of 9 resumptions.

**C. Hawkish dissents at the prior meeting.**
- Two voters, Cook and Hammack, dissented in favor of an immediate hike in October.
- Dissents for tighter policy usually show that the committee's center is moving that way, but they don't guarantee a move. An example is the Sep 2023 to early 2024 period, when hawks wanted more and the Fed never moved.
- I did not compute a precise base rate for this.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (a December hike):**
- Inflation stays hot. Core PCE is around 3.1% and rising, against the committee's own 3.4% Q4/Q4 projection. Services inflation is accelerating.
- The labor market stays firm.
- The September dots plus hawkish regional presidents (Logan, Musalem, Hammack, Barkin, Goolsbee) commit the committee. December is the SEP meeting where it can "deliver" the projected hike.
- Cook's dissent shows that even a governor generally seen as dovish wants a hike, so the hawkish lean is broad.
- A further move up is helped by the fact that the Fed has already shown it will defy Trump.

**Paths to NO (a hold, or less likely a cut):**
- **Weak data:** A soft October CPI (0.1%), a soft October core PCE, or a weak November jobs report.
- **Energy:** A reversal in energy prices, with gasoline easing in October, could let the Fed argue headline inflation is fading.
- **Markets and growth:** Financial-market stress (long yields reportedly near two-decade highs) or a slower AI capex boom could weigh on growth.
- **Warsh's preferences:** He visibly resisted the hike pressure in October and may prefer to watch "more information". His reformist framing (balance sheet, fewer meetings) may make him tolerant of waiting.
- **Political pressure:** After the midterms, pressure from the White House could matter at the margin.
- **Sep 2023 analogue:** As in Sep 2023, the dots promised one more hike and the committee simply never delivered it.

## 5. Signposts

- **October CPI core print, today:**
  - At or above 0.3% supports YES.
  - At or below 0.1% is a strong move toward NO.
  - 0.2% (in line with consensus) probably keeps the hike as the base case.
- **Oct 27–28 minutes, Nov 18:** Language like "many participants judged further firming likely appropriate soon" supports YES. Emphasis on "patience" or "risk management" supports NO.
- **October PCE, about Nov 25:** Core PCE above 0.3% m/m supports YES.
- **November jobs report, about Dec 4:** A strong report locks in YES. A sub-50k print or a jump in unemployment raises the chance of NO.
- **Warsh, Vice Chair, and governor speeches before the blackout** (likely starting about Nov 28): A Warsh signal that the hike is "data confirmed" would be decisive. Watch Waller in particular; he spoke Oct 8 and his recent stance is unknown.
- **Pre-meeting reporting by Fed-watching journalists** (WSJ/Nick Timiraos-type pieces) in early December.
- **Financial stress or an energy-price collapse:** Either would argue for NO.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I don't have the actual October CPI result, released today.
- I could not read the Fed's primary documents (the fetch was blocked). The Sep and Oct outcomes are confirmed by many consistent secondary sources but not read directly. The Sep 16 statement and the 12–0 vote are taken from search snippets citing the release.
- Warsh's personal reaction function is unclear. His October hold, despite two dissents, conflicts with his strong-growth, high-inflation language.
- Some reported numbers conflict:
  - September core PCE monthly: +0.3% vs +0.5%.
  - Core CPI 2.6% y/y is far below core PCE 3.1%. One explanation is shelter weighting; the figure itself is reported but odd.
  - One source gives the range as 4.00–4.25%, which looks wrong.
- I did not find current forecasts from Goldman Sachs or Morgan Stanley.
- I did not verify the exact date of the November jobs report.
- On purpose, I did not collect or report market-implied probabilities.
