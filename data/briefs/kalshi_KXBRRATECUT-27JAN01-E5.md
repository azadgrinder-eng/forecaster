# Research brief: will the Central Bank of Brazil make exactly 5 rate cuts in 2026?

**Bottom line:** By my count, Brazil has already had **5 qualifying cuts in 2026**. The only scheduled Copom (monetary policy committee) meeting left this year is **8–9 December 2026**. A hold or a hike in December resolves YES. Any December cut, or a cut at an unscheduled meeting before 31 December, makes the total 6 and resolves NO. So the question comes down to whether Copom holds or hikes in December.

One limit on this research: the proxy blocked every attempt to open full articles, so all facts below come from search-result summaries of news outlets and BCB documents. I could not read any original text in full.

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **YES condition:** the number of separate decreases in Brazil's policy rate (the Selic target) during calendar 2026 is exactly 5. The size of each cut doesn't matter, and hikes don't cancel earlier cuts.
- **Count so far (2026 calendar):**
  - 28 Jan: held at 15.00%. This was unanimous, and Copom signalled it would start cutting in March (InfoMoney / Itaú Private, 28 Jan 2026).
  - **Cut 1:** 18 Mar, to 14.75% (Correio Braziliense, 19 Mar 2026). The question text confirms this one.
  - **Cut 2:** 29 Apr, to 14.50%. The question text confirms this one.
  - **Cut 3:** 17 Jun, to 14.25%, unanimous (Itaú Private / JB, 17 Jun 2026).
  - **Cut 4:** 5 Aug, to 14.00%. There was no July meeting in 2026 (O Povo / Forbes Brasil, 5 Aug 2026).
  - **Cut 5:** 16 Sep, to 13.75%, unanimous. Media called it the "fifth consecutive cut" (Itatiaia; Times Brasil, Sep 2026).
  - 4 Nov (meeting 282): **held at 13.75%**, unanimous. Media described it as "interrupting five consecutive cuts" (Sou Brasília; Piranot, 5 Nov 2026).
- **Remaining event:** the Copom meeting on 8–9 Dec 2026. The decision is announced on 9 Dec and the minutes come out on 15 Dec (Rankia, "Próxima reunião do Copom", Nov 2026).
- **Edge cases:**
  - An unscheduled cut before 31 Dec would count. Copom has essentially never cut between scheduled meetings in the inflation-targeting era, so this risk is negligible.
  - A December hike does not change the count, so it still resolves YES.
  - The count rests on the Selic target. One ISTOÉ snippet said the September move was "14% → 13.75%", which is consistent with the timeline above. One article dated the June statement "17/07/2026", which is almost certainly a typo.
  - Whoever resolves this should confirm the official BCB series. I could not open bcb.gov.br directly.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
- **(a) What Copom did at the meeting after its first hold following a cutting cycle (my count from memory of BCB history; not checked against a source this session):**
  - 2007–08: last cut Sep 2007, held, then hiked in Apr 2008.
  - 2009: last cut Jul 2009, held, then hiked in Apr 2010.
  - 2012–13: last cut Oct 2012, held, then hiked in Apr 2013.
  - 2018: last cut Mar 2018, held until Jul 2019.
  - 2020: last cut Aug 2020, held, then hiked in Mar 2021.
  - 2024: last cut May 2024, held, then hiked in Sep 2024.
  - Result: in about **6 of 6** cases, the meeting after the first hold was **not a cut**. I found no clear modern case of a one-meeting pause followed straight away by another cut.
- **(b) How often Copom surprises the consensus:** decisions that differ from the Focus/Bloomberg median are uncommon, roughly 5–15% of meetings in recent years (my rough estimate). Two caveats:
  - The November 2026 hold itself was described as a partial surprise. Agência Brasil called it a "surpresa", and Piranot said it went against most bets. Before that meeting, the Focus survey expected 13.50% at year-end, which implied cuts.
  - So Copom has shown it is willing to go against consensus, though this time in the hawkish direction.
- **(c) Hike vs hold:** both resolve YES, so the only relevant split is cut vs no cut.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)
- **26 Nov 2026 – November IPCA-15 (mid-month inflation preview):**
  - Rose 0.22% month on month, below every estimate in the Bloomberg survey (median 0.36%).
  - The 12-month rate was 4.64%, up from 4.62% and above the 4.5% top of the target band.
  - Analysts said the figure "reinforces the outlook for a hold in December" (BPMoney; CNN Brasil; InvestNews, 26 Nov 2026).
  - Gazeta Mercantil wrote that persistent inflation limits room for Selic cuts (26 Nov 2026).
- **23 Nov 2026 – Focus survey:**
  - Median year-end 2026 Selic stayed at **13.75%**, meaning no December cut.
  - The 2027 median rose from 12.75% to 13.00% (Jornal do Commercio, ~23 Nov 2026).
- **23 Nov 2026 – Galípolo (BCB governor):** spoke at the Itaú BBA Macro Vision panel. He defended not cutting in Dec 2025 and gave no guidance for December 2026 (Jornal de Brasília / O Povo, 23 Nov 2026).
- **~23 Nov 2026 – CalcBank "Radar Copom":** a probability tool based on the DI interest-rate futures curve (not a betting market). It showed about **59% hold and 41% hike to 14.00%**, with no meaningful weight on a cut. Treat this source as low reliability.
- **16 Nov 2026 – Focus:** projections for 2027 Selic and IPCA rose (Suno, 16 Nov 2026).
- **11 Nov 2026 – Galípolo:**
  - At the Bradesco Asset forum, he said the BC should not pre-announce Selic moves given the uncertainty.
  - He said the economy is slowing while inflation expectations remain under pressure, and that rates must stay "substantially contractionary" (Brasil 247; DGABC, 11 Nov 2026).
  - He called the current scenario more challenging than 2025–26 (Jornal do Comércio, Nov 2026).
- **10 Nov 2026 – November minutes (ata):**
  - Unanchored inflation expectations call for higher rates for longer, and risks are skewed to the upside.
  - The minutes made no commitment to a hold, cut or hike.
  - Risks cited: the exchange rate (one outlet says the USD/BRL rate near 5.51 is about 5.8% above Copom's assumption), oil and the Middle East, El Niño, energy costs, ending the 6x1 work schedule, and fiscal risk.
  - Copom's own inflation projection: about 5.6% for 2026 and 4.7% for 2027.
  - Sources: Atlas Público; Melhor Investimento; Remessa Online; Gazeta Mercantil; Avantgarde, Nov 2026.
- **9 Nov 2026 – Focus:** the year-end 2026 Selic median moved from 13.50% to **13.75%** (ES Brasil; WSCOM, 9 Nov 2026).
- **4 Nov 2026 – November hold:**
  - Unanimous decision to hold at 13.75%.
  - The statement dropped the language about the total size of the easing ("calibration") cycle, which analysts read as the cuts ending, at least for now.
  - Nomad expects a hold in December (Nomad, Nov 2026).
- **Scheduled before the deadline:**
  - IPCA (full November inflation): 11 Dec, after the decision.
  - Copom decision: **9 Dec**.
  - Copom minutes: 15 Dec.
  - December IPCA-15: 23 Dec.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
- **YES (hold or hike on 9 Dec), the dominant path:**
  - Copom has just paused and removed its cycle-size guidance.
  - The Focus consensus and analysts expect a hold.
  - The governor's language is hawkish, inflation expectations are unanchored, the real has weakened, and there is fiscal uncertainty around the new government.
  - The DI-based tool, if it is reliable, puts the residual risk on a hike, which still resolves YES.
- **NO (a December cut, most likely 25 bp to 13.50%), which would need:**
  - a dovish surprise driven by a sharp economic slowdown (Galípolo has noted the slowdown);
  - the IPCA-15 downside surprise continuing;
  - a quick fall in USD/BRL or a credible fiscal announcement that pulls inflation expectations down;
  - pressure from the new government.
- Even with those, cutting one meeting after a pause, with 12-month inflation above the target ceiling, would break Copom's historical pattern (section 2a).
- An unscheduled cut is a negligible path.

## 5. Signposts
- **Toward NO, would move the probability most:**
  - Galípolo or other directors signalling that the "calibration" can resume.
  - The Focus survey of 30 Nov or 7 Dec moving its year-end median back to 13.50%.
  - The DI curve pricing a cut.
  - A sharp BRL rally or a sharp drop in inflation expectations.
  - Dissenting votes in favour of a cut.
- **Toward YES:**
  - More hawkish speeches.
  - Further BRL weakness.
  - Inflation expectations for 2026–27 still rising in Focus.
  - Hike pricing.
  - A worsening fiscal package.
- **Resolution:** effectively known on 9 Dec, after Brazilian market hours.

## 6. Uncertainties
- I could not open the official BCB pages or the November minutes text. The cut count of 5 is consistent across many outlets and matches the year's levels (15.00 → 13.75 = five 25 bp steps), but it is not directly verified against the BCB source.
- I could not verify the CalcBank hike/hold split, the current DI-curve pricing, or the latest USD/BRL level.
- I don't know the new government's fiscal plans or how the October 2026 election result affects Copom.
- I found no public pre-meeting guidance from Copom members specifically about December.
