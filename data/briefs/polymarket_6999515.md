# Research brief: Will a fourth person be reported as having an affair with Jensen Huang by December 31, 2026?

*Note: The market resolves on a count that starts at 3 and ends at some final number. The title asks about "3". Forecasters should treat the key quantity as P(count stays at 3), meaning no qualifying 4th person is reported by Dec 31, 2026, and also estimate P(count ≥4). All my page fetches were blocked by the proxy. Everything below comes from search-result summaries, so treat article details as secondhand.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **Starting count: 3.** These are Alice Huang (Huang acknowledged an affair Nov 8; outlets identified her), Kimberly Powell (NYT, Nov 11, unconfirmed) and an unnamed second NVIDIA employee (Reuters, Nov 12).
- **The count rises by 1** for each new person in a qualifying news article published by one of 12 outlets (NYT, WSJ, WaPo, FT, Reuters, AP, CNN, Bloomberg, ABC, CBS, NBC, Fox) between market creation and Dec 31, 2026, 11:59 PM ET. Only web news articles count. Opinion pieces, broadcast segments, podcasts and social posts do not.
- **Four ways a report qualifies:**
  1. The outlet states the affair as fact.
  2. The outlet's own sourcing supports it.
  3. The outlet reports an allegation about a *specific*, distinct person, even if it doesn't vouch for it. This is a low bar if someone is named.
  4. The outlet reports an explicit acknowledgment by Huang or his representatives.
- **Unnamed people** count under (1) or (2) only if the report clearly sets them apart from those already counted, for example "a third NVIDIA employee" or "someone who never worked at NVIDIA".
- **Ties go to the lower number.** If it's unclear whether someone is new, they are treated as already counted.
- **Reports that don't count:**
  - Identifying the Reuters employee by name.
  - General statements about infidelity.
  - Unnamed allegations the outlet only relays from social media or other outlets.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Market creation date isn't stated.** A new person first reported between Nov 12 and creation would not count, though I found none.
  - **A report that Huang had affairs with "at least N" people sets the count to N.** For example, a Wachtell finding or a congressional transcript saying "relationships with four employees" would resolve higher without names.
  - **Naming the Reuters employee adds nothing.** A big follow-up story could look like a new person and not be one.
  - **Plural wording is ambiguous.** "Multiple relationships with employees" language (e.g., NBC's headline "reports of affair and extramarital relationships") is probably too vague under rule (iii) or (ii).
  - **The "WSJ second affair" report (~Nov 14) is almost certainly Powell.** That makes it double-counting, not a new person.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

All of the following are from my own background knowledge, not searched this session.

**A. Executive/public-figure infidelity scandals where the count had already grown in the first week.** Once a scandal reaches "multiple partners" stage, more often keep surfacing.
- **Tiger Woods (2009):** 1 to more than 10 women reported by mainstream outlets within about 3 weeks of the Nov 27 crash.
- **Les Moonves (2018):** misconduct, not affairs. 6 accusers in the New Yorker, then 6 more about 5 weeks later.
- **Steve Wynn (2018):** many accounts in the first WSJ story, with more after.
- **Bill Gates (2021):** a Microsoft employee affair (May 2021), then within days NYT/WSJ reported other advances toward employees. Further women surfaced over the following months.
- **Steve Easterbrook (McDonald's, 2019):** 1 relationship at ouster. Relationships with 3 more employees came out about 9 months later via the company's own investigation, so beyond a 6-week window.

Rough tally from these escalating cases: about 3–4 of 5 added people within 6–8 weeks.

**B. CEO ousters over a single consensual relationship.** Brian Krzanich (Intel 2018), Mark Hurd (HP 2010), Jeff Bezos (2019), Darren Woods-type cases and Petraeus (2012) mostly stayed at 1 person. This class is less relevant because Huang already has 3, but it shows many scandals stall after the first wave.

**C. Timing pattern.** The window is about 40 days and comes 2–7 weeks after the scandal broke. In most escalation cases, new names came out in the first 1–3 weeks, and after that discoveries mostly came from formal processes such as investigations, lawsuits or testimony. Here the count went 1→3 in 4 days (Nov 8–12). I found no 4th person in the Nov 13–21 period.

**Rough base rate:** a person already reported to have had several affairs, under intense media focus, gets another person reported within the next ~6 weeks about 40–60% of the time. The escalation trend seems to have paused for over a week here.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (a 4th or later person):**
- **Investigative journalism.** At least five qualifying outlets (NYT, Reuters, WSJ, Bloomberg, CNBC-adjacent NBC) have teams on this. Five former employees already talked to the NYT, and Reuters found a separate source pair. Former employees seem willing to talk now that Huang is gone and can't retaliate.
- **The board/Wachtell probe** covers governance and is reportedly looking at relationships with employees. Leaks of "relationships with X employees" with X ≥4 would set the count.
- **Congressional testimony.** The Dec 1 transcript or a public Senate hearing could bring direct questions about other relationships, especially with foreign nationals relevant to security. Closed-door transcripts often leak or get summarized by members. A public Senate hearing gives senators the chance to ask "were there others?"
- **Huang's own "women" (plural) remark** hints he knows of more than one. A fuller apology, which Trump is urging, could include acknowledgments.
- **Divorce filing or legal discovery** in the securities suits. Discovery is slow, so this probably won't land before Dec 31.
- **A named allegation under rule (3)** is a low bar. A qualifying outlet only needs to report someone's allegation about a specific named person, even one it doesn't vouch for.

**Paths to NO (count stays at 3):**
- **The news cycle moves on.** Huang is out, and the story has shifted to China/export controls, a possible return and Trump. National-security questioning focuses on Alice Huang, not his personal life.
- **The board probe may wrap up quietly or slowly** (Wachtell investigations often take months). Its scope is security and governance.
- **Paul, Weiss will likely keep Huang from saying more.** Closed-door transcripts may never be released before Dec 31.
- **Strict counting rules:** a "relationships with several women" story wouldn't count, and naming the Reuters employee doesn't add.
- **No 4th person surfaced Nov 13–21** despite peak attention, which suggests the easiest-to-find stories may already be out.

## 5. Signposts

**Toward YES (big moves):**
- An NYT, WSJ or Reuters story describing "a third employee" or a non-employee (e.g., someone in Taiwan or a partner-company executive).
- Leaks from the Dec 1 transcript, or senators asking about other women at a public hearing.
- Wachtell findings citing a number of relationships.
- A divorce filing with allegations.
- Huang giving a broader apology or interview.

**Toward YES (smaller):** tabloid or Taiwanese media naming a specific woman, which qualifying outlets might then report as an allegation under rule (3). Wording matters here: "relays from other outlets" only fails for *unnamed* people.

**Toward NO:**
- Huang's testimony sticks to export controls.
- Transcripts aren't released.
- The board closes the investigation with a narrow statement.
- Coverage pivots to the CEO search or a Huang return.
- Several more weeks pass with no new names. Each quiet week lowers the odds, because hazard rates fall over time.
