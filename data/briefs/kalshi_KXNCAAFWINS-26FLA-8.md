# Research brief: Will Florida (college football) win 8 or more 2026 regular-season games?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **YES condition:** Florida finishes the 2026 regular season with at least 8 official wins. Ties, bowl games, SEC Championship games and College Football Playoff games do not count. The question closes 2026-12-20, after the regular season ends, so timing is not an issue.
- **Current position:** Florida is **5–4 (3–4 SEC)** after a 23–7 home loss to Oklahoma on Nov 7 (ESPN recap, Nov 7 2026; Wikipedia 2026 Florida page, as of Nov 8). It is a 12-game schedule with **3 games left**, so YES requires **winning all three**. A single loss means NO.
- **Remaining games:**
  1. **At No. 11 (CFP ranking) Kentucky**, Sat Nov 14, Kroger Field, Lexington, ESPN (National Football Post, Nov 11 2026).
  2. **Vanderbilt at home** (Ben Hill Griffin Stadium), Sat Nov 21 (Vanderbilt schedule; Wikipedia).
  3. **At Florida State**, Tallahassee. The ACC moved this game. Current listings show Fri Nov 27; older ones show Nov 28 (SI, "ACC changes date…"; FSU schedule listings).
- **Edge cases:**
  - If a game is canceled and not made up, Florida cannot reach 8 wins (only 11 games played). That makes the outcome NO in practice.
  - A forfeit or vacated win could create ambiguity. Kalshi decides, presumably based on official records.
  - One USA TODAY bowl-projection blurb says Florida "will also face Tennessee." That looks like an error. Every schedule source lists Vanderbilt on Nov 21, and Tennessee is Vanderbilt's Nov 28 opponent.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **Three-game parlay with one road underdog game.**
   - **Kentucky:** Florida is about a 4-point road underdog (betting preview, docsports, ~Nov 11; this is a single-game line, not this market). A 4-point underdog historically wins about 35–38% of the time. ESPN FPI gave Florida 69% at Kentucky in a preseason or early-season snapshot. That number predates Florida's slump and Kentucky's 7–2 start, so it is stale.
   - **Vanderbilt:** Vanderbilt is 4–5 (1–5 SEC). For a home SEC favorite over a weaker conference team, roughly 65–78% is a reasonable range. Earlier FPI gave Florida 77.7%.
   - **Florida State:** FSU is 4–5 with an interim coach, and Florida plays on the road. Roughly 50–62% is reasonable. Preseason FPI gave Florida 57.1%.
   - **Combined:** Treating the games as independent, the product is about 0.37 × 0.70 × 0.57 ≈ **15%**. Plausible bounds are about 9% to 25%. Results are probably somewhat positively correlated through team quality and QB play, which would widen the tails a little.
2. **Teams with a losing streak needing to win out.** Florida has lost 4 of its last 5 games and scored 15 points in its last two (8 vs Georgia, 7 vs Oklahoma). I could not find hard data on how often teams in this kind of slump win their last three, so I offer this only qualitatively. It argues against assuming Florida's early-season strength (four wins, including 52–28 over then-No. 4 Ole Miss) will return. Florida's most recent preseason projections were 6.7–7.2 wins (Hail Florida Hail, summer 2026). Its 5 wins plus about 1.4–1.8 expected over the last three would land near that range.
3. **Head-to-head history.**
   - **At Kentucky:** Florida's last road win at Kentucky was in 2019 (Hail Florida Hail, ~Nov 10 2026).
   - **Vanderbilt and Florida State:** I did not search the series histories. From general knowledge, Florida has dominated Vanderbilt historically. Florida beat FSU in Tallahassee in 2024. FSU won the 2023 meeting, and Florida lost to FSU in Gainesville in 2025. Please verify these.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
- **Path to YES (all three needed):**
  - Florida's offense recovers, through a QB change to Philo or better play from Jones, and wins at Kentucky. Kentucky's defense is middling (76th), and Kentucky may have a hangover from the 34–6 Tennessee loss.
  - Florida then handles a 4–5 Vanderbilt team at home. Vanderbilt is playing for bowl eligibility.
  - Florida then wins in Tallahassee against an FSU team in turmoil under an interim coach.
  - Florida has already shown it can do this kind of thing: it beat Ole Miss and won at Texas.
- **Paths to NO (any one is enough):**
  - **Most likely:** a loss at Kentucky. Kentucky is favored, ranked No. 11, and Florida has not won in Lexington since 2019.
  - Continued poor offense, with turnovers, weak rushing and unsettled QB play.
  - FSU rallies around the interim coach in a rivalry game.
  - A Vanderbilt upset. Vanderbilt is fighting for bowl eligibility (it needs 2 of its last 3).
  - Morale or effort fades if Florida drops to 5–5.

## 5. Signposts
- **Nov 14 at Kentucky (biggest).** A loss makes this almost certainly NO. A win roughly doubles to triples the chance of YES, to about 40% (≈0.70 × 0.57).
- **QB announcement or injury report** before Kentucky, including Vernell Brown III's status. A healthy receiver corps or a clearly better QB is a modest positive.
- **FSU's results** at Pitt (Nov 13) and vs NC State (Nov 21). Two FSU losses would leave FSU eliminated from bowl eligibility and possibly demoralized, which is good for Florida. FSU wins would mean the Florida game decides FSU's bowl eligibility, which is a negative for Florida.
- **Vanderbilt's form and QB health** heading into Nov 21.
- **Updated ESPN FPI or SP+ win probabilities** for each remaining game. The only FPI numbers I found are preseason or early-season and are stale.
- **Weather or scheduling changes** to the FSU game date.
