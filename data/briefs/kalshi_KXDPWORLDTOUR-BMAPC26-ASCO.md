# Research brief: Will Adam Scott win the 2026 BMW Australian PGA Championship?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Adam Scott must be the official winner of the 2026 BMW Australian PGA Championship. A playoff win counts. A tie for second, or losing a playoff, is NO.
- **Event:** The tournament runs Thursday 26 to Sunday 29 November 2026 at The Lakes Golf Club in Sydney. The DP World Tour and the PGA Tour of Australasia co-sanction it, and it is the first event of the DP World Tour's 2027 season (Citizen.co.za preview, Nov 2026; GolfMagic, Nov 2026).
- **Timing:** The market's close date (13 Dec 2026) is two weeks after the scheduled finish. That leaves room for a weather delay to a Monday finish without affecting the result.
- **Who decides:** The official tournament or DP World Tour result decides. Presumably the market settles on that leaderboard.
- **Withdrawal rule:** If Scott withdraws or doesn't start before teeing off, the Winner market resolves NO. A withdrawal after he tees off would also mean he can't win, so it's effectively NO too.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Shortened event:** If weather shortens the event, whoever is declared the official winner should count. The 2024 edition was cut to 54 holes, and Elvis Smylie won at 199 (−14).
  - **Inconsistent stated close date:** The stated close date is far later than the scheduled finish. That probably doesn't matter.
- **Status today (25 Nov):** Scott is confirmed and has a tee time. He goes out in the first group on Thursday at 6:55 am with Min Woo Lee and Alex Noren (Aussie Golfer / FOGOLF tee-time reports, Nov 2026).

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Winners of this event since 2013.** My reconstruction from past-champion lists is below. The year labels conflict between sources around 2019–2024, but the names are consistent.
- **Winners:** Scott in 2013 and 2019; Greg Chalmers in 2014; Nathan Holman in 2015; Harold Varner III in 2016; Cameron Smith in 2017, 2018 and 2022; Jediah Morgan in the 2021 edition, played in Jan 2022; Min Woo Lee in 2023; Elvis Smylie in 2024; David Puig in 2025 (PGA of Australia past champions; Where2Golf; TheGolfNewsNet).
- **Marquee Australians:** In 6 of the 12 editions, the winner was one of the two or three headline names (Scott, Smith or Lee).
- **Scott specifically:** He won 2 of those 12 editions. He has won 2 of his roughly 8–10 starts in the event; I could not confirm his full list of appearances.
- **Implication:** A top marquee name wins this event at a much higher rate than in a full-strength PGA Tour field. But that roughly 50% share is split among about 3–5 star names each year.

**B. Scott's recent win rate.**
- His last win was the Genesis Invitational in February 2020 (France24/AFP, 24 Nov 2026). That is 0 wins in about six and a half years and well over 100 starts.
- He has had several close calls: runner-up at the 2024 Scottish Open; a playoff loss at the 2024 BMW Championship, from memory and not re-verified; T2 at the 2026 BMW PGA at Wentworth, 2 shots behind JJ Spaun (Canberra Times/AAP, 20 Sep 2026).
- As a rough guide, 0 wins with 3 or more seconds in roughly 130 starts implies a per-start win rate in strong fields of perhaps 1–2%.

**C. Scott in weak co-sanctioned fields in Australia and the DP World Tour.**
- He finished 7th (−13) at the 2025 Australian PGA, 5 shots behind Puig at −18 (TheGolfNewsNet final results, 30 Nov 2025).
- He finished T3 at the 2024 DP World Tour Championship.
- An elite player in a home field like this one, where only a handful of players rank in the world top 50, plausibly wins about 5–12% of starts. Scott's two wins in this event fit the upper end of that range.

- **Wider context:** LIV Golf announced it is winding down (5 Nov 2026). This doesn't directly affect this event's field.
- **Next event:** The Australian Open follows on 3–6 Dec. Scott is confirmed for it, and there is no sign he plans to skip this week.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES:**
- **Strong form carries over:** He has been in contention twice this autumn, at Wentworth and in Dubai. He has won this event twice and plays it with home support. A good week with the putter, which he himself names as the missing piece, could be enough against a field where only about 3–5 players are clearly his equals or better: Smith, Lee, Conners, Noren, possibly Puig and Herbert.
- **Experience on a tough course:** A firm, windy Sydney sandbelt-style course in tough conditions suits his ball-striking and experience. Golf Channel made the same point about Dubai: "tough conditions play in 46-year-old Scott's favor."

**Paths to NO:**
- **Field strength:** In most scenarios someone else wins. These events often go to lower-ranked players or young DP World Tour players: Morgan, Holman, Smylie and Puig all won as outsiders.
- **Putting and closing:** His putting and ability to close out wins have failed for six years, including several second places.
- **Age and fatigue:** He is 46. He finished a long season in mid-November and has had to travel back from Dubai.
- **Withdrawal or weather:** A withdrawal is unlikely given the tee time and his stated commitment. A weather-shortened event adds randomness.

## 5. Signposts

- **Before Thursday:** A late withdrawal or injury report would make NO near certain. DeChambeau or McIlroy being added would lower Scott's chances slightly.
- **After round 1 (26 Nov) and round 2 (27 Nov):**
  - Players within about 3 shots of the lead after 36 holes win far more often. If Scott leads or is co-leader after 54 holes, his chance would rise to roughly 30–45%, though his recent record of closing out wins is poor.
  - If he is 6 or more shots back at halfway, his chance falls to around 1–2%.
- **Weather in Sydney:** Strong wind would favour experienced ball-strikers. A storm-shortened event would add variance.
- **Rivals:** Watch whether Cam Smith, Min Woo Lee and Conners start well.
