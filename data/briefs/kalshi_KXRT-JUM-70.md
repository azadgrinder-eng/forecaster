# Research brief: will *Jumanji: Open World* have a Tomatometer above 70 on Dec 28, 2026?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What resolves YES:** the film's Tomatometer (critics) score on Rotten Tomatoes is **above 70**, meaning 71% or higher, at **10:00 AM ET on Monday, Dec 28, 2026**. A score of exactly 70% resolves NO. The "Fresh" and "Certified Fresh" labels don't matter.
- **The date lines up with the release.** The rules say the market settles "the Monday after wide release." The US release is **Friday, Dec 25, 2026**, after a move from Dec 11 (Wikipedia; Geek Culture; RT movie page, "In Theaters Dec 25"). The Monday after that is Dec 28, which matches the close date.
- **What is being measured:** a snapshot of the live score on Dec 28, not the final settled score. The Popcornmeter (audience score) is irrelevant.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Another date change.** It has already moved once, from Dec 11 to Dec 25. A later or earlier release could create a mismatch between "the Monday after wide release" and the fixed Dec 28 close. A move after mid-November looks unlikely.
  - **International release dates differ.** One source lists the UK on Dec 23; others give Dec 25 for both the UK and US. Australia and New Zealand open Dec 26 (Flicks.com.au; Hoyts NZ). UK reviews could land a day or two before the US embargo.
  - **A score sitting right at 70–71%.** The displayed number is rounded, and during release week it can move a point or two in a day as reviews arrive.
- **Who decides:** Kalshi, reading the RT page.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Same franchise, same director and writers.** Jake Kasdan directs again and co-wrote with Jeff Pinkner and Scott Rosenberg, the same team as the last film. *Welcome to the Jungle* also had additional credited writers. (TV Insider; Wikipedia)
- *Jumanji: Welcome to the Jungle* (2017):
  - About 79–80% before release (CinemaBlend; Whatculture, Dec 2017).
  - About 76% final, 87% audience score (itsastampede, Dec 28, 2019).
  - It was **above 70 at the equivalent snapshot.**
- *Jumanji: The Next Level* (2019), the key comparison:
  - 77% at 31 reviews, then 70% at 90 reviews.
  - **67% at about 148 reviews on opening weekend** (Comicbook.com, Dec 13, 2019; Toonado; Bounding Into Comics).
  - Around 66% in Dec 2019 box-office reporting.
  - Roughly 71% final (itsastampede, Dec 28, 2019).
  - It would have **failed "above 70" at a Monday-after-opening snapshot.** It only crept past 70 later.
- Older films: *Jumanji* (1995) is about 52% and *Zathura* (2005) about 76%. Both are from memory, not verified this session.
- **Franchise tally:** 1 of 2 Kasdan Jumanji films cleared 70 at the equivalent snapshot. The trend runs downward: about 76, then about 67 on opening weekend.

**B. Dwayne Johnson studio tentpoles since 2018.** These are mostly from memory; only Red One was checked here (33%, Business Insider).
- Approximate scores: Rampage about 52, Skyscraper about 48, Hobbs & Shaw about 67, Jungle Cruise about 62, Black Adam about 39, DC League of Super-Pets about 71, Red One about 31–33, Moana 2 about 61. *The Next Level* fits in this group too.
- **Roughly 1–2 of about 9 scored ≥71.** Johnson's recent tentpoles skew rotten to middling. The exception is *The Smashing Machine* (2025), a prestige drama that doesn't fit this class.

**C. Third films and late sequels in live-action family or comedy franchises, especially holiday releases.** Approximate, from memory:
- Night at the Museum: Secret of the Tomb about 50%
- Alvin and the Chipmunks: Chipwrecked about 12%
- Little Fockers about 10%
- Men in Black 3 about 68%
- Bad Boys for Life about 77%
- Bad Boys: Ride or Die about 66%
- Despicable Me 3 about 59%

Third entries usually score at or below the second; maybe **25–35%** match or beat a part 2 that was in the 70s. With part 2 at 67–71, holding above 70 needs the run of declining scores to stop.

**Synthesis:** all three reference classes point to roughly 25–40% for ≥71 at a Monday snapshot. That would rise if early reviews show a real creative step up.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (≥71% on Dec 28):**
- **A fresh premise and a finale.** The "real-world" setup and the nostalgic ties to the 1995 film give critics something new. Critics mildly liked *Welcome to the Jungle* for the same reason.
- **The same filmmakers who delivered 76% and 71%.** Kasdan's Jumanji films stay consistently in the high 60s to mid 70s, so a result in the 71–76 range is plausible.
- **The holiday-week critic pool.** Over Christmas, more reviews may come from regional, family and genre outlets, which tend to be kinder to crowd-pleasers. This is speculative.
- **Early-score optimism.** Release-week scores often start high, as with *The Next Level* at 77% on 31 reviews. If reviews land late, around Dec 23–24, the count by Dec 28 may be small and still in that early, inflated phase.

**Paths to NO (≤70%):**
- **Sequel fatigue.** The seven-year gap and a third run of the same body-swap gags could make "more of the same" the critical consensus. *The Next Level* already dropped to 67% on opening weekend.
- **Weak test-screening signal.** If the test screening was representative, reviews come in rotten-leaning.
- **Johnson's recent run.** His recent tentpoles mostly score in the 30s to 60s.
- **Scores tend to drop through opening week.** The Monday snapshot falls when the score is often near its lowest, after mainstream reviews pile in. That is exactly what happened to *The Next Level*.

**What it hinges on:** whether critics see this as a creative refresh like the 2017 film, or a tired retread like the 2019 film, which started fresh then went mixed.

## 5. Signposts
- **Embargo timing:**
  - Reviews lifting a week or more early (by about Dec 16–18) would show studio confidence: **up**.
  - An embargo on the eve of release or release day (Dec 24–25) signals worry: **down**.
- **Social-media first reactions after premiere or press screenings.** Expected mid-December. These are almost always positive, so only lukewarm or absent reactions carry much information: **down if muted**.
- **Early Tomatometer:**
  - At 30–60 reviews, a score in the low 80s or higher suggests about 72–78% by Monday.
  - A score of 75 or below at 40 or more reviews looks a lot like *The Next Level* (77, then 70, then 67): **down sharply**.
- **The trades.** Variety, THR, IndieWire and The Wrap reviews set the tone, and top-critic negatives tend to spread to other critics.
- **Metacritic.** About 60 or higher is consistent with a Tomatometer of 71 or more. About 55 or lower points to NO. *Welcome to the Jungle* was about 58 on Metacritic and *The Next Level* about 58 (from memory).
- **Release-date changes or a runtime/rating announcement:** minor signals.

## 6. Uncertainties
- **No reviews exist yet,** and the embargo date hasn't been announced.
- **The test-screening report** comes from one anonymous insider and is unverified.
- **I couldn't check the exact live or historical RT scores** because the RT and Wikipedia pages were blocked. The franchise figures (*Welcome to the Jungle* about 76, *The Next Level* 67 on opening weekend and about 71 final) come from secondary articles. The other franchise and comparison scores in section 2 are from memory and could be off by several points.
- **I don't know how many reviews will be counted by Dec 28.** Over the Christmas holiday the count may be lower than usual, which makes the score swing more.
- **RT changes:** possible changes to RT's critic pool or methods since 2019 could make comparisons with the earlier films less reliable.
- **The UK date** (Dec 23 vs Dec 25) is still unclear and could affect when the first reviews appear.
