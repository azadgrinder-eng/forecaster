## Research brief: Harvard at Penn, Nov 14, 2026. Market resolves YES if Penn wins.

### 1. Resolution mechanics
- **YES:** Penn wins the official final result of Harvard at Penn, scheduled for Saturday, Nov 14, 2026 at 1:00 p.m. ET at Franklin Field in Philadelphia. It is Penn's Homecoming and its last home game, streamed on ESPN+ and possibly NBC Sports Philadelphia (ESPN game page; Harvard Athletics, Nov 7, 2026). College football can't end in a tie, so overtime decides it and a Penn overtime win counts as YES.
- **NO:** Harvard wins.
- **Postponement:** If the game is delayed but starts within 48 hours of the original kickoff, it still resolves on the final result. If it is cancelled or doesn't start within 48 hours, Kalshi settles at a "fair price," a value Kalshi chooses rather than 0 or 1. Kalshi decides the outcome. The market closes Nov 16, 18:00 UTC.
- **Edge cases:** A weather delay is very unlikely to push the game past 48 hours. A forfeit or a result changed after the fact is not addressed in the rules. Both are negligible risks.

### 2. Reference classes and base rates
- **Recent series:** Harvard leads 54-39-2 all-time and has won five straight (2021–2025) (AP via Penn Athletics, Nov 15, 2025). The last two meetings were very close: Harvard won 31-28 at Franklin Field in 2024 on a late field goal (CBS/AP, 2024) and 45-43 in 2025 on a 53-yard field goal as time expired (Harvard Athletics, Nov 15, 2025). Penn's last win was 24-20 at Cambridge in 2019, its third straight win there (ESPN recap). So Penn has won 0 of the last 5 meetings, but the last two were decided by 3 and 2 points. Harvard went 9-0 into the 2025 game and Penn still nearly won.
- **Generic underdog base rate:** Across college football, a home underdog against a clearly better team wins roughly 15–30% of the time. That is about 25–30% for a 7-point underdog, about 20% at 10 points and about 13–15% at 14 points. This is a general rule of thumb from point-spread data, not a figure I looked up for this game. Harvard is about 7-1 and ranked in the FCS Top 25. Penn is 4-4 with a −16 point differential, so this probably fits the 7–14 point underdog range.
- **Harvard bouncing back:** Harvard is 10-0 in its last 10 games after a loss (Harvard Athletics recap via Columbia/Harvard reporting, Nov 7, 2026). Harvard has been the dominant Ivy program lately, sharing or winning several recent titles, including at least a share in 2025, its third straight (Harvard Athletics, Nov 15, 2025).

### 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES (Penn wins):**
- Harvard turns the ball over a lot again, as in the 5-interception loss at Dartmouth.
- Penn's passing game with Siqueiros-Lasky keeps it close, as it did in the 2024 and 2025 one-score games.
- Home and Homecoming energy helps.
- Harvard looks ahead to the Yale game at Fenway.
- Bad weather lowers scoring and adds randomness.
- Penn has already beaten Yale and played close games all year. Five of its eight games were decided by 4 points or fewer.

**Paths to NO (Harvard wins):**
- Harvard is the better team on both lines. Penn's running game is very weak (38 and 58 yards in its last two games), so it struggles to control the clock against a strong Harvard defense. Harvard's Xaden Benson set a program record with 9 interceptions (Harvard Athletics, Oct 30).
- Harvard has to win to stay level with or ahead of Princeton in the title race, so it has high motivation.
- Harvard's history after losses and its five-game winning streak in the series point the same way.

### 5. Signposts
- **Injuries and depth charts (Nov 9–13):** a Charlie Smith or DJ Gordon injury would favor Penn. Who starts at QB for Penn (Siqueiros-Lasky or Teodecki) matters less.
- **Weather forecast for Philadelphia on Nov 14:** heavy wind or rain probably helps the underdog a little.
- **Updated FCS rankings and computer ratings** (Massey, Sagarin) published this week.
- **Ivy League rules** on the FCS-playoff automatic bid and tiebreakers. If Harvard needs this win to keep its playoff path, motivation is maximal. Penn has no title stake but has the Homecoming and senior-class angle.
