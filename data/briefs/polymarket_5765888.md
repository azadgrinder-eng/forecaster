## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **YES condition:** Vance must have the highest hourly "Odds of Happening" value in the "2028 Presidential Winner by Candidate" listing for the one-hour Time Window (ET) that ends at 12:00 PM ET on Nov 30, 2026. Values are compared to 0.1%.
- **NO_PRINT:** If the site shows NO_PRINT for a candidate in that window, the candidate's last published value is used instead. If nothing at all is published for that window, the market can stay open until Dec 7, 11:59 PM ET, and after that the last earlier value is used.
- **Ties:** A tie goes to whichever tied candidate was higher in the most recent earlier window where they differed. If that doesn't settle it, alphabetical order decides; "JD Vance" or "Vance" would lose to "Jon Ossoff" or "Ossoff" depending on how names are listed. An exact tie at 0.1% precision is unlikely.
- **"Other":** If the top-ranked candidate is not a listed outcome of this market, it resolves "Other". This doesn't change the Vance question: YES needs Vance himself on top.
- **Early decision:** If the underlying market is officially decided early, that outcome wins. This won't happen before 2028.
- **Errors:** A clearly wrong print can delay resolution by up to 48 hours to allow a correction.
- **Ambiguities:**
  - The site's method is unknown. It may be a single venue or a blend; the sources below differ by several points between venues.
  - It is a single hourly snapshot, so a thin-liquidity spike in that hour could decide the result.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **Persistence of the leader over about two weeks in long-dated, multi-candidate presidential winner markets.** The front-runner in these markets is usually sticky over a 15-day window unless there is a shock. Vance led the 2028 winner markets for most of the period from 2025 through October 2026:
   - Kalshi at about 19.5% versus Rubio 15.5% in early September (Stokastic, Sept 5, 2026).
   - Kalshi at about 20% versus Newsom 14% in late October (search summary of a Kalshi snapshot from about Oct 26, 2026).
   - The lead then flipped within days of a shock (the Nov 3 midterms).

   My rough estimate is that a leader with a lead of 4 or more points keeps the top spot over a 2-week window without a new shock about 85–90% of the time. That figure is my judgment, not a counted statistic. **This reference class now favours the current leader, Ossoff, not Vance.**
2. **Fading of post-event "momentum" candidates.** Candidates who jump on one event, such as a big election win or a viral moment, often give back part of the gain over weeks to months. Examples are Newsom's 2025 surges and earlier boomlets around DeSantis in 2022–23. Giving back enough to lose the lead within under four weeks of the event is less common. I'd put it at roughly 20–30% of cases when the margin is moderate (about 4–8 points); this is also a judgment.
3. **Party structure.** Markets price Democrats at about 65% to win in 2028 (Kalshi, cited in a search summary, Nov 2026). The Republican share (about 35%) goes mostly to one consolidated front-runner (Vance at 32–35% for the nomination), while the Democratic share is split among many names. That structure tends to favour the leading Republican for the top single-name spot. Ossoff's consolidation (about 26–27% for the nomination) has offset it for now.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES (Vance retakes first place by Nov 30):**
- **Ossoff fades.** Buzz cools after the midterms, he repeats "zero interest" or says something firmer, or the Democratic nomination money spreads back to Newsom, Ocasio-Cortez and Buttigieg. Ossoff would need to drop about 4–8 points or Vance to gain that much, depending on the venue.
- **Vance gets a boost.** Trump publicly endorses or reaffirms him, Vance signals he will run, or the Iran war de-escalates in a way that vindicates him.
- **Method quirk.** The site's blend may weight a venue where the gap is narrower; Covers, for example, shows Ossoff 17 versus Vance 11, and Polymarket 17 versus 13. A thin-liquidity hourly print could also do it.

**Paths to NO:**
- **Status quo.** Ossoff stays ahead, which is the default with about two weeks left.
- **Vance slips further.** More reporting that he may skip 2028, a further split with Trump, or Rubio or DeSantis gaining.
- **Another Democrat overtakes everyone.** For example, a Newsom announcement surge. Any non-Vance leader resolves NO for Vance.
