# Research Brief: Will the Israel–Iran ceasefire hold through November 15, 2026 (11:59 PM IRST)?

*Prepared 2026-11-08. The fetch tool was blocked for most news sites, so this brief relies on search-result summaries rather than full articles. Treat exact dates with some caution.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **YES** if neither Israel nor Iran carries out a qualifying strike that hits the other's land between market creation and Nov 15, 2026, 23:59 IRST (20:29 UTC Nov 15). The close time listed is 04:59 UTC Nov 16.
- **What counts:** air strikes (bombs, air-to-surface missiles, air-launched drones) or surface-to-surface strikes (ballistic or cruise missiles, one-way attack drones). The strike must be launched by Israel or Iran and must hit the other's land or internal waters. Strikes at sea or in airspace don't count.
- **What doesn't count:** intercepted munitions and debris from interceptions, whatever the damage. Also excluded are artillery and rocket artillery, short-range loitering munitions, FPV drones, ATGMs, cyber attacks, ground raids, and threats.
- **Edge cases that matter:**
  - **US-only strikes on Iran do not count.** That includes the US strikes near Hormuz and Qeshm on Nov 2 and Nov 6–7. Iranian attacks on US bases, Gulf states or shipping also don't count. Neither do strikes by proxies (Hezbollah, Houthis, Iraqi militias).
  - **A joint US-Israeli operation would count** if Israeli aircraft or munitions hit Iran. Attribution could be disputed at first if Washington leads and Israel's role is unclear. That would trigger the 3-day "consensus" window, which can push resolution past the close date.
  - **An Iranian barrage that Israel fully intercepts does not count.** On June 7, 2026 the IDF said it had intercepted all incoming Iranian missiles. That episode only qualified because Israel struck Iran in return.
  - **Unknown market creation date.** Only actions after creation count. Any exchange before creation is irrelevant.
  - **No formal ceasefire needed.** Resolution depends only on whether strikes happen. Netanyahu never formally acknowledged a ceasefire after June 2026.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Direct Israel–Iran exchanges since the April 8, 2026 ceasefire.** About 214 days to Nov 8. I could confirm one qualifying episode: Iran's missile barrage on June 7 after an Israeli strike in Beirut, and Israel's retaliatory strikes on central and western Iran on June 7–8 (Axios, Jun 8 2026; Washington Post, Jun 7 2026).
   - When US–Iran fighting resumed on July 8 (Dawn live blog; Wikipedia "2026 Iran war ceasefire"), I found no report of a direct Israel–Iran exchange. Searches for Iranian missile fire at Israel in July–September found nothing either.
   - One AJC explainer says "the US and Israel resumed military operations" after a June memorandum of understanding collapsed. This is unverified and could mean there was a second episode.
   - With 1–2 episodes in about 7 months, a simple Poisson model gives roughly 4–8% for an 8-day window.
2. **The Twelve-Day War ceasefire (June 24, 2025 to Feb 28, 2026).** About 249 days with no direct Israel–Iran strikes. It ended in one large planned joint US-Israeli attack, not in gradual erosion. That works out to about 3% per 8-day window. Breakdowns in this conflict have tended to come as deliberate, large operations timed to political windows. Feb 28 came during nuclear talks. ABC reports the US and Israel have twice attacked Iran during negotiations.
3. **US limited strikes on Iran in the current phase (July–Nov 2026).** Several US "limited" or "defensive" strikes, including Nov 2 and Nov 6–7. As far as I found, none led Iran to strike Israel directly, and none involved Israel. Through this phase, US–Iran fighting has not spread to Israel–Iran.

**Adjustment:** these base rates understate the current risk. The US is openly weighing a major round of strikes right after the midterms, which is right now. Israel is pushing hard for it, and Iran has promised retaliation against Israeli targets "without geographic limits."

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to NO (a qualifying strike happens):**
1. **Major joint US-Israeli round of strikes.** This is the largest single risk. It depends on Trump choosing strikes over the deal or continued blockade. Ratcliffe, Bessent and Rubio reportedly favour keeping the blockade. Israel would very likely take part, as on Feb 28. Iran's retaliation would almost certainly include missiles at Israel, and some would get through, as in past rounds.
2. **US-only escalation that Iran answers against Israel.** So far Iran has hit back at the US, Gulf states and shipping, not Israel. But Khamenei's expanded target list makes Israel a possible target. To qualify, a missile must actually hit Israeli land.
3. **Israel acting alone.** Netanyahu is a caretaker under domestic pressure and is pushing for strikes. But Trump warned him in June that he would "isolate himself" if he kept striking Iran, which limits this path.
4. **Proxy or Lebanon spiral.** The June 7 template: an Israeli strike in Lebanon prompts an Iranian "warning" barrage, then Israel retaliates.

**Paths to YES:**
- The Qatari track holds or makes progress.
- Trump keeps the blockade and occasional "minor" US strikes, which don't count.
- Gulf allies and Qatar keep restraining Trump.
- Iran keeps retaliating at sea and in the Gulf rather than at Israel.
- The window is short: about 7.5 days from today.

## 5. Signposts

**Toward NO (large moves):**
- Reports of a US decision or execute order, CENTCOM surge, or carrier and bomber moves.
- IDF Home Front Command raising alert levels, school closures, or airspace closures in Israel or Iran.
- Netanyahu or the Israeli cabinet authorizing action.
- Iran formally rejecting the Qatari draft.
- A mass-casualty incident in Hormuz involving US forces.
- A major Israeli strike on Hezbollah in Beirut.

**Toward YES:**
- Iran accepting or counter-proposing the Doha draft, or the US easing the blockade.
- Trump publicly giving talks more time, or the "Nov 9" timing passing quietly.
- Continued reporting that US strikes are "limited and defensive."

## 6. Uncertainties

- **Market creation date.** If it predates an exchange I couldn't verify (in July or later), the market may already be resolvable. I found no direct Israel–Iran strike after June 8, but I couldn't read full articles to confirm. The AJC line about the US and Israel "resuming operations" is unresolved.
- **The Nov 6–7 Qeshm strikes.** Trump described them as US strikes. I found nothing suggesting Israeli involvement, but attribution wasn't fully confirmed.
- **Strike timing.** The "around November 9" report comes from one liveblog summary.
- **Israeli politics.** The state of government formation and how far a caretaker Netanyahu would act without US backing are both unclear.
- **Iran's targeting choice.** Whether Iran would retaliate against Israel for US-only strikes is untested since July.
