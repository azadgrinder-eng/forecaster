# Research brief: Will the 2-year Treasury par yield print at 4.92% or higher on any business day from Nov 1–30, 2026?

*Prepared Nov 16, 2026. Note on sourcing: every attempt to open a full page, including Treasury.gov, failed with a proxy error. All figures below come from search-result summaries. Where sources disagree, I say so.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Treasury's official **Daily Treasury Par Yield Curve Rate** for the 2-year tenor must be **4.92% or higher** on **at least one business day** between Nov 1 and Nov 30, 2026. "Above 4.91%" means the same thing, because Treasury publishes to two decimals.
- **What does not count:** Intraday prices, market closing quotes, and Bloomberg/CNBC "US2Y" generic yields. These can differ from the Treasury figure by about 1–3bp. For example, CNBC showed 4.926% intraday on Sep 28 and the Treasury figure that day was 4.92%. A headline saying a yield "hit 4.93% intraday" does not settle the question.
- **Early resolution:** The market can resolve YES early once a qualifying print appears (Rule 7.2). Otherwise it expires at 10:00 AM ET on Dec 1. Revisions published after expiration are ignored.
- **Days left:** Nov 16 (today), 17, 18, 19, 20, 23, 24, 25, 27 and 30. That is about **10 prints**. Nov 26 (Thanksgiving) is closed. Nov 27 is normally an early-close bond day, but Treasury still publishes a curve.
- **No YES print so far:** The highest November print found is **4.88% on Nov 13**. Nov 12 was 4.86% (Fed H.15), Nov 10 was 4.73%, Nov 6 was 4.51%, and Nov 4 was about 4.58%.
- **Edge cases:** Rounding is the main one. A true yield of about 4.915% or more prints as 4.92%. If Treasury fails to publish (shutdown, outage), Rule 7.1 applies.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Starting distance versus recent volatility (random-walk barrier estimate)**
- **Starting point:** 4.88% (Nov 13 close; Nov 16 print not yet known). The threshold is about **+4bp away**, or about 3.5bp counting rounding.
- **Recent daily moves:** +17bp on Nov 10, the biggest one-day rise since April 2025 (CNN, Nov 10, 2026). +37bp over the week ending Nov 13 (Wolf Street, Nov 14, 2026). Earlier, a −37bp drift from about 4.88 at the end of September to 4.51 on Nov 6.
- **My assumption:** a typical daily standard deviation of about 5–7bp, which implies roughly 16–22bp over 10 days.
- **Result:** A driftless random walk with daily observations crosses +4bp with roughly **70–80%** probability. The reflection-principle formula gives about 80%, and adjusting for once-a-day observation lowers it a few points.
- **Caveats:** This ignores mean reversion after a 37bp weekly spike. It also ignores that the December hike is already priced.

**B. Path in 2026 near this level**
- **2026 high:** **4.92% on Sep 28**, the year's closing high (Portfolio Terminal; 24/7 Wall St., Nov 11, 2026). Treasury's own table also shows 4.92 that day.
- **Other approaches to the threshold:**
  - Sep 29–30: 4.89–4.91 and 4.88.
  - Early October: 4.78–4.84.
  - Around Oct 14: about 4.89 (cited as "highest since Oct 14" when the yield hit 4.892% in the week of Nov 13).
  - Oct 21: 4.81.
  - Late October: 4.67–4.70.
- **Takeaway:** In this regime the 2-year has come within 0–4bp of 4.92 about three times (late September, mid-October, mid-November). Only once did it reach 4.92 on a Treasury print, and it never went above. The level has acted as a ceiling, though that is a weak statistical signal.

**C. What it takes to move the 2-year after a hike is priced**
- **Current policy setting:** Fed funds is 3.75–4.00%. The Fed hiked in September and held in October on a 10–2 vote, with Cook and Hammack dissenting in favor of a hike. The 1-month bill is about 4.03%.
- **What is priced:** A December 25bp hike is about 90–94% priced (CME FedWatch per Wichita Liberty, Nov 13; FXStreet, Nov 16), with about a 10% chance of 50bp. Axios (Nov 12, 2026) reports about two more 25bp hikes priced by June 2027.
- **Implication:** With the 2-year at 4.88%, the market already expects funds near 4.5–4.75% by mid-2027. Reaching 4.92 needs a little more hawkish repricing, such as higher odds of a 50bp move or a third 2027 hike. It does not need a regime change.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (one print at 4.92% or higher)**
- **Strong data:** Retail sales well above the flat consensus pushes the 2-year up 4bp or more in one day.
- **Hawkish minutes or speakers:** The minutes show many participants leaning toward a hike, or Fed officials (Williams, Hammack, Musalem, Warsh) endorse 50bp or a sustained series of hikes. That would raise December 50bp odds above about 10% and add 2027 hikes.
- **Hot PCE on Nov 25:** Core PCE above expectations, consistent with the hot CPI.
- **Spillover from longer yields:** Continued selling at the long end (weak auctions, deficit or oil/Iran worries). The 10-year above 5% has been pulling the curve up.
- **Volatility alone:** Even with no clear news, recent 5–17bp daily moves make a 4bp gap small.

**Paths to NO**
- **Mean reversion:** After a 37bp weekly jump, positioning is stretched and the December hike is fully priced, so yields consolidate or fall back.
- **Soft data:** Weak retail sales (consensus is already 0.0%), soft PCE, or rising claims.
- **Calming Fed talk:** Warsh or others ease 50bp talk. Warsh has advocated a "quieter" Fed, which could mean little guidance.
- **Risk-off rally:** An equity sell-off (AI-related cracks were noted in October) or a geopolitical flight to safety.
- **Resistance at the year high:** 4.92 has capped the yield at least twice before (Sep 28 and around Oct 14).

## 5. Signposts

- **Nov 16 Treasury print:** 4.92% or higher resolves YES immediately. At 4.90–4.91 the odds rise sharply. Below 4.83 they fall meaningfully.
- **Nov 17 retail sales:** Upside raises the odds, downside lowers them.
- **Nov 18 FOMC minutes:** Count how many participants favored a hike in October. A large hawkish majority raises the odds.
- **FedWatch for December:** The odds of a 50bp hike, and the total hikes priced through mid-2027. Rising 50bp odds raise the chance of YES.
- **2-year note auction (late November):** A weak result, meaning a tail or low bid-to-cover, pushes yields up.
- **Nov 25 PCE:** Core PCE at 0.3% m/m or more raises the odds.
- **Oil/Iran and tariff headlines, and the 10-year's level:** The 10-year holding above about 5.1% supports the front end.

- **Exact pricing beyond December:** The FedWatch figures for December vary by source (87–94%). I found no exact pricing for the 2027 path.
