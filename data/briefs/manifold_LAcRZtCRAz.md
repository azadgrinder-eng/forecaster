# Research brief: Will a Millennium Prize Problem be solved in November 2026?

*Prepared 2026-11-08. The page fetcher was blocked by the network proxy, so every fact below comes from search-result summaries and not from reading the full articles. Treat exact wording and dates as approximate unless they come from more than one source.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **No resolution criteria were given.** The plain reading is that one of the six unsolved problems is "solved" between 2026-11-01 and 2026-11-30 23:59 UTC. The six are Riemann, P vs NP, Navier–Stokes, Yang–Mills, Hodge and Birch–Swinnerton-Dyer. Poincaré was already settled by Perelman in 2002–03 (Wikipedia: Millennium Prize Problems).
- **What "solved" could mean.** This ambiguity drives the answer more than anything else:
  - **(a) Official recognition by the Clay Mathematics Institute (CMI).** CMI's rules require publication in a qualifying outlet, a wait of at least two years, and general acceptance by mathematicians before it considers a prize (Wikipedia: Millennium Prize Problems; The Decoder on CMI's statement, Sept 2026). Under this reading, YES in November 2026 is essentially impossible. The most advanced candidate, Navier–Stokes, was only published in September 2026.
  - **(b) A credible new claimed solution announced in November**, for example by a major AI lab with a Lean formalization and serious expert attention, as OpenAI's Navier–Stokes claim had in September. This is the reading most likely to produce YES.
  - **(c) Navier–Stokes counted as "solved" in November** because of some new event: a verification, a CMI statement, or journal acceptance. Under the plain reading, Navier–Stokes was "solved", if at all, in September 2026, not November. Forecasters should consider whether the resolver would count a November verification or CMI acknowledgement. I could not establish how they would.
- **Edge cases:**
  - Partial or special-case results, like the October 6 OpenAI manuscripts on Hodge for CM abelian varieties and on partial Riemann and BSD results, should not count.
  - Amateur "proofs" on figshare, Medium or ResearchGate appear constantly and should not count.
  - A disproof counts as a solution. OpenAI's Navier–Stokes result is a blow-up construction, so it disproves global smoothness.
  - It is unclear whether a solution to a forced or variant version counts. CMI's own statement includes forced alternatives (C/D).

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **CMI-recognized solutions per month since 2000.** One solution (Poincaré) in about 26.8 years, or about 320 months. That is about 0.3% per month. Under reading (a), the two-year rule pushes the November 2026 probability to about 0.
2. **Credible, widely covered claimed solutions per month.**
   - Before 2026, Perelman's arXiv preprints (Nov 2002 – Jul 2003) are the only clear case, and they were the right one. Other high-profile claims were fragile: Deolalikar's P≠NP (Aug 2010) and Atiyah's Riemann claim (Sept 2018) both failed quickly. That gives about 3–4 notable claims in about 26 years, roughly 1% per month.
   - The 2026 regime is very different. OpenAI's Navier–Stokes claim came on Sept 8, 2026. OpenAI then said it had made "substantial progress" on a second, unnamed problem and was "preparing to announce" (Andrew Curran on X, citing NYT, ~mid-Sept 2026). That makes 1 credible claim in roughly the last 2–3 months of the AI era, with a second one pending.
3. **AI-lab claims that are "coming soon".** OpenAI's second-problem statement (~mid-Sept) has not turned into an announcement after about 7–8 weeks. A report says OpenAI may delay a Hodge result because of backlash from mathematicians (Superpower Daily, date unclear, ~Sept–Oct 2026). On Oct 7, OpenAI pulled three of its 722 manuscripts over a sign error, one of which touched a Hodge-type result (AI Weekly, Oct 2026). Pending claims are therefore slipping, not arriving on schedule.

  - Several expert groups say the 166-page proof does not follow the Córdoba–Martínez-Zoroa program that OpenAI cited (Scientific American; The Decoder, ~Oct 18, 2026).

- **~Sept 2026:**
  - The Information (single anonymous source) reported OpenAI staff expect to crack Hodge "relatively soon" and might consult mathematicians first (Gizmodo).
  - OpenAI told the NYT it had made "substantial progress" on a second problem and was preparing to announce (Decrypt; Andrew Curran on X).
  - Rumors on X say OpenAI or Anthropic is close on BSD. Tech Insider traces these to one user's prediction.
- **Sept 11, 2026:** CMI said "the Navier–Stokes problem has apparently been settled" but that its review is "deliberately unhurried" (The Decoder; NYU Shanghai RITS).
- **Sept 8, 2026:** OpenAI announced an internal model had proved finite-time blow-up for 3D Navier–Stokes with smooth forcing and finite energy (CMI alternatives C/D). It cited about 10,000 agents, 88 hours, a 166-page proof and a Lean formalization, and said it will not claim the prize (Quanta; CNN; Washington Post).
  - Tristan Buckmaster has raised a priority dispute.
  - Luis Silvestre: "The Clay problem is settled, but the main problem for the Navier-Stokes equations is not."

**Scheduled before Nov 30:** No CMI decision is scheduled, and the two-year rule rules one out. The only plausible trigger is a possible next-GPT release "soon" (unconfirmed timing). I found no scheduled conference or announcement tied to any specific problem.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES**
- **(i) OpenAI announces a full Hodge proof, or another problem, in November.** This depends on whether the "preparing to announce" result really exists. It also depends on OpenAI's willingness to publish amid backlash and the Oct 7 retractions; the delay reports cut against this. A model launch is a natural moment for an announcement.
- **(ii) Another lab makes a credible claim** (Anthropic, Google DeepMind/Gómez-Serrano on unforced Navier–Stokes or Euler-type blow-up). There is no concrete evidence of this, only rumors.
- **(iii) The resolver counts a November event about Navier–Stokes** (expert verification, journal acceptance, a CMI update) as "solved in November". This depends on resolver interpretation. Expert opinion is currently moving toward caveats, not confirmation.

**Paths to NO**
- No new full-problem claim appears in the 22 remaining days. This is the default, given slipping timelines.
- A claim appears but the resolver doesn't count it because it is unverified, partial, or a variant.
- Under a strict CMI-recognition reading, NO is almost certain.

## 5. Signposts

- **Strongly toward YES:**
  - An official OpenAI, DeepMind or Anthropic blog post or paper claiming a full proof of Hodge, BSD, Riemann, Yang–Mills or P vs NP, especially with Lean and a full manuscript.
  - Major outlets (Quanta, NYT, Nature) reporting it as a solution.
- **Moderately toward YES:**
  - A next-GPT launch date set within November.
  - Credible reports (The Information, NYT) that an announcement is days away.
- **Toward NO:**
  - Further reports that OpenAI is holding results back to consult mathematicians.
  - More retractions or errors in OpenAI manuscripts.
  - No announcement by about Nov 20.
- **Interpretation-dependent:** Any CMI statement on Navier–Stokes, or a decisive expert verification or refutation of the September proof. This matters only if the resolver might count Navier–Stokes events in November.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **Resolver intent** is the biggest uncertainty: official recognition, a credible claim, or announcement date.
- **Whether the unnamed second problem was ever announced.** I could not confirm whether OpenAI's "substantial progress" result was released, abandoned or delayed, or whether it is Hodge. The date of The Information report is unclear.
- **Altman's next-GPT timing.** The "release soon" claim and its link to new math results are secondhand.
- **The Nov 7 "Anthropic quantum proof" item.** Its content is unknown; it probably isn't a Millennium problem.
- **Full article text.** I could not read any article in full, so dates for several items (Superpower Daily, Gizmodo/The Information, the three-mathematician limitation proof) are approximate.
