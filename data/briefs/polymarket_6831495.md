# Research brief: Will the US go 14 straight days without striking Iran, with the period starting by Nov 22, 2026?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** a full, continuous 14-day period in which the US makes no "qualifying military action" against Iran. The first day of that period must fall between market creation and Nov 22, 2026, on Iran Standard Time (IRST, UTC+3:30).
- **When the period starts:**
  - If there has been a qualifying strike since market creation, the period starts the day after the most recent one.
  - If there has been none, it starts on the day the market was created.
  - A strike before market creation does not count.
  - **I could not find the market creation date.** If the market was created before Nov 2, the US strikes of Nov 2–8 restart the clock (see section 3).
- **Latest possible path to YES:** the last strike happens on Nov 21 (IRST). The period then runs Nov 22 through Dec 5. Because results only become final at 11:59 PM IRST on the third day after day 14, the latest possible YES is final around Dec 8.
- **Path to NO:** a strike on Nov 22 or later, with no strike-free 14-day period already started and completed before it, makes the next start date later than Nov 22. In practice the question is whether the US stops for 14 days starting no later than Nov 22.
- **If the last strike was Nov 8:** using the latest strike I found (Nov 8), a period starting Nov 9 IRST would finish on Nov 22 if the US launches no qualifying strike from Nov 9 to Nov 22.
- **What counts as a qualifying action:** a US-initiated air strike (bombs, air-to-surface missiles, air-launched drones) or surface-to-surface strike (cruise or ballistic missiles, one-way attack drones) that hits Iran's land territory or internal waters.
- **What does not count:**
  - strikes on ships or boats in Iran's territorial sea, or on drones and aircraft in its airspace
  - intercepted munitions and their debris
  - naval gunfire and artillery
  - cyber operations
  - threats
  - strikes by Israel
- **Edge cases that matter here:**
  1. **Islands count.** Qeshm, Hengam and Larak are land territory, and most recent US strikes hit these islands.
  2. **Strikes on IRGC boats in the Strait or on blockade-runners at sea** (for example, a cargo ship disabled in the Gulf of Oman) probably do not count.
  3. **Strikes the US never announces.** The Nov 2–3 strikes were not publicly announced. Iranian state media reports of "explosions" or "projectiles" may be ambiguous; some of the Nov 7 blasts were later linked to Iran's own military operations. The rules say the "totality of information" decides, not any single statement.
  4. **Naval gunfire.** If a reported "projectile" turns out to be naval gunfire, it would not count.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
- **A. US strike tempo since fighting resumed on July 8, 2026 (about 125 days).** Known clusters of US strikes on Iranian land:
  - something in late July (inferred: the Sept 1 exchange was called "the heaviest … in more than a month")
  - the Sept 1–2 wave of about 5 hours, hitting IRGC air defense, radar, mine-laying and communications sites (11 reported killed)
  - Nov 2–3, Nov 6–7 and Nov 8
  - PBS says early September was the last *known* US attack before November. That would mean a gap of roughly 8–9 weeks, containing several back-to-back 14-day strike-free periods.
  - WSWS, an advocacy outlet, claims "several weeks of smaller US attacks" that were never announced. If true, there were fewer clean 14-day gaps.
  - **Rough base rate:** after a strike cluster, a 14-day quiet stretch has usually followed within weeks. Clusters have lasted from 1 day (Sept) to at least 7 days (Nov).
- **B. Earlier truces in this war.**
  - April 7–8 ceasefire: held, with breaches such as the May 7 US "self-defense" strikes, until June 7 (Israel–Iran).
  - June memorandum of understanding (June 14 or 17, sources conflict): extended the truce until about July 8.
  - So roughly 3 months with mostly no US strikes, and Washington did strike even during a declared truce (May 7).
- **C. Limited "self-defense" strike rounds** (Nov 2025-style tit-for-tat after Iran fires at US drones). In November 2026, each US strike followed Iranian fire at US aircraft or drones within about 1–3 days. Three qualifying strike days in 7 days (Nov 2–8) is a high recent rate. If it continued, the chance of a 14-day gap starting soon would be low. The pattern depends on Iran's activity, though.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES**
- Trump's stated "status quo" posture holds, with pressure from the blockade instead of bombing. Gulf states and the domestic backlash after the midterms argue for restraint. Iran avoids firing at US drones or aircraft, or the US tolerates it.
- If nothing qualifying happens Nov 9–22, YES is confirmed around Nov 25.
- Even one more strike before about Nov 21 still leaves YES possible, if a quiet stretch follows.
- A deal or framework agreement (a return to the June MoU) would make YES very likely.

**Paths to NO**
1. **The tit-for-tat loop continues.** Iran is rebuilding air defenses and radar and shooting at US ISR drones over the Strait, and the US answers with "minor" strikes on Qeshm and the coast. This produced three strike days in a week.
2. **Escalation.** Talks collapse, Iran keeps refusing ("surrender" demands), Netanyahu lobbies, and the post-midterm strike plans are carried out.
3. **Retaliation for Iranian action** such as mine-laying in Hormuz (4 tankers hit mines) or attacks on Gulf bases, as on Sept 1.
4. **Strikes near the deadline.** Any qualifying strike on or after about Nov 22 that comes before a 14-day period has been completed means NO.

## 5. Signposts
- **Toward NO:**
  - Iran fires surface-to-air missiles at US drones or aircraft near Qeshm or Hormuz (the trigger for every November strike)
  - new mine-laying or tanker attacks
  - Iranian missile or drone strikes on Gulf bases or Jordan
  - Iranian state media reporting explosions on Hormozgan islands or the coast
  - Trump setting new deadlines
  - Israeli strikes that draw in the US
- **Toward YES:**
  - several more days with no reported US strikes; each quiet day after Nov 9 matters, and if Nov 9–15 is quiet the 14-day period is half done
  - talks resuming in Doha
  - any easing of the blockade
  - Trump repeating "status quo / unlikely"
  - Iran tacitly stopping fire at US drones
- **Ambiguity risk:** strikes the US never acknowledges, and Iranian reports that blur US strikes with Iran's own explosions or naval gunfire.
