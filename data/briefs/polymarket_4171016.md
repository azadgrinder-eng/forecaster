# Research brief: Will DFW (KDFW) record any snow by November 30, 2026?

*Prepared November 14, 2026. I could not open the NWS, CPC or newspaper pages directly in this session, so the facts below come from search-result snippets. Treat specific numbers as needing a check against the primary pages where noted.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** the NWS Daily Climate Report for KDFW (product CLIDFW, listed as "DALLAS/FORT WORTH") shows snowfall of any amount, including "T" (trace), for any climate day from market creation through November 30, 2026. A climate day runs midnight to midnight Central time.
- **Which report counts:** only the first final daily report for each day. Intermediate same-day reports don't count, and later corrections are ignored. If a day's snowfall shows "M" (missing), the next report that has a value is used. If the month-to-date or season-to-date snowfall total goes up while the daily value is missing, that counts as YES. A day still unpublished on December 3 at 11:59 PM ET is treated as zero. The archive is on the Iowa Environmental Mesonet (IEM).
- **Window:** effectively about now through November 30. December 1–4 does not count, even though the market closes December 4.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Traces are easy to log.** KDFW is an automated station with human augmentation, and a trace can be recorded for flurries or snowflakes mixed with rain or sleet that leave nothing on the ground. That makes "T" much more common than measurable snow.
  - **Sleet and freezing rain alone are not snowfall**, although sleet is sometimes folded into the snowfall column. A wintry mix needs a close reading of the report.
  - **Market creation date is unknown.** Early November was warm (highs in the 80s; see section 3), so it is very unlikely any snow has fallen yet. I did not check the IEM archive for November 1–13 directly.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Any November snow (trace or more) at DFW, all years since 1898.**
- The NWS Fort Worth "Monthly Top 10" list ranks the snowiest Novembers as 1976 (5.0") and 1937 (5.0") tied first, then 1929 (2.2"). Trace Novembers are tied at rank 8 and include 2022, 2014 and 2013 (NWS FWD DFW Top 10 Monthly, undated).
- So about 7 Novembers had measurable snow in roughly 128 years, or about 5%.
- A search summary said "about two dozen" more years had a November trace. I could not verify that. If it's right, the share of Novembers with any snow is about 20–25%. If the trace list is shorter, it's closer to 10–15%.
- Other early-season events: the earliest measurable snow on record is November 2, 1951 (weather.com and FOX Weather "earliest snow" lists). Nov 13, 1976 brought about 4.7" in one storm (iweathernet top-20 list). A Dallas list also shows Nov 25, 1993 at 0.9", and the 1937 storm was on Nov 22.

**B. Recent era, roughly the last 15 seasons (2011–2025).**
- At least 3 Novembers had a trace or more: 2013, 2014 and 2022. That's about 20% (≥3/15). I found no evidence of November snow in 2015–2021 or in 2023–2025, but I didn't check every year.
- The November 18, 2022 trace is well documented. NWS logged it in the daily climate report, and local media called it the "earliest on record," beating November 19, 1930 (Local Profile, Nov 21, 2022; KRLD/Audacy, Nov 2022). That "earliest" claim conflicts with the 1951 and 1976 events, so it was probably a garbled media claim.

**C. Conditioning on timing (no snow by mid-November).**
- Nearly every known November event fell between November 13 and 30: Nov 13 1976, Nov 18 2022, Nov 19 1930, Nov 22 1937, Nov 25 1993. The only early-month case I found is November 2, 1951.
- So the November 14–30 window holds most of the monthly rate, perhaps 80–90% of it. That implies an unconditional chance of roughly 8–20% for this window, before using the current forecast.
- The climate-normal first freeze at DFW is about November 22 (NWS FWD, cited by Dallas Morning News, Nov 2026). Snow before the first freeze is uncommon.
- The NWS lists DFW's normal November snowfall as "T" (NWS FWD winter climatology).

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 13, 2026, CPC 6–10 and 8–14 day discussion:** the outlook for Nov 21–27 favors above-normal temperatures for much of the US. The Nov 11 week-2 hazards outlook said there was "no sign of any early season outbreaks of anomalous cold." The only cold-leaning area is the Southwest, centered on Nevada (CPC 610-day discussion, Nov 13, 2026; CPC hazards outlook, Nov 11, 2026). An earlier release leaned warm for Nov 18–24 from the Rockies east.
- **Nov 13, 2026, media summary of NOAA:** "After this week's cold blast… most of the US could turn warmer before Thanksgiving." That cold was focused on the East and the Tennessee and lower Mississippi valleys (Yahoo/Men's Journal, about Nov 13, 2026).
- **About Nov 11–14, 2026, local DFW forecasts:**
  - Two cold fronts passed around November 11–13.
  - Highs in the upper 60s to low 70s through Saturday, November 14.
  - Rain chances of 50% Saturday and 80% Sunday, up to about 1 inch.
  - Next week turns much cooler, with highs in the 50s–60s.
  - Possible first frost only in the northeastern counties on Saturday. Metro lows were expected in the upper 40s to low 50s, and the first freeze may come later than normal because the fall has been warm (Dallas Morning News; CBS Texas, Nov 11, 2026; Yahoo/DMN, Nov 2026).
  - None of the reports I saw mention wintry precipitation.
- **Early-to-mid November 2026:** a warm stretch with highs in the mid-to-upper 80s (DMN, "Temps could swing from 80s to near-freezing this week").
- **NWS extended outlook (cited by DMN, about Nov 12):** the rest of November is likely warmer than normal with slightly below-normal precipitation. The CPC November outlook gave North Texas equal chances for temperature and leaned wet.
- **ENSO:** a very strong El Niño. CPC said in August 2026 there was a >90% chance of a very strong event, and its October 8 update cited Niño anomalies above +4°C in parts of the eastern Pacific (KBTX, Oct 18, 2026). El Niño's cool, wet signal for Texas is mainly a December–February effect. Its effect on November snow at DFW is weak or unclear.
- **Scheduled:** Thanksgiving is November 26. Normals for that date are about 63°F high and 43°F low. CPC's 6–10 and 8–14 day outlooks are updated daily and week 3–4 weekly. The CPC 8–14 day issued around November 16–17 will be the first to cover November 30.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (all need an arctic or strong polar air mass reaching North Texas plus some moisture):**
1. **A late-month (Nov 27–30) arctic front with post-frontal drizzle or flurries,** or a mix that the observer logs as "T." This is the most plausible path. Days 14–17 are beyond current skill, and the CPC 8–14 day only covers through about Nov 26–27.
2. **A closed upper-level low or a southern-stream system.** A strong El Niño enhances the subtropical jet and could pair with a cold air mass to give a rain-to-snow changeover, like 2022 or the 1976 and 1937 storms. This needs below-freezing air aloft and near-freezing surface temperatures.
3. **The next-week cool spell** (highs in the 50s–60s). This is very unlikely to bring snow: lows stay above freezing in the metro and there is no moisture behind the Sunday rain.

**Paths to NO:**
- The warm pattern favored by CPC through about Nov 26–27 holds, which removes about 12 of the 16 remaining days.
- Even if a cold front comes Nov 27–30, it may be dry, or not cold enough for snow at the airport.

**What the outcome depends on:** the pattern change after Thanksgiving, mainly whether a western trough and ridging near Alaska or western Canada send cold air south, and the timing of moisture.

## 5. Signposts

- **Toward YES (large move):**
  - CPC 8–14 day maps issued Nov 16–20 turning to below-normal temperatures for the Southern Plains for Nov 27–30.
  - The NWS Fort Worth Area Forecast Discussion mentioning a "wintry mix," "flurries" or temperatures in the 30s with precipitation.
  - Ensemble models showing 850 mb temperatures below 0°C over North Texas.
  - Any precipitation chance with highs below 45°F.
- **Toward YES (moderate):** WPC or CPC hazards outlooks flagging much-below-normal temperatures for the Plains in week 2. Media stories on a "Thanksgiving week arctic blast."
- **Toward NO:**
  - Forecasts confirming the warm pattern through Thanksgiving.
  - The first freeze slipping past late November.
  - A dry pattern or ridging over Texas through Nov 30.
  - Each warm, dry day that passes removes 1/16 of the remaining window.
- **Resolution check:** confirm that the IEM CLIDFW archive shows 0.0 snowfall so far in November, especially if the market was created before November 14.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **How many Novembers had a trace at DFW** since 1898, which could be anywhere from about 10% to 25% of years. The "two dozen" figure is unverified, and I could not open the NWS top-10 or monthly snowfall pages directly.
- **Dates of the 2013 and 2014 November traces**, and whether 2015–2021 and 2023–2025 had any November traces.
- **The "earliest snowfall" claim for November 18, 2022** conflicts with the 1951 and 1976 records, so the media framing is unreliable. The trace itself appears real.
- **No direct Area Forecast Discussion or CPC map was read**, so the CPC outlook for Texas specifically, as opposed to "much of the US," is inferred from summaries.
- **How a strong El Niño affects November cold-air outbreaks in Texas** is not well established. I did not verify November snow in past strong El Niño years (1972, 1982, 1997, 2015).
- **The market creation date**, and whether anything was logged earlier in the window. That's very unlikely given the warmth.
