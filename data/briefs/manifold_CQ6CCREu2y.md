# Research brief: Will Asahifuji win the Juryo Yusho at the November 2026 Kyushu basho?

*Prepared 2026-11-14, Day 7 of 15. I could not find any Day 7 results. Every standing below is as of the end of Day 6 (Nov 13).*

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **YES** only if Asahifuji (Isegahama stable, East Juryo 12, age 24, from Mongolia) is the official juryo division champion of the Kyushu basho. The basho runs Nov 8–22, 2026 at Fukuoka Kokusai Center, and the final day is Sunday Nov 22. Close is 2026-11-22 23:59 UTC, after the basho ends (Japan is UTC+9).
- If two or more wrestlers tie on the best record, a playoff (kettei-sen) decides the title. The question says a playoff loss counts as NO. Asahifuji therefore has to finish alone on the best record or win any playoff.
- The Japan Sumo Association (JSA) decides. In practice the champion is whoever has the best record after Day 15, plus any playoff.
- **Edge cases:**
  - If Asahifuji withdraws (kyujo), the result is effectively NO. This matters because he showed a knee concern after Day 6 (see §3).
  - Enho is in the same stable (Isegahama), so the two do not meet in regular bouts. They could only meet in a playoff.
  - Don't confuse him with the earlier Asahifuji, the 63rd yokozuna. He inherited that ring name from his former stablemaster.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **Any one juryo wrestler, before the basho:** about 28 wrestlers, so a naive 1/28 ≈ 3.5%. That figure is no longer useful. One sumo preview had already picked Asahifuji for 11-4 and said he had "a good chance" at the title (Sumo Stomp 2026 Kyushu predictions, early Nov 2026).
2. **First-time juryo wrestlers who win the juryo title:** this is uncommon, but top prospects do it fairly often. Examples I remember, but could not check in this session:
   - Ochiai (now Hakuoho) is the case I'm most confident about: he won as a new juryo wrestler in Jan 2024.
   - Kusano contended as a new juryo wrestler in March 2025 and set a winning-streak record for new juryo. The Kumamoto Nichinichi headline from Day 10 says he was "closing in on the title" (Kumanichi, Mar 2025).
   - Fujiryoga was tied for the lead on Day 9 of Kyushu 2025 (Daily Sports, 2025-11-17).

   My rough estimate: a new juryo wrestler wins in perhaps 10–15% of recent basho. Elite prospects (top amateur or record-pace risers) win far more often.
3. **A chaser one loss behind a lone undefeated leader after Day 6:** juryo titles are usually won with 11-4 to 13-2. A lone 6-0 juryo leader probably wins about 35–45% of the time, and the 5-1 group shares most of the rest. With about 3–4 wrestlers at 5-1, an average chaser has roughly 10–15%. This is my estimate, not a computed frequency. Juryo playoffs are common (my guess is roughly a third of basho, not verified), and the rules count a playoff loss as NO.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES**
- Asahifuji finishes 12-3 or 13-2. The most likely way is beating Wakatakakage head-to-head, which the schedulers will probably arrange in week 2 (around Days 9–13) if both stay in contention, while Wakatakakage drops 2–3 bouts as his body tires.
- Or he wins a playoff against Wakatakakage, Enho, Tobizaru or others.

Things in his favour:
- He is the lowest-ranked contender, so his early opponents are weaker.
- He has an exceptional winning rate (more than 90% in his career so far).
- Enho is a stablemate, so that is one fewer strong opponent in regular bouts.

**Paths to NO**
- Wakatakakage keeps up a pace near 13-2 or 14-1. A healthy former sekiwake in juryo often does.
- Asahifuji slips against the stronger ex-makuuchi wrestlers, such as Shodai, Tobizaru and Wakatakakage, in week 2.
- The knee (or the earlier rib injury) gets worse and he loses form or withdraws.
- He loses a playoff.
- Several 5-1 rivals split the outcomes, and every additional strong chaser lowers his chance.

## 5. Signposts
- **Day 7–8 results.** A Wakatakakage loss to Mitakeumi helps YES a lot. Asahifuji losing a second bout early hurts.
- **Schedule.** Watch when Asahifuji is matched with Wakatakakage, Tobizaru and Shodai. A win over Wakatakakage would be the single biggest move toward YES.
- **Knee.** Reports of tape or a brace, or interview comments, would point toward NO.
- **Wakatakakage's leg.** Any sign of fatigue or a strapped leg in week 2 points toward YES.
- **Day 13–14 standings.** If several wrestlers are tied, playoff risk rises, and only a playoff win counts.
