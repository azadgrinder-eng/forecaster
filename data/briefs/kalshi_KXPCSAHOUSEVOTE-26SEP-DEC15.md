# Research brief: Will the House hold a recorded vote on the Protect College Sports Act before Dec 15, 2026?

*Note on method: every direct page fetch was blocked by the network proxy. All facts below come from search-result summaries of the cited articles, so treat exact quotes and dates with some caution. Where sources disagree, I say so.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **Which bill:** The Protect College Sports Act of 2026. The Senate bill is S.4668, sponsored by Sen. Ted Cruz (R-TX) with Ranking Member Maria Cantwell (D-WA). The House companion is H.R.9137, referred to Judiciary, Energy & Commerce, and Education & Workforce. S.4668 passed the Senate 77-22 on Sept 28, 2026, and by about Oct 5 was listed as "held at the desk" in the House (Congress.gov S.4668; Senate Commerce press release, Sept 28, 2026). A House recorded vote on either bill, or on an amended House version under the same name, should count. Do not confuse it with Rep. Greg Steube's 2025 "Protection of College Sports Act" (Steube press release, Aug 29, 2025).
- **What counts as YES:** Any recorded House vote (roll call, yea-and-nay, electronic, or division) taken after the market was issued and before Dec 15, 2026, 04:59 UTC. In practice, House votes must happen by the end of Dec 14 Eastern time.
- **Votes that count:**
  - final passage;
  - a motion to proceed;
  - a vote on a rule, if the rule specifically provides for consideration of this bill. This is a key lower bar: in Dec 2025 the House adopted the rule for the predecessor SCORE Act 210-209 even though the bill itself was pulled the next day.
  - A motion to suspend the rules and pass would also be a recorded vote on the bill, even if it failed to reach two-thirds.
  - A recorded vote on a motion to concur in a Senate amendment, or to concur with a House amendment, would also plausibly count.
- **Votes that don't count:** Voice votes, unanimous consent, committee votes, and amendment votes. An important edge case: if the PCSA text were folded into a continuing resolution, the NDAA, or an omnibus, a vote on that vehicle would probably not count unless the market rules treat the vehicle as referring to the bill. This is ambiguous.
- **Issuance date:** Unknown. A vote between issuance and the deadline is what matters. As of Nov 6 no House vote had occurred.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **The House's own record on college-sports bills this Congress.** The SCORE Act (H.R.4312) was scheduled for the floor about three times:
   - Dec 2–3, 2025: the rule passed 210-209 by recorded vote, then the bill was pulled (Roll Call, Dec 3, 2025).
   - Late Dec 2025 / Jan 2026: a planned vote was delayed for lack of votes (Front Office Sports, Dec 28, 2025).
   - May 19, 2026: the vote was cancelled after the Congressional Black Caucus (CBC) announced unanimous opposition (College Help Guide; I could only confirm this with one source).

   That is roughly one recorded vote in three attempts. Counting the months when leadership was nominally trying, recorded votes happened far less than half the time. Final passage: 0 of 3.
2. **Senate-passed bills in a lame-duck session after the House flips party.** Historically, the outgoing majority holds lame-duck votes on Senate-passed bills mainly when House leadership actively wants the bill. Examples: the First Step Act in Dec 2018 (outgoing GOP House) and DADT repeal in Dec 2010 (outgoing Democratic House). Contested, non-must-pass bills that leadership wants to amend usually die. Recent lame ducks have mostly handled appropriations, the NDAA, and nominations (CRS R45154, lame-duck sessions 1935–2024). My rough judgment, not a counted statistic: well under 25% of contested, non-must-pass Senate bills get a House floor vote in such sessions.
3. **Expert estimates of passage (not of a vote):**
   - Darren Heitner: 80% that it does not pass the House.
   - Mit Winter: "slim-to-none."
   - Seth Davis: the midterm results "pretty much kills" the bill.

   These describe passage. A recorded vote, including a rule vote, is a lower bar (On3 and Yahoo, Nov 4–5, 2026).

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES:**

- **A. Leadership puts the Senate bill or an amended House version on the floor under a rule.** The rule vote alone resolves YES, even if the bill is later pulled or fails, as with the SCORE Act in Dec 2025. This depends on:
  - Trump/White House pressure (Levine) on Johnson and Scalise;
  - Scalise deciding to add an "athletes are not employees" amendment and send the bill back to the Senate;
  - the GOP conference holding together on the rule.

  Rule votes are usually party-line, so a rule could pass even if final passage needed Democrats.
- **B. A suspension vote** (two-thirds needed). Leadership might try this to put members on record or to test support. A failed suspension still counts. This is unlikely, given CBC and union opposition and Democrats' incentive to wait.
- **C. A deal folded into must-pass legislation.** This would probably not count unless there were also a standalone vote or the vehicle explicitly refers to the bill.

**Paths to NO:**

- Leadership never schedules the bill. Reasons: a crowded agenda (CR deadline Dec 11, NDAA), no Democratic votes, GOP attendance problems, and division within the GOP over the employee-status question.
- A vote is scheduled after Dec 14. The CR and NDAA endgame could push lame-duck business into the week of Dec 14–18 or later, which falls outside the window.
- Leadership whips the bill, finds it short, and pulls it before the Rules Committee or a rule vote. This is the common pattern: two of three SCORE Act attempts ended this way.

## 5. Signposts

- **Toward YES (large moves):**
  - The bill appears on the Majority Leader's weekly floor schedule or the Rules Committee posts a meeting notice for S.4668/H.R.9137. That would push the chance of at least a rule vote well above 50%.
  - Trump publicly demanding a House vote.
  - Scalise or Johnson announcing an amended version, or a bicameral deal with Cruz on employee-status language.
  - A bloc of Democrats (beyond the CBC holdouts) or the Big Ten/SEC endorsing a House version.
- **Toward NO:**
  - Leadership saying the bill will wait, or Scalise's continued focus on the SCORE Act.
  - The CR/NDAA fight consuming the floor through mid-December.
  - Jeffries or the CBC whipping hard against it.
  - Reports that defeated Republicans are absent.
  - The week of Dec 7 passing with no Rules Committee notice. After that, only the Dec 14 window would remain.

## 6. Uncertainties

- Whether the "21 House Republicans" letter exists and who signed it. My search could not confirm it.
- Whether the May 2026 SCORE Act attempt included any recorded rule vote. It was cancelled; that is from one source, and it is unclear whether a rule vote happened.
- The exact lame-duck margin and how many members will attend.
- How the market will treat rule votes, folding the bill into must-pass legislation, or a renamed House substitute.
- The market's issuance date.
- Reporting conflicts: some outlets misdate the Senate vote (Nov 9 vs. Sept 28; Sept 28 is right, per the Senate Commerce press release). Johnson's personal status in January is also unclear.
- The precise wording of Scalise's latest statements could not be checked.
