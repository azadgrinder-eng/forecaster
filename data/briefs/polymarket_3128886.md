# Research brief: Will the US officially announce the end of its Iran naval blockade by Dec 31, 2026?

*Prepared 2026-11-25. Direct page fetches were blocked in this environment, so every fact below comes from search-result summaries of the cited articles. The dates are probably reliable, but exact quotes should be checked against the originals.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

**What counts as YES:**
- An official US government announcement of the end, termination, lifting or suspension of the naval blockade on Iranian ships and Iran's customers.
- It must be made between market creation (presumably soon after the July 13, 2026 reinstatement) and Dec 31, 2026, 11:59 PM ET.
- Acceptable sources are the President, DoD, State, CENTCOM, or another authorized official.

**What the announcement must be:**
- It must be a present, decided position: an end that has already happened, an end that is happening now, or a definitive decision to end it.
- Conditional or prospective statements don't count, e.g. "we will lift the blockade if Iran reopens Hormuz."
- It must clearly announce a general end. Exemptions for a single vessel, cargo or port don't count.
- Some remaining restrictions are allowed, such as fees (Trump paired the July blockade with a proposed 20% Hormuz fee).
- Indirect wording can count, e.g. "a return to a previously agreed baseline," as long as ending the blockade is unambiguous.
- Once a qualifying announcement is made, the question resolves YES even if the decision is later reversed or never carried out.

**Precedent for what qualifies:** On June 18, 2026, CENTCOM posted that US forces "lifted the blockade on all maritime traffic entering and leaving Iranian ports and coastal areas" and that "all blockade enforcement efforts have ceased" (USNI News, Jun 18 2026; Al Jazeera, Jun 18 2026). That is a clean example of a qualifying statement. Note that the Islamabad MoU only committed the US to begin lifting immediately and finish within 30 days (intellinews, Jun 2026). A signed deal with that kind of language would probably count as a "definitive decision" even before the lifting is complete.

**Edge cases and ambiguities:**
1. A Trump Truth Social post would probably count as official, since he uses it for policy announcements (the July 13 reinstatement was announced that way, per Axios, Jul 13 2026). Offhand remarks to reporters could be disputed.
2. Trump often says things like "the deal is largely done" or "the blockade will end when…". These would not qualify.
3. A deal announced by a third party, such as Pakistan's PM, does not count unless the US confirms it.
4. Lifting sanctions is not the same as lifting the blockade.
5. If the US replaced the blockade with "fees" or a "toll" regime that still let Iranian ships move, that might qualify. This is untested.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Blockade episodes within this conflict**
- Blockade #1 ran Apr 13 to Jun 18, 2026 (66 days). It ended with a negotiated MoU and an explicit CENTCOM announcement (Anadolu; USNI, Jun 2026).
- Blockade #2 has run since Jul 14, 2026: 134 days as of Nov 25, already about twice as long as the first.
- Rough hazard rate: one lifting in about 200 blockade-days, or about 0.5% per day. Over the remaining ~36 days that gives roughly 15–17%.
- This is probably an upper bound for now. The first blockade ended during an active, high-tempo negotiation. Today the US President has publicly stepped away from talks (see section 3).

**B. Trump's "deal is close" claims vs. actual outcomes in this war**
- Analysts count nearly 40 claims since late February that a deal was imminent (Al Jazeera analysis, via search summary).
- Only one produced a signed agreement and an end to the blockade (June 17–18), and that deal collapsed within about 4 weeks (NPR, Jul 18 2026; GMA timeline).
- So upbeat signals convert into actual outcomes at roughly 1 in 40, or about 2.5% per claim. Optimistic rhetoric should be discounted heavily.

**C. Modern naval blockades and quarantines in general**
- 1962 Cuba quarantine: about 4 weeks, ended by a formal announcement after a negotiated deal.
- 2017–21 Qatar blockade: about 3.5 years.
- Israel's blockade of Gaza: from 2007, many years.
- Saudi-led blockade of Yemen: from 2015, eased only in part.
- Once the first-month crisis peak has passed, blockades used as an economic-pressure tool tend to last for years rather than weeks. Short ones end through a negotiated bargain, not by quietly expiring.
- These durations are from my background knowledge and were not searched this session.

**Other context**
- Shipping attacks: 34 in October (the most since the war began) and 16 so far in November (USNI/gCaptain).
- The US has carried out limited strikes, e.g. on Qeshm Island.
- The Senate again rejected a war powers resolution.
- Iran's currency is in sharp decline, and Iran's judiciary is seeking $290B in damages from the US (CBS; ABC live updates).

**What Iran is demanding (the "seven conditions")**
- Araghchi says these, not the Islamabad MoU, are now the basis for any deal (IRNA; Anadolu).
- Conditions: lift the blockade; guarantee safe passage; remove unilateral sanctions; exempt Iranian oil exports; release frozen assets; let Iran keep its nuclear material in-country; formally end the war.
- Iran offers to reopen Hormuz within "two days," or even "two hours," after the US acts.
- The asks on sanctions, nuclear material and reparations go well beyond what the US has shown it will accept.

**Scheduled events before Dec 31:** I found nothing scheduled, such as a summit, deadline or formal talks round. Trump's own framing points to a decision in early 2027. The holiday period usually slows diplomacy.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (all unlikely within 36 days)**
1. **A quick negotiated deal.** Qatar, Pakistan, Egypt, Saudi Arabia or Turkey revive talks, and a deal like the Islamabad one, with "Iran reopens Hormuz, US lifts the blockade," is signed and announced in December. This needs:
   - Trump to reverse his Nov 12 "no deal needed" stance;
   - Iran to drop or soften the seven conditions (nuclear material, reparations);
   - both to happen within weeks.
   The June precedent shows this can move fast once both sides are willing: the MoU was signed June 17 and the blockade lifted June 18.
2. **A US-only pause as a goodwill gesture or to calm oil prices.** For example, suspending the blockade to open talks, or a "fee instead of blockade" structure. Nothing suggests this is under consideration; the administration is widening enforcement.
3. **Political or economic pressure** (oil prices, shipping-insurance costs, allies, Congress) pushing Trump to trade the blockade for Hormuz reopening. The Senate's rejection of the war powers resolution shows weak congressional pressure.

**Paths to NO (the default)**
- The status quo continues. Trump has said he's in "no hurry," his next decision may come in 2027, and he is inclined to resume strikes after New Year if there's no deal.
- Escalation: an Iranian convoy attempt, tanker attacks, or strikes on leaders (NBC reports an internal debate about this) would harden positions further.
- Any deal reached late in December might only be announced as conditional or phased ("will lift once Iran…"), which wouldn't qualify.

## 5. Signposts

**Would raise the probability a lot:**
- Trump or the White House announcing renewed talks with a named envoy, especially if Vance is reinstated or Witkoff is involved.
- A face-to-face round in Islamabad, Doha or Cairo.
- Iran publicly dropping its reparations or nuclear-material conditions.
- Trump saying a deal is "signed" (as opposed to "close").
- CENTCOM easing enforcement or rolling back the Nov 18 ship-to-ship rule.
- Iran letting commercial traffic through Hormuz as a unilateral gesture.

**Would raise it moderately:**
- A sharp rise in oil prices or a market shock tied to Hormuz.
- A Qatar or Pakistan PM visit to the White House followed by "positive momentum" remarks. Discount heavily given the 1-in-40 conversion rate.

**Would lower it:**
- Further expansion of enforcement, such as an Iron Gate land interdiction incident.
- The US sinking a blockade runner, or Iran attempting a convoy.
- US strikes on Iranian leaders.
- Trump repeating "no deal needed" or "2027."
- Iran formally adopting reparations as a precondition.

- **Market creation date:** Unknown, but probably mid-July. No qualifying announcement appears to have been made since July 14.
- **How a fee or toll regime would be judged:** If Trump replaced the blockade with a "pay-to-pass" system for Iranian-linked ships, it's unclear whether that would count as a "general end."
