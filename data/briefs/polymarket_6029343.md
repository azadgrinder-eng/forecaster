# Research Brief: Will PSOE win the most seats in Almería in the 29 November 2026 Spanish general election?

*Method note: the page fetcher was blocked in this environment, so I could not open any page directly. Every figure below comes from search-result snippets and summaries. Treat precise decimals as approximate, and check key numbers against Interior/BOE if they matter at the margin.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **Event:** the snap general election for the 350-seat Congress on **29 Nov 2026**. Sánchez announced it on 5 Oct 2026. Royal Decree 806/2026 was published in the BOE on 6 Oct 2026 (Moncloa, 5 Oct 2026; Newtral, 5 Oct 2026). The campaign runs 13–27 Nov and polls are open 09:00–20:00 on 29 Nov.
- **Constituency:** Almería elects **6 deputies** by D'Hondt with a 3% threshold. The BOE of 3 Nov 2026 proclaimed **12 candidate lists** for Congress there (La Crónica del Parque / Democrata, Nov 2026).
- **How the winner is ranked:** most seats first. A seat tie is broken by more valid votes in Almería, then by alphabetical order. **PSOE wins YES** if it wins outright on seats, or ties on seats and has the most votes among the tied parties. A 2–2–2 split or a 2–2 PP–PSOE tie therefore comes down to votes.
- **Lists:** the PSOE list is led by **Anabel Mateos**, followed by Juan Antonio Lorenzo, Inés Plaza, José Ramón García Túñez, Yolanda Lozano and Damián Requena (COPE, 15 Oct 2026; La Voz de Almería).
- **Source of truth:** a consensus of credible reporting, with Interior (infoelectoral) as fallback. If results are not definitive by 30 Nov 2027 the market resolves "Other". Election-night provisional results are normally decisive within hours, but the CERA overseas vote and the final count (escrutinio) could matter only in a knife-edge tie on votes.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
**A. Who topped Almería in past general elections, ranked under these rules (seats, then votes).** I put this together from search snippets of the Wikipedia constituency page; entries marked (?) are from memory and unverified.
- 2023: **PP** 3 seats (41.0%), PSOE 2 (29.0%), Vox 1.
- Nov 2019: PSOE 2 (29.5%), Vox 2 (~26.7–26.9%), PP 2. Seats tied 2–2–2, so **PSOE** wins on votes. Euro Weekly News called it a "three-party tie" (11 Nov 2019).
- Apr 2019: **PSOE** 2 (30.15%), tied on seats with PP 2 and ahead on votes. Cs 1, Vox 1.
- 2016: **PP** 3 (42.98%).
- 2015: **PP** 2 (37.96%), tied on seats with PSOE 2, PP ahead on votes.
- 2011: **PP** 4 (57.6%), PSOE 2.
- 2008: 3–3 split, PP likely ahead on votes (?). 2004: PSOE (?). 2000: PP (?).
- **Since 2011, PSOE topped Almería 2 times in 6 elections. Since 2000 it is about 3 of 9.** Every PSOE win came when PSOE led nationally and the right was split three ways (PP/Cs/Vox), so PP fell to about 25% locally.

**B. Almería compared with the national result.** Almería is consistently one of PSOE's weakest Andalusian provinces relative to the right:
- 2023: PP ran about 8 points above its national 33.1% in Almería. PSOE ran about 2.7 points below its national 31.7%. Vox ran well above its national 12.4%; its exact 2023 Almería share was not retrieved.
- Applying those gaps to current national averages (PP ~31, PSOE ~27, Vox ~19) gives roughly **PP ~39 / Vox ~25–26 / PSOE ~24** in Almería.

**C. The most recent election in the same territory: the Andalusian regional vote of 17 May 2026, Almería result.**
- PP 42.7%, 6 regional seats.
- **Vox 23.2%**, 3 seats.
- **PSOE-A 21.7%**, 3 seats.
- Vox overtook PSOE in Almería for the first time in a regional election (Wikipedia; Democrata; Infobae/EFE, 17 May 2026). In 2022 the figures were PP 45.6 / PSOE 22.1 / Vox 20.7.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)
- **~2 Nov 2026: SocioMétrica / El Español, Almería breakdown.** PP first in the province, **Vox 28.2% in second, PSOE 22.3% in third**, a 5.9-point gap (El Español, ~2 Nov 2026). The earlier SocioMétrica wave (26 Oct) had Vox 27.7%, PSOE 22.8%. Reports disagree on that wave's seat split: Vox 2 / PSOE 1, or Vox 2 / PSOE 2 (El Español, 26 Oct 2026; La Bandera).
- **2 Nov 2026: Sigma Dos after the Urda crash.** Sigma Dos (El Mundo) is the only poll fielded after the **Urda (Toledo) train crash**, reported at about 69 dead around 28–29 Oct. It shows PSOE below 25% nationally for the first time in the campaign, a loss of about 450,000 votes in three days (Infobae, 2 Nov 2026; Telemadrid; Euronews, 29 Oct 2026). NC Report has PSOE at 25.6%.
- **1 Nov 2026: Vox internal poll** (The Objective). PSOE 29.6%, PP 28.1%, Vox 20.2%, with 71 seats for Vox. This is an outlier and partisan.
- **~6 Nov 2026: national averages** (Periodista Digital). PP ~31.2%, PSOE ~26.8%, Vox ~19.3%. Democrata's average of seven pollsters gives roughly PP 134 / PSOE 106 / Vox 68 seats. The PSOE-friendly outlier SW Demoscopia (La Séptima) has PSOE at 29.1–30.8%, close to PP. The CIS reportedly shows PSOE well ahead, as it usually does; the date was not established. El Español (2 Nov) found PP's worst case at about 130 seats and PSOE's best at about 111.
- **SocioMétrica/Democrata on Andalusia:** PSOE is projected to lose about 3 seats in the region versus 2023, its biggest regional loss, and PP's lead there doubles.
- **19 Oct 2026: Sigma Dos provincial map. Almería: PP 3, Vox 2, PSOE 1** (Sigma Dos, 19 Oct 2026).
- **Scheduled before close:** the campaign (13–27 Nov), debates, the last day to request a postal vote (19 Nov), the end of publishable polls on about 23 Nov (the legal ban covers the final five days), and election day on 29 Nov.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES (all require large moves):**
1. **PSOE wins 3 seats, or ties PP and beats it on votes.** That needs PSOE to roughly match or pass PP in Almería, where PP leads by about 15–20 points in polls and won by about 21 points in May 2026. It would take a national swing of more than 10 points. Effectively negligible.
2. **A 2–2–2 split with PSOE first on votes**, a repeat of Nov 2019. That needs PP down to about 27–29% and PSOE above both PP and Vox. Current polling has PSOE *third* in Almería, so this also needs a collapse of about 10 points or more in PP's vote share.

**Paths to NO (dominant):** PP first on seats, with 3 seats on most projections and at least 2 in nearly any plausible scenario. The fight for 2nd and 3rd between Vox and PSOE does not affect this market, except that a stronger Vox further reduces PSOE's chances.

**What YES would depend on:** a dramatic late national shift toward PSOE plus a PP collapse in Almería, or a major polling error. Momentum after the Urda crash points the other way.

## 5. Signposts
- **Toward YES (each would only move it modestly):** any Almería-specific poll showing PP below 32% or PSOE above Vox; national polls showing PSOE leading PP by 3 points or more after Urda; a PP-specific scandal; Vox surging at PP's expense to about 30% locally, splitting the right three ways as in 2019.
- **Toward NO:** further post-Urda polls with PSOE at or below 25%; continued SocioMétrica/Sigma Dos maps with PSOE on 1 seat in Almería.
- **Election night:** an early count with PP above 35% in Almería settles the question.

## 6. Uncertainties
- I could not open any primary page, so seat splits and percentages come from search snippets. I found no PP vote share for Almería in the November SocioMétrica wave, only the statement that PP leads.
- The exact Vox share in Almería in 2023, and the 2000–2008 Almería winners, are not verified.
- The size and duration of the Urda effect is unclear, with only one poll fielded fully after it. CIS figures are undated and diverge from private pollsters.
- Almería-specific poll samples are small, so provincial estimates carry errors of several points. That still is not enough to bridge a gap of 15 points or more between PP and PSOE.
