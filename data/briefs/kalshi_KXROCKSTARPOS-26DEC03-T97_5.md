## Research brief: Will Carbon Arc's Rockstar Energy Point of Sale Growth index for November 2026 be above 97.5?

### 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** Carbon Arc's Rockstar Energy Point of Sale Growth index for November 2026 must be strictly above 97.5. The index is year-over-year, with 100 meaning the same spending as November 2025. Above 97.5 therefore means Rockstar's November 2026 spending in Carbon Arc's panel fell by less than about 2.5% from November 2025, or grew. A value of exactly 97.5 resolves NO.
- **Data source:** Carbon Arc's point-of-sale receipt panel. It captures in-store and online purchases through receipt-scanning and rewards apps and compares them with the same period a year earlier. The figure appears in Carbon Arc's "Prisms" daily reference product, which describes its values as "relative growth indices against the same period last year" (Carbon Arc Prisms page, snapshot about mid-Sept 2026).
- **Timing:** The value is "finalized" as recorded at 11:59 p.m. ET on December 2, 2026. The market closes on Dec 3, 2026 at 04:59 UTC, which is the same moment.
- **Edge cases and ambiguities:**
  - The panel "updates daily as new data arrives," so the figure can be revised until the Dec 2 snapshot. Receipts from late November may still be arriving.
  - Carbon Arc does not publish details on panel reweighting, how it handles changes in panel composition, or how it matches products to the Rockstar brand. For example, it is unclear whether the brand's new products are counted.
  - The brand is small, about 2% of US energy-drink dollars. That means fewer receipts in the panel and probably more noise from month to month than in Circana's store scanner data.
  - In the US and Canada the brand now belongs to Celsius Holdings, with PepsiCo still distributing it. PepsiCo keeps the brand internationally. The panel appears to be US-focused.

### 2. Reference classes and base rates
**A. Rockstar's own year-over-year retail sales in Circana tracked channels, the period most similar to this one.**
- Full-year 2025: −11% (Celsius Q4/FY2025 release, Feb 26, 2026).
- Q1 2026 (13 weeks to Mar 29): −13% (Zacks, 2026).
- Q2 2026 (13 weeks to Jun 28): −13% (Celsius Q2 2026 materials, Aug 2026).
- Q3 2026 (13 weeks to Sep 27): −13.2% (Celsius Q3 2026 release, Nov 5, 2026).
- In 0 of these 4 observations was the decline smaller than −2.5%; the YES bar is an index of 97.5 or higher. The trend has been very steady at about −13% for three quarters.
- Rockstar was also losing ground under PepsiCo before the sale: its distribution points were down 17% in FY2025 (BevLab summary of the FY2025 results, secondary source). I did not verify a 2023–2024 base rate.

**B. Carbon Arc's own readings for Rockstar.**
- The only reading I could find is 87.4 (−12.6 points) on the Prisms page, which was about 56 days old when indexed. That probably reflects August 2026 or a recent window. Rockstar's point-of-sale share was shown as 2.6% (−0.6).
- Known Carbon Arc readings above 97.5: 0 of 1. This matches Circana's roughly −13%.
- I could not obtain the September and October 2026 values. Kalshi pages were blocked for this research, and the October market settled around Nov 2–3.

**C. How fast a steadily declining brand's year-over-year rate can recover.**
- In consumer packaged goods, year-over-year growth rarely improves by more than 10 points in a single month. It usually takes one of three things: lapping a base-period disruption, a big distribution gain, or a large promotion.
- The decline here comes mainly from planned SKU cuts. A decline like that typically persists until the cuts are a year old, which in this case means into 2027.
- Rough heuristic, not computed from data: a 10-point-plus improvement within a quarter, with no identifiable trigger, happens in well under 10–15% of brand-month observations. Small-brand receipt-panel noise widens that range.

  - Rockstar revenue was about $80.3M, against $12.2M in a partial prior-year quarter (Aug 28 – Sep 30, 2025). The strength was partly due to Canada and the timing of orders across Q2 and Q3 (Celsius Q3 release / GuruFocus, Nov 5, 2026).

  - Dollars per point of distribution rose about 14% from the prior quarter.

  - The 2027 plan is to expand distribution, grow core velocity and recruit new consumers (Benzinga transcript). The emphasis on 2027 suggests a turnaround is not expected in Q4 2026.

### 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES (index above 97.5) require an improvement of about 10 points or more from the recent run rate of about 87.**
- **Easy comparison:** Something in November 2025 depressed Rockstar's base, such as ownership-transition disruption, out-of-stocks, or an early wave of SKU cuts. Lapping it would mechanically lift the November 2026 year-over-year figure. I found no evidence of such a disruption specific to November 2025.
- **Lapping the SKU cuts:** If the SKU rationalization happened mostly in late 2025, the drag would start to fall away in Q4 2026. Q3's −13.2% and the "stable quarter-over-quarter" comment argue against an early lap.
- **Momentum:** The "green shoots," 14% higher dollars per distribution point, possible holiday promotions and the new can would all need to translate into a sharp jump in spending.
- **Panel effects:** Changes in Carbon Arc's panel or receipt noise for a small brand could push one month's reading up by several points. Late receipt arrivals and revisions could also matter.

**Paths to NO**
- The status quo: the SKU-driven decline continues at about −10% to −14%, and the index lands around 85–92.
- Even if the core range stays flat (carried-forward SKUs at about −1%), the lost revenue from discontinued SKUs keeps the brand total well below the threshold.

### 5. Signposts
- **Carbon Arc readings for September and October 2026, the most important item:** These have already been published, and the October value settled around Nov 2–3. A reading near 87 confirms NO is very likely. A reading of 93 or higher would show a real upward trend and raise the probability of YES.
- **Month-to-date November reading:** If the Prisms panel shows the running November value, a figure persistently above about 97 into late November would point strongly to YES. A figure below about 92 points strongly to NO.
- **Comparable brands:** Whether Carbon Arc's Monster, Celsius and Pepsi indices for October and November show broad upward or downward moves across the panel, which would signal a panel-wide artifact.
- **Weekly Circana or Nielsen data:** Any weekly Rockstar scanner data from Oct–Nov 2026 (for example, sell-side notes from Goldman, Stifel or Piper on Celsius scanner trends) showing the 4-week trend moving toward −5% or better.
- **Supply or distribution news:** Any news of Rockstar shelf gains, holiday promotions, or a PepsiCo distribution disruption in November.

### 6. Uncertainties
- I could not obtain Carbon Arc's September and October 2026 values or its history before 2026. Kalshi and Carbon Arc pages were blocked, so I only have the 87.4 snapshot, and its exact month is uncertain.
- I don't know how closely Carbon Arc's receipt panel tracks Circana for this brand, or how volatile its monthly readings are. Circana itself argues that standalone receipt panels cover a small share of trips and are noisy.
- I don't know the timing of the SKU cuts, specifically whether the discontinuations began before November 2025, which determines when the drag stops counting against prior-year sales. I also don't know Rockstar's November 2025 base conditions.
- It is unclear whether new items, such as the Energy+ Hydration line and the new cans, are counted in Rockstar's brand mapping, and when they reach shelves.
- I don't know whether Carbon Arc's "finalized" Dec 2 value differs much from the in-month readings.
