# Research Brief: Will a Mountain West player win the 2026 Heisman Trophy?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** This is one outcome of a multi-outcome conference market. It resolves YES only if the 2026 Heisman winner plays for a Mountain West (MW) team during the 2026 season, judged at the time the award is presented.
- **Who decides:** The Heisman Trophy Trust (heisman.com) and the NCAA, or a consensus of credible reporting. If sources conflict, the affiliation listed by the player's school and the NCAA controls.
- **Timing:** The ceremony is scheduled for **Saturday, Dec. 12, 2026, in New York City**, on ESPN/ABC, with the start time not yet announced. This comes from a single preview page; prior-year patterns support it (Legion Report / Sporting-events.org, 2026). Finalists are usually named after conference championship weekend, and the Trust has invited four finalists each year since 2021. The ballot deadline is probably about Monday, Dec. 7, but that is inferred from past years and not confirmed. If no award is given by Dec. 26, 2026, 11:59 PM ET, the market resolves "Other."
- **Which schools count as MW in 2026:** The conference changed a lot this year. Boise State, Colorado State, Fresno State, San Diego State and Utah State left for the Pac-12. North Dakota State (football-only), Northern Illinois (football-only) and UTEP (all sports) joined. The 10 football members are Air Force, Hawai'i, Nevada, New Mexico, San José State, UNLV, Wyoming, UTEP, Northern Illinois and NDSU (fbschedules / NDSU news, Feb 2026; La Nación/AP, Aug 21, 2026).
- **Edge case:** NDSU plays a full MW schedule but cannot play in the MW title game or the CFP during its two-year transition. It should still count as MW. It does not matter in practice because no NDSU player is a contender.
- **Edge case:** Former MW powers such as Boise State are now Pac-12 and would **not** count. There is no other real ambiguity.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **MW players winning the Heisman:** 0 wins in 27 awards (1999–2025) since the conference was founded. The best MW finish is Boise State RB Ashton Jeanty in 2024: second place with 2,017 points to Travis Hunter's 2,231 (CBS Sports, Dec 2024). That result came from a historically dominant season by a Boise program that has now left the MW.
2. **Any non-power-conference winner:** 0 wins in 35 years (1991–2025). The last one was Ty Detmer of BYU, then in the WAC, in 1990 (BYU / Heisman.com). Before Jeanty, the last Group of 5 runner-up was Marshall Faulk of San Diego State in 1992 (Boise State Athletics, Dec 14, 2024). So in about 35 years, non-power players have finished second only about twice and won zero times.
3. **Winners who were outside the national conversation in late November:** In the modern era the winner has almost always been a top-3 or top-5 name in media rankings by Week 11–12. I know of no winner who was absent from national top-10 Heisman lists in the third week of November. This is my qualitative judgment from recent cycles, not a computed number.

**Implied base rate:** The unconditional rate is well under 1%. Conditional on the evidence in section 3, it is close to zero.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 17–19, 2026, the national race:** Media and odds coverage describes a race led by Ohio State WR Jeremiah Smith (Big Ten), with Miami QB Darian Mensah (ACC) and Utah QB Devon Dampier (Big 12) behind him. Notre Dame QB CJ Carr (Independent) led in early November but has faded (VegasInsider, Nov 19; Fox Sports; Yardbarker, Week 11). Bleacher Report (after Week 11) calls it "Jeremiah Smith's award to lose."
- **Week 12 rankings:** ClutchPoints highlights Dampier, whose Utah team is 10-0. Miami WR Malachi Toney suffered a season-ending injury (ClutchPoints, Week 12). No MW player appears in any 2026 national Heisman ranking, watch list or odds summary I found.
- **About Nov 16, 2026, MW standings:** Wyoming leads at 6-1 in conference (7-3 overall), followed by New Mexico (5-1), NDSU (5-2), and Air Force and UNLV (both 4-2) (themw.com standings, page about 5 days old). No MW team at 7-3 or similar is a CFP-ranking power that could generate national Heisman attention.
- **Week 12 MW preview:** Air Force has the nation's best rushing offense, but as an option team that spreads carries across several backs. New Mexico's run defense ranks third in FBS. These are team strengths, not an individual Heisman case (MWC Connection, Week 12 preview).
- **Nov 14, 2026, sample stat lines:** Wyoming QB Landon Sims threw for 289 yards in a loss at UTEP, and Nevada RB Herschel Turner ran for 153 yards (ESPN schedule page). These are solid but not national-leader numbers.
- **Preseason (July 15, 2026):** Hawai'i QB Micah Alejado was named preseason MW Offensive Player of the Year (themw.com). His early 2026 line was about 1,017 passing yards, 3 TD and 1 INT through four games (RotoWire), and he gets no Heisman coverage.
- **Week 0 (late Aug 2026):** One MWC Connection recap called a San José State QB a Heisman candidate. I could not verify this, and the player has not appeared in any later national coverage.
- **Scheduled events before close:**
  - Remaining regular-season games (Thanksgiving weekend).
  - MW Championship Game (early December).
  - Conference championship weekend (about Dec 5).
  - Finalists announced (about Dec 7).
  - Ceremony on Dec 12.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (all extremely remote):**
- A MW player is a surprise winner. This would need an unprecedented national write-in surge for someone not on any current top-10 list, with about two games left and ballots due in roughly two weeks. Nothing suggests this is happening.
- A resolution quirk. For example, the winner's school is somehow reclassified as MW. No plausible mechanism exists for this.

**Paths to NO:**
- The default. The leading candidates are from the Big Ten (Smith), ACC (Mensah), Big 12 (Dampier) and Independent (Carr). Even if the front-runners stumble, the next names are power-conference or Notre Dame players.
- The weakened post-realignment MW, with no ranked contender and no standout statistical star, removes even the "Jeanty-style" path.

## 5. Signposts

- **Would raise the probability (only slightly):** A MW player appearing in national Heisman top-10 lists or the Heisman Trust's finalist list (about Dec 7). This is highly unlikely, and it would still not imply a win.
- **Would lock in NO:** The finalist announcement with no MW player, which is near-certain. After that, YES is effectively impossible.
- **Would not matter for this outcome:** Front-runner injuries or upsets. They shift the race among power-conference players and Notre Dame, not toward the MW.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not open several 2026 pages directly because the proxy blocked them, including full Week 12 top-10 lists. My finding that no MW player is in the conversation rests on search summaries of multiple 2026 rankings and odds pieces, none of which named a MW player.
- The exact 2026 finalist-announcement and ballot-deadline dates are not confirmed. Dec 12 for the ceremony comes from a single preview source.
- I did not confirm the 2026 national statistical leaders, though no source suggested a MW player leads FBS in a headline category.
- There is minor ambiguity about whether NDSU counts as "MW" during its transition. It is irrelevant in practice.
