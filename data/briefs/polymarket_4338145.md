# Research brief: Russia–Ukraine ceasefire agreement by Nov 30, 2026 (11:59 PM ET)

*Prepared Nov 11, 2026. About 19 days remain. Note: all my page fetches were blocked by the network proxy, so everything below comes from search-result summaries, not full articles. Treat exact quotes and small details as provisional.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Russia and Ukraine must mutually agree, before Nov 30, 2026, 11:59 PM ET, to suspend direct military engagement. Either both governments announce it officially, or a consensus of credible reporting confirms both sides agreed to it.
- **Effective date:** The agreement only has to be reached by the deadline. The ceasefire itself can start later, but the agreement must commit to stop fighting on a specified date.
- **What else can count:** A peace deal, political framework, truce or humanitarian pause counts only if it includes a dated, general suspension of fighting.
- **What does not count:**
  - Agreements to hold future talks or to "de-escalate" without a dated commitment to stop fighting.
  - Informal understandings, backchannel deals, or a de facto lull.
  - Unilateral pauses.
  - Partial deals limited to a category or a place. Examples: an energy-infrastructure strike moratorium, a Black Sea deal, or a local truce like the Oct 27–Nov 1 one in Oleshky.
- **Edge cases and ambiguities:**
  - **Short holiday truces.** This rule wording appears to include them if they are general and mutually agreed. Some earlier Polymarket ceasefire markets explicitly excluded "Easter truces" as humanitarian pauses; this version explicitly includes truces and humanitarian pauses (third-party mirrors such as Orrery show both wordings). So a 1–3 day, nationwide, mutually agreed pause would likely count. This matters because two such truces already happened in 2026 (see §2).
  - **"Mutually agreed" vs "dueling unilateral" declarations.** In May 2026, Russia (May 8–9) and Ukraine (May 5–6) first announced separate unilateral truces. Those would not count. The later Trump-announced May 9–11 pause, which both sides accepted, probably would.
  - **Expanding a local truce.** If a local truce like Oleshky is widened, it would only count once it covers the whole front.
- **Who decides:** The market operator, based on official Russian and Ukrainian government statements plus consensus reporting.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Mutually agreed general truces during the full-scale war (Feb 2022 to Nov 2026, about 57 months)**
- **Likely qualifying, 2 events:**
  - The Orthodox Easter truce of about 32 hours, Apr 11–13, 2026. Al Jazeera reported that "both sides agreed," following a Ukrainian proposal passed on by US mediators (Al Jazeera, Apr 11, 2026; Euronews, Apr 9, 2026).
  - The Victory Day pause of May 9–11, 2026, announced by Trump together with a 1,000-for-1,000 prisoner swap (Washington Post, May 8, 2026).
- **Did not qualify:**
  - The 2025 Easter and May truces, which Russia declared unilaterally (Al Jazeera, Apr 19, 2025).
  - The March 2025 energy moratorium, which was limited to one category of targets.
  - The Oleshky local truce, Oct 27–Nov 1, 2026 (Kyiv Independent; Al Jazeera, Oct 30, 2026).
- **Rough rate:** about 2 events in 57 months, or about 3.5% per month. Both came in 2026, so the recent rate is higher: about 2 in 10.5 months, roughly 19% per month.
- **Timing pattern:** Both were tied to holidays (Easter, Victory Day). There is no comparable Orthodox or Russian state holiday between Nov 11 and Nov 30; Unity Day, Nov 4, has already passed. Taking the 2026 rate at face value gives roughly 10–12% for a 19-day window. Adjusting for the missing holiday hook lowers that to low single digits.

**B. Episodes where the US said a deal was "very close" (Trump era, 2025–26)**
- **The episodes:**
  - April 2025: "very close" after Witkoff met Putin.
  - August 2025: the Alaska summit.
  - December 2025: "95% settled" and "closer than ever" (Axios, Dec 28, 2025; Moscow Times, Dec 16, 2025).
  - January–February 2026: the Abu Dhabi and Geneva rounds.
  - September–October 2026: the Witkoff/Kushner–Putin meetings.
- **Outcome:** None of these 5–6 episodes produced a general ceasefire within about a month, apart from the holiday truces. Base rate is about 0 of 6, so perhaps under 10% after smoothing.

**C. Interstate wars where talks resume after a long pause**
- Comprehensive ceasefires rarely follow within weeks of talks restarting when the territorial issue is unresolved. The Korean armistice took about 2 years of talks. The Abu Dhabi talks on Nov 10 resumed after a 9-month pause, and negotiators reportedly do not expect to settle the territorial dispute in this round (PBS/NBC, Nov 10, 2026).

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES**
1. **A short, US-brokered general truce** (most plausible YES path). Trump or Melania Trump announces a multi-day nationwide pause, perhaps tied to a prisoner swap or as a "goodwill" step from Abu Dhabi, and both capitals confirm it. There is precedent from May 9–11, 2026. This depends on Moscow deciding a short truce costs little. That cuts against its Nov 9 refusal of "short-term ceasefires," and there is no holiday to justify one.
2. **A framework deal with a dated ceasefire.** Abu Dhabi and envoy shuttles produce a security-guarantee and territorial package, and both sides sign on with a ceasefire date. This requires settling the Donbas: either Ukraine gives up the rest of Donetsk, or Russia drops that demand. Negotiators themselves don't expect that soon. Very unlikely within 19 days.
3. **A local truce that becomes general.** The Oleshky model is extended to the whole front. Russia's explicit refusal to link Oleshky to a broader ceasefire makes this unlikely.

**Paths to NO** (the default)
- Talks go on without a breakthrough, and Russia keeps its "no freeze / no short ceasefire" line.
- Any agreements are partial: energy strikes, prisoner swaps, humanitarian corridors, local pauses. These do not qualify.
- Dueling unilateral declarations, as in early May 2026, do not count.

**What the outcome depends on:** the Kremlin's view of the battlefield (Russia lost ground in September–October), US leverage (sanctions, or the trade incentives Trump mentioned), Ukraine's willingness to accept terms on Donetsk, and Trump's appetite for a headline "win" (for example, a pause around Thanksgiving).

## 5. Signposts

**Would raise the probability (big moves):**
- Day-two or post-Abu Dhabi statements mentioning a "truce," "pause" or "ceasefire" with dates.
- The Kremlin (Peskov or Ushakov) softening its "no short-term ceasefire" line.
- A Trump or White House announcement of a pause tied to a prisoner exchange.
- Witkoff and Kushner going to Moscow and then Kyiv within days.
- A Trump–Putin call or summit announced for November.
- Zelensky publicly signalling flexibility on Donetsk.

**Would raise the probability (smaller moves):**
- A new Melania Trump humanitarian initiative that is broader than one town.
- Agreement on the "security guarantees" text.

**Would lower the probability:**
- Russian statements after Abu Dhabi that repeat the Donbas withdrawal demand as a precondition.
- No date set for the next round.
- A large missile or drone campaign against energy infrastructure as winter starts.
- New US sanctions, or Trump blaming Moscow (a sign talks have stalled).
- Ushakov again linking the process to "battlefield conditions."
