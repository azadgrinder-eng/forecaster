# Research brief: Will Kelly Loeffler leave as SBA Administrator before 2027?

*Prepared 2026-11-20. Many full pages could not be opened (the proxy refused them), so several facts below come from search-result summaries. Those are flagged.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Loeffler must actually stop holding the SBA Administrator post, with a departure date on or before Dec 31, 2026. The market closes 2027-01-01 14:59 UTC.
- **Qualifying ways out:** resignation, retirement, removal or firing, or any other way she ceases to occupy the role.
- **Announcement is not enough:** a resignation or firing announced in December that takes effect in January 2027 or later should resolve NO, because the rules require "an actual departure date by vacating the role within the time period". This matters a lot. Post-midterm Cabinet exits are often announced in Nov–Dec but take effect in January or later (see section 2).
- **Things that do not count:** temporary leaves, suspensions and recusals, unless she formally and permanently stops holding the position.
- **Death in office:** contracts may settle at the "last fair price," at the Exchange's discretion. It is not a clean YES.
- **Possible ambiguities:**
  - **Moving to another post:** if Trump nominated her to a different job, she would normally stay SBA Administrator until she is confirmed and sworn in elsewhere. Only that swearing-in would count as vacating. A nomination alone would not.
  - **Who decides:** the Exchange, at its discretion, on reported facts.
  - **The post has no fixed term:** the SBA Administrator serves at the President's pleasure, so the "expiration of term" route does not apply.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Trump second-term Cabinet-level turnover.** Through Nov 12, 2026, I found about four Cabinet-level departures, all in 2026 (Trump Cabinet tracker / search summaries, accessed Nov 2026):
- Homeland Security: Noem removed around March 5, 2026 (one list says March 31). Replaced by Markwayne Mullin.
- Attorney General: Bondi fired April 2, 2026. Replaced by Todd Blanche, confirmed August 8, 2026.
- Labor: Chavez-DeRemer resigned April 20, 2026. Replaced by Keith Sonderling.
- Director of National Intelligence: Gabbard announced May 22, 2026, effective June 30. Replaced by Jay Clayton.

Separately, White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles resigned around Nov 6, 2026, and Stephen Miller started Nov 9.

That is roughly 4–5 exits across about 25 Cabinet-level seats over about 21 months. Treating Wiles as a fifth exit gives about 5 / (25 × 21) ≈ 0.95% per seat per month, or about 0.8% excluding her. The window left is about 1.35 months, so a naive per-seat estimate is about 1–1.3%.

- That gives about 3 effective departures in about 90–95 seat-instances, roughly 3% per seat over the full post-midterm-to-year-end window.
- Even in Trump's 2018 purge year, it was about 2 of 24 (about 8%).
- Since Nov 20 is already partway through this window, the remaining hazard is lower.
- These departures cluster in the high-profile posts (Defense, Justice/Attorney General, Interior, Chief of Staff), not the SBA.

**C. SBA Administrators' tenures.** Again from historical knowledge, not this session's searches:
- Linda McMahon (Trump 1): about 26 months. She resigned April 2019 to work for Trump's re-election.
- Karen Mills: about 4.5 years, with her departure announced months ahead.
- Steven Preston: about 2 years, then moved to HUD in mid-2008.
- Hector Barreto: about 5 years.

Mid-term SBA exits happen, but they are usually announced weeks ahead. Loeffler is about 21 months in; she was confirmed Feb 19–20, 2025 by a 52–46 vote (Congress.gov PN11-18). That is near McMahon's exit point, but nothing points to an imminent move.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (all need an actual exit by Dec 31):**
1. **Fired in the post-midterm shakeup.** This depends on Trump or Miller targeting her. There is no reporting of that, and she is a loyal, on-message official. Trump's 2026 firings have hit people with scandals or bad press (Bondi and the Epstein files, Noem, Chavez-DeRemer's conduct probe). Loeffler has nothing comparable at that level.
2. **Moved to a bigger job.** For example, filling a vacancy created by firing Hegseth or someone else, or a White House role. A White House staff role needs no Senate confirmation and could be immediate. A Senate-confirmed post would need lame-duck confirmation by about mid-to-late December, which is tight. Nothing found suggests she is being floated.
3. **Voluntary resignation effective by year end** (personal or business reasons, a political run). No signals found.
4. **Death or incapacity.** This settles at "fair price," not YES, and is negligible anyway.

**Paths to NO:**
- The status quo, by default. She is visibly active as of Nov 17–18.
- A departure announced in December but effective in January 2027 or later is also NO.

## 5. Signposts

**Would raise the probability a lot:**
- Reporting (Axios, Politico, CBS, NBC) that Loeffler is among Trump's "few changes," or is being considered for Defense, Homeland Security, Chief of Staff, Commerce or another post.
- A White House staff appointment for her, which could take effect immediately.
- A lame-duck nomination of her to another post, or of a new SBA Administrator.
- Reports of clashes with Miller, or a sudden scandal (for example an inspector-general finding).
- Missed public appearances.

**Would lower the probability:**
- Continued routine SBA events through December.
- Trump or the White House publicly saying the shakeup is complete.
- The Senate adjourning for the year with no SBA-related nomination.
- Any departure news dated after mid-December, which would probably be effective in 2027.
