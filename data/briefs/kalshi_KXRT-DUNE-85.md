# Research Brief: Dune: Part Three Rotten Tomatoes score, "Above 85"

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** The Tomatometer must be strictly above 85 when checked at 10:00 AM ET on the Monday after wide release. RT displays whole numbers, so in practice the page needs to show 86% or higher. A score of exactly 85% resolves NO.
- **Which date:** The US wide release is Friday, December 18, 2026. Avengers: Doomsday opens the same day, and Warner Bros. has said it will not move the date (Hollywood Reporter, Nov 2026; Flickering Myth / Soapcentral). The check therefore falls on **Monday, December 21, 2026 at 10:00 AM ET**, which matches the market close of 15:00 UTC.
- **Early screenings:** IMAX 70mm "Insider" screenings start Monday, Dec 14 in about 34 North American theaters. Early showings in all IMAX theaters follow on Dec 15, with Thursday showings and other premium formats on Dec 17. UK previews begin Dec 15 (Flickering Myth; IMAX.com; CTV News; Hollywood Reporter). These are previews, not the wide release, so the Dec 21 date should hold. There is a small chance of a dispute if someone argues the Dec 15 all-IMAX shows count as "wide." That reading would move the check to Monday, Dec 21 anyway, because Dec 14 and Dec 15 fall in the same week. The edge case is effectively moot.
- **Which score:** The question is about the critics' Tomatometer (all critics), not the Popcornmeter or the Top Critics score. Who decides: the market operator (Kalshi), reading the live RT page.
- **Review count at check time:** If reviews are released on Dec 9 as reported, the film will have about 12 days of reviews by Dec 21. That probably means 300 to 400 or more reviews, so the score should be fairly stable by then. Small 1–2 point moves between Dec 9 and Dec 21 are still common.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

RT figures below come from the cited pages or, where marked, from my own memory. Memory figures are approximate to about ±2 points.

**A. Denis Villeneuve's feature films since *Incendies*.**
Incendies about 93 (memory), Prisoners 81, Enemy 73, Sicario 92, Arrival 94, Blade Runner 2049 88, Dune (2021) 83, Dune: Part Two about 92–93. The last figure is my memory of the final score; it opened at 97% with about 100 reviews and was 95% at about 200 reviews (Beebom; RT Villeneuve page; Yahoo/GamesRadar, Feb 2024).
- **5 of 8 are above 85 (about 63%).**
- **Since 2015: 4 of 5 (Sicario, Arrival, BR2049, Dune 2). Only Dune (2021), at 83, misses.**
- Notably, the first Dune film scored below the threshold, and this is the franchise entry most comparable to Part Three.

**B. Third films whose second film scored about 90% or higher (from memory, approximate).**
- Above 85: Return of the King (94), The Dark Knight Rises (87), Toy Story 3 (98), War for the Planet of the Apes (94), Before Midnight (98), Captain America: Civil War (90).
- 85 or below: Spider-Man 3 (63), The Godfather Part III (67), Return of the Jedi (83), Mad Max Beyond Thunderdome (80), Mockingjay Part 1 (70).
- **Overall: about 6 of 11 (55%).**
- **Same auteur returning: about 4 of 7.**
- Threequels tend to fall from the previous film, typically by 5 to 30 points (SlashFilm on Avatar: Fire and Ash at about 70% vs 76/81 for the earlier films; Looper on trilogy finales).
- Dune 2 sits at about 92, so even a modest drop of 7 or more points lands at 85 or below.

**C. High-profile prestige or event blockbusters, 2023–2025 (from memory).**
- Above 85: Oppenheimer 93, Dune: Part Two 92, Barbie 88, Furiosa 90.
- 85 or below: Gladiator II about 71, Joker: Folie à Deux about 32, Superman (2025) about 83, F1 about 83, Mission: Impossible – The Final Reckoning about 80, Avatar: Fire and Ash about 66–70.
- **Overall: about 4 of 10 (40%).**
- Several of the misses landed in the 80–84 range, so the 85 threshold sits close to the middle of where well-made blockbusters end up.

**Score drift.** Opening Tomatometers usually drift down a few points as more reviews arrive. Dune 2 went from 97% (about 100 reviews) to 95% (about 200) to about 92–93% (final). An opening score of 88–90% on Dec 9 could plausibly sit at 85–87% by Dec 21.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 11, 2026 — embargo dates.** World of Reel reports a critics' screening around Dec 1, social reactions allowed Dec 2 at 12:30 AM ET, and full reviews on **Dec 9**, more than a week before release. The outlet reads the early embargo as a sign of studio confidence. One outlet (Comicbasics) says social reactions lift Dec 1. Another report says reviews won't come until the "mid-December premiere," which conflicts. I could not find a primary source to confirm the dates.
- **Nov 3, 2026 — runtime.** The Irish film classification office lists 151 minutes, shorter than Dune 2 (166) and Dune (155) (World of Reel; Dark Horizons). An earlier 2h20m figure from IMAX and Marcus Theatres may have been a placeholder.
- **Oct–Nov 2026 — record presales.** London's BFI IMAX sold 33,000 tickets in 24 hours, beating The Odyssey's record. US ticket sites crashed when early IMAX 70mm tickets went on sale (Hollywood Reporter; NBC News). Presales say more about fan enthusiasm than critic reception.
- **About early October 2026 — first screenings.** Variety reported the first small-group screenings and called early word "very positive." Clayton Davis projects a major Oscar run, with about 4 Golden Globe nominations. Jeff Sneider framed Best Picture as possibly coming down to Dune 3 versus Nolan's The Odyssey (Comicbasics, Netflix Junkie, Fiction Horizon, ixbt.games Oct 4, all relaying Variety; I could not read the Variety original).
- **Dissenting view.** Skip the Critics gives the film a pre-release "Hype Score" of 68/100 and calls the buzz "deeply split." Praise centers on the visuals, while reactions to the cast are negative or indifferent. That rating is based on footage, not the finished film.
- **Apr 2026, CinemaCon.** The first 7 minutes drew rave reactions, with comparisons to the Omaha Beach landing in Saving Private Ryan (World of Reel, Apr 15; TheWrap; Dexerto). Villeneuve called the film a "redemption story" and a "broken love story," which suggests possible changes from the ending of Dune Messiah (Inverse).
- **Production changes.** Linus Sandgren replaces Greig Fraser as cinematographer, and this is the first film in the series shot on film. The source novel, Dune Messiah, is more interior and less spectacle-driven than the first Dune book. Its story is widely considered harder to adapt.
- **Scheduled before resolution:**
  - Dec 1–2: critics' screenings and social-media reactions
  - **Dec 9: review embargo lifts and the first Tomatometer appears**
  - Dec 14–17: IMAX and premium previews
  - Dec 18: wide release
  - **Dec 21, 10 AM ET: resolution check**

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (86% or higher):**
- Villeneuve keeps his post-2015 hit rate. Critics treat the film as a fitting finale, the way Return of the King or War for the Planet of the Apes were received, and the score opens at 90 or above with enough cushion to absorb drift.
- The early embargo and strong word from trade-press insiders reflect real quality. Oscar-campaign framing tends to go with high critic scores.
- The tighter 151-minute runtime answers the "bloated" complaint, and the craft (IMAX, sound, battle scenes) wins over critics who score on spectacle.

**Paths to NO (85% or lower):**
- The usual threequel decline. Messiah's anti-hero, interior story and any changes Villeneuve made split critics, giving something like Dune (2021)'s 83, or a Return of the Jedi style 80–84.
- Pacing problems from compressing the story into 151 minutes. Some critics found Dune (2021) "half a story," and a short finale could be called rushed.
- Weak supporting performances, which the "split" footage reactions hint at, plus higher expectations after Dune 2 lead to "good, not great" reviews of about 3/5. Those count as positive on RT but lower the share of fresh reviews.
- Drift: an opening score of 87–89 drifts to 84–86 by Dec 21.

**Key dependency:** Nearly all of the uncertainty resolves on Dec 9. After that the question mostly comes down to modeling 12 days of drift.

## 5. Signposts

- **Dec 2 social reactions.** Near-universal "masterpiece" or "best of the trilogy" posts point up. Recurring "divisive," "uneven," or "rushed" wording points down. Social reactions skew positive, so even mild hedging is a meaningful negative signal.
- **Opening Tomatometer on Dec 9** (the strongest signal):
  - 93 or higher with 100+ reviews: YES very likely
  - 88–92: YES likely, but watch the drift
  - 85–87: toss-up leaning NO because of drift
  - Below 85: NO very likely
- **Metacritic opening score.** Dune 2 was at about 79–80 with a 92–97% Tomatometer. A Metacritic score of 75 or more is consistent with a Tomatometer above 85. A score of 70 or below usually comes with a Tomatometer in the low 80s.
- **Top Critics share and rotten reviews from major outlets** (NYT, New Yorker, Guardian) in the first 48 hours.
- **Embargo changes.** A reported move of the review date to release day would be a bearish sign. A festival-style premiere with raves would be bullish.
- **Score direction during Dec 9–18.** A steady decline signals a NO risk.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not open Variety's original screening report or World of Reel's embargo article directly; page fetches were blocked. Embargo dates are second-hand, and sources conflict on whether social reactions lift Dec 1 or Dec 2.
- I could not confirm Dune: Part Two's current final Tomatometer in this session. The 92–93% figure is from memory, and the confirmed early snapshots are 97% and 95%.
- Reference-class scores marked "from memory" are approximate.
- I don't know whether the film departs from the Dune Messiah story, or how critics will react if it does.
- The quality of the early buzz is unclear. "Very positive" small-group screenings may have been friendly audiences. The "split" footage reactions come from a low-authority aggregator.
- There is some chance the review embargo moves.
