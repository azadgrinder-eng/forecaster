# Research brief: Will the PBoC cut the 7-day reverse repo rate by 31 Dec 2026?

WebFetch was blocked in this environment, so every fact below comes from search-result snippets. I read none of the full articles. Treat the details as likely but not fully verified.

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts.** The market resolves on the first change to the PBoC's 7-day reverse repo rate between market creation and 31 Dec 2026, 23:59 CST (15:59 UTC). It resolves "decrease" if the rate moves below the most recent effective 7-day rate. That rate was 1.40% at least through 30 Oct 2026. An official announcement is enough even if the change takes effect later. If nothing changes, it resolves "No Change".
- **Who decides.** The primary source is PBoC open market operations announcements. A consensus of credible reporting can also be used.
- **The 30 Oct 2026 framework change is the main ambiguity.** On 30 Oct 2026 the PBoC made the **overnight** reverse repo its main policy rate, at 1.25%. It runs daily from 2 Nov at a fixed rate, with quantities set by bidding. The 7-day tool was kept but changed in two ways (Bloomberg, 30 Oct 2026; Caixin, 2 Nov 2026; Xinhua, 2 Nov 2026):
  - It is now a fixed-quantity, rate-bid auction with **multiple winning prices**, so there is no longer one fixed rate.
  - It is used mainly around tax periods and month-ends.

  This raises three edge cases:
  - **(a) Does the switch itself count as a cut?** Some coverage frames it that way. Sina's headline says the policy rate fell "from the 7-day's 1.4% to the overnight's 1.25%", and The Star/NST ran "China lowers funding costs with shift to overnight policy rate" (2 Nov 2026). If the market was created before 30 Oct and has not resolved, the resolver apparently has not treated the switch as a decrease, at least not yet. I could not confirm the market's creation date or whether anyone has disputed it.
  - **(b) Variable 7-day rates.** Post-switch 7-day operations, such as CNY 300bn on 13 Nov 2026, were reported without a single rate (CLS/Sina, 13 Nov 2026). If the PBoC publishes winning bid rates below 1.40%, someone could argue that is a "decrease". If it publishes none, there may be no 7-day "rate" left to change, which points toward "No Change".
  - **(c) A cut to the overnight rate.** If the PBoC cuts the overnight rate and the media report it as a "policy rate cut", it is unclear whether that counts, because the question names the 7-day rate. The PBoC said other tools are priced off the overnight rate with existing spreads unchanged. That suggests an overnight cut could carry the 7-day pricing down with it, but this is inference.
- **Precedent.** When the MLF moved to multiple-price auctions in March 2025, the PBoC stopped announcing a single MLF rate. The 7-day tool may follow the same pattern.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Frequency of 7-day rate cuts since 2020.** Compiled from memory of PBoC actions and consistent with search results. There were about 9 cuts in roughly 82 months (Jan 2020 to Oct 2026):

| Date | Change |
|---|---|
| Feb 2020 | 2.50 → 2.40 |
| Mar 2020 | 2.40 → 2.20 |
| Jan 2022 | 2.20 → 2.10 |
| Aug 2022 | 2.10 → 2.00 |
| Jun 2023 | 2.00 → 1.90 |
| Aug 2023 | 1.90 → 1.80 |
| Jul 2024 | 1.80 → 1.70 |
| Sep 2024 | 1.70 → 1.50 |
| May 2025 | 1.50 → 1.40 |

- That is about 0.11 cuts per month. Over a 1.5-month window that gives a naive chance of about 15% of at least one cut.
- No hikes since March 2018.

**B. Seasonality.** None of those 9 cuts fell between 17 Nov and 31 Dec. The PBoC has usually eased around Jan–Mar, May–Sep, or right after key Politburo signals. The year-end window has 0 cuts in about 6 years.

**C. Long holds.** The 7-day rate has been unchanged since May 2025, about 18 months. The LPR was unchanged for 17 straight months through 20 Oct 2026 (NST, Oct 2026; Morningstar/Dow Jones, 19 Oct 2026).
- Earlier long holds (Apr 2020–Dec 2021, about 20 months; Sep 2022–Jun 2023, about 9 months) ended with cuts in January or June, not December.
- A major framework change was made two weeks ago. Central banks rarely change rates within weeks of one, because the new anchor needs time to settle.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (decrease):**
1. **A true policy cut, about 5–10%.** The PBoC cuts the overnight rate, for example 1.25% → 1.15%, and states that 7-day pricing moves with it, or keeps a fixed 7-day reference rate and lowers it.
   - What it would take: a sharp growth shock, equity or property stress, or a strongly dovish December Politburo/CEWC followed by quick action.
   - Working against it: the framework change is two weeks old, PPI is at a 4-year high, and December cuts have not happened historically.
2. **A resolution interpretation.** The resolver treats one of these as a decrease:
   - the 30 Oct anchor switch (only relevant if the market was created before 30 Oct);
   - published multiple-price 7-day winning rates below 1.40%;
   - media describing an overnight-rate cut as a "policy rate cut".

**Paths to NO / "No Change":**
- The PBoC stays on hold, as the 18-month pause, strong PPI, Citi's no-cut call and the June consensus for a 2026 hold all suggest.
- 7-day auctions continue with no published single rate, or with rates around 1.40%.
- The easing that does happen goes through RRR cuts, relending, PSL or LPR reform. None of these count.

**Increase:** very unlikely given CPI of 0.7% and core CPI of 0.8%.

## 5. Signposts

- **Toward YES:**
  - PBoC announcements that give a 7-day winning rate below 1.40%, or a weighted average below it.
  - Politburo or CEWC wording such as "cut interest rates" or "lower policy rates" in a timely way.
  - A November LPR cut, which would signal transmission of the new 1.25% anchor.
  - Weak November credit or activity data.
  - A sharp drop in equities.
  - Pan signalling a cut.
- **Toward NO:**
  - 7-day auction results reported at 1.40%, or with no rate disclosed.
  - The LPR held on 20 Nov and 21 Dec.
  - The CEWC repeating "moderately loose" without a call for rate cuts.
  - PPI staying above 5%.
- **On the rules:** any resolver clarification or dispute about the 30 Oct switch or about multiple-price 7-day rates.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **Market creation date.** It determines whether the 30 Oct switch falls inside the window. I could not confirm it.
- **Disclosure of 7-day rates.** I could not establish whether the PBoC publishes winning or weighted rates for the multiple-price 7-day operations after 2 Nov, or at what levels.
- **Year mix-ups in search results.** Some returned items, such as MNI summaries showing 1.40% on "Nov 24/27", are from 2025, not 2026.
- **Missing data.** I found no October 2026 credit data, no Q3 2026 GDP figure, and no current economist consensus for a December cut.
- **The exact link between the 7-day tool and the overnight rate.** Is the 7-day still referenced to 1.40% as a fixed spread, or is it fully auction-determined? If an overnight cut happens, this decides whether it counts.
