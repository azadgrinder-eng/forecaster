### 1. Resolution mechanics
- **YES** only if Lens is ahead after 90 minutes plus stoppage time. A draw resolves YES on the separate "Tie" market and NO here. An Angers win is NO. Extra time and penalties don't apply to a league match anyway.
- **Kickoff:** Saturday 28 Nov 2026, 17:15 Paris time (16:15 UTC), at Stade Raymond Kopa in Angers (t-online/Sky listings, Sofascore). The close time, 2026-11-30 16:15 UTC, is exactly 48 hours after kickoff. That lines up with the rule that if the match is postponed by more than 48 hours, the market settles at a "fair price".
- **Edge cases:** A postponement, for example from weather or a security order, would trigger the fair-price settlement. An abandoned match decided later by the league's disciplinary body (LFP) is not clearly covered. Kalshi decides how the market resolves. I found no current risk of postponement.
- **Matchday number:** Sources disagree. Gidstats calls it Round 12, which fits Matchday 11 being played on 21–22 Nov (Lens–Toulouse, Paris FC–Angers). WorldFootball labels Paris FC–Angers as Matchday 12. This makes no difference to resolution.

### 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **All Ligue 1 away wins.** Over the last decade, roughly 29–32% of Ligue 1 matches ended in an away win, 43–46% in a home win and 24–26% in a draw. This is from my general knowledge of league-wide splits; I did not look it up this session. Starting point: about 30%.
2. **A mid-table away side at a bottom-three home side.** When the visitors are around 9th–12th and the hosts 16th–18th, away wins typically rise to about 35–42% and home wins fall to about 33–38%. This is an approximation from general knowledge of league tables and team-rating models, not a computed sample. Here Lens are 11th with 14 points and Angers 17th with 8 points.
3. **Recent head-to-heads.** Lens won both meetings in 2025-26: 2-1 at Angers on 30 Nov 2025 and 5-1 in March 2026 (TheScore matchup history). Lens were a top side that season and qualified for the Champions League. Both squads and coaches have changed since, so these results tell us little about this match.
4. **Modifiers:**
   - New-manager effect: published studies find the bounce is small, a few points of win probability at most, and partly just a recovery from bad luck.
   - Champions League matches midweek: teams playing away on Tuesday and again on Saturday usually see a small dip of perhaps 2–4 percentage points, depending on how much the coach rotates.

### 4. Drivers and scenarios
- **Paths to YES (Lens win):**
  - Lens have a clearly stronger squad on paper. They were a top-two side in 2025-26 and are a Champions League team, while Angers are a relegation-zone side.
  - Angers have the worst attack in the league (0.9 goals per game, none in their last three) and a leaky defence (1.9 conceded per game).
  - The new coach may lift performances; there were signs of that against Toulouse.
  - A Lens win here would look a lot like the 2-1 win at Angers in Nov 2025.
- **Paths to NO (draw or Angers win):**
  - Lens' morale is fragile: three coaches this season, the 1-9 loss and only one league win in their recent run.
  - They come in tired, three days after Leipzig. Digard might rotate players, or might put the league first.
  - Injuries thin their defence (Nawrocki, possibly Gradit) and their attack (Édouard).
  - Angers' low-scoring games make a draw more likely. Low-quality matches between struggling teams end level more often than average, around 27–30%.
  - Angers may be strengthened by Lefort's return.
