# Research Brief: Will Ukraine give up territory to Russia under a peace deal or other official arrangement by Dec 31, 2026?

*Prepared Nov 16, 2026. About 45 days remain before the market closes. I could not open any pages directly because the fetch tool was blocked, so every fact below comes from search-result summaries of the cited articles. Any figure that matters should be checked before relying on it.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** By the close (2026-12-31 05:02 UTC), Ukraine gives up control of *any* territory to Russia through a peace agreement or other official arrangement. Formal or legal recognition is not required. Giving territory up "for now" as part of a deal is enough.
- **What counts as NO:** Ukraine keeps refusing to give up territory in any peace effort through the close.
- **Who decides:** The market creator. No named authority or source is given, so the outcome depends on the creator's reading.
- **Edge cases and ambiguities:**
  1. **Freeze along the current line.** Ukraine would give up nothing it now holds, so a strict reading says NO. The "give it up for now" wording could tempt a lenient resolver toward YES, because Ukraine would be accepting that the occupied areas stay under Russian control. This is the biggest ambiguity. Zelensky has said Ukrainians would accept a freeze (Axios, Feb 17, 2026), so a freeze is the most likely kind of deal.
  2. **Donbas "free economic zone" or demilitarized zone.** Under the US proposal, Ukraine would withdraw from the part of Donbas it holds, and the area would become a demilitarized or economic zone rather than a formal Russian takeover. Russia calls the same idea a "demilitarized zone" (Yahoo/AFP, Dec 2025). Whether that counts as giving control "to Russia" is unclear. It probably leans YES if Russian police or administration move in.
  3. **Signed but not yet carried out.** Zelensky says any withdrawal from Donbas needs a national referendum first (Belga, Dec 2025; Pravda, Jan 2026). A framework could be signed before Dec 31 with the actual handover planned for 2027. The rules suggest signing an "official arrangement" might be enough, but this is not explicit.
  4. **Small swaps.** Examples include mutual pullbacks or the Enerhodar demilitarized-zone idea (Belga, Dec 2025). Any transfer of "any territory" technically qualifies.
  5. **Battlefield losses don't count.** Territory lost in fighting (for example, Kostiantynivka) is not a cession under any arrangement.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Negotiation rounds in this war that produced a territorial agreement: 0 of about 5 episodes.**

| Abu Dhabi, Nov 10–11, 2026 | Prisoner swap only (CBS, Nov 2026) |

- The US-set June 2026 deadline passed with no deal (PBS/NPR, Feb 7–8, 2026).
- Roughly 22 months of Trump-era mediation have produced no territorial deal. Over that time, the share of any 45-day window that contained a signed territorial agreement is 0%.

**B. Wars that ended with the defending side giving up land it still held.** These cases happen mainly after a battlefield collapse or a decisive defeat:
- Armenia, Nov 2020: handed over Agdam, Kalbajar and Lachin after a military collapse.
- Finland, 1940: Moscow Peace, gave up unconquered Karelia including Vyborg.
- Serbia, 1999: withdrew from Kosovo under the Kumanovo agreement.

Stalemates usually end in a freeze with no transfer:
- Korea, 1953
- Minsk I and II, 2014–15
- Georgia, 2008
- Moldova, 1992

My rough count is that in about 3 of 7 of these cases, land the defender still held was handed over, and those 3 all followed a collapse. Ukraine's front is described as stabilized around Sloviansk (Euromaidan Press, Nov 13, 2026), which fits the stalemate pattern.

**C. How long it takes to go from talks to a signed deal.** Korean armistice talks took about 24 months. The Nov 2020 Karabakh ceasefire came about 44 days into the war, after collapse. Restarted talks with a core territorial deadlock rarely produce a signed settlement within 6–7 weeks. In this war, the "close" moments of Aug 2025, Nov–Dec 2025 and Feb 2026 all failed.

  - A senior US official says Trump will focus on the Iran war and "steer away" from Ukraine.

  - (Al Jazeera, Nov 14; HNGN, Nov 15; Kyiv Post, Nov 2026)

  - He repeated that Ukraine is not ready to give up territory.
  - He would put the question to a national referendum only if offered a full package: US and NATO-confirmed security guarantees, a strong, well-funded army, and money to rebuild. He said "specifics do not yet exist."
  - In Dublin he called firm US guarantees and *not giving up Donbas* non-negotiable.
  - Kushner said the US is arranging direct talks between Europe and Russia.
  - (CNBC/NBC/RTÉ, Nov 14; CBS, Nov 14)
- **Nov 12–13:** Witkoff and Kushner met Zelensky in Dublin. Europe is expected to join the next round. No date or venue has been set (Kyiv Independent; CBC; RFE/RL, Nov 2026).
- **Nov 12:** Witkoff and Kushner met Putin in Moscow.
  - Putin again rejected foreign peacekeepers.
  - The envoys were seeking Putin's approval to bring Europe into the talks.
  - Nothing was reported on any Russian movement on territory.
  - (Time; Euronews; ABC, Nov 12)
- **Nov 10–11:** The Abu Dhabi trilateral talks were the first in about 9 months, after a pause caused by the US focus on its war with Iran, which began in late Feb 2026.
  - The only concrete result was a prisoner-exchange agreement (reportedly 1,000–1,500 each).
  - Russia's negotiator vowed to reclaim "every inch" of the land Moscow claims.
  - Kyiv rejected a US proposal to redraw the border in Donbas.
  - Kushner said postwar security arrangements will not take effect until territory is agreed.
  - Medinsky said a settlement "seemed closer."
  - (Kyiv Post; Dawn; Euronews, Nov 11; CNBC, Nov 11)
- **Nov 13 (front line):** ISW estimates Ukraine still holds about one-fifth of Donetsk oblast, including Sloviansk and Kramatorsk. The Sloviansk front has stabilized after months of Russian attacks (Euromaidan Press, Nov 13). Russia claimed Kostiantynivka in July 2026, which is disputed (Eastern Herald, Jul 4, 2026).
- **Oct 8–16 (polling):** KIIS found 57–62% oppose withdrawing troops from Donbas (KIIS, published Oct 19). Earlier polls showed about 60% backing a freeze along current lines with security guarantees (Kyiv Independent, 2026). One framing experiment found 61% would vote yes on a deal giving up Donbas if it was presented positively (Intellinews).
- **Russia's demands:**
  - Since Dec 2025, the Kremlin has made full withdrawal from Donbas a precondition for a ceasefire.
  - By May 13, 2026, Peskov's wording covered "Russian regions," which would include Zaporizhzhia and Kherson oblasts.
  - (Ukrainska Pravda, Dec 29, 2025; Euromaidan Press and Sputnik, May 13, 2026)
  - I found no sign of Russian softening in November.
- **Legal barriers in Ukraine:**
  - The constitution protects territorial integrity and bars constitutional amendments under martial law.
  - Zelensky says a withdrawal from Donbas needs a referendum, while a freeze would not.
  - About 50 NGOs called a peace referendum unlawful.
  - Opora (an election-monitoring group) says a fair vote needs a sustained ceasefire.
  - No enabling law or scheduled vote was found as of mid-2026.
  - (ThePrint factbox; Kyiv Independent; Opora; Pravda, Jan 22, 2026)
- **Mar 2026:** Zelensky said the US tied security guarantees to Ukraine giving up all of Donbas. US officials disputed this (Reuters via aaj.tv/Kyiv Post, Mar 2026).

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES**

1. **Comprehensive deal including withdrawal from Donbas.** Requirements:
   - Kyiv accepts a NATO, guarantees and reconstruction package.
   - The US and allies firm up guarantees quickly.
   - Russia accepts European involvement.
   - Ukraine signs before Dec 31, with the referendum to follow.
   
   This needs a reversal on positions Zelensky called non-negotiable on Nov 14, inside about 6 weeks. It is the main YES path, but the slowest one.
2. **Interim "official arrangement."** Examples are a ceasefire-plus-DMZ or a free economic zone where Ukraine pulls back from part of Donbas. It could resolve YES under the lenient "for now" wording. It would still need Russia to accept something short of full Donbas, and the Kremlin has refused that so far.
3. **Small swaps or mutual pullbacks** inside a ceasefire, such as the Enerhodar demilitarized zone.
4. **Lenient resolution of a freeze** along the current line by the creator. Even this needs a signed ceasefire, which Russia has refused without a Donbas withdrawal.
5. **Coercion scenario.**
   - Trump and Putin reach an understanding at Shenzhen (Nov 19).
   - The US then squeezes Kyiv by cutting intelligence or weapons, as it did in Mar 2025.
   - Alternatively, a sudden front-line collapse near Sloviansk or Kramatorsk forces a deal like Armenia's in 2020.

**Paths to NO**

1. **Status quo, the default:** talks continue into 2027 with prisoner swaps and no territorial deal.
2. **A freeze is signed but read strictly as no cession.**
3. **A framework is agreed in principle, but signing or carrying it out slips past Dec 31,** pending a referendum or approval from Europe and NATO.
4. **Talks collapse** amid winter strikes on energy infrastructure.

**Key dependencies**
- Whether Russia drops its demand for all of Donbas.
- Whether the US gives concrete guarantees or NATO-linked offers.
- Domestic legitimacy in Ukraine: the referendum question and polls showing about 60% opposed.
- How much bandwidth the US has, given the Iran war.
- The front line.
