# Research Brief: Will Linkin Park have a top-20 Billboard Hot 100 single by the chart dated Dec 26, 2026?

**Summary:** Very unlikely. Linkin Park has not had a Hot 100 top 20 since 2009, nothing new is announced, and about five charts remain.

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** a Linkin Park single must reach No. 1–20 on the Billboard Hot 100, on or before the chart issue dated Dec 26, 2026. The market closes Dec 29, 2026. Billboard's published chart is the authority, and Kalshi decides how the rules apply.
- **Charts left:** As of Nov 15, 2026, the remaining chart issues are dated Nov 21, Nov 28, Dec 5, Dec 12, Dec 19 and Dec 26. Each covers a tracking week (Friday to Thursday) that ends about nine days before the issue date. Rough timing:
  - The Nov 21 issue (tracking about Nov 7–13) is probably already set, or published Nov 17–18.
  - The Dec 26 issue covers tracking through about Dec 11 to 18.
  - So a new song would have to come out by about Dec 11–12 and hit the top 20 in its first week or two. Effectively there are about five charts left in which a song could enter.
- **Edge cases:**
  - The title says "this year," so the realistic reading is a top-20 position on a 2026-dated chart. The rules text says "by the issue for Dec 26, 2026" and gives no start date. A literal reader could argue a past top-20 hit counts. That would be a strange reading, and Kalshi's "this year" series normally means within the calendar year.
  - "Linkin Park has a top 20 single" probably includes a credited feature on someone else's song, but the rules don't say.
  - A re-entry of an old song would probably count if it charted. Hot 100 re-entries face recurrent rules (Billboard rules, not checked this session).

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
**A. Linkin Park's own Hot 100 top-20 history.** These peaks are from background knowledge of Billboard chart history. I could not verify them this session because the Billboard pages were unreachable.
- Years with a top-20 Hot 100 hit:
  - 2002: "In the End," No. 2
  - 2004: "Numb," No. 11, and "Breaking the Habit," No. 20
  - 2005: "Numb/Encore," No. 20
  - 2007: "What I've Done," No. 7
  - 2008: "Shadow of the Day," No. 15
  - 2009: "New Divide," No. 6
- Since 2010 there have been **no top-20 Hot 100 hits in 16 calendar years (0/16)**. That includes their two album-release years 2012 ("Burn It Down," No. 30) and 2017 ("Heavy," No. 45), and the much-hyped 2024 comeback.
- "The Emptiness Machine" (Sept 2024), the comeback lead single, debuted and apparently peaked at **No. 21**. It was the band's best Hot 100 showing in 15 years, but it still missed the top 20 (Billboard "Five Burning Questions," Sept 2024; Wikipedia chart table).
- Later singles did worse:
  - "Heavy Is the Crown" debuted at No. 79 (2024 report).
  - "Two Faced" did not enter the Hot 100 and peaked at No. 7 on Bubbling Under (Wikipedia-derived table).
  - "Up From the Bottom" (2025) peaked at No. 5 on Bubbling Under and No. 15 on Hot Rock & Alternative Songs (Wikipedia, "Up From the Bottom").

**B. Rock bands without a fresh pop crossover making the top 20 in a given year.** In the streaming era, bands debut in the Hot 100 top 20 almost only with lead singles from huge album launches, and even then rarely. Linkin Park's best such moment, a global-news comeback with a new singer, landed at No. 21. My rough estimate is well under 5% for any given six-week window with no announced release.

**C. December specifically.** In recent years, from about late November into the chart dated around Christmas, holiday catalog songs (Mariah Carey, Brenda Lee, Bobby Helms, Wham! and others) fill much of the top 20. That raises the bar for any new non-holiday song in exactly the window that remains. This is a general pattern from recent Decembers, not checked for 2026.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES (all low probability):**
1. **A surprise lead single from the next album, released by about Dec 11.** It would need a launch at least as strong as "The Emptiness Machine" (No. 21) plus December holiday competition. That needs a surprise drop and a bigger first week than their best result in 15 years.
2. **A high-profile collaboration** with a pop, rap or country star whose name drives Hot 100 points. Nothing like this is reported.
3. **A viral or sync-driven catalog surge** (TikTok, a film, or a news event). Older songs usually land on the Recurrent charts or low on the Hot 100, not in the top 20.

**Paths to NO:** the default. No release, or a release that charts in the 21–100 range or on Bubbling Under. Holiday songs crowd out the top 20.

## 5. Signposts
- **Strong move toward YES:** an official announcement of a new single or album for late Nov or early Dec, with first-day Spotify US global numbers matching or beating "The Emptiness Machine" (about 2–3M+ US daily streams), or a feature with a top pop act.
- **Moderate move toward YES:** a song inside the Hot 100's 21–40 range with rising airplay and streams. In December that is still unlikely to break the top 20.
- **Move toward NO:** every week passing without an announcement. After about Dec 12, YES is effectively impossible.
