# Research Brief: Will the Bank of Canada hold its policy rate at the December 9, 2026 meeting?

*Prepared 2026-11-13. My page fetches were blocked by the proxy, so all facts come from search-result summaries of the cited articles. Treat exact figures as needing a check wherever this brief says so.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **YES** if the Bank of Canada leaves its target for the overnight rate unchanged at its scheduled December 9, 2026 announcement. The rate has been 2.50% since October 28, 2026 (Bank of Canada press release, 2026-10-28). With the rate unchanged, the Bank Rate stays at 2.75% and the deposit rate at 2.45%.
- **NO** if the Bank raises the rate (the main alternative, most likely to 2.75%) or cuts it (very unlikely given current guidance).
- **Who decides:** the BoC Governing Council. The official press release is the source of truth.
- **Timing:** The BoC normally announces at 9:45 a.m. ET, which is 14:45 UTC. The market closes at 14:29 UTC, just before the announcement, so the close should not cut off the decision. The December meeting is the last scheduled decision of 2026. The next Monetary Policy Report (MPR) is due January 27, 2027 (MPR, 2026-10-28).
- **Edge cases:**
  - An unscheduled move between meetings, followed by no change on December 9. The BoC has made unscheduled moves only in crises (2001, 2008, March 2020).
  - A postponed meeting.
  - Wording: if the Bank changed only the deposit or Bank Rate spread while keeping the target unchanged, that should still count as "maintain."

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. The decision after the first hike of a BoC tightening cycle (fixed-announcement-date era, since December 2000).** I built this from my own knowledge of BoC history; please verify.
- 2004: hiked September 8, hiked again October 19, then held from December.
- 2005 restart: hiked September 7, then October 18.
- 2010: hiked June 1, then July 20 and September 8.
- 2017: hiked July 12, then September 6.
- 2022: hiked March 2, then 50 bp on April 13.
- **Result:** in 5 of 5 cases the next meeting brought another hike, and none were holds. A Canadian Press analysis said holding after a single hike would be "atypical" (Yahoo/Canadian Press, late October 2026).
- **Caveat:** every one of those cycles was demand-driven, with output gaps closing or growth strong. The current cycle is a supply shock from oil, with growth stalling and unemployment rising.

**B. BoC responses to oil-driven inflation while the economy was soft (supply-shock "look-through").**
- 2008: headline CPI rose with oil above $140, yet the BoC held at 3.00% in June, July and September 2008.
- 2011: CPI was around 3% or higher for much of the year, and the BoC held at 1.00% throughout.
- **Result:** 0 hikes in about 8 to 10 meetings across these episodes. This points toward a hold. The 2026 BoC has already broken from this pattern by hiking in October.
- **Close international parallel:** the ECB hiked on oil in April and July 2011, then reversed course. That episode is often cited as a reason to be cautious.

**C. Unconditional share of holds at BoC meetings.**
- Since December 2000, holds make up roughly 55–60% of the roughly 200 scheduled decisions (my approximate count).
- The recent run was 7 straight holds (December 2025 through September 2026), then one hike in October (CBC, 2026-10-28).
- **Non-MPR meetings:** December is a non-MPR meeting. The BoC moves at non-MPR meetings regularly (for example, September 2017, December 2022 and December 2024), so this is not a strong reason to expect a hold.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (hold at 2.50%):**
1. **Oil keeps easing.** Brent is already down about 20% in November; if it stays around $85–95 and a truce or supply normalization holds, the case for preventing pass-through gets weaker. The October CPI (November 16) could show headline inflation easing.
2. **The real economy keeps weakening.**
   - Q3 GDP on November 30 comes in at or below the roughly 1.3% tracking estimate.
   - The October GDP flash is negative.
   - November jobs on December 4 are weak, with unemployment at 6.8% or higher.
   - This is exactly the trigger Standard Chartered named.
3. **Core inflation stays tame** and the BoC's surveys show little pass-through. The deliberations noted that pass-through "largely hasn't happened."
4. **"Proceed carefully" read as delay.** The BoC could take its "one meeting at a time" stance as a reason to wait for the January MPR (January 27), giving itself a full forecast round before the next move.
5. **A U.S. tariff escalation** hits confidence, and the BoC waits.

**Paths to NO (hike to 2.75%; a cut is very unlikely):**
1. **Oil spikes again** if fighting resumes (Rapidan puts this at 80%) or Houthi and tanker attacks continue. A return to Brent above $100–110 would revive the pass-through concern.
2. **October CPI surprises upward,** especially core measures or broad-based price increases, or inflation expectations in surveys move higher.
3. **The BoC follows its own playbook and history:** every cycle in the fixed-date era saw a back-to-back hike, and the guidance still leans toward hikes ("could be a need to increase further").
4. **Growth holds up better than tracked:** Q3 GDP is revised up or November jobs rebound strongly.
5. **A NO via a cut** would take a sudden financial or economic shock. I'd put it in the low single digits.

## 5. Signposts (what would move the probability most)

| Signpost | Date | Points toward a hold (YES) | Points toward a hike (NO) |
|---|---|---|---|
| October CPI | Nov 16 | Headline below 3%, trim/median near 2% or lower | Headline at or above 3.3%, core rising, broad-based increases |
| Brent / Iran war | Ongoing | Sustained below $90, ceasefire or deal | Renewed strikes, Brent above $105 |
| Q3 GDP and October flash | Nov 30 | Q3 below 1% annualized, October negative | Q3 at or above 2%, upward revisions |
| November LFS | Dec 4 (likely) | Fourth straight job decline, unemployment at or above 6.8% | Strong gain, unemployment at or below 6.5% |
| BoC communications | Any time | Officials stress downside risks or "time to assess" | Officials repeat "further increases" without hedging |
| Swap-market pricing (not a prediction market) | Ongoing | December hike odds below 30% | December hike odds above 70% |
| U.S. tariffs | Ongoing | New escalation hurts Canadian exports or confidence | De-escalation |

The BoC rarely surprises markets that are clearly priced. A large shift in swap pricing after the November 16 CPI or the December 4 jobs report is the single best guide to the outcome.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **Core inflation level.** I could not verify the CPI-trim/median figure of about 1.9% for September 2026 (see the October 19 entry). It conflicts with an inflation forecast near 3.5% and needs checking.
- **Why the MPR expects 3.5% by year-end when September was 3.1%.** It could reflect base effects or projected pass-through. I could not read the MPR (fetch blocked).
- **No post-October-jobs economist previews or swap pricing.** The 57% hike pricing and the bank forecasts all predate the October jobs report (November 6) and the oil drop to about $90–95, so market expectations now are unknown.
- **Federal budget contents** (November 5) and their implications for growth and inflation.
- **The November LFS date.** December 4 is assumed from the usual first-Friday schedule.
- **The Iran war path is binary.** Rapidan's 80% chance of resumed fighting and JPMorgan's view of an oil surplus point in opposite directions.
- **Reliability of the base-rate list.** Reference class A comes from my own knowledge, not a sourced dataset. Its sample (5) is small and all demand-driven.
