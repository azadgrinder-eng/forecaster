## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Iowa State is selected for one of the NCAA-certified Division I bowl games on the market's list, or for the College Football Playoff, during the 2026-27 season. Being selected is enough. The team doesn't have to actually play. FCS playoffs don't count.
- **Timing:** The market closes Jan 7, 2027, but the outcome will almost certainly be known on bowl selection day, around Sunday Dec 6, 2026. That follows conference championship weekend, and the Big 12 title game is Dec 4 at AT&T Stadium (2026 Big 12 Championship Game, Wikipedia).
- **Who decides:** Bowls and conferences make the picks under their tie-in agreements, within NCAA bowl-eligibility rules.
- **Normal route:** Iowa State is 5-6. If it beats Kansas State on Nov 28 and finishes 6-6, it is bowl-eligible. A 6-6 team from a power conference almost always gets a bid.
- **Backup route at 5-7:** A 5-7 team can only get in if there aren't enough teams with six or more wins to fill the slots. Under the old rule, the extra spots went to 5-7 teams in order of their multi-year Academic Progress Rate (APR, an NCAA academic score). On May 8, 2026, the FBS Oversight Committee approved a change: once every team at 6-6 or better has been placed, a conference may pick any of its own 5-7 teams with a multi-year APR of at least 930 to fill its bowl commitments. That still needed Division I Cabinet review in June, and I couldn't confirm whether it was finalized (Fox Sports/AP, May 2026; ESPN, May 2026). If it took effect, the Big 12 could choose a 5-7 Iowa State directly if it is short of eligible teams for its tie-ins. I couldn't find Iowa State's APR. The program has historically been in the upper range, but treat that as unverified.
- **Edge case 1, opting out:** In 2025, Iowa State finished 8-4 and declined the postseason on Dec 7, 2025. The school said the players voted not to play, citing a shortage of healthy players, after Matt Campbell left for Penn State. The Big 12 fined Iowa State $500,000 (Iowa State Athletics, Dec 7, 2025; Herald Extra, Dec 7, 2025). If the school opts out before selection, it is never "selected," so the market would very likely resolve NO. If it declines after formally receiving an invitation, the wording is ambiguous.
- **Edge case 2, surplus teams:** If more teams are eligible than there are slots (one tracker says 84), a few 6-6 teams can be left home. Historically that has almost always hit Group of 5 teams, not power-conference teams.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Five-point home favorites in college football:** Iowa State is about a 5 to 5.5-point favorite, total 53.5, moneyline roughly −188/+155 (Heartland College Sports, Nov 22, 2026; Picks and Parlays, Nov 2026). From general knowledge, not looked up this time: teams favored by 5 to 5.5 points have historically won about 65–70% of the time outright, and −188 implies about 65%, or roughly 63% after removing the bookmaker's margin. **Base rate for winning: about 63–70%.**
2. **Power-conference 6-6 teams getting a bid if they want one:** about 95–99%. Exclusions in recent years have been rare and mostly Group of 5 teams. Recent opt-outs (Iowa State, Kansas State and Notre Dame in 2025) were tied to coaching changes or playoff snubs, which isn't Iowa State's situation now.
3. **5-7 teams getting bids:** In roughly half of seasons since 2015, at least one 5-7 team received a bid, usually 1–3 teams. Examples: 2022, Rice; 2025, Mississippi State, Rice and Appalachian State after the opt-outs (AP via Fox Sports, Dec 2025; theScore/AP, May 2026). The exact counts for each year are from memory and not verified. Any given 5-7 power-conference team had a much lower chance than that. It depends on the national shortfall, APR order (old rule), or conference discretion (new rule).
4. **Iowa State's own history:** bowl or CFP appearances in 2017–2021, 2023 and 2024. It missed in 2022 at 4-8 and declined in 2025. That's background only. The current question comes down to one game.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES**
- **A. Beat Kansas State and accept a bid (main path):** about 63–70% to win, times roughly 95–98% that Iowa State accepts and gets picked. Rogers has every reason to want the extra bowl practices in year one. The Big 12's $500K fine discourages another opt-out, and players are publicly committing for 2027. The things that could still go wrong are a 6-6 surplus squeeze or an opt-out driven by injuries.
- **B. Lose, finish 5-7, and still get selected:** This needs either (i) fewer than ~84 eligible teams after rivalry week and conference title games, or opt-outs that open slots (2025 had three), plus a high enough APR under the old ordering, or (ii) the new conference-choice rule being in effect and the Big 12 being short of eligible teams for its tie-ins. As of ~Nov 21, eight Big 12 teams were reportedly eligible, with UCF now added. With 16 teams in the league, the Big 12 is probably not far short. Rough chance of this path given a loss: 5–15%.

**Paths to NO**
- Losing to Kansas State and having no 5-7 slot open up. This is the dominant NO path, roughly 30–37% before considering the 5-7 backup.
- Winning but opting out or being squeezed out. Low probability, a few percent.

## 5. Signposts

- **Nov 28 result:** This settles most of the question. A win pushes YES to about 95% or higher, and a loss drops it to about 5–15%.
- **Pregame news:** Injuries to Iowa State QB Raynor or to Flora, or which Kansas State quarterback starts. A healthy Duff instead of Barnett slightly helps Kansas State.
- **Line movement:** If the spread moves past 7, that's good for Iowa State. If it drops below 3, that's bad.
- **Final eligible count after Nov 28–Dec 5:** If it is below 84, the 5-7 path opens. Also watch for any program announcing a bowl opt-out (Notre Dame-type snubs, coaching changes), which creates slots.
- **APR ranking or rule confirmation:** Confirmation of whether the conference-choice 5-7 rule took effect, and where Iowa State's APR ranks among 5-7 teams.
- **Opt-out signals from Iowa State:** Any statements from Rogers or athletics director Jamie Pollard about whether the team will accept a bid.
