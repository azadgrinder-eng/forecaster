# Research brief: Will China, India, or the US sign the COP31 "35 by 35" electrification pledge?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What the pledge is.** The COP31 Presidency (Türkiye, co-led with Australia) launched the "35-by-35 Global Electrification Pledge." It calls for electricity to reach 35% of global final energy consumption by 2035, up from about 20–23% today. It is voluntary and sits in the Action Agenda, outside the negotiated decision text. The official text is at cop31.tr (COP31 35-by-35 Global Electrification Pledge, cop31.tr). The target comes from IRENA analysis, and the IEA was commissioned to map pathways to it.
- **What counts as YES.** At least one of China, India or the US signs or joins the pledge, and either a formal press announcement or a statement on cop31.tr confirms it. This must happen by 20 Nov 2026, 22:59 UTC, which is the last scheduled day of COP31 (9–20 Nov, Antalya).
- **Edge cases and ambiguities:**
  - **"Endorse" vs. "sign."** Reporting so far talks about "endorsing" and "backing," not signing. A national statement that clearly endorses the pledge, made in a formal press release, would probably count. Saying the goal "is in line with our policy," as China has, probably would not.
  - **US states and cities.** Other COP31 texts, such as the Antalya AI Pledge, invite "non-Party stakeholders" to sign. I could not confirm whether the electrification pledge does too. If California or the US Climate Alliance joined, a lenient resolver might be tempted to count it. A strict reading of "the US" means the federal government.
  - **The goal ending up in the negotiated outcome text.** Türkiye wants the 35% goal written into the consensus COP decision. If China or India agreed to a cover decision that mentions electrification, that is not the same as signing the pledge, but it could cause disputes.
  - **Timing.** Climate Home News (12 Nov) said the Presidency would publish the full list of backers "next week," meaning 16–20 Nov. COPs often run past their scheduled end, so a list or late sign-on could land after the close.
  - **Late joiners.** Earlier COP pledges kept adding signatories for months. Anything after 20 Nov does not count.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. China and India on voluntary COP-presidency energy pledges with numeric targets.** Some of these entries come from my background knowledge and are marked as unverified.
- COP26 Global Methane Pledge (2021): China no, India no, US yes. *Unverified.*
- COP26 Global Coal to Clean Power statement (2021): China no, India no, US no. *Unverified.*
- COP28 Global Renewables and Energy Efficiency Pledge (tripling/doubling, 2023): China and India did not sign, even though China backed the tripling goal elsewhere (G20, US–China Sunnylands statement). US signed. *Unverified.*
- COP28 Global Cooling Pledge: China no, India no, US yes. *Unverified.*
- COP29 Global Energy Storage and Grids Pledge (2024): China and India were listed among the notable absences at launch, while the US signed (solarquarter, 12 Dec 2024; Argus). By April 2025 the Global Renewables Alliance listed India as a signatory, but that was months after the COP.
- **Rough rate:** China signed about 0 of 5 of these energy-sector pledges during the COP itself. India signed about 0 of 5 during the COP, joining one later. China does sometimes sign non-energy declarations (the Glasgow forests declaration in 2021, and I believe the COP28 food and agriculture declaration; both unverified).

**B. US federal government under Trump on COP initiatives.** No federal delegation went to COP30 (2025) or COP31 (2026). In 2025–26 the administration joined no new COP climate pledges that I am aware of. Rate: about 0%.

**C. Pledge-friendly signals by China that later became signatures at the same COP.** I found no clear precedent. The COP28 tripling goal is the closest analogue: China supported the substance but did not sign the pledge.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **12–13 Nov 2026.** At least 14 countries have publicly endorsed the pledge, counted from speeches and statements. They include Germany, Norway, Finland, South Korea, Pakistan, Zimbabwe, Eswatini, Togo, Liechtenstein, Australia, Ethiopia and small island states. Seychelles and the UK attached caveats about clean power. The Presidency has not published a full list and is expected to do so "next week" (Climate Home News, 12 Nov 2026; ABC Australia, 11–12 Nov 2026). A Cyprus Mail analysis (11 Nov) said many major economies remain uncommitted. Commentary (Ara.cat) notes the pledge struggles without China, India or the US.
- **9–13 Nov.** There is an Electrification Ministerial during COP31. India's Bhupender Yadav and Germany's Carsten Schneider were named to facilitate electrification discussions; this appears to be the negotiated-text track. On 9 Nov the ICC endorsed the pledge (ICC, 9 Nov 2026). Critics, including the EU and Portugal, object that the pledge does not require the extra electricity to be clean (Climate Home News, 22 Sep 2026; Karmactive).
- **10 Nov.** The US has no federal delegation for the second year in a row. A White House official called the summit one that "rejects reality." Gov. Newsom is leading a California delegation, and about 120 US state and local leaders are attending (Office of Gov. Newsom, 10 Nov 2026; NPR, 9 Nov 2026; Spectrum News, 9 Nov 2026).
- **5 Nov.** China "has so far stopped short of endorsing" the pledge, though Vice Minister Li Gao, who heads China's delegation, says the target is in line with Chinese policy (Climate Home News, 5 Nov 2026). China's electricity share of final energy rose from 23% in 2015 to 30% in 2025, and its energy plan targets 35% by 2030, five years ahead of the global goal (Climate Action Tracker).
- **3 Nov.** India's Environment Minister Yadav said: "We are not going to sign any pledge… This is their agenda." He cited affordable energy access, national circumstances, and finance needs of about $3 trillion against the $100bn goal (Down To Earth; Climate Fact Checks, ~3–4 Nov 2026). An earlier report said India had "yet to commit" (ANC).
- **21–22 Oct.** At a press conference Li Gao said "This is also our policy direction" about electrification. Bloomberg headlined this as China backing the push, but other outlets described his remarks as vague. He urged the Presidency to "consult widely and seek broad support." China's COP31 priorities are cleantech trade barriers and unilateralism, finance, and implementation (Bloomberg / EnergyConnects, 21 Oct 2026; SCIO, 22 Oct 2026; Table.Media, 22 Oct 2026).
- **Context.** Türkiye has a roughly $15bn China deal covering about 7 GW of solar and wind (ESG News), so there is a warm bilateral energy relationship.

**Scheduled before close:** the second week of COP31 (16–20 Nov) with ministerial negotiations, publication of the backer list "next week," and the closing plenary on 20 Nov, which may overrun.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (almost entirely through China):**
- China decides that signing costs it nothing, since it already targets 35% by 2030. Signing would also showcase Chinese cleantech and help Türkiye, a friendly host with Chinese energy investment. A plausible trigger is the backer-list publication or a high-level Türkiye–China moment in week 2.
- The Presidency trades concessions, for example on trade or unilateral measures language, or on finance, for Chinese endorsement.
- China endorses with caveats such as "national circumstances" or "voluntary," and the Presidency lists it as a supporter.
- **India: low.** It would take a reversal of a minister's explicit public refusal from 3 Nov. The only path is softer wording, such as India's co-facilitation role producing a "support for the goal" statement, which probably would not meet the bar.
- **US: near zero** for the federal government. The only YES path is a loose resolver counting a subnational US signature.

**Paths to NO:**
- China follows its usual pattern: it supports the substance, but as a BASIC/LMDC member it avoids presidency-led voluntary pledges, preferring the consensus UNFCCC process and avoiding commitments framed as global targets.
- China ties its support to the trade or unilateral-measures fight and holds back.
- The backer list arrives after 20 Nov, or the outcome is only negotiated-text language.

## 5. Signposts

- **Strongly toward YES:** China appears on the Presidency's published backer list; a MEE or Xinhua release says China "joins/supports the 35-by-35 pledge"; a joint China–Türkiye statement on electrification.
- **Moderately toward YES:** Li Gao or a Chinese leader speaking at the electrification ministerial and explicitly "welcoming" the pledge; other BASIC members such as Brazil or South Africa signing.
- **Toward NO:** the backer list is published without China; Chinese statements stress "voluntary, nationally determined" approaches and criticise top-down targets; BASIC statements opposing global sectoral targets; the list is delayed beyond 20 Nov.
- **India reversal (unlikely):** any statement by Yadav or the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change contradicting the 3 Nov remark.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not open cop31.tr, the Climate Home News article from 12 Nov, or CATF's COP31 declaration tracker because the fetch tool was blocked. The endorsement list therefore comes from search snippets, and China may or may not appear in the full Presidency list.
- I could not establish whether the pledge allows subnational or non-Party signatories.
- It is unclear how the resolver treats "endorsed in a speech" versus "formally signed."
- Bloomberg's "China backs push" framing conflicts with others' "stopped short." China's true intent is unclear.
- Several base-rate data points, especially the COP26 and COP28 signatory statuses, come from memory and were not re-verified.
- The exact date the backer list will be published is unknown.
