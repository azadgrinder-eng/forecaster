# Research brief: Will Miami (FL) be in the top 4 of the Dec 1, 2026 CFP rankings?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Miami must be ranked No. 1–4 in the CFP Selection Committee ranking labeled "Dec 1" for the 2026-27 season. If no ranking carries that label, the next ranking published after Dec 1 is used.
- **When it comes out:** 2026 rankings come out every Tuesday through the next-to-last ranking on Tuesday, Dec 1, at about 7 p.m. ET on ESPN. The final ranking and the 12-team field come on Sunday, Dec 6 (College Football Playoff sets 2026 rankings release schedule, fbschedules.com). The two fbschedules pages disagree on the date of the final ranking, but both list Tuesday, Dec 1 as a release.
- **Timing is the key point:** The Dec 1 ranking reflects rivalry week (Nov 27–28) only. It comes before the conference championship games, including the ACC title game, Miami vs. Wake Forest in Charlotte on Dec 5 (ACC.com, Nov 22, 2026). So an ACC title win cannot help Miami on this question. The only results that matter are Miami vs. Boston College and the rivalry-week games of the six teams ranked ahead of Miami.
- **Edge cases:**
  - The AP poll is irrelevant. Boston College's own media notes call Miami "#4", which is probably the AP rank (BC Athletics, Nov 24, 2026). Only the committee ranking counts, and there Miami is No. 7.
  - The fallback rule only applies if the CFP delays or relabels the release.
  - Ties are not possible because the committee ranks teams 1–25 in a strict order.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **How often favorites at these point spreads win.** Using the standard historical conversion from spread to win probability:

   | Team | Opponent and spread | Est. win chance | Est. loss chance |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Georgia | vs. Georgia Tech, about -35.5 | about 99% | about 1% |
   | Notre Dame | at Syracuse, -24.5 opening line | about 95–96% | 4–5% |
   | Alabama | vs. Auburn, -14 to -16.5 | about 85–88% | 12–15% |
   | Tennessee | at Vanderbilt, -13.5 | about 84% | about 16% |
   | Texas | vs. Texas A&M, -9.5 to -11.5 | about 78–81% | 19–22% |
   | Utah | vs. West Virginia | no 2026 line found; probably a double-digit favorite | maybe 5–10% |

   Treat the six games as roughly independent. On that basis:
   - All three two-loss SEC teams ahead of Miami (Texas, Tennessee, Alabama) losing: about 0.2 × 0.16 × 0.14 ≈ **0.5%**.
   - Exactly two of those three losing: about **8%**.
   - Each of those losses probably drops a 9-3 team below an 11-1 Miami.

2. **Big upward moves in a non-championship week.** Under the committee format, teams rarely climb three or more spots in a week where they only beat a losing team, unless several teams ahead of them lose. The committee generally holds order among teams that win, and holding order is exactly what it did for Miami. Miami was No. 7 on Nov. 10 and stayed No. 7 on Nov. 17 and Nov. 24, even after UCLA's first loss (Canes Warning, Nov 24, 2026). I could not compile an exact historical count of 3+ spot jumps. Treat this as a qualitative prior that strongly favors standing pat.

3. **Head-to-head results the committee has applied.** Notre Dame beat Miami 31-24 and is ranked ahead of it. Media analysis expects that head-to-head result to keep Notre Dame ahead (On3; Yahoo, Nov 2026). Tennessee only passed Alabama on Nov 24, despite owning the head-to-head win since the first ranking (ESPN, Nov 24, 2026). So the committee applies head-to-head results, but slowly.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

Miami must beat Boston College, which is very likely given a lopsided matchup. Beyond that, it needs three of the six teams ahead of it to fall below it.

**Paths to YES:**
- **(a) SEC chaos (about 0.5%):** Texas, Tennessee and Alabama all lose. Miami then moves to No. 4 behind Georgia, Utah and Notre Dame.
- **(b) Two SEC losses plus a committee jump (perhaps 2–3%):** Two of the three lose, which happens about 8% of the time. Miami then rises to No. 5 and also needs to pass the remaining SEC winner. The "razor thin" Alabama comment makes that plausible if Alabama is the survivor and wins ugly. It is less plausible against Texas (currently No. 3) or Tennessee. Alternatively, Notre Dame or Utah loses as well.
- **(c) An upset of Notre Dame or Utah combined with SEC losses (well under 1%):**
  - A Notre Dame loss to Syracuse would probably still leave Notre Dame near Miami, because of the head-to-head win.
  - A 11-1 Utah after losing to West Virginia might or might not fall below 11-1 Miami.
- **(d) A pure committee re-evaluation with no upsets (about 1% or less):** Miami passes three winning teams after beating a 5-6 opponent. This runs against the committee's behaviour over the last three weeks.

**Paths to NO:** Most of the top six win, which is the overwhelmingly likely outcome. Or Miami is upset by Boston College, or wins unconvincingly.

**Rough total:** Adding up the paths gives roughly 2–5%. Treat this as an input for the forecaster, not a final estimate.

## 5. Signposts

- **Friday, Nov 27: Texas vs. Texas A&M.** This is the single biggest swing game. A Texas loss is necessary for nearly every YES path. If Texas wins, YES becomes very unlikely.
- **Saturday: Iron Bowl and Tennessee–Vanderbilt.** Each additional SEC loss multiplies Miami's chances.
- **Margins and style.** A dominant Miami win over BC combined with a narrow Alabama or Tennessee win could matter for path (b).
- **Upsets of Notre Dame or Utah.** These help only in combination with SEC losses.
- **Committee chair remarks on Dec 1.** These come too late to forecast from, but would explain any surprise.
- **A Miami loss or key injuries.** These would move the probability to near zero.

- I am not sure where an 11-1 Utah or 11-1 Notre Dame would land relative to 11-1 Miami after an upset loss.
- I have no compiled historical count of committee jumps of 3+ spots in non-championship weeks.
