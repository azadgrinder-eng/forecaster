# Research brief: Will Jordan formally join the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement by December 31, 2026?

*Note on method: I ran 12 web searches. Every attempt to open a full page (Wikipedia, Dawn's copy of the treaty text, Al Jazeera, Assawsana, The Thursday Times) was refused by the proxy. Everything below comes from search-result summaries and snippets, so quotations and exact wording should be treated as approximate.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Jordan must formally join by **Dec 31, 2026, 11:59 PM ET**, about 33 days after today (Nov 28, 2026). There are two ways:
  - (a) Jordan signs the agreement, or a formal accession protocol or instrument, that makes it a party, member or signatory; or
  - (b) Jordan and/or the existing parties officially announce that Jordan has become a party, member or signatory.
- **What does not count:** statements of intent, applications, invitations, negotiations, parliamentary authorization, approval in principle, or general defence cooperation. A separate Jordan–Saudi, Jordan–Türkiye or Jordan–Pakistan defence deal also fails unless it is officially called accession to the Makkah agreement.
- **Ratification is not needed.** Signing an accession instrument is enough. That matters because Türkiye's own ratification took about 3.5 months: signed Aug 7, parliament approved 279–119 on Nov 5, and Erdogan completed it in late November (Express Tribune / Dawn, "Erdogan completes Türkiye's ratification of Makkah defence pact," late Nov 2026).
- **Who decides who gets in:** The reported treaty text gives the "Strategic Political and Defence Committee" (S-PDC) authority to set accession procedures and principles, and to approve them, by consensus of the "core parties" (Dawn, "Text of the Makkah defence agreement," Aug 2026, via search snippet). In practice Riyadh, Ankara and Islamabad must all agree.
- **Edge cases:**
  - The body was renamed the "Makkah Defence Alliance" (Geo, ~Aug/Sep 2026). Joining the "Alliance" should count as joining the Agreement if officials describe it as membership.
  - The alliance's website says related cooperation "does not establish additional Makkah Alliance membership" (makkahalliance.com). Observer status, partner status or joint exercises would therefore probably not count.
  - "Consensus of credible reporting" is allowed as a fallback source.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **This alliance's own expansion pace.**
   - The Saudi–Pakistan Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement (Sep 17, 2025) took about 10.5 months to add a third member (Türkiye, Aug 7, 2026).
   - In about 3.7 months since Aug 7, 2026, **no** country has acceded. That includes Egypt, which Türkiye's foreign minister called a "natural partner" with only "technical issues" left (Arab News PK / Khaleej Times, Aug 8–9, 2026).
   - Egypt's foreign minister replied that Cairo must weigh "legal, constitutional and technical issues" (The National, Aug 14, 2026).
   - Syria said on Oct 31 that "discussions are underway" but has filed no formal application (Express Tribune, Oct 31, 2026).
   - The base rate so far is 0 accessions out of several interested states in about 4 months. The founders only agreed admission "principles" on Nov 27.
2. **New mutual-defence pacts adding members within about 5 months of signing.**
   - NATO (founded April 1949) first expanded with Greece and Türkiye in Feb 1952, after about 34 months.
   - The GCC Joint Defence Agreement (2000) and the Warsaw Pact (1955) added no new members.
   - The Arab League Joint Defence Treaty (1950) was the main exception: some Arab states, Jordan among them, joined within about 1–2 years.
   - Rough rate of a binding collective-defence pact adding any member within 5 months of founding: well under 10%. The chance that a *specific* country that has not been named does so is far lower.
3. **Jordan joining Saudi-led frameworks.**
   - Jordan joined the Saudi-led Yemen coalition (March 2015) and was a founding member of the Islamic Military Counter Terrorism Coalition (Dec 2015). Amman will join Saudi-led groupings when they are loose and non-binding.
   - Those were not mutual-defence commitments. Jordan has no history of signing an "attack on one is an attack on all" pact with Pakistan or Türkiye.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 27–28, 2026:** The second S-PDC meeting in Islamabad.
  - The founders agreed "principles" for admitting new members. They named no candidates and set no timetable (Arab News PK, "Pakistan reaffirms support… roadmap for expansion," Nov 27; Thursday Times, Nov 28; Khlaasa, Nov 28).
  - Fidan said "several decisions were made in principle regarding the roadmap" for countries wishing to join.
  - The statement condemned Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and noted progress on the secretariat in Riyadh. No deployment or doctrine was announced (Al Jazeera, Nov 27; Dawn, Nov 27–28).
- **Late Nov 2026:** Erdogan completed Türkiye's ratification. With that, all three founders have finished ratifying (Express Tribune / Dawn).
- **Nov 5, 2026:** The Turkish parliament approved the pact 279–119.
- **Oct 31, 2026:** Syria's foreign minister said talks on Syria's entry are underway. Reports say none of the founders has publicly endorsed Syria's bid (Express Tribune; House of Saud).
- **Oct 5, 2026:** Türkiye and Pakistan agreed to "collective deterrence measures" and deployments to Saudi Arabia against the Houthis (Al Jazeera, Oct 5). Analysts later questioned credibility because no Turkish or Pakistani military response followed (Wikipedia summary; Outlook India).
- **Oct 20, 2026:** A Congressional Research Service report (RL33546) covered Jordan in the 2026 Iran war:
  - Iran had fired more than 400 projectiles at Jordan by September 2026.
  - Three US soldiers were killed at Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in July 2026.
  - Pending FY2027 appropriations include ≥$150M for Jordan's border security and up to $500M for its armed forces.
- **Sep 2026:** Iranian strikes on Jordan tested its US alignment (Boston Globe, Sep 4). An August report said King Abdullah's preference for deep ties with Washington makes a change of stance unlikely.
- **Sep 1, 2026:** Riyadh was chosen to host the secretariat, with a Pakistani secretary-general. Fidan has said institutions must be set up before new members are admitted (Express Tribune; Hurriyet Daily News).
- **Aug 9, 2026:** The only Jordan-specific outside assessment I found. An Al Jazeera analyst (Bakir) said Azerbaijan, Syria and **Jordan** could join "relatively quickly," and called Qatar the "most natural" Gulf candidate (Al Jazeera, "Which other countries could join…," Aug 9).
- **Mid-Aug 2026, Jordanian commentary:**
  - A columnist wrote that Jordan had issued **no statement** on the pact and might not, seeing it as a matter for the three states (Nesan / Abdullah al-Majali).
  - A writer in *Al-Rai* urged Jordan to join before it gets harder.
  - Another piece said Jordan officially keeps its distance from alignments, and that nothing public shows Jordan was invited or declined (Assawsana, "Makkah agreement without Jordan").
- **No result** in English or Arabic showed a Jordanian government statement, application, invitation or negotiation about the pact.

**Scheduled events before Dec 31:** I found none specific to accession. No third S-PDC meeting or summit date was found. A GCC summit usually happens in December, but Jordan is not a GCC member.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (all look unlikely in 33 days):**
- **Surprise summit accession.** Saudi Arabia wants to show the pact is credible amid the Houthi attacks and stages a quick expansion with a "safe," friendly state. Jordan is a plausible low-friction candidate: a Sunni monarchy close to Riyadh, good ties with Ankara and Islamabad, and itself hit by Iran. This would require Amman to have been negotiating quietly. I found no evidence of that.
- **A Jordanian push to diversify its security partners** after Iran's strikes and domestic anger at the US presence.

**Paths to NO (dominant):**
- **Process lag.** Admission principles were only agreed on Nov 27. The roadmap has not been published, the secretariat is still being set up, and Fidan has said institutions come first. Egypt and Syria are ahead in the queue, and neither has joined.
- **Jordan's strategic constraints:**
  - Major non-NATO US ally, 2021 defence cooperation agreement giving US access to 12 sites, and large US aid.
  - Peace treaty with Israel.
  - Traditional caution about binding alignments.
- **Iran.** Joining an alliance tied to the Houthi/Iran conflict would raise Jordan's exposure. Türkiye has stressed the pact is "not aimed at Iran."
- **Jordanian domestic law.** Treaties affecting public rights generally need parliamentary approval (Constitution Art. 33). That is not required for YES, but it pushes toward a slower, deliberate process.
- **No public signal at all** from Amman in nearly 4 months.

## 5. Signposts

**Large moves toward YES:**
- Any official Jordanian statement of interest.
- A founder naming Jordan as a candidate.
- A Royal Court or Petra News readout on the pact from a King Abdullah visit to Riyadh, Ankara or Islamabad.
- A summit or S-PDC session announced for December with accession on the agenda.

**Moderate moves toward YES:**
- Egypt or Syria actually acceding. This would show the mechanism works, though it would not mean Jordan is next.
- A trilateral or quadrilateral exercise or defence MoU involving Jordan explicitly framed under the alliance.

**Moves toward NO:**
- Continued Jordanian silence.
- Published accession criteria that require lengthy steps.
- The next S-PDC meeting scheduled for 2027.
- Jordan deepening US/CENTCOM arrangements.
- Official comments that Jordan does not join axes.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not read the full treaty text, the content of the Nov 27 admission principles, or the roadmap. All page fetches failed.
- I could not confirm whether Jordan has been privately approached. Arab-press commentary from August says no invitation was public.
- I found no Jordanian official statement (Safadi, the Royal Court or Petra) on the pact.
- I do not know the date of the next S-PDC meeting or any December summit.
- I cannot tell whether a founder (Pakistan in particular) might object to Jordan, or whether the US has privately discouraged US partners from joining.
- I did not search Iran-war ceasefire status or Jordan–Iran dynamics after October, so how much these affect Jordan's appetite for joining is unclear.
