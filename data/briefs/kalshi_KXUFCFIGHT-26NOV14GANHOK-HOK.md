# Research Brief: Will Josh Hokit beat Ciryl Gane at UFC 334 (Nov 14, 2026)?

*Note on method: every direct page fetch was refused by the network proxy (Wikipedia, CBS, Cageside Press, AgentMMA). The facts below come from search-result summaries. Items marked "from memory" are my own background knowledge and could not be checked this session. I left out all betting and prediction-market prices.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** Hokit is the official winner of Gane vs. Hokit at UFC 334. Any method counts: KO/TKO, submission, decision or disqualification.
- **Draw or no contest:** the market resolves at 0.50. This matters more than usual here. Gane's last title fight against Tom Aspinall (UFC 321, Oct 2025) ended in a no contest after accidental eye pokes (Reuters via The Star, 2026-09-14).
- **Timing:** the event is Saturday, Nov 14, 2026, at Madison Square Garden in New York. The main card starts at 9 p.m. ET, which is 02:00 UTC on Nov 15. The main event will probably start around 04:30–05:30 UTC on Nov 15 (Yahoo Sports / UFC 334 guide, Nov 2026).
- **Postponement:** if the fight starts within 48 hours of the original start time, the market stays open. Otherwise it resolves to a "fair price," which the rules do not define. The close date is 2026-11-17 03:30 UTC, which fits the 48-hour window.
- **Results changed after expiration:** these don't count. Examples are an overturned result after a failed drug test or a successful appeal. Only the result official at expiration matters.
- **Who decides:** in practice, the official result announced by the UFC and the New York State Athletic Commission.
- **Edge cases:**
  - A foul that ends the fight early becomes a no contest (0.50) if it is ruled accidental, or a Hokit win if it is ruled intentional (DQ). Curtis Blaydes has publicly predicted that Gane will foul Hokit in round 1 (MMASucka, Nov 2026).
  - If either fighter withdraws and a replacement steps in, it is no longer "Gane vs. Hokit." It most likely resolves as cancelled at a fair price.
  - This is a 5-round title fight. Hokit has never gone past 3 rounds.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **UFC title defenses, all divisions.**
   - Champions won 77 of 109 defenses (70.6%) from UFC 28 to UFC 162. They won 71.3% from 2013 to Nov 2022 (MMAOddsBreaker; Yahoo Sports).
   - The rate dropped to 59.4% over the roughly two years before that Yahoo piece (Yahoo Sports, ~2023).
   - Takeaway: challengers win about 29–40% of the time.
2. **Undisputed UFC heavyweight title defenses since 2001 (my own count, from memory).** Champions went about 20–13, roughly 61%. Sherdog notes that heavyweight champions have historically had the worst retention rate of any division (Sherdog, "Stipe Miocic's Uphill Climb"). Takeaway: challengers win about 35–40%.
3. **Undefeated UFC title challengers.** AgentMMA counts 17 wins and 22 losses (43.6%) through Oct 13, 2026 (AgentMMA MMA Lab, Oct 2026). This sample probably includes vacant-title fights, so it likely overstates the rate against a sitting champion.
   - A relevant example: Gane himself was 10-0 when he lost his first title shot to Ngannou at UFC 270 (Sportskeeda).
- **Adjustment to consider:** this is Gane's first fight as undisputed champion, but he has 6+ years of elite heavyweight experience. Hokit has only about 12 months of UFC experience. Both factors argue for treating him as weaker than a typical challenger.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES (Hokit wins):**
- **Wrestling control.** Both of Gane's losses came against fighters who grappled him: Ngannou's later-round takedowns and Jones's immediate takedown into a submission. Hokit's wrestling base and his coaches' stated plan point this way (Bisping, Heavy). It depends on Hokit actually committing to takedowns, which he has barely done in the UFC, and on Gane's improved takedown defense not holding up.
- **Early power and pressure.** Hokit has four UFC wins in 7 months and three finishes. Gane is 36 and has been out 5 months. This depends on Hokit getting inside an 8-inch reach gap.
- **Foul or DQ.** Gane has a record of eye pokes. An intentional-foul DQ would count as a Hokit win, but an accidental foul gives a no contest (0.50). Both are low probability.

**Paths to NO (Gane wins):**
- **Range kickboxing.** Gane's reach, footwork and volume against a defensively loose challenger who absorbed about 175 significant strikes from Blaydes.
- **Late stoppage or decision.** Hokit has never fought rounds 4–5, and his aggressive pace may fade. Gane has gone 25 minutes before.
- **Experience.** Gane has faced Ngannou, Jones, Aspinall, Pereira and Volkov. Hokit's best wins are over a 35-year-old Blaydes and a 41-year-old Lewis.

**Other outcomes:** draw or no contest, roughly 1–3% (above the usual rate given the foul history). Cancellation is possible, but there is no injury news.

## 5. Signposts (before the fight)
- **Press conference (Nov 12) and weigh-ins (Nov 13):**
  - A missed weight, visible depletion, or a heavyweight coming in well under the limit would matter; Hokit is a smaller heavyweight.
  - Any withdrawal pushes resolution toward fair-price or cancellation.
- **Credible camp reports** (training partners, coaches) about Hokit's wrestling-heavy plan, or about Gane's grappling camp. These move the estimate a little.
- **During the fight, if trading is live:**
  - An early Hokit takedown or clean knockdown points strongly to YES.
  - Gane landing at range freely in round 1 and stuffing takedowns points strongly to NO.
  - An early foul raises the chance of a no contest.
