# Research brief: Will the press misspell Robert Neuman's name at least once? (closes 2026-11-30)

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES.** No written criteria were given, so the plain meaning applies: at least one press outlet prints his name wrong. Likely forms are "Newman," "Neumann," "Nueman," "Bob/Rob Newman," or a wrong first name. The misspelling must refer to Robert Neuman, the 2026 Oregon Republican candidate for governor.
- **Who decides.** Almost certainly the market creator, on their own judgment. That person probably needs to see a link or screenshot of the error before resolving YES. In practice the question is "will someone find and show an example before Nov 30, 2026," not "does one exist."
- **Edge cases the forecaster should weigh:**
  - **Timing.** "Running for governor" suggests the question was written while his campaign was live, before or around the May 19, 2026 primary. Does an error from before the question opened count? Do errors from his earlier races count (2022 labor commissioner, 2024 state Senate District 30)? Plain meaning ("at least once") suggests any press error tied to his governor run counts, but this is unclear.
  - **What counts as "press."** Ballotpedia, iVoterGuide, GoodParty, BallotReady, Wikipedia, campaign sites and aggregators are probably not press. Newspapers, TV and radio sites (KATU, OPB, Oregon Capital Chronicle, Statesman Journal, Corvallis Advocate, Street Roots, KBOO, Blue Mountain Eagle) clearly are.
  - **Errors that get fixed.** A typo corrected later, or a "REVISED" version of an article, probably still counts if someone can document it.
  - **Mix-ups that aren't spelling.** Confusing him with California's Robert Newman, or with a different Neuman, may not count unless the spelling itself is wrong. A related question on another platform asks whether anyone will confuse him with "California's Robert Newman" (I did not look at its price). The name confusion is plausible: a Robert Newman ran for governor of California in 2018 and in the 2021 recall (from my background knowledge, not checked this session).

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **How often newspapers get facts wrong.** Accuracy audits of US newspapers (Maier, 2005, *Journalism & Mass Communication Quarterly*, about 14 papers; from memory, not checked here) found that sources reported a factual error in roughly 40–60% of local news stories. Misspelled names made up a small share of those errors, low single-digit percent of stories. A rough rate for a name misspelling is therefore about 1–5% per article mention. That rate is likely higher for an obscure name that sounds like a much more common one.
2. **How common the competing spelling is.** "Newman" is far more common in the US than "Neuman." From memory of Census surname data, Newman ranks around #500 with about 60,000 people, against well under 10,000 for Neuman. When a name sounds like a more common spelling, writers tend to slip into the common one, especially in radio and TV web copy taken from audio. This could push the per-mention error rate toward 3–10%.
3. **How much press he got.** His coverage is spread across four campaigns (2022 labor commissioner, the 2024 Senate District 30 primary, the 2026 governor primary, and a write-in push). Combining them:
   - I estimate roughly 30–100 distinct press mentions.
   - At a 2–5% error rate per mention, the chance that at least one misspelling exists somewhere is about 45–95%: 1−(0.97)^50 ≈ 78%, and 1−(0.98)^30 ≈ 45%.
   - The chance that one appears in the 16 days left is far lower, since he gets close to zero coverage now (see section 3).

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES:**
- **Someone digs up an existing misspelling.** Candidates include a 2022 BOLI story (the U of O PDF hit), a TV or radio web story, a candidate-list roundup, or the "REVISED" East Oregonian piece. This depends on the resolver or traders actually searching archives. Search engines treat "Newman" and "Neuman" as the same name, which makes this harder.
- **The resolver counts confusion with California's Robert Newman,** or counts errors in voter-guide pages and aggregators as "press."
- **A new mention between Nov 14 and Nov 30** gets his name wrong. This is unlikely given how little he is covered now.

**Paths to NO:**
- Nobody produces a documented example before the close date, and the creator defaults to NO.
- The creator counts only errors made after the question was created, or only during the governor run, and none turns up.
- Every outlet copied the spelling from the official Secretary of State candidate filing. Copying from filings tends to keep names accurate in candidate-list stories.

## 5. Signposts
- **Moves toward YES:** anyone posting a link or screenshot of "Robert Newman" (or "Neumann" etc.) in an Oregon outlet; confirmation that the U of O 2022 PDF says "Newman"; the East Oregonian correction turning out to be a name fix; the creator saying earlier or aggregator errors count.
- **Moves toward NO:** the creator saying only errors after the question opened count, or that it must be a mainstream outlet; nobody producing an example as the close date nears; checks of the main 2026 articles (KATU, Capital Chronicle, OPB, Corvallis Advocate, Street Roots) all showing "Neuman."

## 6. Uncertainties
- I could not open any full article: the proxy blocked every WebFetch. Search snippets cannot show whether a page actually says "Newman," because the engine treats the spellings as the same.
- I don't know when the question was created, how strict the creator is, or whether old errors count.
- I could not confirm that the 2022 BOLI candidate, the 2024 SD-30 candidate (Greenhorn), and the 2026 governor candidate (White City) are the same person.
- I don't have his 2026 primary vote total, how many press mentions he got in total, or whether Oregon reports write-in totals by name.
- The base-rate figures for newspaper errors and surname frequency come from my memory of the literature, not sources checked this session.
