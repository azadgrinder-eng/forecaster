# Research brief: Will China restrict ammonium sulfate exports as of December 31, 2026?

Prepared November 16, 2026. Some web pages could not be opened in this environment, so many details come from search-result summaries of the articles. Figures I could only see in one source are marked as unverified.

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** At 23:59 China Standard Time on Dec 31, 2026, China must have an *operative, commodity-specific* export **prohibition, quota, or licensing requirement** that covers ordinary ammonium sulfate (HS 310221).
- **Who decides:** The resolver uses official MOFCOM and General Administration of Customs (GACC) announcements, backed by major news and trade-press reporting.
- **The key ambiguity:** One official, commodity-specific measure already exists. **GACC Announcement No. 97 of 2026** was published around July 9–13 and took effect July 16, 2026. It adds customs supervision condition "B" (mandatory export inspection, often called CIQ inspection) to 10-digit codes 3102210000 (ammonium sulfate, previously only "A", import inspection) and 3102290000 (double salts and mixtures). Each export batch is sampled. Cargo can only clear customs after it passes and receives an inspection clearance document (Jiemian, July 16, 2026; Keyuan Fertilizer, 2026; ChemNet, July 2026). Whether this counts as a "licensing requirement" decides the question:
  - **Reasons it could count:**
    - It is official, specific to this commodity, and in force.
    - No export can clear without the inspection document.
    - In practice, that document is rationed through industry-association allocations (see section 3).
    - When China put urea and other fertilizers under the same kind of inspection in October 2021 (GACC Announcement No. 81 of 2021, 29 codes, with ammonium sulfate excluded at the time), the international press widely called it "China curbs/restricts fertilizer exports." That characterization is my background knowledge, not something I re-verified this session.
  - **Reasons it might not count:**
    - Legally it is a product-quality inspection, not a license or quota.
    - Chinese industry commentary says it is about "regulating trade order, not tightening exports" (Jiemian / Sina, July 16, 2026).
    - Profercy said it was not expected to affect overall volumes, only how long shipments take (Profercy, July 2026).
- **A second ambiguity: informal quotas.** China's fertilizer quotas are usually unannounced: the urea quotas, the March 2026 bans on nitrogen-potassium blends and phosphates "never formally announced", and the urea quota of about 1.5 Mt in May 2026 (Reuters via Jakarta Post / Producer, Mar 20, 2026; Datamar, May 27, 2026). Any ammonium sulfate cap is likely to be an association "self-discipline" volume (自律量), not a MOFCOM/GACC document. That conflicts with a strict reading of "resolved from official MOFCOM and GACC announcements."
- **Minor:** The market closes at 2026-12-31 00:00 UTC, which is 08:00 CST on Dec 31, before the 23:59 CST resolution moment. A measure that ended exactly on Dec 31 could create an edge case. Announcement No. 97 has no expiry date that I could find.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **How long China keeps fertilizer export inspection regimes:** The Oct 2021 CIQ regime (Announcement 81/2021, 29 fertilizer codes) is still in force about 5 years later. Jun He law firm notes that "all codes in the fertilizer chapter" are now under mandatory inspection (Jun He legal update, 2026). Removals I know of: 0 of 29 codes in about 5 years. **The chance that Announcement 97 is revoked within 6 weeks of its fifth month is near zero.**
2. **How long China keeps fertilizer export quotas and bans once started:** Urea quotas or bans have run almost continuously from late 2021 to 2026. Phosphate curbs were extended through at least August 2026 (Fertilizer Daily, July 29, 2026). Persistence across years is high, roughly 90% or more.
3. **How often China imposes a *formal, announced* quota or ban on a surplus byproduct fertilizer:** Low. Ammonium sulfate is largely a byproduct of caprolactam and coking, with about 90% export dependence: 21.36 Mt exported in 2025, an export ratio of 89.82% (Longzhong via Jiemian, July 16, 2026), and capacity of about 25 Mt/yr (Argus, April 2026). It was spared in March 2026 even while about half to three-quarters of China's fertilizer exports were restricted (Reuters via Jakarta Post, Mar 20, 2026). A contrasting case: China fully suspended ordinary **sulfuric acid** exports from May 1 to Dec 31, 2026 through a joint MOFCOM–GACC announcement on Apr 10, 2026 (10jqka, Apr 22, 2026; Xueqiu). This shows formal byproduct bans do happen. It is a different product.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 6, 2026:** Coverage of the effect of China's sulfuric acid export suspension on India. Sulfuric acid, not ammonium sulfate (China.com, Nov 6, 2026). I found no November 2026 report of a new ammonium sulfate ban, suspension, or formal quota.
- **Oct 19, 2026, Zhuochuang analysis (via Sina):**
  - Exports in November–December are expected to fall as previously issued inspection clearance documents are used up.
  - "In December the 2027 self-discipline volume may be implemented early," giving sellers some buffer.
  - About 3.2 Mt of exports was forecast for September.
  - July exports dropped as the policy started; August–September rebounded during a customs-filing buffer window.
  - Q3 output was 6.36 Mt.

  This points to an **industry-administered annual volume cap** that continues into 2027 (Sina / Zhuochuang, Oct 19, 2026).
- **Mid-Sept 2026:** The Nitrogen Fertilizer Association reportedly gave an extra **900,000 t of "inspection quota"** to Sinochem, CNAMPGC and CNOOC. Total 2026 exports are reportedly "capped below 2025's record 21.36 Mt" (ChemAnalyst, about Sept–Oct 2026). A LinkedIn trade post says Jan–Sep exports were about 16.2 Mt and full-year exports "should not exceed 21 Mt per industry association guidance." The trade post is the only source for these figures (unverified).
- **September 2026 export volume:** Sources disagree. JLC says about 2.85 Mt, a monthly record, up 26% year on year. SunSirs says about 1.58 Mt, but its own numbers are internally inconsistent. October customs data are expected in late November.
- **July 16, 2026:** Announcement 97 takes effect. Caprolactam-grade product sold at a premium, while coking-grade auctions failed and that material went back to the domestic market (ChemAnalyst; Sina, July 28, 2026). Jun He warned that evading inspection carries criminal exposure at more than RMB 3M of goods or three or more evasions.
- **Spring 2026:**
  - An Argus article said exports were "expected to continue" for now, but "quotas could still be introduced" (Argus, about April 2026).
  - Reuters reported that Qingdao customs stepped up ammonium sulfate inspections after urea and potash were found falsely declared as ammonium sulfate (The Standard / Yahoo, late April 2026).
  - An Enongzi article said quotas were unlikely "for now" because the product is in domestic surplus.
- **Mar 2026:** China banned nitrogen-potassium blends and some phosphates and kept the urea quotas. Ammonium sulfate, about half of 2025 fertilizer shipments, was left untouched (Reuters via Jakarta Post, Mar 20, 2026).
- **Scheduled before Dec 31:**
  - November customs data (around Dec 18–20).
  - Possible early release of the 2027 self-discipline volume in December.
  - MOFCOM/GACC usually publish the next year's export-license catalogue and tariff schedule in late December. The 2026 catalogue covers 43 goods and does not appear to include fertilizers (CIRS, 2026).
  - The sulfuric acid ban is due to expire Dec 31, 2026, so watch whether it is extended.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

- **YES path A, interpretive (most important):** The resolver treats the Announcement 97 inspection requirement, or the association-allocated inspection quotas, as a "licensing requirement" or "quota." The underlying facts almost certainly hold on Dec 31: the measure has no sunset, the 2021 precedent shows about 5 years of persistence, and a 2027 allocation is already expected. Under this reading, P(YES) is roughly 95% or more.
- **YES path B, new formal measure:** Within about 6 weeks, MOFCOM/GACC announce an ammonium sulfate quota, license, or suspension. For example, this could happen alongside a 2027 policy package or a sulfuric acid extension, or because of food-security or spring-planting concerns. The surplus byproduct economics make this unlikely; I would put it in the low single digits to roughly 10%.
- **NO path:** The resolver reads the criteria strictly. Under that reading, CIQ is a quality inspection, not a license, and association self-discipline volumes are not "official MOFCOM/GACC" quotas. With no new formal measure, the answer is NO. Trade press characterizing the measure as "not a tightening" supports this reading.
- **So:** The probability depends mainly on how the criteria are interpreted, not on what happens in the world. The world facts are close to settled.

## 5. Signposts

- **Toward YES:**
  - Any MOFCOM/GACC document naming ammonium sulfate in a quota, license, or suspension (including a 2027 export-license catalogue that adds fertilizers).
  - Reuters, Bloomberg or Argus describing ammonium sulfate as "subject to export quotas" or "restricted."
  - Formal confirmation of a 2027 allocation.
  - Clarification from the question creator that CIQ inspection counts.
- **Toward NO:**
  - Clarification that inspection-only regimes do not count.
  - Reports that inspection allocations have been dropped or are unlimited.
  - Revocation or relaxation of Announcement 97 (very unlikely).
- **Useful context:** Whether the question creator resolves sibling questions (urea, phosphates) as YES on informal, unannounced quotas. That would show how lenient the "official announcement" standard is in practice.

## 6. Uncertainties

- The resolver's interpretation of "licensing requirement" for a CIQ legal-inspection regime is the largest uncertainty.
- I could not open the original GACC Announcement 97 text. Code coverage and dates come from secondary reports, which give July 9 or July 13 as the publication date.
- The existence, size, and formality of the association "self-discipline" cap and the "inspection quota" (900 kt extra; total below 21.36 Mt or 21 Mt) rest on one or two trade sources and are unverified.
- Monthly export data conflict across sources (1.58 Mt versus 2.85–3.2 Mt for September).
- I found no reporting dated November 2026 on ammonium sulfate export policy.
