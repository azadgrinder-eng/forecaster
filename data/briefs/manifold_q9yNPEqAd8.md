# Research Brief: Will 2026 US midterm turnout (as % of CVAP) exceed 2022's 46.8%?

*Prepared Nov 6, 2026, three days after Election Day (Nov 3, 2026). I could not open the primary pages (UF Election Lab, McDonald's Substack, Al Jazeera) because the proxy refused the connection. Figures from those sources come from search-result summaries of them and should be treated as second-hand.*

---

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **YES condition:** 2026 general-election turnout, measured as total ballots counted divided by citizen voting-age population (CVAP), is above 2022's 46.8% (112,054,124 ballots out of 239,035,960 CVAP).
- **Official source:** the EAC's 2026 Election Administration and Voting Survey (EAVS). In the EAVS, turnout is "total ballots counted" (item F1a) divided by CVAP, according to the EAC's data-brief footnote (2022 EAVS Data Brief US, Oct 2023; 2024 EAVS Data Brief US). The 2022 figure is confirmed: the EAC reported 46.8%, with more than 112M ballots counted (EAC press release, Jun 29, 2023).
- **Timing:** The EAVS report usually comes out around June 30 of the year after the election (2022 report: Jun 29, 2023; 2024 report: Jun 30, 2025). The 2026 report would therefore be expected around late June 2027. The rules allow early resolution on "a consensus of credible reporting" if the result is clear. If it is close, the market waits for the EAC.
- **Will the 2026 EAVS happen?** The EAC published a 30-day Federal Register notice for the 2026 EAVS (Apr 23, 2026), after a 60-day notice (Jan 12, 2026). A final 2026 EAVS instrument was posted on eac.gov in Aug 2026. If the report is not published, "a successor report or other comparable official data" is used.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Denominator.** The EAVS uses Census ACS-based CVAP. McDonald's figures use voting-eligible population (VEP), which is a different measure. EAVS percentages have run slightly higher than McDonald's VEP rates: 46.8% vs 46.1% for 2022.
  - **Federal politics.** Executive Order 14248 (Mar 25, 2025) targeted the EAC and was challenged in court, and there are reports of EAC budget pressure. These could in principle delay or change the report. They would not plausibly change whether turnout went up.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Midterm-to-midterm change (McDonald VEP series, from memory; please verify):** 1982 ~42%, 1986 ~38%, 1990 ~38%, 1994 ~41%, 1998 ~38%, 2002 ~40%, 2006 ~41%, 2010 ~41%, 2014 ~37%, 2018 50.0%, 2022 46.1%. Turnout rose over the previous midterm in about 6 of 10 transitions. This was the ex-ante prior. It is now largely irrelevant because ballots have been cast and mostly counted.
2. **Post-election counts versus final certified totals:** National ballot totals only grow after election night as mail ballots, provisional ballots and late-arriving ballots (California, Washington) are added. In recent cycles (2018, 2020, 2022, 2024), totals grew by roughly 1–4% between the day after the election and certification. That is my approximate recollection, not computed here. I know of no modern case in which a preliminary national lead of 2–3 points over a comparison year reversed after certification.
3. **CVAP denominator growth:**
   - 2022 EAVS CVAP: 239.0M.
   - 2024 EAVS: about 158M ballots at 64.7%, which implies a CVAP of about 244–245M.
   - ACS 5-year CVAP: 241.6M (2023) vs 235.4M (2019) (ProximityOne).
   - That is growth of roughly 1.5–3M a year, so the 2026 EAVS CVAP is likely about 247–251M. McDonald's 2026 figures imply a VEP of about 251.5M.

**Break-even ballot counts** for 2026 to exceed 46.8%:

| 2026 CVAP | Ballots needed |
|---|---|
| 246M | 115.1M |
| 248M | 116.1M |
| 250M | 117.0M |
| 252M | 117.9M |
| 255M (stress case) | 119.3M |

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 5, 2026:** Al Jazeera, citing the UF Election Lab, reported 122.7M ballots tallied and turnout of 48.76% of eligible voters. That would be the second-highest midterm turnout in more than a century, just below 2018 (Al Jazeera, Nov 5, 2026).
- **Nov 4–5, 2026:** McDonald's preliminary estimate is about 124.3M voters and 49.42% of VEP. That is second only to 2018 (50.05%) since 1914 and compares with 46.1% VEP in 2022. McDonald says there is "a fair amount of uncertainty" until California and Washington mail and provisional ballots are counted (McDonald Substack, Nov 4, 2026, via search summaries).
  - The 122.7M and 124.3M figures differ. One may be ballots counted so far and the other a projection of final turnout.
- **The UF Election Lab's 2026 page** says its estimates rely mainly on AP's day-after figures. It notes that California and Washington accept ballots postmarked by Election Day and that firm figures will take days.
- **ABC News (Nov 2026):** Reported a record of more than 124M ballots, citing the Election Lab.
- **State reports (Nov 3–5, 2026):**
  - Minnesota: more than 2.8M ballots, a midterm record (2018: 2.6M); turnout about 65% (Star Tribune; MPR, Nov 4).
  - Maine: highest turnout at 65.78%.
  - Michigan: 63.65%.
  - West Virginia: lowest at 33.33% (Al Jazeera).
  - Iowa: record raw midterm vote; rate of 63.34%, just short of 1990 (Iowa Capital Dispatch, Nov 4).
  - South Carolina: more than 1.93M ballots, a gubernatorial-year record (Fox Carolina, Nov 3).
- **California is still counting.** Los Angeles County had received more than 1.9M ballots, with hundreds of thousands still being processed (LAist; CBS LA, Nov 4–5). More ballots will be added nationally.
- **Before the election:** More than 46M early votes were cast, above the 45.9M early votes in all of 2022 (No Labels/Election Lab, late Oct 2026). Youth turnout was about 27% (±2), the second-highest midterm youth rate in 30 years (CIRCLE, Nov 2026).
- **Scheduled events before Nov 30 close:** State canvassing and certification run through late November and December. California's deadline is about 30 days after the election. Updated Election Lab estimates are likely as counting finishes. The EAVS itself will not be out before the close.

**Implied EAVS-basis rate:** about 123–126M final ballots over a CVAP of 247–251M gives roughly **49–51%**, about 2–4 points above the threshold. That is a cushion of about 6–9M ballots.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Path to YES (dominant).** Final certified ballots end up near 124M or more, and CVAP lands around 250M. The EAVS shows about 49–50%. Credible reporting is already consistent, so the market may resolve early.

**Paths to NO (all low-likelihood):**
- **Denominator surprise.** The EAC would need a CVAP of about 265M or more to push 124M below 46.8%. That is far above any Census trajectory.
- **Ballot-count collapse.** Final EAVS F1a would need to be about 117M or less, roughly 5–7M below current tallies. No mechanism for that is evident, since counts only rise. EAVS reporting gaps, such as states leaving F1a blank, have not historically lowered the national total much; 2022 EAVS (112.05M) roughly matched McDonald's count.
- **Procedural risk.** The EAC does not publish the 2026 EAVS, and a successor source is disputed. The rules provide a fallback to comparable official data, which would also show an increase, so this risk is mainly about timing and ambiguity rather than direction.

## 5. Signposts

- **Updated UF Election Lab totals and rate.**
  - Toward YES: totals rising toward 125M or more. Any VEP rate above about 47.5% makes YES near-certain.
  - Toward NO: a sharp downward revision below about 118M would be the main thing that matters, and nothing like this is reported.
- **California and Washington final counts:** these can only push totals up.
- **Census release of ACS-based 2025 CVAP (the likely 2026 EAVS denominator):** a figure above 255M would narrow the margin slightly.
- **News on EAC operations or funding, or the 2026 EAVS timeline:** this affects resolution timing, not direction.
- **Whether the market operator resolves early on credible reporting:** this affects timing only.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not read the Election Lab or McDonald pages directly. The 122.7M vs 124.3M discrepancy (counted so far vs projected final) is not resolved.
- The exact CVAP vintage the EAC will use for 2026 is not established. It is likely an ACS 2025 estimate of about 247–251M, but I have not verified this.
- Whether the EAC will publish the 2026 EAVS on schedule given political and budget pressure. The survey instrument was finalized in 2026, but I found no post-election confirmation.
- The historical gap between EAVS F1a and McDonald totals is only confirmed for 2022 (very close). I did not verify it for other years.
- The VEP series values in §2 are from memory and should be verified if needed.
