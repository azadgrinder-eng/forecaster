# Research Brief: Will Abiy Ahmed stop being Prime Minister of Ethiopia by December 31, 2026?

*Prepared 2026-11-24. About 37 days remain in the window (Nov 24 – Dec 31, 2026, 11:59 PM ET). Every attempt to open a full page failed ("proxy refused the connection"). This brief therefore rests on search-result summaries of the cited articles, not on the articles themselves. Treat figures from single sources with caution.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Abiy Ahmed stops being Prime Minister "for any period of time" between market creation and Dec 31, 2026, 11:59 PM ET. Any of these also counts:
  - an *announcement* of his resignation or removal within the window, even if it takes effect later;
  - his detention;
  - his "effective removal" from the post;
  - his being "permanently prevented from fulfilling the duties."
- **Who decides:** The primary source is official information from Abiy and the Ethiopian government. A consensus of credible reporting can also be used.
- **Edge cases and ambiguities:**
  - **Re-inauguration on Oct 5, 2026.** Parliament re-elected him and he was sworn in for a new five-year term after the June 2026 election (Africanews, Oct 5, 2026; Council of Ministers of Abiy Ahmed, Wikipedia). There was no break in office, and the creation date of this "in 2026" market is unknown. I see no realistic way that handover would be read as him "ceasing" to be PM. He held office continuously.
  - **Death or incapacity.** Death would clearly resolve YES. Temporary incapacity, such as a hospital stay with a deputy acting, is murky. The clause says "*permanently* prevented," so a short absence probably does not count.
  - **Rumor risk.** Ethiopian diaspora and insurgent social media regularly spread unverified claims about the leader's health, whereabouts or a coup. The "consensus of credible reporting" standard should screen these out, but a genuinely chaotic event, such as a coup attempt with unclear results, could produce a disputed resolution.
  - **Leaving Addis Ababa.** Moving to the front, as Abiy did in Nov 2021, is not removal.
  - A government in exile or a rival "transitional government" declared by rebels would not count unless he actually lost control.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Ethiopian heads of government since 1974 (my own count from historical knowledge).**
- Exits: Haile Selassie (deposed Sept 1974); Aman Andom (killed Nov 1974); Tafari Benti (killed Feb 1977); Mengistu (fled May 1991); Tesfaye Gebre Kidan (May 1991, one week); Meles Zenawi (died in office Aug 2012); Hailemariam Desalegn (resignation announced Feb 2018).
- That is about 7 exits in 52 years (≈0.13 per year), but they cluster in the revolutionary periods of 1974–77 and 1991.
- Counting only the long-serving consolidated leaders (Haile Selassie, Mengistu, Meles, Hailemariam), it is 4 exits in about 52 years, roughly 0.08 per year.
- **Implied rate for a 37-day window: about 0.8–1.3%.**
- Only one of these exits (Mengistu, 1991) came from an armed rebellion beating the state. That followed years of military collapse, with rebels at the capital's gates.

**B. Authoritarian or hybrid incumbents worldwide, outside an election or term-limit window.**
- Leader-tenure datasets such as Archigos put the overall annual exit rate for non-democratic leaders at roughly 10–15%. Most of that comes from scheduled or regular transitions.
- Irregular exits (coup, revolt, assassination, forced resignation) run roughly 2–4% per year. These are approximate figures from memory of the literature, not something I recomputed.
- Abiy has just started a fresh five-year term with about 90% of seats (438 of 547 per one report; Rio Times / Africanews, Oct 2026). Regular exit is close to zero, so the irregular rate is what matters.
- **Implied rate for 37 days: about 0.2–0.4% irregular; about 0.5–1% including death from natural causes. Abiy is roughly 50, so actuarial risk is tiny.**

**C. Incumbents facing an active multi-front civil war and/or interstate war (Abiy's present situation).**
- Irregular removal rates for wartime leaders in fragile states plausibly run 2–3 times the authoritarian baseline: perhaps 5–10% per year, or **about 0.5–1% over 37 days**.
- **Abiy's own record (from historical knowledge) shows resilience:**
  - survived a grenade attack at a June 2018 rally;
  - survived armed soldiers marching on the palace in Oct 2018;
  - survived the June 2019 Amhara "coup attempt," in which the army chief and the Amhara regional president were assassinated;
  - survived the Nov 2021 TPLF advance to within about 200 km of Addis, under a state of emergency.
- None of these removed him. Today the military threat to the capital is clearly smaller than in Nov 2021.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (all low-probability within 37 days):**
1. **Military coup or palace coup.** This would most likely be triggered by battlefield disaster in a full war with Eritrea, or by mass casualties in Tigray or Amhara. It depends on officer-corps cohesion. Abiy has rotated and promoted loyal commanders, and army chief Birhanu Jula is a loyalist; I found no reported dissent. Signs of strain exist inside the Prosperity Party's regional branches (Addis Standard; The Reporter, both undated), but nothing points to an imminent move.
2. **Assassination or targeted strike.** There is precedent: the 2018 grenade attack and the 2019 killings. Rebels have shown drone reach near Addis (the Bishoftu strike, Sept 27). The chance of this within any given 37 days is very low, but it is not zero.
3. **Rebel military victory or collapse of the capital, as in 1991.** This would require Fano, OLA or TPLF forces, possibly with Eritrean backing, to threaten Addis within weeks. Current reporting shows federal forces *on the offensive*: they hold Mekelle, retook towns in Amhara, and have pushed the TPLF toward the border. This path is very unlikely by Dec 31.
4. **Negotiated exit or forced resignation under foreign pressure.** No sign of it. Abiy was just re-elected and is consolidating; the US, G7 and AU are calling for restraint, not transition.
5. **Sudden death or incapacitating illness.** Base-rate risk only, roughly 0.05% over 37 days for a man around 50.
6. **Resolution error from a false rumor.** Possible during a fog-of-war episode, but bounded by the "credible consensus" standard.

**Paths to NO (the default):** Abiy stays in office as a freshly re-elected PM with a supermajority, centralized control of the security services, and fresh Russian air assets. He is visibly governing and receiving foreign leaders as late as Nov 23. Every crisis he has faced since 2018 has left him in office.

## 5. Signposts

**Would raise the probability of YES (most to least important):**
- Credible reports, from Reuters, AP, AFP or BBC Amharic, of a coup attempt, unusual troop movements in Addis, a communications blackout in the capital, or Abiy missing from public view for more than about 10 days.
- Full-scale war with Eritrea going badly: Ethiopian forces routed near Badme or Assab, mass casualties, or Eritrean or TPLF advances into Afar or Amhara.
- A coordinated Fano and OLA push to within about 50 km of Addis, embassies ordering staff to leave, or a nationwide state of emergency.
- Senior defections or arrests: the army chief, the intelligence head Redwan Hussein, or regional Prosperity Party leaders, especially in Oromia or Amhara.
- A successful drone or other attack on government sites in Addis.

**Would lower the probability (reinforce NO):** routine public appearances (parliament Q&A, foreign visits); ceasefire or mediation progress with Eritrea or the TPLF; continued federal battlefield gains.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not read any primary article in full. All page fetches were refused, so details come from search snippets. Exact dates, such as the June 1 election date and the 438-of-547 seat figure, rest on secondary summaries.
- I found nothing dated Nov 23–24 on the Eritrea front. The scale and outcome of the reported Nov 22 renewed offensive are unconfirmed.
- The internal state of the military and the Prosperity Party is opaque. A coup in a closed system can come without public warning.
- Whether a full Ethiopia–Eritrea war breaks out before Dec 31, and how it goes, is the biggest swing factor and is unresolved.
- The base-rate figures in Section 2 are my approximations from historical knowledge and the leader-tenure literature, not freshly computed from a dataset.
- I do not know when the market was created, which only matters if it predates the Oct 5 re-inauguration, and that is very unlikely to matter.
