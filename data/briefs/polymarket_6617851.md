# Research brief: Will Israeli airspace (Tel Aviv FIR/LLLL) close by Nov 30, 2026?

*Method note: Almost every WebFetch call failed with "proxy refused the connection," so this brief is built from search-result summaries of the cited articles. I could not read the full articles. Treat fine detail as less certain than usual.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Between the market's creation and **Nov 30, 2026 at 11:59 PM ET** (Dec 1, 04:59 UTC), Israel's own authorities (Ministry of Transport, Civil Aviation Authority or Israel Airports Authority) issue a NOTAM or official order that, by default, suspends commercial flights across the whole Tel Aviv FIR. That means flights transiting, arriving and departing. Exceptions for humanitarian, repatriation or specially approved flights do not disqualify it.
- **Time left:** About 11.5 days from today (Nov 19).
- **No minimum duration.** A closure of a few hours appears to qualify. It counts from when it takes effect. If no effective time is stated, it counts from when the order is issued.
- **What does not count:**
  - Weather or seismic closures.
  - Holiday closures. Yom Kippur 2026 has already passed.
  - Closures announced before the contract was issued.
  - Technical or ATC failures.
  - Restrictions on particular airlines or countries.
  - Closures of only part of the airspace, such as the north or certain altitudes.
  - Ground stops, flow or capacity limits. This matters: Israel has often used "reduced operations" or caps on flights per hour instead of a full closure.
  - Foreign actions, such as EASA bulletins, FAA bans or airlines cancelling routes.
- **Edge cases and ambiguities:**
  - **Airport vs. airspace.** A halt at Ben Gurion during a missile barrage (as reported in March 2026) is not necessarily a closure of the whole FIR. In past episodes, though, Israel issued airspace-wide closures, and press reports often blur the two.
  - **Short pauses.** If Israel stops all departures and arrivals for under an hour during sirens and calls it an "operational halt," it is unclear whether that is a closure or excluded ATM measure (viii).
  - **Market creation date.** The exact date is not known to me. A Nov 2 version of the market page exists, so it was created on or before then. No qualifying closure has been reported since then.
- **Who decides:** The market operator, based on official Israeli sources and a consensus of credible reporting.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Israeli full airspace closures that were not for holidays, since April 2024.** Distinct trigger events:
1. Apr 13–14, 2024: Iranian drone and missile attack; overnight closure.
2. Oct 1, 2024: Iranian missile attack; brief closure.
3. Jun 13, 2025: Israel strikes Iran; multi-day closure, and closed again on Jun 22, 2025 after US strikes on Fordow, Natanz and Isfahan (Free Press Journal / GMA, Jun 22, 2025).
4. Feb 28, 2026: joint US–Israeli attack on Iran; closure until further notice, then a phased reopening in March (Israel Hayom, Feb 28, 2026; Haaretz, Mar 4–5, 2026). Safe Airspace records several further closures and reopenings between February and July 2026.

That is roughly 4–5 trigger episodes in about 31 months (~950 days), or about 0.004–0.005 per day. Over a 12-day window that gives about **5–6%**. This is an unconditional base rate. It understates risk during periods of high tension and overstates it during calm.

**B. What triggers a closure.**
- **Israeli-initiated or joint strikes on Iran:** 3 of 3 led to a closure (Jun 2025, Jun 22, 2025 US strikes, Feb 2026).
- **Iranian missile salvos at Israel outside a full war:** 2 of 3 led to a closure (Apr 2024 yes, Oct 2024 yes, **Jun 7–8, 2026 no**). In June 2026 the Transport Ministry deliberately decided not to close despite about 30 Iranian ballistic missiles plus Houthi fire (Globes, Jun 8, 2026; Calcalist).
- **US-only strikes on Iran during the 2026 ceasefire:** 0 known closures. These include the May 7, 2026 strikes and the Nov 17, 2026 Qeshm strike. I could not fully confirm May.
- **Hezbollah or Houthi fire alone:** no full closures found for 2024–2026. Responses were partial or northern-only.

So the probability depends mostly on whether a large Israeli or joint offensive against Iran, or a large Iranian salvo at central Israel, happens before Nov 30.

**C. Trump's announced pauses on Iran strikes in 2026.** Pauses were announced in May (May 19), August (Aug 2) and November (Nov 9). Reports say earlier pauses "unraveled" and fighting resumed, but the time from pause to resumption was usually weeks, not days (Gulf Today, Nov 10, 2026; Business Today, May 19, 2026).

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES:**
1. **A joint US–Israeli or Israeli-only offensive on Iran resumes before Nov 30.** This is the main path. Every precedent led to a closure, and the Nov 2 plan explicitly calls for a closure of up to 12 hours. Its likelihood depends on:
   - whether Mossad and Dermer's lobbying overcomes Trump's Nov 9 pause and the Gulf states' opposition;
   - Israel's own threat assessment, which the IDF currently says is not "imminent";
   - Iranian provocations.
2. **A large Iranian salvo at central Israel.** For example, in retaliation for US strikes if Iran decides Israel is complicit. This might prompt a precautionary closure, but the June 2026 precedent shows Israel may now stay open.
3. **A surprise preemptive Israeli strike** in a short window when Washington is distracted. In past episodes the closure came at H-hour, often in the early morning.

**Paths to NO:**
1. US–Iran tit-for-tat stays confined to the Gulf (Qeshm, Kuwait, Bahrain), as on Nov 17–18.
2. Trump's pause holds through the end of the month, with blockade and pressure instead of strikes.
3. Even if Iran fires at Israel, Israel applies its "stay open" or reduced-operations approach. Flow restrictions are excluded under (viii).
4. Any escalation slips into December. A sibling market for Dec 31 exists, and the time window here is very short.

## 5. Signposts

**Toward YES (strong):**
- Israeli or US statements that talks have failed, or a Trump ultimatum.
- Large deployments: more tankers or bombers, carrier movements.
- Embassy ordered departures.
- El Al or Israir suddenly moving aircraft abroad, or Israel Airports Authority (IAA) or Transport Minister Regev statements about "preparations."
- Home Front Command changing guidance nationwide.
- Iran explicitly threatening Israel in retaliation for US strikes.
- Any NOTAM restricting the LLLL FIR.

**Toward YES (moderate):**
- More Hezbollah or Houthi long-range fire at central Israel.
- Reuters or Axios reporting that Trump has "approved" joint strikes.

**Toward NO:**
- Qatar- or Oman-mediated talks moving forward.
- Statements from Trump or the White House again ruling out "major combat operations."
- More foreign airlines announcing a return to Tel Aviv for December.
- Iranian retaliation staying focused on the Gulf.
- Calm passing Nov 25–26 with no buildup, since only a few days would remain.
