# Research brief: "NNN האם אייל ישרוד את ה…" ("NNN: Will Eyal survive the…")

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **The question is cut off and has no resolution criteria.** The Hebrew reads "NNN: Will Eyal survive the [missing word]". The market closes 2026-11-30 at 20:07 UTC, which is 22:07 Israel time.
- **Most likely reading: "No Nut November."** "NNN" is the common internet shorthand for "No Nut November," a challenge to abstain from masturbation and orgasm for the whole calendar month. The missing word is probably "החודש" ("the month") or "האתגר" ("the challenge"). The close date at the end of November fits this well. If so, this is a personal or social market about a private person named Eyal, probably a friend of the creator, or the creator himself.
  - **YES** if Eyal gets through all of November 2026 without "failing."
  - **NO** if he fails at any point. This could already have happened in the first eight days.
  - **Who decides:** the market creator, almost certainly based on Eyal's own report. Nobody can check it independently.
  - **Edge cases:**
    - The rules of NNN differ from person to person (masturbation only vs. any orgasm, and whether sex with a partner or nocturnal emissions count).
    - The market closes about 3.5 hours before November ends in UTC and about 2 hours before it ends in Israel time, so the last evening may not be covered.
    - The creator may resolve whenever he learns of a failure, or may wait until December.
    - Eyal may be the creator or may be trading in the market himself, which creates conflicts of interest.
- **Other readings I checked and judged less likely:**
  - **(b) A reality-show contestant surviving an eviction.** Survivor Israel 2026 ("הישרדות קרמואן," Channel 13, premiered 24 Oct 2026) is on air now. The cast lists I found show no contestant named Eyal. The 2026 Big Brother season ended in June.
  - **(c) IDF Chief of Staff Eyal Zamir "surviving" in his post.** He is the only prominent current "Eyal" in Israeli news, and there were election-eve reports that Netanyahu would fire him. But nothing in the question links it to him, and the "NNN" prefix fits poorly.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
- **Completion rate for NNN (main reading).** I found no rigorous data. Informal online polls in NNN communities, and the meme culture around the challenge, generally treat failing as the norm and finishing as the exception. Treat any specific number as weak. A rough prior for a self-selected participant finishing all 30 days might be somewhere around 20–50%. That range is my estimate, not a sourced statistic.
- **Self-reported commitment markets on Manifold.** Markets where the subject reports his own success tend to lean toward YES. Possible reasons:
  - The subject wants to look successful.
  - Small failures can be redefined or explained away.
  - A public market adds social pressure to finish, or at least to say he finished.
  
  On the other hand, friends often make these markets as jokes expecting failure, and a friend may happily report a failure.
- **Conditional survival.** Today is November 8. If the market is still open and unresolved, Eyal has probably lasted about 8 days, or at least has not reported a failure. Failures in abstinence challenges tend to come early in the month, so having lasted 8 days raises the chance of finishing compared with the day-1 prior.
- **Zamir reading (c).** Early exits by IDF chiefs of staff are rare:
  - Elazar resigned in 1974 after the Agranat report.
  - Halutz resigned in 2007 after the Second Lebanon War.
  - Halevi announced his resignation in January 2025 over October 7.
  
  That is about 3 of roughly 23 chiefs, and none was fired outright by a government mid-term. The chance of an exit in any given 3-week window is well under 1% normally. It is higher now because of political tension, but forming a new government takes weeks.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
- **YES (NNN):** Eyal's commitment, the social pressure of a public market, money he may have bet on himself, generous self-reporting, and the possibility that the market closes before the last evening of November.
- **NO (NNN):** the usual high failure rate. A friend running the market as a joke may resolve NO quickly on any admission. The question may also already be decided if Eyal failed during Nov 1–8 and the creator hasn't resolved yet.
- **Under reading (c):**
  - NO would need Netanyahu to form a government quickly and move to fire Zamir before Nov 30, or Zamir to resign over a clash.
  - YES is the default, especially if Eisenkot holds the mandate.

## 5. Signposts
- **Market comments or updates from the creator or Eyal**, such as "still going on day X." Each day he survives raises the chance of YES. Any admission of failure means NO.
- **Whether Eyal is the creator or holds shares.** This makes self-reported YES bias more likely.
- **Any clarification from the creator about the missing word or the rules.** If it turns out to be about Zamir or a TV show, the analysis changes completely.
- **For reading (c):** who forms the government. A quick Netanyahu coalition plus renewed firing reports would lower the chance; an Eisenkot mandate or a deadlock would raise it.
