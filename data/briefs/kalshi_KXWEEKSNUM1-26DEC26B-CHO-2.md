# Research Brief: Will "Choosin' Texas" by Ella Langley hold #1 on the Billboard Hot 100 for at least 2 weeks in December 2026?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** "Choosin' Texas" must be #1 on at least 2 Billboard Hot 100 charts that fall "in December 2026."
- **Which charts those are:** Billboard dates each chart for a Saturday. The December 2026 charts are dated **Dec 5, Dec 12, Dec 19 and Dec 26**. Billboard publishes each one on the Tuesday before its date (Dec 1, 8, 15 and 22).
- **Same answer under either reading:** It doesn't matter whether the question means the date printed on the chart or the date Billboard publishes it. Either way the same four charts count.
  - The Nov 28 chart is published Nov 24, so it is out.
  - The Jan 2, 2027 chart is published about Dec 29, which is after the Dec 26 04:59 UTC close, so it is also out.
- **Tracking weeks behind each chart (Friday to Thursday):**
  - Dec 5 chart: Nov 20–26. This window includes Thanksgiving Day, Nov 26.
  - Dec 12 chart: Nov 27–Dec 3. This is the first full week after Thanksgiving.
  - Dec 19 chart: Dec 4–10.
  - Dec 26 chart: Dec 11–17.
- **Who decides:** Billboard's published Hot 100, using Luminate data.
- **Edge cases:**
  - The weeks don't need to be consecutive. Any 2 of the 4 charts are enough.
  - Billboard sometimes delays a chart around holidays. That could matter if the Dec 26 chart came out late, but publication should be Dec 22, before the close.
  - Holiday songs are fully eligible for the Hot 100.
  - The recurrent rule (which drops songs over 52 weeks old once they fall below #25) does not apply to a #1 song.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Who is #1 on the December charts.** Holiday songs have taken most December #1s for several years:

| Year | December #1s | Weeks for a non-holiday song |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 | Dec 6: Taylor Swift, "The Fate of Ophelia" (its 8th week). Dec 13, 20, 27: Mariah Carey, "All I Want for Christmas Is You" (Top40Weekly; Popculturemadness; KPBS, Dec 11 & 18 2025) | 1 of 4 |
| 2024 | Dec 7: Kendrick Lamar, "Squabble Up" (from my memory). Dec 14, 21, 28: Mariah Carey (Uproxx; KBTX, Dec 12 2024) | 1 of 4 |
| 2023 | Dec 2: "Lovin On Me" (from my memory). Dec 9 and 16: Brenda Lee, "Rockin' Around the Christmas Tree". Dec 23 and 30: Mariah Carey | 1 of 5 |
| 2022 | Dec 3 and Dec 10: Taylor Swift, "Anti-Hero". Dec 17, 24, 31: Mariah Carey (Uproxx; ABS-CBN, Dec 15 2022) | **2 of 5** |

- **Base rate:** In 2022–2025, a non-holiday song got 2 or more December #1 weeks in **1 of 4 years** (25%).
- **Better match for 2026:** 2024 and 2025 had four December charts and the same Thanksgiving timing as 2026. In both, the non-holiday song got only 1 December week (0 of 2 years reached 2).
- **Earlier years (from memory, not verified):** In 2019–2021 the holiday takeover came later, around the third December chart. The trend is toward earlier holiday #1s.

**B. The first chart covering a full week after Thanksgiving.** This chart is where the question will likely be decided. A non-holiday song held #1 on it in 2022 (Anti-Hero, Dec 10, with Mariah at #2). It lost in 2023 (Brenda Lee), 2024 (Mariah) and 2025 (Mariah). That is **1 of 4 holds**. In 2026 this chart is dated **Dec 12**.

**C. Christmas-week charts (Dec 19 and Dec 26 equivalents).** A holiday song took #1 on every one of these in 2022–2025, so 0 of about 8 went to a non-holiday song. The one exception I know of from memory is Taylor Swift's "Willow" debuting at #1 in Dec 2020, a surprise-album debut rather than an older song.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Chart dated Nov 21, 2026 (published about Nov 17):** "Choosin' Texas" was #1 for a **28th week**, extending its all-time record. Its numbers that week were **17.8M streams, 48.7M radio audience, 4K sales** (Whiskey Riff, about Nov 17 2026).
  - Search snippets said the rest of the top 3 was Stella Lefty's "Boston" back at its #2 peak and "Dracula" at #3, with "Patient Zero" and a Taylor Swift song back in the top 10. I could not confirm the chart page or the margin over #2.
  - The song also led Hot Country Songs for a 49th week.
  - Whiskey Riff mentioned the song "fresh off" **7 Grammy nominations** for the 2027 Grammys, and the possibility of a 30th week at #1 by late November.
- **Chart dated Nov 14, 2026:** #1 for the 27th week. Michael Jackson's "Thriller" reached a new #2 peak, which suggests a Halloween-driven seasonal spike briefly came close (Billboard countdown; Wikipedia 2026 list).
- **Sept 22, 2026:** The song's 23rd week made it the longest-running Hot 100 #1 of all time, passing Mariah Carey's 21 total weeks for "All I Want for Christmas Is You" (Billboard; NPR/Hawaii News Now, Sept 22 2026).
- **Run history:** It debuted on the chart dated Nov 1, 2025. It first hit #1 in February 2026. The run has not been continuous: at one point it came back "two months after its peak" for a 5th week (AOL).
- **Holiday competitor strength in 2025:** Mariah Carey's song drew **39.5M streams and 22.6M radio audience** in the week ending Dec 11, 2025 (Dec 20 chart). In the week ending Dec 18 (Dec 27 chart) it drew **43.6M streams and 28M audience** (rttnews/KPBS, Dec 2025).
  - That is more than double Choosin' Texas's current streaming, though with about half its radio audience.
  - In 2025, "Fate of Ophelia," a strong current hit with heavy radio play, fell from #1 to #6 on the first full post-Thanksgiving chart, behind several holiday songs.
- **Release calendar Nov 20–Dec 2026:** I found no blockbuster US pop or hip-hop album dated Nov 20 or Nov 27. Listed Nov 20 releases include Take That and Down, plus a Sufjan Stevens Christmas album sometime in November (Rolling Stone; Authority Daily). December releases were not covered.
- **Upcoming:**
  - Nov 24: Nov 28 chart published.
  - Dec 1: Dec 5 chart.
  - Dec 8: Dec 12 chart, the key one.
  - Dec 15 and Dec 22: Dec 19 and Dec 26 charts.
  - Billboard's Holiday 100 chart, which comes out weekly in this season, gives early signals of how strong holiday streaming is.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Path to YES (all of these must happen):**
1. It stays #1 on the **Dec 5** chart (tracking Nov 20–26). I judge this likely if no big release drops on Nov 20: in 2024 and 2025 the non-holiday leader held this week.
2. It also takes **one of Dec 12, 19 or 26**. Realistically that means Dec 12, as Anti-Hero managed in 2022. That requires a weaker or later holiday surge, or Choosin' Texas staying unusually strong.
   - Possible helpers: Grammy-nomination attention, being a Thanksgiving/holiday-gathering crowd favorite, and country radio, which mostly doesn't switch to Christmas music.

**Paths to NO:**
- **Main path:** Holiday songs take #1 from the Dec 12 chart onward, as they did in 2023–2025. Choosin' Texas then gets at most 1 December week.
- **Weak streaming:** Its 17.8M streams are far below Mariah's roughly 35–44M in December. Its radio lead (48.7M vs 22–28M) will shrink as pop and adult-contemporary stations switch to all-Christmas formats and Christmas songs gain radio play.
- **Losing Dec 5 as well:** A new release (surprise album or big debut) or an early holiday surge could take Dec 5, making YES nearly impossible.
- **Other threats:** "Boston" (at #2) overtaking it as Choosin' Texas declines in its 13th month. A surprise single in the style of "Willow" debuting in December.

## 5. Signposts

- **Nov 28 chart (Nov 24):** The size of the lead and the direction of streams. A drop below about 16M streams, or #2 closing in, lowers the odds. Watch how high Mariah and Brenda Lee sit already.
- **Dec 5 chart (Dec 1):** If Choosin' Texas loses #1 here, YES is close to 0. If it holds, check where Mariah ranks. Mariah at #2 with a small gap, like 2022, is bad for YES on Dec 12. Mariah still outside the top 5 is good.
- **Holiday 100 and streaming data in the Thanksgiving week:** If Mariah's streams top about 30M in the Nov 27–Dec 3 week, NO is very likely.
- **Surprise releases on Nov 20, Nov 27 or Dec 4** from Taylor Swift, Drake, Kendrick Lamar, Morgan Wallen or similar artists would push toward NO.
- **Dec 12 chart (Dec 8):** This effectively settles the question. If Choosin' Texas holds, YES is assured, provided it also held Dec 5.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not open the Billboard chart pages (the connection was refused), so I don't know the exact point margin over #2 on the Nov 21 chart. I also couldn't confirm the "Boston" and "Dracula" positions.
- I don't know Billboard's exact formula for weighting streams against radio audience. So I can't say precisely how 17.8M streams plus 48.7M audience compares with Mariah's numbers. The 2025 "Fate of Ophelia" result suggests a song with that kind of profile loses to the holiday surge.
- Mariah's stream and radio numbers for her first #1 week of 2025 (Dec 13 chart) were not found. I only have data for later weeks.
- Late-November and December 2026 release schedules are incomplete, and surprise drops can't be known in advance.
- The 2019–2021 holiday timing and the 2023–2024 early-December #1s are from my memory, not verified sources.
- One snippet said Mariah's record was "27 weeks." That conflicts with Guinness and NPR, which say 20–21, so I treated it as an error.
