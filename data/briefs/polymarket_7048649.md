# Research brief: Will IMF PortWatch's 7-day average of Strait of Hormuz transit calls reach 40 or more on any date through Dec 31, 2026?

*Prepared 2026-11-22. I could not open any page directly; every fetch was blocked by the network proxy. The figures below come from search-result summaries of secondary trackers and news outlets that cite IMF PortWatch, not from the PortWatch dashboard itself. Treat exact values as approximate.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** the 7-day moving average of transit calls for the Strait of Hormuz must be **≥40** on at least one date between market creation and Dec 31, 2026. The figure is the **rounded value shown in the chart** on PortWatch's chokepoint page. So an unrounded 39.5 probably displays as 40 and counts, though PortWatch's rounding rule is not confirmed.
- **Fallback:** if the chart is unavailable, the downloadable figure, rounded to the nearest whole number, is used.
- **Timing:**
  - PortWatch publishes with a lag of several days. On Nov 19–21, the latest cited daily value was for Nov 15 (Straits Daily Brief, Nov 19–21, 2026).
  - The market waits until data for every date through Dec 31 is published. If anything is still missing 14 days after Dec 31, it resolves on whatever is published then.
  - Revisions after that point are ignored, except under the data-integrity clause (a 7-day correction window for clearly erroneous data).
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Start date:** "Market creation" is undefined in the text I have. The 7-day averages in the first days of the war (Feb 28 to early March) probably exceeded 40 because they still included pre-war days. That would only matter if the market was created before then, which looks unlikely. Forecasters should check the creation date.
  - **Revisions:** PortWatch is AIS-based and sometimes revises recent days. A brief spike above 40 that is later revised down, or the reverse, could be contested.
  - **What PortWatch counts:** it counts only AIS-visible ships, reportedly limited to vessels of at least 80 m or 5,000 dwt (hormuz.now, Nov 2026). It **sharply undercounts "dark" transits** (ships with AIS switched off). This is the single most important measurement issue (see §3).

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. De-escalation episodes in this crisis (most relevant).**
- **April 8 two-week ceasefire with "safe passage."** The US blockaded Iranian ports at the same time (Apr 13 – May 29). PortWatch monthly averages were about 8.5/day in April and 6.5/day in May (secondary tracker figures). Bloomberg did report a war-high weekly rolling average in early April (Bloomberg, Apr 4, 2026), but nothing close to 40.
- **June 17 Islamabad Memorandum.** The US lifted its blockade on June 18 and the reopening was toll-free.
  - The PortWatch 7-day mean rose from about 5 to a **peak of about 31.9 on June 29**, 12 days after signing (single unofficial analysis citing PortWatch).
  - The best single day was **51 on June 24** (hormuz.now timeline).
  - Al Jazeera, citing PortWatch, counted 513 transits from June 18 to July 5, about **28/day** (Al Jazeera, Jul 9, 2026).
  - The truce collapsed on July 8 and the blockade was reimposed on July 14.
- **Base rate: 0 of 2 de-escalation episodes produced a PortWatch 7-day average of 40 or more.** The best one, a signed agreement plus the blockade lifted, peaked about 20% below the threshold after roughly 12 days.

**B. Whole-war history since Feb 28, 2026.** Since about the first week of March, the 7-day average appears never to have reached 40 in nearly 9 months. I could not verify a full series. Monthly averages run from roughly 3 to 15/day: June was the busiest month at 12.9–15.1/day depending on the tracker, October about 3.8/day, and early September about 3/day.

**C. Other chokepoint disruptions.** After the Houthi campaign began in late 2023, Red Sea / Bab el-Mandeb traffic stayed far below normal for years, even through ceasefire periods. Insurers say they want "months of sustained stability" before premiums ease (Khaleej Times; S&P Global, 2026). This is from general knowledge plus 2026 insurance reporting, not a computed frequency. Chokepoint traffic recovers in weeks to months, not days.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 21–22:**
  - Two tankers were struck near Khasab, Oman (UKMTO via Al Jazeera, Channels TV, Critical Threats, Nov 21).
  - Iran's deputy foreign minister said a Qatari delegation visited Tehran, but the talks are "not yet formal negotiations." Araghchi said reopening is "not difficult" if the US accepts Iran's conditions, and insists a Hormuz deal must come before nuclear talks (Al Jazeera, Nov 21).
  - Trump told Fox he wants a "permanent" deal preventing an Iranian nuclear weapon.
- **Nov 20:**
  - Trump told Axios he would **not decide on his next steps on Iran until the new year** (reported Nov 20–21). This is a key bearish timing signal for a December resolution.
  - Windward counted 16 transits on Nov 20, of which 7 were dark.
- **Nov 19–21:** Straits.live describes the strait as "effectively closed to commercial shipping." PortWatch recorded **2 transits on Nov 15**, against a pre-crisis baseline of about 85/day.
- **7 days to Nov 15:** about **2.3/day** on PortWatch (hormuz.now). Another figure puts it at 3.9/day, or 4% of the 87.4/day average for Nov 2024 – Nov 2025.
- **Week ending Nov 18:** UKMTO reported 263 transits, down from 308 the week before, and said traffic was about 65% below pre-war levels, with 6 tanker attacks that week and 106 incidents since Feb 28 (Iran International, Nov 21).
  - **These counts include dark and US-facilitated transits that UKMTO says it cannot verify.** They are roughly 10x PortWatch's count.
- **Oct–Nov:** attacks in and around the strait were higher in October and November than in any earlier month of the war (ABC News, Nov 2026).
  - S&P Global counted about 750 observed and assumed crossings in October, roughly 24/day.
  - Lloyd's List counted 550 non-Iranian-linked transits in October, about 18/day and 121/week on average from Oct 5–25. Its monitor shows 145 transits for Oct 26 – Nov 1.
  - PortWatch's October average was about 3.8/day.
- **Nov 2–4:** Iran floated a plan to reopen the strait, with implementation shortened from 7 days to 2. The US reply was called "unconvincing," and US strikes hit Qeshm Island (Al Arabiya, Nov 2; Al Jazeera, Nov 4).
- **Nov 9:** Trump met Qatar's prime minister. Rubio said the talks are "not close to a deal" (Axios, Fox, Nov 9).
- **Ongoing:**
  - The US blockade of Iran-linked shipping, reimposed in mid-July, was still in force in mid-November (hormuz.now FAQ).
  - War-risk premiums were 3–8% of hull value at end-August, against 0.25% before the war (Al Jazeera, Jul 23; S&P).
  - Mine-threat concerns persist (BIMCO, INTERTANKO).
- **Scheduled before close:** no known scheduled summit or deadline. The Trump "new year" statement points to US decisions after Dec 31. The holiday period may slow diplomacy. PortWatch data for late December will be published in early January.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES** (all require a large, fast increase from about 2–4/day to an average of 40, meaning roughly 280 PortWatch-counted transits in some 7-day window ending by Dec 31):

1. **A quick deal by about Dec 10–15.** This would most likely be the Qatari draft: the blockade eased and funds unfrozen in exchange for reopening and inspections. After that, a **faster and stronger ramp than in June** would be needed. Possible reasons it could be faster:
   - pent-up demand, with ships stuck inside the Gulf and loaded cargoes waiting;
   - Iran's proposed 2-day implementation;
   - ships switching AIS back on once the threat is gone, which would raise PortWatch counts sharply even before real traffic changes much.
   - **Against this:** in June the same setup topped out at about 32 after 12 days.
2. **AIS behaviour changes without a deal.** If US-escorted convoys or tolerated passages began broadcasting AIS, PortWatch counts could converge toward UKMTO's (~38/day) or Lloyd's (~20/day) levels. This would be odd given current attacks, but the gap between data sources is large enough to matter.
3. **Data error or reclassification.** A PortWatch glitch could produce a spike. The integrity clause makes such a spike unlikely to count if it is clearly erroneous.

**Paths to NO:**

- **Status quo (most likely):** no formal talks, a stated US decision timeline after Dec 31, attacks at a war high, the blockade in force, and PortWatch at about 2–8/day.
- **A deal arrives late in December.** Even a deal signed around Dec 15–20 probably ramps too slowly, based on the June pattern.
- **Escalation:** renewed US strikes and further tanker attacks.

## 5. Signposts

- **Strongly toward YES:**
  - announcement of a signed US–Iran framework or ceasefire, or the blockade being lifted, **before about Dec 15**;
  - Iran publicly guaranteeing passage, or the IRGC halting attacks;
  - PortWatch daily counts above 30 for several consecutive days;
  - Lloyd's or UKMTO reporting AIS-on convoys;
  - Joint War Committee or insurers easing the war-risk listing;
  - confirmed mine clearance.
- **Moderately toward YES:** formal expert-level talks starting; Witkoff or Kushner meeting Iranian officials; Qatar announcing an agreed text.
- **Toward NO:** further tanker strikes; US strikes on Iranian territory; Trump reaffirming a January decision; PortWatch weekly averages staying below 10 into mid-December. Any day after about Dec 20 without a deal makes reaching 40 nearly impossible given ramp times.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **No direct PortWatch access.** I could not view the PortWatch chart or download the series. The June peak of 31.9 comes from a single unofficial source. The June monthly figures conflict (12.9 vs 15.1/day), and the current 7-day value conflicts across sources (2.3, 3.9, 7.7/day).
- **I could not confirm whether any post–Feb 28 date reached 40.** This matters only if market creation predates early March.
- **The PortWatch-vs-other-trackers gap is unexplained.** PortWatch shows about 2–4/day while UKMTO shows about 38/day and Lloyd's about 20/day. Its cause (dark ships, vessel-size filters, Iranian-linked ships, unverified US-escort claims) determines how much a political shift would move PortWatch specifically.
- **The rounding rule for the chart is unknown.** It decides whether 39.5 counts as 40.
- **Status of the Qatari draft and US red lines** (enrichment, uranium stockpile) is unclear. So is whether Iran's "2-day reopening" would mean commercial ships returning in practice, given insurance and mine risk.
- **Current insurance premiums and mine-clearance status for November** are unknown; the latest data I found is from August.
