# Research brief: will November 2026 core CPI rise more than 0.3% month over month?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** BLS's seasonally adjusted month-over-month change in CPI-U all items less food and energy for November 2026 must be **0.4% or higher**, as BLS rounds it to one decimal. A print of 0.3% (unrounded roughly 0.25% to 0.349%) resolves NO. In practice the unrounded figure needs to be about 0.35% or more.
- **Release timing:** BLS publishes November 2026 CPI on **Thursday, December 10, 2026 at 8:30 a.m. ET** (BLS release schedule; FedRateCalc). The market closes at 13:25 UTC, which is 8:25 a.m. ET, five minutes before the release.
- **Who decides:** The single-decimal figure published by BLS. The first print should govern. Seasonal factors are not revised until February 2027.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Shutdown clause:** If a federal shutdown delays the data, the expiration moves to the earlier of the release or six months after the shutdown ends.
  - **Shutdown risk:** BLS has been operating normally (the October CPI came out on schedule on Nov 10). I could not confirm the status of FY2027 funding or any continuing-resolution deadline before Dec 10.
  - **2025 data gap:** The 2025 lapse means BLS publishes no 12-month changes for October 2026. That does not affect this month-over-month question.
  - **Housing distortion is finished:** The rent/OER distortion from the missing October 2025 data was absorbed in April 2026 (core printed 0.4% that month). No equivalent mechanical effect is documented for November 2026.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **Recent regime, all months (Jan 2024–Oct 2026):** core printed 0.4% or higher in about 5–6 of roughly 32 published months, about **15–19%**.
   - 2024: Jan, Feb and Mar at 0.4.
   - 2025: Jan at 0.4. October and November 2025 were not published monthly.
   - 2026: Apr at 0.4, the shutdown-related housing catch-up. July 2026 is unknown; it was not 0.3, since August–October were "three straight" 0.3% prints.
   - Monthly values come from my recall of BLS first prints and YCharts. 2026 figures for Jan–Jun were 0.3, 0.2, 0.2, 0.4, 0.2, 0.0 (YCharts).
2. **Novembers specifically (first prints, 2011–2024):** only **1 of 14** was 0.4% or higher (2021, 0.5%). Recent Novembers: 2022 0.2, 2023 0.3, 2024 0.3. Typical 2010s Novembers were 0.1–0.2 (from memory of BLS data). November has tended to run soft, with holiday discounting in apparel and lodging and weak used-car seasonals.
3. **After a run of 0.3% prints:** the closest analogue is Aug–Oct 2024, all 0.3%, followed by a November 2024 print of 0.3%. In 2023, the 0.3% prints in Aug–Sep were followed by 0.2% in October. Within this small sample, persistent 0.3% runs did not tip into 0.4%.
4. **Forecast-error framing:** consensus misses on core CPI are typically about ±0.1pp. In 2026 the misses have been to the upside: the consensus was 0.2% for both September and October, and both printed 0.3%. If the true expected value is about 0.25–0.30% with a standard deviation of about 0.08–0.10pp, the chance of reaching 0.35% or more is roughly 15–30%.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)
- **Nov 20, 2026:** The Cleveland Fed nowcast puts November core CPI at about **0.21% m/m** and headline at about **0.07% m/m**; core year over year about 2.87%, headline about 3.86% (Cleveland Fed via MacroMicro and search summaries). Its core model mostly tracks past core CPI and is weak. For October it nowcast about 0.20–0.21%, and the print was 0.3%.
- **Nov 19–20:** Fed officials are weighing a December hike, with some discussing 50bp. A Reuters poll expects a hike at the **Dec 8–9 FOMC**; the policy rate is now 3.75–4.00%. The October FOMC minutes flag upside inflation risks from tariffs, energy and the AI buildout (Reuters/Yahoo; FinScans, Nov 20).
- **Mid-November:** The Manheim used-vehicle index was **206.8, flat** from October on a seasonally adjusted basis. Unadjusted prices fell 0.7% in early November and are up 0.7% from a year earlier (Cox Automotive, mid-Nov 2026). This points to neutral or slightly soft used-car CPI. CPI used cars fell 1.2% in October.
- **Nov 13:** Governor Waller floated a 50bp December hike if inflation data worsen (Forbes, Nov 13; CNBC, Nov 10).
- **Nov 10, October CPI:** headline +0.4%, core **+0.3%**, against a 0.2% consensus. This was the third straight 0.3% core print.

  - KPMG puts three-month annualized core at **3.7%**, the hottest since April 2024.

- **Background:** Headline inflation is running about 3.7–3.9% year over year because of an energy shock. The Fed is in a hiking cycle. Tariffs are still feeding into prices.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES (0.4% or higher):**
- **Energy pass-through continues:** higher fuel costs keep pushing airfares and transport services up, despite the slowdown to +0.4% in October.
- **Tariffs:** core goods acceleration resumes, as in September's +0.3%.
- **Shelter firms:** OER at 0.3–0.4% plus continued strength in motor insurance and medical care.
- **Rebounds:** used cars bounce back after October's −1.2%, or lodging and apparel rise.
- **Momentum:** if October's true unrounded figure was about 0.35% rather than about 0.30%, a 0.4% print needs less help.

**Paths to NO (0.3% or lower):**
- **Seasonal softness:** November has historically been a soft month.
- **Falling energy:** the near-zero headline nowcast implies gasoline is falling, which could pull airfares and transport services lower.
- **Used cars:** flat Manheim prices in early November.
- **Mean reversion:** lodging and airfares give back their August–October spikes.
- **Steady trend:** a stable underlying trend of about 0.25–0.30% stays inside the 0.3% rounding band.

## 5. Signposts
- **Early-December consensus for November core** (Bloomberg/Reuters/Dow Jones):
  - If the consensus is 0.3% with unrounded forecasts around 0.30–0.33%, YES becomes more likely.
  - If the consensus is 0.2%, the cushion favors NO, although 2026 has repeatedly beaten 0.2% forecasts.
- **Full November Manheim (Dec 7):** a seasonally adjusted rise would point up, a decline down. Used-car CPI follows wholesale prices with a lag of about two months.
- **Jet fuel and gasoline in November:** declines point to softer airfares and therefore down.
- **Oct PCE (Nov 25):** gives more detail on whether the October core strength was broad-based.
- **Nov 3–20 Fed remarks and the Dec 9 statement:** could signal how strong officials expect inflation to be.
- **Confirmed unrounded October core** from the BLS index levels: about 0.35% would point up, about 0.30% would mean no change.
- **Shutdown or funding news** before Dec 10: this would affect timing only.
