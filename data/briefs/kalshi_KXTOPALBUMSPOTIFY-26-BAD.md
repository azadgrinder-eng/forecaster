# Research brief: Will DeBÍ TiRAR MáS FOToS (DtMF) be #1 on Spotify Wrapped 2026's global Top Albums chart?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Bad Bunny's *DeBÍ TiRAR MáS FOToS* is #1 on Spotify's official 2026 Wrapped "Top Albums Globally" list on the day Spotify publishes it.
- **Who decides:** Spotify's own published list, normally a post on newsroom.spotify.com plus press coverage. The 2025 list was posted that way on Dec 3, 2025 (Spotify Newsroom, 2025-12-03).
- **Timing:** Spotify had not announced a 2026 date as of mid/late November 2026. Most outlets expect Wednesday, December 2, 2026, the Wednesday after US Thanksgiving. Past dates: Dec 1 2021, Nov 30 2022, Nov 29 2023, Dec 4 2024, Dec 3 2025 (Official Charts, Nov 2026; Jamwise / freeyourmusic, Oct–Nov 2026). The market closes Dec 31, 2026, which leaves ample room for a slip.
- **What period is counted:** Spotify hasn't published a 2026 cutoff. Wrapped has usually counted listening from about Jan 1 to mid-November (freeyourmusic, Oct 2026). So the race is effectively already over. The result depends on streams from Jan 1 to about Nov 15, 2026.
- **Release year doesn't matter:** Albums from any year are eligible. DtMF came out Jan 5, 2025 and won 2025. *Un Verano Sin Ti* (May 2022) topped both 2022 and 2023.
- **Edge cases:**
  - Spotify's internal method for Wrapped album totals isn't public. It may handle deluxe or alternate editions, filtered streams, or the exact cutoff differently from the fan trackers (kworb, ChartMasters).
  - A tie, a corrected list, or a delayed release looks very unlikely to matter.
  - Watch for "most-streamed album *released in* 2026" claims. They exclude DtMF and are not the Wrapped measure (see section 3).

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Last year's #1 repeating the next year.** From memory, unverified this session: Scorpion (2018), WWAFAWDWG (2019), YHLQMDLG (2020), SOUR (2021), Un Verano Sin Ti (2022 and 2023), TTPD (2024), DtMF (2025).
- A #1 repeated once in 7 transitions (UVST, 2022→2023), so about 14%.
- The closer analogue is a blockbuster album in its second calendar year with no newer album from the same artist. UVST is the only clean case, and it repeated.
- This class is weak because it ignores the year-to-date data, which is available here.

**B. Fan-tracker year-to-date leader in mid-November vs. the official Wrapped #1.** From memory, the Wrapped #1 album has matched the album leading the kworb/ChartMasters year-to-date totals every recent year I know of (2022 UVST, 2023 UVST, 2024 TTPD, 2025 DtMF). Rough base rate: about 4/4, likely higher across more years. No clear upset where the tracker leader lost on Wrapped comes to mind. This is the most relevant class.

**C. Leaders with a 5% lead and similar daily rates with about 0–4 weeks left.** Two albums with nearly the same daily streams essentially never trade places when the gap is about 300M streams (about a month of combined streaming). At this stage, the risk is a measurement or method difference, not a change in streaming trend.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (DtMF #1):**
- Spotify's internal count matches the fan trackers, where DtMF leads 2026 streams by about 5% with the counting window essentially closed.
- Bad Bunny's steady catalog listening plus the Grammy and Super Bowl spikes in Q1 built a lead that ARIRANG's March debut surge (110M+ on day 1, about 1B in 16 days) cut but didn't erase.
  - ARIRANG outpaced DtMF from April to August (about 22M vs 16M a day).
  - Since October the two have run about even.

**Paths to NO:**
1. **Method mismatch (main risk):**
   - Spotify could count ARIRANG differently, for example by merging versions or including tracks released early as singles.
   - It could filter streams differently. Coordinated fan streaming might be filtered more heavily, which would actually help DtMF.
   - Its internal "album stream" definition could differ from kworb's sum of track streams.
   - A 4–6% gap is large but not huge for this kind of difference.
2. **My baseline estimate is off:** if DtMF's Jan 1 total was closer to 8.6B+, its lead shrinks toward zero.
   - The two estimates agree with each other, but both depend on unofficial fan tallies.
3. **A surprise contender:** none is visible. No other album is within about 1.5B.
4. **Spotify excludes or splits DtMF** (for example by edition): very unlikely.

## 5. Signposts

- **Spotify's official list (expected about Dec 2):** this settles the question.
- **Bigger near-term signals:**
  - **Toward NO:**
    - A late-November fan or kworb-based "2026 streams, all albums" tally showing ARIRANG at or above DtMF.
    - BigHit or Spotify announcing ARIRANG as "Spotify's most-streamed album of 2026," without the "among 2026 releases" qualifier.
    - Leaks or early Wrapped coverage in local markets.
  - **Toward YES:**
    - Any tracker showing DtMF's 2026 total at 5.6B+ against ARIRANG's 5.4–5.5B.
    - Spotify hinting at Bad Bunny's 2026 dominance, for example as top global artist.
- **Smaller signals:** an official Wrapped date or cutoff announcement (a later cutoff helps ARIRANG very slightly), and any change in Wrapped methodology.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I couldn't access kworb or ChartMasters pages directly. All figures come through search summaries, and the ARIRANG kworb snapshot's date (Nov 15) wasn't fully confirmed.
- DtMF's exact total on Dec 31, 2025 wasn't found. The 2026 year-to-date figure (about 5.6–5.75B) is my calculation from fan tallies plus milestone dates.
- I couldn't confirm exactly how fans' "2026 streams" tallies are calculated (kworb deltas vs. ChartMasters), or how closely they match Spotify's internal Wrapped numbers.
- The 2026 Wrapped release date and data cutoff are unconfirmed.
- My historical match between tracker leaders and Wrapped #1 comes from memory, not verified this session.
- One Paraguayan outlet mentioned a possible new Bad Bunny album announced around July 2026. It wasn't verified and appears misdated. A new album would mainly matter if it drew streams away from DtMF, and none appears in the 2026 top lists.
