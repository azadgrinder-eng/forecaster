# Research Brief: Will US CPI-U inflation for the 12 months ending November 2026 be above 3.6%?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** The 12-month change in CPI-U (all items, US city average, not seasonally adjusted) for November 2026, as BLS publishes it to one decimal place, must be above 3.6%. So 3.7% or higher is YES and 3.6% or lower is NO. In unrounded terms the threshold is about 3.65%.
- **Index level needed:** The November 2025 index is 324.122, confirmed by BLS's Dec 18, 2025 release and by FRED (BLS CPI November 2025 release, Dec 18, 2025). A reading of at least 3.65% needs a November 2026 index of about **≥335.95**.
- **Where things stand now:** The October 2026 index is **337.511**, up 0.2% from September before seasonal adjustment (BLS October 2026 CPI, via search summary, Nov 10, 2026). YES therefore holds unless the index **falls about 0.46% or more** from October to November, before seasonal adjustment.
- **Release date:** The November 2026 CPI comes out **Thursday, Dec 10, 2026, 8:30 a.m. ET** (BLS release schedule). The question closes Dec 10 at 13:29 UTC, right after the release.
- **The 12-month rate will be published:** The 2025 shutdown wiped out October 2025 data only. That is why BLS published no 12-month rate for October 2026. November 2025 data exist, collected late in the month after the shutdown ended, so a November 2026 12-month rate will be published (FXStreet November 2025 CPI preview, Dec 16, 2025; FinancialJuice, Nov 9, 2026).
- **Shutdown edge case:** Current federal funding runs through **Dec 11, 2026** under a stopgap law signed Sept 2, 2026. As of Nov 17 no new stopgap text had been released (governmentshutdowntracker.com, Nov 17, 2026). A lapse would start Dec 12, after the Dec 10 release, so it should not affect this question. November price collection is already complete. If a delay somehow happened, the rules extend expiry rather than resolve NO.
- **Minor ambiguity:** "One-decimal place value reported by BLS" means the published rounded figure. A value like 3.64% would print as 3.6% and resolve NO.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. How often does CPI-U fall ≥0.46% in one month, before seasonal adjustment?**
- This is rare. My recollection of NSA index data, not checked this session, is roughly 8–12 months since 1990, or about 2–3% of months.
- Nearly all of them were driven by gasoline collapses: Nov 2005 (about −0.8%, after the Katrina spike), Oct–Dec 2008, Nov 2012 (about −0.47%), Nov 2014 to Jan 2015 (about −0.5%), and Apr 2020.
- November months are overrepresented, because November seasonal factors already pull the unadjusted index down. Typical Novembers: 2022 about −0.10%, 2023 about −0.20%, 2024 about −0.05%.
- Conditioned on a large November gasoline drop (about 7–14%), this reference class is much less reassuring. The 2012 and 2014 Novembers crossed −0.46%.
- Two things differ now:
  - **Gasoline's weight is smaller.** It is about 4.0–4.3% of CPI today, against about 5–6% in 2005–2014.
  - **Core inflation is hotter.** It is running about 0.3% a month seasonally adjusted (three straight months), against about 0.1% in Nov 2012 and Nov 2014. That adds about +0.15pp relative to those episodes.

**B. Recent 12-month prints.**
- August 2026: 3.4%.
- September 2026: 3.7% (released Oct 14). Headline rose 0.6% on the month and gasoline 9.1% (Advisor Perspectives, Oct 22, 2026; USInflationCalculator).
- October 2026: no official rate. Gap-filled outside estimates were about 4.0–4.1% (EY; UniCredit preview via search, Nov 2026). A direct calculation also gives 337.511 / 324.122, roughly +4.1% over 11 months.
- The 12-month rate has been rising since mid-2026. A drop of ≥0.2pp from the October-equivalent level to below 3.65% would need a big one-month energy decline.

**C. Nowcast accuracy.**
- The Cleveland Fed nowcast usually lands within about 0.1–0.15pp for headline CPI when it is made with most of the month's gasoline prices already known. This comes from my general knowledge of their published accuracy, not something I verified this session.
- The current nowcast is about 0.2pp above the threshold, which is roughly 1.3–2 standard errors.

  - November 2026 12-month CPI **3.86%**, core CPI 2.87%.
  - Month-over-month seasonally adjusted: CPI **+0.07%**, core +0.21%.
  - The column labels were read from a search summary, so the mapping is likely but not certain.
  - 3.86% implies a November index of about 336.6, an unadjusted change of about −0.26% from October.
- **Nov 17, 2026:** Brent is about $92.5 and WTI about $84.7. Both were above $98–100 a month earlier. A US crude inventory build of +5.3M barrels and surplus expectations are outweighing the US-Iran conflict and Strait of Hormuz tanker attacks. WTI briefly fell to about $81 (Oilprice.com, Nov 17; Investing.com).
- **Nov 14, 2026:** Gasoline was $3.84, down from $4.33 a month earlier, about −11% (Yahoo Finance).
- **Nov 12, 2026:** The AAA average fell below $4 for the first time since July, to $3.90. It was down about 20¢ in a week, so about $4.10 around Nov 5. AAA cites cheaper crude and winter-blend gasoline (AAA newsroom).
- **Nov 10, 2026 (October CPI):**
  - Headline +0.4% seasonally adjusted, against a 0.3% consensus. Core +0.3%, its third straight month at that pace.
  - Energy +0.7%. Gasoline +1.0% seasonally adjusted but **−0.7% unadjusted**. Shelter +0.3%.
  - Index 337.511 (CNBC; Stocktitan; cpiinflationcalculator, Nov 10–11, 2026).
  - The Fed is discussing **rate hikes**. A Stocktitan headline referenced Governor Waller's threshold for weighing a 50bp hike.
- **Oct 14, 2026 (September CPI):** 3.7% over 12 months, core 2.6%. This followed a gasoline surge tied to the Iran and Hormuz disruption.
- **Late 2025 base:** November 2025 prices were collected only in the second half of the month, after the shutdown ended Nov 12, 2025, which captured holiday discounts. Rent was also effectively carried forward through October 2025. Both depressed the November 2025 index (324.122, 12-month rate 2.7%). This low base mechanically lifts the November 2026 12-month rate.
- **Scheduled before close:** No other CPI-relevant releases. The government funding deadline (Dec 11) falls after the release.

### Back-of-envelope estimate (mine)

- **October gasoline average:** about $4.25–4.35 (Oct 14: $4.33; CPI gasoline unadjusted −0.7% from September).
- **November gasoline average:** about $3.80–3.90. Roughly $4.10–4.15 in the first week, $3.85–3.90 in the second, about $3.75 in the third and about $3.65 at month end.
- **Gasoline change:** about **−9% to −12%** unadjusted. At about 4.2% weight that subtracts **−0.38 to −0.50pp**.
- **Core:** about 0.2–0.3% seasonally adjusted, which is about 0.0% to +0.05% unadjusted, since the November seasonal gap for core is about −0.25pp. That contributes about 0 to +0.04pp.
- **Food:** about +0.02–0.03pp.
- **Utility gas and electricity:** about 0 to +0.02pp.
- **Net:** about −0.30% to −0.45% unadjusted, giving an index of about 336.0–336.5 and a **12-month rate of about 3.7–3.85%**.
- The Cleveland Fed's −0.26% implies less gasoline drag than my upper-range gasoline estimate. My central estimate is about 3.8%, but the margin over 3.65% may be only about 0.1–0.2pp.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (12-month rate of 3.7% or higher):**
- The base effect locks in a lot. The October index is already 4.1% above November 2025.
- Core stays at about 0.25–0.3% seasonally adjusted, and shelter stays at about 0.3%.
- BLS's measured gasoline decline comes in near −6% to −9%. Its sample timing differs from AAA daily averages, and some of the decline may already have been partly captured in late October.
- Food and holiday-season prices stay firm.

**Paths to NO (3.6% or lower, meaning an unadjusted drop of about ≥0.46%):**
- Measured gasoline falls 12% or more, possibly with fuel oil also dropping sharply.
- Core comes in soft (≤0.15% seasonally adjusted), for example from apparel, lodging or airfare discounting. November seasonal factors amplify this.
- Shelter decelerates. October rent was already a cooler +0.2%.
- Any combination of these: roughly gasoline −11% with core at 0.2% puts the result right at the threshold.

## 5. Signposts

- **Gasoline for the rest of November** (AAA/EIA, Nov 23–30) matters most. An average below $3.65 into month end raises NO risk. A stall or rebound at about $3.75 or higher, for example from Hormuz escalation, strengthens YES. Note that crude moves now mostly affect December, not November.
- **The Cleveland Fed daily nowcast.** If it slides below about 3.75%, the margin is getting thin. If it stays at 3.85% or above, YES is very likely.
- **Street previews and consensus for November CPI,** due around Dec 1–9. Watch for economists' estimates of unadjusted gasoline and core.
- **Other November price data:** Adobe Digital Price Index (online prices), Manheim used-car index, and airfare data. These bear on core.
- **Shutdown news:** Only relevant if the release were somehow moved. Very unlikely before Dec 11.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not open the BLS October 2026 release, CNBC, EY or the Cleveland Fed page directly; every fetch was refused by the proxy. Index levels and nowcast figures come from search-result summaries. 337.511 and 324.122 are each consistent with the published 12-month rates.
- The exact October 2026 average gasoline price and BLS's November gasoline sample are not established. This is the single largest swing factor, worth ±0.1pp or more.
- The current gasoline weight in CPI (about 4.0–4.3%) is my estimate, not a published BLS figure.
- November seasonal factors for core are approximate (gap of about −0.25pp), taken from 2022–2024 patterns as I remember them.
- The historical counts in Section 2A and the nowcast error band are from memory and were not checked this session.
- Whether the Cleveland Fed's 3.86% fully reflects the latest gasoline drop is unclear. It updates daily and fills the rest of the month forward with the latest prices.
- Sources disagree on the details of the stopgap funding law, though most give Dec 11.
