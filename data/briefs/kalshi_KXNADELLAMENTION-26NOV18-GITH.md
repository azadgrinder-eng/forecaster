# Research brief: Will Satya Nadella say "GitHub" at the 2026 NYT DealBook Summit (Nov 18, 2026)?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Nadella must say "GitHub" out loud during his DealBook Summit appearance, speaking in an official capacity. Plurals and possessives count ("GitHub's"). Compound uses also count: "GitHub Copilot," "GitHub Advanced Security," "GitHub Enterprise." Saying "Copilot" alone does not.
- **What doesn't count:** Slides, captions, signage or the moderator saying the word. Hot-mic remarks, clips of earlier recordings, or things he said elsewhere, such as Microsoft Ignite the same week.
- **Who decides:** Kalshi staff, using the NYT broadcast, stream or official video. If staff can't agree from the video, they fall back to official transcripts.
- **Deadline:** The event is Nov 18, 2026, and the market closes Dec 2, 2026 at 15:00 UTC. That close date leaves room for the rule that keeps markets open if a postponement is announced by the next day and the new date is within 14 days.
- **Edge cases:**
  - If Nadella drops out, or the event is cancelled, "Event does not qualify" resolves YES and this market resolves NO. Withdrawals after a lineup is announced are uncommon but do happen at DealBook.
  - If he appears by video link, he is probably still "speaking in an official capacity," but the rules don't say.
  - If he appears on a group panel rather than one-on-one, his speaking time and the topics will be different.
  - "GitHub" is said quickly in a compound like "GitHub Copilot." An unclear or garbled moment could start a dispute, but this is a minor risk.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES:**
- **Coding agents and competition:** Sorkin asks about AI coding agents, or about competition with OpenAI, Cursor/SpaceX or Anthropic. Nadella almost always uses GitHub (Copilot, Agent HQ, pull-request statistics) as his main proof point. This is the most likely path.
- **Proof that AI pays off:** On AI-bubble or capex questions, he usually cites GitHub Copilot revenue or usage as evidence of returns on AI spending.
- **The OpenAI relationship and multiple models:** He may say that GitHub hosts agents from every lab ("Agent HQ").
- **AI and jobs or productivity:** He may cite the share of Microsoft code or GitHub pull requests written by AI.
- **Personal story:** He often tells how seeing GitHub Copilot in 2021–22 convinced him about this AI wave.

**Paths to NO:**
- **Topics stay elsewhere:** The interview focuses on politics (Trump administration, tariffs, China, H-1B), energy and data centres, AI safety or jobs in general terms, the OpenAI contract or AGI clause, or Musk's lawsuits against OpenAI. Nadella could talk about coding with "Copilot," "Codex" or "coding agents" without saying "GitHub."
- **Short or shared slot:** A 20–25 minute session or a panel format leaves less room for product detail.
- **He doesn't appear:** He withdraws, possibly because of Ignite, which would trigger "Event does not qualify," or he appears in a format that doesn't qualify.

## 5. Signposts

- **Toward NO:** The NYT publishes an agenda showing Nadella's slot length, or a panel format, or he is dropped from the lineup. Ignite scheduling that conflicts with Nov 18 also points this way; a Nadella keynote in San Francisco on Nov 18 would be a strong negative.
- **Toward YES:**
  - Large GitHub or coding-agent news between Nov 9 and 18: Ignite announcements, GitHub outages, more OpenAI moves against GitHub, or a Microsoft deal in the coding space. Sorkin likes questions about rivalries.
  - Musk speaking earlier the same day and talking about Cursor/Origin versus GitHub, or Altman discussing Codex. That raises the chance Sorkin brings it up with Nadella.
- **Interviews just before the summit:** If Nadella says "GitHub" in other mainstream interviews between Nov 9 and 17, that is a mild positive. If his talking points are about Copilot in Office and Windows, or about "superintelligence" and the AI workforce, that is mildly negative.
