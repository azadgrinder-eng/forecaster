# Research brief: if a left/right culture war over AI arrives before the US elections, will the right be the pro-AI side?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What the market is.** The question is a Manifold market with this title from user MGG. It is a conditional market. A search summary says Manifold manages and resolves it, so the creator may not be the one resolving (Manifold listing, accessed Nov 2026). I could not open the page itself (access denied), so the exact wording is unconfirmed.
- **How earlier versions worked.** The template is a 2023 market by zzq, which was conditional on Lars Doucet's "Will the left/right culture war come for AI before end of 2023?" The rules were:
  - If the parent resolves NO, this market resolves N/A.
  - If the parent resolves YES, this market resolves YES if the right is pro-AI and the left anti-AI, and NO if it is the other way round (Manifold, zzq/LarsDoucet markets).
  - Doucet's parent resolved YES only if, in his sole opinion, "support for AI clearly becomes politically polarized along left/right lines." His clarifications: knowing someone's pro- or anti-AI position alone should let you guess whether they are left or right, and if both sides are anti-AI the parent resolves NO.
  - Both the 2023 and 2024 parent versions resolved NO (Jan 2024 and Jan 18, 2025).
- **Which election.** "Before US elections" with a close date of Nov 30, 2026 almost certainly means the Nov 3, 2026 midterms. That has already happened, so the deciding evidence is mostly in hand.
- **Edge cases and ambiguities:**
  1. **N/A risk.** If a parent market exists and resolves NO (both sides anti-AI, or no clear polarization), this question would probably resolve N/A rather than NO. In practice, YES vs NO only matters if polarization is judged to have happened.
  2. **No parent at all.** If there is no parent, the resolver may simply judge "which side is pro-AI." That could produce NO if the resolver decides the right is not clearly pro-AI, for example because GOP populists turned against data centers.
  3. **"Pro-AI" means different things.** It could mean pro-industry or deregulation (the right clearly leads), pro-data-center (now mixed), or favorable public attitudes (Republicans modestly more favorable).
  4. **Elites vs voters.** Party elites and voters point in somewhat different directions.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. New technologies or industries that became partisan in the US since about 1970.** My rough hand count:
- Right ended up as the "pro" side: nuclear power, fracking/oil and gas, crypto (2022–24), GMOs (weak).
- Right ended up as the "anti" side: EVs, wind/solar, mRNA vaccines (after 2020).
- Social media/Big Tech: both sides turned hostile for different reasons.

The pattern is that the right takes the "pro" side when the technology is framed as industry, energy, or deregulation and environmentalists or labor oppose it. That fits about 4 of 7 cases, and all 4 where green or labor groups led the opposition. AI's opponents are disproportionately environmental (water, power), labor, and progressive groups, which puts AI in the "right = pro" bucket. The counterweights are "woke AI" grievances and energy-price populism on the right.

**B. Earlier Manifold markets asking whether the AI culture war "arrived."** 0 of 2 resolved YES (end-2023 and end-2024 versions). This suggests resolvers set a high bar for "clearly polarized."

**C. Partisan direction of AI attitudes in polls, 2024 to 2026.** In every recent poll I found, the gap points the same way: Democrats are more negative about AI. The gaps are usually 5 to 23 points:
- Pew 2025: 56% of Democrats vs 49% of Republicans are more concerned than excited. In 2024 the lean had been the other way.
- Pew 2025: 54% of Republicans vs 36% of Democrats trust the US to regulate AI.
- Economist/YouGov: 82% of Democrats vs 59% of Republicans say AI is moving too fast.
- Axios Harris Poll 100 (May 19, 2026): OpenAI's partisan reputation gap grew from 1 point in 2024 to 12 points, with Republicans more favorable.

The direction is consistent, but the gaps are modest and majorities of both parties are skeptical.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 5, 2026.** After sweeping the midterms, top Democrats called for restrictions:
  - Rep. Greg Casar said Democrats must "protect Americans' jobs, freedom and lives from AI billionaires."
  - Rep. Yassamin Ansari called for a pause on advanced AI and a ban on superintelligence.
  - The phrase "national data center moratorium now" was circulating, and AI-exposed stocks sold off (CNBC, Nov 5, 2026; Tom's Hardware, Nov 2026).
  - Democrats plan oversight and investigations of Big Tech (The Hill, Nov 2026; WaPo AI & Tech Brief, Nov 4, 2026).
- **Nov 4–5, 2026: AI super PACs.**
  - Leading the Future (pro-industry, bipartisan) spent about $80M of its roughly $100M war chest. Its largest bet, $4.8M for the Republican in the South Carolina Senate race, lost to Democrat Annie Andrews. Its Republican House picks Buckhout (NC-1) and Flint (MT-1) also lost (TNW; Daily Caller, Nov 5, 2026).
  - Public First (pro-safeguards, linked to Anthropic) won mostly with Democrats (Foushee, Poindexter, Rutinel, Wiener). Its Republicans lost (Fast Company; Wikipedia).
  - Axios says the AI PACs spent more than $50M through affiliated super PACs in nearly 50 races but mostly avoided talking about AI in their ads (Axios, Nov 3, 2026).
- **Nov 3–4, 2026: exit polls.**
  - 56% of voters said AI and data centers were a crucial or major factor in their vote, and 65% of that group voted Democratic (NBC/CNBC exit poll; DCD, Nov 2026).
  - On which party voters trust on AI: Democrats 29%, Republicans 27%, neither 43%. Neither party "owns" the issue.
  - Voters rejected data centers in every ballot measure but one (CNBC, Nov 4, 2026). Daily Caller's headline: "Americans sour on AI… leaving both parties out in cold" (Nov 4, 2026).
- **Oct 2026.**
  - A Politico poll found nearly half of Americans name Republicans as the party most eager to build data centers (cited by Time/Yahoo, Nov 4, 2026).
  - CNBC survey: 63% don't want a nearby data center and 13% do. The framing was "Trump AI push faces voter backlash" (CNBC, Oct 24, 2026).
  - At an Ohio rally Trump said of data centers "we want them" and called them a "Golden Goose" (Axios, Oct 31, 2026).
- **Aug–Sep 2026: GOP state-level pivot.**
  - Texas Gov. Abbott paused grid approvals for data centers pending an audit due Dec 10. He also called for repealing data-center tax incentives and for new rules.
  - Agriculture Commissioner Sid Miller warned that Republicans would lose statewide races over the issue (KERA, Aug 7, 2026; Daily Record, Sep 3, 2026).
  - Time ran "Data Center Panic: Republicans Scramble to Join Backlash" (Aug 25, 2026).
  - Tom Tiffany, the GOP nominee for Wisconsin governor, campaigned on cracking down on data centers.
- **May 2026.** DeSantis signed Florida SB 484 on local power over data centers and an AI "bill of rights," and called "superintelligence factories = surveillance centers" (E&E/Politico). Gallup found 70% oppose local data centers.
- **Mar 2026.** The White House sent Congress a preemption framework that carves out child safety and the zoning of AI infrastructure. Democrats filed a bill to repeal the December 2025 executive order (Law360; RegReview, Feb 26, 2026). Axios reported a widening rift inside the White House over the AI rollout (Mar 27, 2026).
- **Jan 2026.** Sen. Hawley's anti-AI strategy centers on electricity rates, child chatbot harms, and "no more data centers" unless they supply their own power (Axios, Jan 30, 2026). NPR reported that data-center protests are "scrambling political lines" (Jan 25, 2026).
- **Dec 11, 2025.** Trump signed an executive order to challenge state AI laws through a DOJ AI Litigation Task Force (Washington Times, Dec 11, 2025). Two earlier GOP attempts at preemption in Congress had failed: the Senate stripped the 10-year moratorium from the July 2025 reconciliation bill, and a version was dropped from the NDAA.

**Before close:** The lame-duck session of Congress may revisit preemption or the NDAA. Expect post-election GOP soul-searching (Trump, Sacks, Hawley) and more Democratic announcements. Abbott's audit lands Dec 10, after close.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (the right is the pro-AI side):**
- The resolver looks at elites and policy:
  - The Trump/Sacks agenda is acceleration, deregulation, preemption of state laws, and "we want" data centers.
  - Democrats are now loudly calling for moratoriums, pauses, and investigations of "AI billionaires."
  - Pro-industry money (Leading the Future) is widely seen as lining up with the administration.
  - Public First (pro-safeguards) wins came mostly with Democrats.
- The resolver looks at mass attitudes: every poll shows Republicans more favorable to AI companies and less worried.
- This fits the reference-class pattern of the right as pro-industry and the left as green/labor critics.

**Paths to NO, or to N/A if the market is conditional:**
- **Bipartisan backlash.** Hawley, DeSantis, Abbott, Tiffany, Miller, and Bannon-style populists are all anti-data-center. Majorities of both parties want more regulation (Navigator: 63% of Democrats, 59% of Republicans). The "trust on AI" exit poll is 29–27 with 43% saying neither. A resolver could judge "both anti" or "not clearly polarized." That gives NO on the parent, so N/A here, or NO if there is no parent.
- **"Woke AI."** The right's "woke AI" framing casts it as anti the current AI companies. Pro-AI Democrats (abundance liberals, Leading the Future's Democratic picks such as Moskowitz) muddy the picture.
- **Genuine NO, the left as pro-AI side:** this needs a reversal, such as a post-midterm GOP populist pivot led by Trump plus Democrats embracing AI. I see little sign of this before Nov 30.

## 5. Signposts

- **Toward YES:**
  - Trump or GOP leaders doubling down on preemption or data centers in the lame duck.
  - Democratic leadership (Schumer, Jeffries) formally endorsing a moratorium or a superintelligence ban.
  - Commentary describing AI as a "Democratic vs Republican" fight.
- **Toward NO or N/A:**
  - Trump publicly turning against data centers or Big Tech after the losses.
  - The GOP adopting Hawley's agenda.
  - Polls showing Republicans as negative as Democrats.
  - A resolver comment describing "both sides anti-AI."
- **Most important single factor:** the exact resolution text and whether this market depends on a parent market. Read it on Manifold.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not read the market page. Still unknown: the exact criteria, who resolves it, whether there is a parent "did the culture war come" market and how it is defined, and which "US elections" are meant.
- Exit-poll party breakdowns come from secondary write-ups (DCD). Figures such as "76% oppose local data centers, 63% D / 35% R" are ambiguous.
- Pew numbers vary across write-ups, and the 2024→2025 flip in partisan concern should be checked against Pew directly.
- I have not confirmed how the White House responded to the midterm results.
- The resolver's interpretation is subjective: elites vs voters, and pro-industry vs pro-data-center.
