# Research Brief: Will the FDA approve midomafetamine (MDMA) for PTSD before Jan 1, 2027?

*Prepared Nov 28, 2026. I was limited to 12 searches, and every attempt to open a full article failed because the proxy refused the connection. Everything below comes from search-result summaries of secondary reporting (trade press, advocacy sites, newsletters). I could not check any of it against primary FDA or company documents.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** the FDA issues an approval letter for the midomafetamine (MDMA) New Drug Application (NDA) for PTSD dated before Jan 1, 2027 (the market closes 2027-01-01 04:59 UTC). Accelerated approval, approval with a REMS (a mandatory safety program) or approval with restricted distribution all count. Expanded access, Right to Try, Emergency Use Authorization, or simply extending the deadline do not count.
- **Sponsor:** Resilient Pharmaceuticals, formerly MAPS PBC and then Lykos Therapeutics. The original NDA got a Complete Response Letter (CRL, a formal rejection that lists what must be fixed) on Aug 9, 2024 (Lykos Therapeutics press release, Aug 2024).
- **The 2024 CRL does not settle the question.** It was issued before this market opened, and the rules allow YES if the FDA approves after the concerns are addressed. In practice the resubmission has to be approved within about five weeks of today.
- **Immediate NO triggers:** a new CRL before Jan 1, 2027, or the sponsor withdrawing the application.
- **Ambiguities:**
  - **DEA scheduling.** MDMA is Schedule I. Commercial launch would need DEA rescheduling after approval, but the market only asks about the FDA approval letter, so the DEA does not matter for resolution.
  - **Whether the resubmission was formally filed.** If the FDA treated it as incomplete, there is no review clock at all.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. FDA acting well before the goal date on a resubmission after a CRL.** The FDA meets about 90% or more of its PDUFA goal dates (the deadline it commits to for acting on an application), but it usually acts close to the date. Acting more than a month early is uncommon, especially in psychiatry and for controversial or first-in-class drugs. This figure is my estimate, not a computed statistic.
- If the resubmission arrived about Aug 9, 2026 and is a Class 2 resubmission (one that addresses substantive issues), the six-month clock under 21 CFR 314.60 points to a goal date around **early February 2027**. Approval before Jan 1 would need the FDA to act about six weeks early.
- No outlet has confirmed the date the FDA received it or the resubmission class. Class 1 (a two-month clock) is only for minor issues such as labeling, and it does not fit a CRL that asked for a new Phase 3 trial.

**B. Approval of resubmissions that skip the trial the CRL asked for.** Historically, roughly half or a bit more of NDAs that receive a CRL are eventually approved. That figure comes from the general literature (BIO/FDA CRL analyses) and was not recomputed here. The rate is lower when the CRL asked for new efficacy data and the sponsor comes back without it.
- The 2024 CRL cited functional unblinding (participants could tell whether they got MDMA), expectancy bias, selection bias from volunteers with prior MDMA use, durability of response, and gaps in safety data (gooddrugpolicy.org; Healio, Aug 12, 2024).
- The resubmission reportedly adds a third-party audit of the existing trial data, a Phase 1 cardiac-safety study and long-term VA follow-up data, but **no new Phase 3** (Psychedelic Alpha, Aug 9, 2026; Psychedelics Today, Aug 18, 2026; Rize Newsroom, 2026).

**C. First-in-class controlled-substance psychiatric drugs that needed an advisory committee and a REMS.** Esketamine (Spravato, 2019) went through an advisory committee and a REMS. The FDA acted on its PDUFA date, not early. MDMA's June 2024 advisory committee voted 2–9 on effectiveness and 1–10 on benefit-risk. Under the current FDA, which reportedly signalled support for advisory committees (RAPS, Oct 2, 2026), a second advisory committee before approval would be plausible. None has been announced, and a meeting requires a Federal Register notice at least 15 days in advance.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 18, 2026:** The White House withdrew Heidi Overton's nomination for FDA Commissioner hours before a Senate HELP committee vote. Kyle Diamantas remains acting commissioner, and the agency's leadership gap continues (Healio, Nov 18, 2026; STAT, Nov 18, 2026; PharmTech, Nov 20, 2026). There is thin reporting that Roger Marshall could be nominated instead.
- **Nov 12–13, 2026:** Reuters reported that the FDA has quietly shelved the Commissioner's National Priority Voucher (CNPV) pilot, a fast-review program. No new vouchers have been issued since Makary left, and career staff are managing timelines for the remaining voucher holders. HHS disputes this. The FDA also appears to have paused publishing CRLs, so a CRL might not become public right away (Fierce Pharma, Nov 2026; PharmExec, Nov 13, 2026; BioPharm International, Nov 2026).
- **Early Oct 2026:** There was still no public confirmation that the FDA had accepted the resubmission and no public decision date (healingmaps.com; Rize Newsroom). A Psychology Today piece from Oct 2026 describes MDMA as still unapproved.
- **Aug 9–21, 2026:** Psychedelic Alpha reported the NDA resubmission (Aug 9). MAPS responded the next day without confirming it independently (MAPS, Aug 10, 2026). Coverage describes it as "under FDA review again, but without a voucher and without a public decision date" (Lucid News, Aug 21, 2026; HCPLive; Psychedelics Today, Aug 18, 2026). Resilient has not publicly discussed it.
- **July 2026:** The FDA finalized its guidance "Psychedelic Drugs: Considerations for Clinical Investigations." The resubmission came about a month later (Psychedelics Today, Aug 2026).
- **May 13, 2026:** Commissioner Makary resigned (NPR, May 13, 2026). He had been the most public pro-psychedelic voice at the agency. He had suggested revisiting the 2024 rejection, cited 30–40% remission rates, and promised "expeditious" reviews, while also saying "I'm not saying the evidence is strong" (Reason, Apr 20, 2026; BioSpace).
- **May 1, 2026:** House members (Rep. Bergman and others) wrote to Makary urging continued evaluation of MDMA-assisted therapy and asking how the FDA would handle unblinding and expectancy effects (House letter, May 1, 2026).
- **Apr 18–24, 2026:** Executive Order 14401 told the FDA to issue national priority vouchers to psychedelics that hold Breakthrough Therapy designation. On Apr 24 the FDA gave three vouchers, reportedly to Compass, Usona, and a third company that one source says is developing an MDMA metabolite for PTSD. **Resilient was passed over** (BioSpace; Behavioral Wire; Penn LDI, Apr 2026). The order also set up a Right to Try pathway, which would not count for resolution.
- **Aug 2024:** The FDA issued the CRL and asked for an additional Phase 3 trial. Lykos then cut about 75% of its staff (pharmaphorum; Wikipedia).
- **Scheduled before Jan 1, 2027:** I found **no** known PDUFA date, advisory committee meeting or FDA action date in the window.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (all need unusual speed):**
1. **Early action driven by politics.** HHS leadership (RFK Jr. has expressed support for psychedelic therapy (PBS)) or the acting commissioner pushes reviewers to approve the drug on the audited existing data before the roughly February 2027 goal date, probably with a heavy REMS. This would require political appointees to override or steer career reviewers in the Division of Psychiatry. That is harder now that Makary has left and the voucher program is dormant.
2. **The clock started earlier than reported.** If the resubmission actually happened in late 2025, as one low-reliability outlet claims (licensedpsychedelics.com), the goal date could fall inside the window. Most sources say August 2026.
3. **Some other fast-track mechanism.** A voucher could be granted late (one to two month review). This looks very unlikely because the program is reportedly shelved and Resilient was already passed over.

**Paths to NO:**
1. **The default path.** The review runs to the roughly February 2027 goal date or later, possibly with an advisory committee, and nothing happens before Jan 1. This is the most likely path.
2. **A second CRL** before Jan 1, because the resubmission has no new Phase 3 to address the core unblinding and bias concerns. CRLs may not be published right now, which could delay public knowledge but would not change the outcome.
3. **The FDA refuses to treat the resubmission as complete, or Resilient withdraws it.**
4. **Disruption.** The leadership vacuum, staff losses and slowdowns at the agency generally push actions later, not earlier.

## 5. Signposts

- **Strong move toward YES:**
  - The FDA or Resilient confirms an action date in December 2026, or that the resubmission was received in 2025.
  - A late voucher grant to Resilient.
  - HHS or White House statements that MDMA approval is imminent.
  - DEA preparing to reschedule.
  - Reports of label or REMS negotiations.
- **Strong move toward NO:**
  - An advisory committee notice for any date in 2027.
  - Confirmation of a Class 2 resubmission with a February 2027 goal date.
  - Reports of a CRL, or of the FDA asking for more information.
  - Continued silence through mid-December, which is the default.
- **Moderate signals:** who is nominated as commissioner (a strongly pro-psychedelic pick could speed things up, though not within five weeks), and any statements from career leadership in the psychiatry division.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **Receipt date and class.** The exact date the FDA received the resubmission, its class (1 or 2), and whether it was accepted as a complete response are all unconfirmed by the FDA or Resilient. Resilient has been publicly silent.
- **Goal date.** No official PDUFA goal date has been reported. The early-February 2027 date is inferred from the six-month rule.
- **Advisory committee.** I don't know whether the FDA plans one.
- **A quiet CRL.** With CRL publication reportedly paused, a CRL might already have been issued without being made public.
- **Conflicting timing claim.** The "late 2025 resubmission" claim conflicts with the August 2026 reporting. Most sources support August 2026.
- **Dates on general web content.** Some search metadata had inconsistent dates, and full articles could not be opened to verify them.
