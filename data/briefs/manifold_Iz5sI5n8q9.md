# Research Brief: Will the next White House Press Secretary after Katie Zacharia be a blonde woman?

*Note: The question spells her name "Zachariah". News coverage spells it "Zacharia". They are the same person. Network limits blocked full-page fetches, so the facts below come from search-result summaries of the cited articles.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** The next person to hold the job of White House Press Secretary after Katie Zacharia is a woman whose hair is mainly blonde when she takes the job.
  - Dyed blonde counts.
  - Blonde highlights count only if the hair is mainly blonde (creator clarification, 2026-10-20).
  - A naturally blonde woman whose hair is dyed a non-blonde color when she takes the job resolves NO (2026-10-25).
- **Acting or interim press secretaries count** (2026-10-20). This is the most important edge case. If any woman is formally made acting press secretary before a permanent pick, she may decide the question. As of Nov 13, I found no report of anyone formally serving as acting press secretary. The job is described as "vacant since October 16" (Wikipedia, "Katie Zacharia"; Fox News, ~Nov 10).
- **Timing:** The market closes 2026-11-21. The rules don't say what happens if nobody is named by then. Most likely the market stays unresolved until someone is named. A forecaster should treat this as a question about who the eventual appointee is, with some risk of a delayed or contested resolution.
- **Who decides:** The market creator. Hair color is a subjective judgment, probably made from photos. "Dirty blonde", "light brown" or "honey" shades could be disputed.
- **Ambiguities:**
  1. A man named (YES is impossible) versus a woman named (then it depends on hair).
  2. Zacharia was told she would be "transitioning into a new role" (CNBC, Oct 16). That doesn't affect this question.
  3. If Trump names a pick who starts later, while a deputy is formally "acting" in between, the creator would probably count whoever holds the title first.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Gender of recent press secretaries.** The last 7 press secretaries were all women: Sanders, Grisham, McEnany, Psaki, Jean-Pierre, Leavitt and Zacharia (reprint of Fox News reporting, ~Nov 10). That gives roughly 100% women since mid-2017. The last man was Sean Spicer, in 2017. Across Trump's two terms, 5 of 6 press secretaries were women.

**B. Hair color among those women** (my own judgment from well-known public images; this is not from cited sources):

| McEnany | Blonde | Yes |
| Psaki | Red / strawberry | No |

| Zacharia | Not verified | ? |

- Trump-appointed women: at least 3 of 4 known cases were blonde (Grisham, McEnany, Leavitt). That rises to 4 of 5 if Zacharia was blonde.
- All Trump press secretaries, including Spicer: about 3–4 of 6, or 50–67%.
- Women's press staff in the second term are mostly blonde, so "if a woman, then blonde" is likely, at roughly 60–80%.

**C. Gender signal this cycle (the key update).** Fox News Digital reported (~Nov 10) that women are on the shortlist, but "both sources expect the job to go to a man." One source said: "His instincts are to pick a man. Which many people have told him that's a good idea." The leading names in that report are all men: Ben Ferguson, Scott Jennings, Steven Cheung, Matthew Boyle and Ari Fleischer. Political Wire ran it under the headline "Trump Wants a Male Press Secretary" (Nov 10).

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 10–12:** Trump is "closing in" on a pick. Ben Ferguson, a conservative radio host, is called the frontrunner and is being pushed by administration insiders.
  - One source said "POTUS is not sold on the idea" and that Ferguson is pursuing the job himself.
  - On Jennings: "The president keeps going back and forth… I don't think it will happen."
  - Trump "intends to choose someone… soon."
  - Sources: Fox News; Washington Times "Trump gets closer to choosing a new press secretary", Nov 10; Raw Story.
- **Nov 9:** Dana White appeared at a White House briefing with Trump. White said he has no political ambitions. Trump said the offer was a joke (Fox News; NPR, Nov 8; Deadline).
- **Nov 7:** Trump said he had offered the job to UFC CEO Dana White (NBC live blog).
- **~Early November:** Press coverage named women as possible picks (Newsweek; Daily Beast):
  - Danielle Alvarez: former Trump 2024 senior adviser and former RNC communications director, now at Mercury Public Affairs.
  - Anna Kelly: principal deputy press secretary, seen as the "natural successor".
  - Elizabeth Pipko: RNC spokesperson, described as a long shot.
  - Katie Miller.
  - Natalie Harp, whom the Daily Beast called "Trump's blonde aide".
- **Oct 22:** Semafor reported that a new press secretary would "definitely not" be named before the midterm election. Cheung said that even if one were named before then, that person probably wouldn't be at the podium (Semafor, Oct 22).
- **Oct 16:** Zacharia was pushed out after 5 days, following a widely criticized Oct 13 briefing and reports that she had presented herself as a lawyer without passing the bar. The job has been vacant since (NBC; CNBC, Oct 16).
- **Oct 9–12:** Zacharia, a Trump Media adviser, was named and took office Oct 12 as the 37th press secretary (CNBC, Oct 9 and 11; whitehouse.gov).
- **August 2026:** Karoline Leavitt announced she was leaving.

**Scheduled events before Nov 21:** None found. The midterms are over, which removes the "not before the election" constraint.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to NO:**
- **A man is named (most likely, per current reporting).** Ferguson, Jennings, Cheung, Boyle or Fleischer. This depends on Trump following the reported "instinct" to pick a man.
- **A non-blonde woman is named.** Danielle Alvarez (dark hair, from my knowledge) or Alina Habba (dark hair; floated earlier, after Leavitt).
- **No pick by Nov 21, then a man later.**

**Paths to YES:**
- **A blonde woman is named.** Possibilities include Anna Kelly (blonde), Katie Miller (blonde), Natalie Harp (blonde, wild card), or a returning figure such as Kayleigh McEnany (blonde, not reported as a candidate).
- **A blonde deputy is formally made "acting" press secretary** before the permanent pick. Anna Kelly is the obvious choice. This depends on whether the White House gives anyone the title, which it has not done in about 4 weeks.

**Unclear:**
- Elizabeth Pipko's hair (it looks light brown to dark blonde; I couldn't verify it).
- Hair coloring at the time of appointment.

**What it hinges on:** whether Trump's gender preference holds up, and whether the "pick a man" sourcing is accurate. It comes from two anonymous sources in one outlet, Fox News.

## 5. Signposts

- **Formal announcement (Truth Social or White House):** decisive in either direction.
- **More outlets confirming "Trump wants a man":** pushes toward NO. Reports that Ferguson or Jennings accepted would be close to decisive NO.
- **Anna Kelly or another blonde deputy formally designated acting press secretary, or briefing under that title:** strong move toward YES, given the rule that acting holders count.
- **Danielle Alvarez named or surging in reporting:** pushes toward NO.
- **Reports that Trump is "rethinking" a woman, or meetings with Kelly, Miller or Harp:** pushes toward YES.
- **Long silence past Nov 21:** the market probably stays open; there's no direct effect on the answer.

## 6. Uncertainties

- Whether anyone formally holds the title "acting press secretary" right now. I found no evidence of it, and Kelly or Cheung may be handling duties informally.
- Hair colors of Zacharia, Pipko and Alvarez, which I couldn't verify from sources in this session. Kelly, Miller, Harp and McEnany being blonde comes from general knowledge and the Daily Beast's description of Harp.
- How reliable the "will be a man" reporting is: one outlet, two anonymous sources, and one of them hedges.
- How the creator resolves if nobody is named by close, and how they judge borderline hair shades.
- Whether a decision lands before Nov 21. Reporting says "soon", but the vacancy has already run about 4 weeks.
- I found no news dated Nov 12–13 or later.
