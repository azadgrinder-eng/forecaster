# Research brief: Will Jasmine Blandford (Nicaragua) win Miss Universe 2026?

*Prepared 2026-11-15. All page fetches were blocked by the network proxy, so every fact below comes from search-result summaries and headlines. Treat the details as moderately reliable.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **YES condition:** Jasmine Blandford, Miss Universe Nicaragua 2026, must be announced and crowned winner at the final of the 75th Miss Universe pageant. Any other winner resolves NO.
- **Timing:** The final is set for Tuesday, November 24, 2026, at 8 p.m. local time, at the José Miguel Agrelot Coliseum in San Juan, Puerto Rico (GMA News; Hola, Nov 2, 2026; Rio Times guide). The market closes December 4, 2026, so a delay of up to about 10 days would still fit before close.
- **Who decides:** The Miss Universe Organization's judging panel, through cuts from the semifinals to the top group to the winner. In 2025 the top 30 was chosen from the national costume show, swimsuit and evening-gown rounds, and closed-door interviews (Miss Universe 2025 Wikipedia mirror). I could not confirm the 2026 cut structure or whether there is a fan-vote spot.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Her name:** Some English outlets call her "Jasmine Chola", her professional name (Hola, Sep 8, 2026). It is the same person, so this should not affect resolution.
  - **Later dethronement or controversy:** The wording "announced and crowned … at the final" suggests the on-stage result controls even if she were later dethroned. The 2025 result was disputed (judge resignations, rigging accusations), but the crown stood.
  - **Ownership dispute:** A fight between JKN Universe LLC and the MU East / Nawat Itsaragrisil side over six Southeast Asian delegates (including Thailand and Singapore) could add legal noise. It does not involve Nicaragua (Hola, Sep 18, 2026; El Heraldo).

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **Uniform prior across the field.** Reports put the field at roughly 120–130 delegates: "130 confirmed" (El Nuevo Día), 121 still in activities, and "120 countries" on the final night (late-Nov reports). Visa withdrawals include Zambia, Nepal and possibly Zimbabwe (Telemundo 51 / Vistazo, Nov 11, 2026). A uniform prior gives about **0.8% per contestant**.
2. **Winners versus pre-pageant favorites.** Recent winners were mostly on the consensus favorites lists before the pageant, though not always ranked first. Examples: Mexico 2025 (Fátima Bosch), Denmark 2024 (Victoria Kjær Theilvig) and USA 2022 (R'Bonney Gabriel). Palacios in 2023 was a mid-tier pick who peaked during pageant week, so a dark horse from outside the top 10 is possible but uncommon. I would roughly estimate 10–20% of winners since 2015 came from outside the consensus top 10. That figure is my judgment and was not counted from a source.
   - **Where Blandford stands:** I found no evidence that Nicaragua is on any major favorites list. Missosology's first ranking had France (Garance Authié) first, then Puerto Rico (Jennifer Barreto), with Philippines (Bea Millan-Windorski) third and Venezuela (Clara Vegas) and Ethiopia (Bruk Belete) also in the top five (GMA). Pageant Circle's top 15 pre-arrival favorites (Oct 23, 2026) and Metro.style's "strongest contenders" (Nov 9, 2026) name Colombia, Mexico, Puerto Rico, Venezuela, the Philippines, New Zealand, Chile and France. The search summaries did not mention Nicaragua.
3. **Nicaragua's own record.**
   - **Overall:** Nicaragua's only Miss Universe win is Sheynnis Palacios in 2023 (NBC, Nov 2023).
   - **Before 2023:** Only about 4 placements in total, with a best of top 10 in 2007 (Miss Universe Nicaragua placements table).
   - **2020–2025:** 2020 top 21; 2021 and 2022 unplaced; 2023 winner; 2024 (Geyssell García) and 2025 (Itza Castillo) both top 30, per the Wikipedia mirror. That is 4 placements in 6 years, and only one went past the top 30.
   - **Repeat wins:** Back-to-back wins by one country are rare. The only one in the modern era is Venezuela in 2008–09. Repeats within 3–5 years do happen, for example Mexico in 2020 and 2025 and the Philippines in 2015 and 2018.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES (each is a long shot):**
- She performs strongly at the preliminaries and reaches the top 30, then the top 12, then the final five. The Palacios story in 2023 shows a mid-tier Latin American delegate can peak during the live show.
- A very large Nicaraguan and diaspora fanbase could matter if any fan vote feeds into the cuts. Its existence in 2026 is unconfirmed.
- The organization may value her story: exile, Afro-Caribbean coastal roots, and the racist-harassment backlash. Its "Gran Despedida" award shows some engagement with her.
- **What this depends on:** her stage and interview performance, the judges' preferences, and how strong the frontrunners turn out to be.

**Paths to NO (dominant):**
- About 120 competitors, with several strong favorites such as Puerto Rico (host), France, Venezuela, the Philippines, Colombia and Mexico.
- Nicaragua's record outside 2023 rarely goes beyond the top 30.
- Judges may be reluctant to crown the same country twice in four years.
- Nicaragua's strong fan following has not shown up in favorites rankings.

## 5. Signposts
- **Nov 22 preliminary reviews** (Missosology and Sash Factor hot picks, pageant press): if Nicaragua enters anyone's top 10–15, the probability rises meaningfully. If she is absent, it stays near the base rate or below.
- **National costume (Nov 21) and other side awards:** a judge-scored award would be a mild positive. Fan awards are not.
- **Final night (Nov 24):** making the top 30, then top 12, then top 5 is the decisive sequence. Missing the top 30 means resolution is effectively NO.
- **Format news:** an announced fan-vote route into the semifinals would help her somewhat, given her fanbase.
- **Controversies:** a scandal, injury or withdrawal is negative. Any sign the organization is promoting her, such as heavy official social-media coverage, would be a weak positive.

## 6. Uncertainties
- The 2026 judging format, the number of semifinalists, and whether a fan vote exists.
- The exact field size (121 to 130 reported).
- Whether Nicaragua appears in the full hot-pick lists. I could not open the Pageant Circle, Metro.style or Missosology pages, only search summaries.
- Missing Nicaraguan placements for 2017–2019 and 2021, and conflicting accounts of 2024–2025.
- How the ownership dispute (JKN versus Nawat) and the 2025 rigging accusations might affect judging in 2026.
- How well she performs in pageant week. No preliminary-round evidence exists yet.
