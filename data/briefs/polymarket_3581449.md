# Research brief: Will Ko Chih-en (柯志恩, KMT) win the 2026 Kaohsiung mayoral election?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** Ko Chih-en is elected Mayor of Kaohsiung in the local elections on **Saturday, November 28, 2026**. Interim or acting mayors don't count. Credible media consensus decides the outcome. If that is ambiguous, the official Central Election Commission results at db.cec.gov.tw decide.
- **Timing:** Votes are hand-counted and results normally come in on election night, November 28 (Taiwan time). The market closes on November 29 at 04:59 UTC, after counting. The new mayor would take office around December 25, 2026, but resolution looks at who wins the election.
- **"Other" fallback:** If no result is known by June 30, 2027, the market resolves "Other". That is very unlikely. The realistic triggers are a candidate dying before the vote, which would postpone the election, or a lawsuit. Under Taiwan's election law, a margin under 0.3% allows a requested recount, but that would still finish well before June 2027.
- **Field:** Five people registered as candidates, but in practice it is a two-way race: Lai Jui-lung (賴瑞隆, DPP legislator) against Ko Chih-en (KMT legislator) (Search summary of PTS/News Lens/SETN, Sep 2026). I could not confirm the other three candidates' names or ballot numbers. The CEC was due to publish the municipal candidate list on November 12.
- **No new polls after November 18:** Taiwan bans publishing polls in the last 10 days before the vote. Expect almost no new public polling after about November 17–18.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
**A. Party results in Kaohsiung mayoral races since 1998.** These figures are from my background knowledge and the CNA/PTS figures I found, so check the exact percentages with the CEC.
- 1998: Frank Hsieh (DPP) won about 48.7% to 48.1%.
- 2002: Hsieh (DPP) won about 50.0% to 46.8%.
- 2006: Chen Chu (DPP) won by 1,114 votes.
- 2010: Chen Chu won 52.8% in the newly merged city.
- 2014: Chen Chu won about 68%.
- 2018: Han Kuo-yu (KMT) won with 892,545 votes against Chen Chi-mai's roughly 742,000 (about 53.9% to 44.8%) (CNA, Jan 11, 2020).
- 2020 by-election: Chen Chi-mai (DPP) won 70.0% (671,804 votes to 248,478) (PTS, Aug 2020).
- 2022: Chen beat Ko by **17.94 points**, roughly 58% to 40% (Taipei Times, Nov 27, 2022).

**DPP won 7 of 8 contests; the KMT won 1 of 8 (12.5%).** The one KMT win came from the 2018 "Han wave", a national anti-DPP swing led by a celebrity candidate. Since the 2010 merger, which added the former Kaohsiung County's strongly DPP districts, the DPP has won 4 of 5. Ko has run once before and lost by about 18 points, even though 2022 was a strong year for the KMT nationally (KMT won 14 local executive seats to the DPP's 5).

**B. Challengers trailing in most public polls a month out in Taiwan's six big cities.** The only clear comeback I know of is Kaohsiung 2018. Chen Chi-mai led by double digits in mid-2018 and Han had moved ahead by October, so the deficit was already gone a month before the vote. In 2014 and 2022, municipal candidates trailing by 7+ points in most polls four weeks out usually lost. My rough sense, which I have not tallied, is that well under 20% of such candidates won.

**C. Candidates in open seats (no incumbent) in a party's stronghold.** Open seats are more competitive than races with an incumbent. Even so, the DPP held its southern strongholds (Tainan, Kaohsiung, Pingtung) in the bad DPP year of 2022, and the only loss in the last 25 years was 2018.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES (Ko wins):**
1. **Undecided voters break to Ko and pan-green turnout is low.** This depends on DPP primary wounds staying open (82% consolidation) and on light-green voters staying home after 8 years of Chen Chi-mai.
2. **KMT–TPP vote consolidation.** About 60% of TPP supporters already back Ko, and Huang Kuo-chang is helping her. Full consolidation would matter a lot in a race where the opposition combined took about 40% in 2022.
3. **Momentum after the debate and polls that understate the KMT,** as in 2018. This would also need a late shock: a Lai scandal, a gaffe, or a national event that hurts the DPP.

**Paths to NO (Lai wins):**
1. **The structural DPP majority holds.** The 2010-merger electorate, the 2022 margin of about 18 points, and Lai leading in nearly every independent poll all point this way.
2. **A favourable national environment for the DPP in 2026,** the opposite of 2018 and 2022. Rising approval for the president should help pan-green consolidation as polling day approaches.
3. **Undecided voters split along party lines.** Kaohsiung's undecideds historically lean green, and voters expect Lai to win by roughly 49% to 21% in UDN's question. That kind of expectation tends to feed turnout and bandwagon effects.

## 5. Signposts
- **Last-allowed polls (around Nov 15–17), especially TVBS, UDN, ETtoday and My-Formosa.** If any neutral or KMT-leaning poll taken after the debate shows Ko tied or ahead, the probability of YES rises a lot. If those polls show Lai at +7 or more with green consolidation above 85%, it falls.
- **Scandals or legal problems** for either candidate in the last two weeks. These have a large effect on the probability.
- **Size of closing rallies and who appears.** Visible primary rivals campaigning for Lai means NO is more likely. Visible TPP mobilization for Ko pushes toward YES.
- **National shocks:** cross-strait incidents, controversy over the DPP central government, or the budget fight with the legislature.
- **Election night:** Results from early-reporting districts. Fengshan, Sanmin and Zuoying are bellwethers, and Ko needs a lead in the old city districts that is big enough to survive the green former county.

## 6. Uncertainties
- The pollsters disagree badly, from Ko +4.8 to Lai +20.4, partly because of partisan sponsors and different methods (landline vs. mobile, whether leaners are pushed). There is no public poll taken after the debate.
- The ETtoday October figures conflict (43.2–43.1 vs. 42.0–46.8). I could not check them because full-text pages could not be fetched; the proxy refused every fetch, so this brief relies on search-result summaries.
- I could not establish the names or vote potential of the three minor candidates.
- My exact historical vote shares for 1998–2022 come partly from memory and should be checked against the CEC database.
- I could not quantify how much Taiwanese municipal polls have historically missed by in Kaohsiung (2018 and 2022).
