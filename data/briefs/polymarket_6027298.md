# Research brief: Will Podemos win exactly 1 seat in Congress at the 29 November 2026 Spanish general election?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Podemos must win exactly one seat in the 350-seat Congress of Deputies. Only seats won on lists that Podemos presents on its own count.
- **Seats that do not count for Podemos:**
  - **Catalonia:** Podemos is not running there. It struck a deal with ERC, and Podem candidates appear as independents on ERC's lists (on the last place in Tarragona, for example). The rules give these seats to ERC (ERC will incorporate Podemos candidates…, Ara, Oct 2026).
  - **Valencian Community:** Podemos is part of the joint list "Compromís–Front Valencià" (with Compromís, EU and ERPV). The rules name this list as Broad Front (FA).
  - **Canary Islands:** Podemos is part of "Unidad del Pueblo Canario" (with NC-BC, IU and Sumar). The rules name this as FA too (Demócrata, Oct 2026).
- **Where Podemos runs alone:** Every other province, with Irene Montero heading the Madrid list. She won the primaries on 14–15 October with about 90.7% (24,136 votes). Podemos and the Broad Front did not reach a national agreement (Política Electoral; Diari de Tarragona, Oct 2026). The official candidacies were published in the BOE on 3 November 2026 (Artículo 14, 6 Nov 2026).
- **Timing:** Election day is Sunday 29 November 2026. Sánchez dissolved the Cortes on 5 October after his housing decrees were defeated (Artículo 14, 5 Oct 2026; Forbes Colombia, 5 Oct 2026). The provisional count comes out on election night. The formal count, including votes from Spaniards abroad (CERA), follows a few days later and can flip the last seat in a large province like Madrid. The fallback date of 30 November 2027 is very unlikely to apply.
- **Who decides:** A consensus of credible reporting, with the Interior Ministry (infoelectoral) as tie-breaker.
- **Edge cases:**
  - If Podemos runs under a variant name in some province (for example "Podemos–Alianza Verde", the brand it used in the 2026 regional elections), it is unclear whether that counts as "on its own". I could not check the exact ballot names for every province. This matters only if such a list wins a seat, which is unlikely outside Madrid.
  - **Madrid's seat count:** Madrid reportedly elects 38 seats this time, up from 37 in 2023, under Royal Decree 806/2026 (e29n.com / Mapa Electoral). That would make a first seat slightly easier to win.
- **Practical reading:** YES almost certainly means "Podemos wins exactly one seat in Madrid and none anywhere else."

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Seat arithmetic in Madrid (the binding constraint)**
- Each province has a 3% legal threshold. In a 37–38 seat province, the D'Hondt cutoff for a first seat is about 2.5–2.6% (Demócrata), so the 3% legal threshold is the real bar.
- A second Madrid seat would need roughly 5%+.
- Outside Madrid, the next-largest provinces where Podemos runs (Seville 12, Málaga 11, Alicante and Valencia excluded) need about 7–8% for a seat. Podemos polls nationally at 1–3%, so a seat outside Madrid is practically impossible.
- So the outcome is effectively: 0 seats if Podemos gets under about 3% in Madrid, 1 seat between about 3% and 5%, and 2 seats above about 5%.

**B. Podemos's recent record of underperforming its polls (approximate, partly from memory)**
- **Madrid regional, May 2023:** Polls had Podemos around 5–6%. It got 4.7% and missed the 5% threshold. Tactical "useful vote" squeezed it.
- **European election, 2024:** Podemos got about 3.3% nationally (2 MEPs), roughly in line with or slightly below polls.
- **Castilla y León regional, 15 March 2026:** Podemos–Alianza Verde got about 0.7–0.75% and 0 seats (The Objective; El Español, Mar 2026).
- **Aragón (Feb 2026) and Andalusia (May 2026):** Described as defeats for Podemos. El Debate (21 May 2026) reported Podemos falling to third force on the left and dropping out of regional parliaments.
- **Extremadura (Dec 2025):** The one bright spot, but Podemos ran jointly with IU there and won 7 seats.
- Overall, about 3 of the last 4 contests where Podemos ran in its own brand (Madrid 2023, CyL 2026, plus Aragón and Andalusia as described) ended at or below its polling and short of thresholds. The base rate of "matched or beat polls" is low.

**C. Small left splinter parties fighting a single-province threshold late in a polarised campaign (from memory)**
- Más País in November 2019 polled roughly 3–6 seats nationally and won 3, with 2 of them in Madrid on about 5.7% there. It underperformed but did not collapse.
- Small parties near the threshold in a polarised PP+Vox versus PSOE contest tend to lose late "useful vote" to PSOE. The Montero–Rufián event in Madrid was explicitly framed against that useful-vote squeeze (Ara, Nov 2026).
- Rough base rate: parties polling right at the threshold in the final two weeks lose about 1 point on average and miss maybe 40–60% of the time. This is a judgement call, not a counted dataset.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**YES (exactly 1 seat): Podemos gets about 3.0–5.0% in Madrid**
- Montero's name recognition and core activist vote hold.
- Its national share of about 1.7% is spread over only about 70% of the electorate (it is absent from Catalonia, Valencia and the Canaries). That implies roughly 2.4% where it actually runs. Madrid has historically been above Podemos's average, which makes about 3–3.5% in Madrid plausible.
- Rufián's endorsement helps marginally.

**NO via 0 seats: Madrid share under 3%**
- The 40dB and CIS figures (1.1–1.3% nationally) imply only about 2–2.5% in Madrid. That points to 0, and the CIS microdata recalculation already shows 0.
- The useful-vote squeeze toward PSOE or the Broad Front (Más Madrid is strong in Madrid) intensifies in the final week. Podemos's 2023 Madrid and 2026 regional record shows this pattern.
- This looks like the main competing outcome.

**NO via 2+ seats: Madrid share above about 5%**
- Only Data10 (3.1% nationally) and Electomanía point this way, and ElectoralLab's interval reaches 3. A late surge or a major polling miss would be needed. This looks low-probability (roughly 5–10% judging by the poll spread).

## 5. Signposts

- **Latest Sigma Dos and other trackers up to 23 Nov (last legal publication day):** A Madrid estimate under 3% or national under 1.5% shifts toward 0. Madrid at 3.5% or above firms up 1.
- **Madrid-specific polls (GAD3, Sigma Dos, Telemadrid):** These are the most informative signals.
- **Endorsement or withdrawal news:** Any late Broad Front–Podemos rapprochement or call for a useful vote toward the Broad Front in Madrid points to 0. A media surge from the debates points to 1.
- **Election night:** Watch where Podemos stands against the 3% Madrid line in the provisional count. If it is within about 0.1 points of the line, CERA votes and the formal count could change the result.
- **Ballot-name check:** Whether any Podemos list runs under a coalition name such as "Podemos–AV", in case of attribution disputes.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not open the polling tables directly (fetches were blocked). Figures come from search summaries and may have small errors. This includes the exact Sigma Dos 17 Nov seat count and the exact Madrid percentages in most polls.
- Madrid's seat count (38 versus 37) comes from secondary sources only.
- I did not verify Podemos's 2024 European election share in Madrid or its exact Aragón and Andalusia 2026 results.
- The e29n "93%" model and the CIS/40dB readings strongly disagree, and I could not determine how each model was built.
- I could not confirm that every non-Catalan, non-Valencian, non-Canarian Podemos list runs under the plain "Podemos" brand.
