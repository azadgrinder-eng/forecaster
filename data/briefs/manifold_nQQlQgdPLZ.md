# Research Brief: Will Inatsisartut pass a resolution scheduling a Greenland independence referendum by November 15, 2026?

*Prepared 2026-11-06. Every page fetch failed ("proxy refused the connection"), so this brief rests on search-result summaries and not on full articles. The prop text on braff.co could not be read either.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Greenland's parliament (Inatsisartut, 31 seats) must pass a resolution that schedules a national referendum on independence, on or before November 15, 2026. That leaves 9 days from today. The Manifold market closes November 17, 2026.
- **Who decides:** A panel of human judges for the 11th annual Narcissist Forecasting Contest (braff.co), under the fine print of the entry form. The market text says the prop "may resolve differently from how other, similar-looking Manifold props resolve." I could not read the exact prop wording or any notes.
- **Unclear points the judges would have to rule on:**
  - **Advisory vs. binding vote.** Under §21 of the Self-Government Act, the legally required referendum comes *after* Naalakkersuisut (Greenland's government) and Denmark have negotiated an independence agreement and Inatsisartut has consented to it. An earlier advisory "do Greenlanders want independence" vote is legally possible. It has been discussed since at least 2024: a legal review of an advisory referendum under §21 was promised. Probably either type would count if a date were actually set (Statsministeriet, "Grønland"; Sermitsiaq, "Siumut vil aktivere Selvstyrelovens § 21", ~2024).
  - **Scheduling vs. intent.** A resolution that only asks the government to *prepare* a referendum, or commits to one "within the term" with no date, might not count as "scheduling."
  - **Other referendums don't count.** A referendum on the US–Denmark–Greenland defence agreement, on the draft constitution, or on the national anthem would not be an independence referendum. Naalakkersuisut has proposed an anthem referendum to be held alongside the next ordinary Inatsisartut election (Sermitsiaq, "Folkeafstemning om nationalsang kan komme på tale", ~mid-Oct 2026). Siumut has said a vote on the draft constitution is "not the same as a vote on independence" (KNR, older).
  - **First reading isn't passage.** Inatsisartut normally adopts proposals at a second or third reading. A first-reading debate or a committee referral is not passage.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Has Greenland ever scheduled an independence referendum?** Never. Greenland's referendums have been on EC membership (1982), home rule (1979) and self-government (2008). Despite near-universal party support for eventual independence, no independence vote has ever been scheduled, mainly because of economic dependence on Denmark (DIIS, "Greenland's course towards independence was steered by referendums"; Wikipedia, "Greenlandic independence"). Since §21 took effect in 2009, that is 0 scheduled in about 17 years, or about 0 in roughly 34 parliamentary sessions (spring and autumn). Read as a rate for any 9-day window, the chance is far below 1%.
2. **Do opposition referendum motions pass in Greenland?** Naleraq is the only party pushing for a fast referendum. In 2026 its proposals have generally been rejected or recommended for rejection by the government. Examples:
   - Its Inuit-registration proposal was recommended for rejection (High North News, 2026).
   - Its call for a referendum on the defence agreement got no support. Its committee minority opinion "regrets that there is no support for a referendum" (Sermitsiaq, "Forsvarsaftale med USA skal andenbehandles i Inatsisartut i dag", ~Nov 3, 2026).

   Opposition seats are roughly Naleraq 8 and Siumut ~4, about 12 of 31. Even unanimous opposition support is short of the 16 needed for a majority.
3. **Comparable independence movements:** In sub-national independence movements with self-rule (Faroe Islands, New Caledonia, Scotland, Bougainville), parliaments moving from "eventual independence" to a dated referendum usually do it after a long, public build-up: a manifesto pledge, a government white paper and a commission report. All three are missing in Greenland's current term.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 12 and Nov 19, 2026 (scheduled):** The Danish Folketing holds its first and final debates on the defence agreement (KSL/AP; Nordisk Post, Nov 4, 2026). This is relevant only as context, since it is a Danish vote.
- **Nov 12, 2026 (scheduled):** Inatsisartut makes its final decision on the time-zone change. This shows the chamber is sitting in the days before November 15 (Sermitsiaq, "Afgørelsen af tidens bestemmelse har fået ny dato").
- **Nov 3, 2026:** Inatsisartut approved the US–Denmark–Greenland security agreement 16–0 with 7 abstentions (PBS/AP; TASS; All Things Nordic, Nov 5, 2026).
  - The deal updates the 1951 defence agreement and names Narsarsuaq and Mestersvig as planned US base sites.
  - It reportedly commits Greenland to join NATO if it becomes independent.
  - Naleraq wanted a referendum on the agreement. It objected to the lack of guaranteed financial compensation and to commitments that would bind an independent Greenland. No referendum was approved.
  - The committee majority recommending adoption was Demokraatit, IA and Atassut (Sermitsiaq, ~Nov 3, 2026).
- **Sept 22, 2026:** The trilateral agreement was signed in New York (Sermitsiaq).
- **Sept 18, 2026:** The autumn session opened with 108 agenda items: 52 new member proposals and 16 from Naalakkersuisut. The session reportedly runs to January 29, 2027 (Sermitsiaq, "Efterårssamlingen skudt i gang").
  - The only independence-related item found: Aki-Matilda Høegh-Dam (Naleraq) asked whether it is appropriate that there is no longer a Department for Independence "in both name and function." It was scheduled for September 29.
  - **No independence-referendum motion was found on the agenda.**
- **June 2026:** An Altinget opinion piece mentions that a separate §21 commission was "set in motion" in March 2026 (Altinget Arktis). The UK House of Commons Library says a government commission on activating §21 was set up in September 2024 and is "expected to present its conclusions in late 2026," and that "none of Greenland's main political parties plan to launch the independence process before then" (House of Commons Library CBP-10234, Feb 3, 2026). I found no report that the commission has delivered.
- **Mar 24, 2026:** Naleraq won its first seat in the Danish Folketing (The Star/Reuters, Mar 26, 2026).
- **Mar 13, 2026:** Siumut left the coalition over ministers running in the Danish election. Vivian Motzfeldt resigned as foreign minister and later left Siumut. The remaining coalition of Demokraatit, IA and Atassut still holds a majority (about 19 of 31) and reportedly "has no plans to seek independence from Denmark in the immediate future" (The Local DK, Mar 14, 2026; Arctic Today; Arctic Portal).
- **Early 2026:** The "Greenland crisis" with US pressure peaked. Nielsen's line was "We choose Denmark" (TIME, Jan 2026).
- **Polling:**
  - A Verian poll for Berlingske/Sermitsiaq found 56% would vote yes to independence "if held today," 28% no and 17% undecided. 45% would oppose independence if it lowered living standards. Reports are confused about whether this poll is from January 2025 or January 2026.
  - A 2026 poll cited by The Northern Voices found 62% saying Greenland should *not* become independent *now*, against 25% saying it should.
- **Premier's position:** Jens-Frederik Nielsen (Demokraatit, premier since April 7, 2025) backs eventual independence and gradual economic build-up, without a referendum date. A DR article headline sums up the long-standing pattern: "no one dares put a year on an independence referendum" (DR, undated, likely 2024–25).

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (all unlikely within 9 days):**
- **The coalition fast-tracks a referendum.** For example, it could answer the defence deal's NATO-on-independence clause, or a new US provocation, by setting a date for an advisory referendum. This would require a sharp policy reversal by Demokraatit/IA and a passed resolution within days. Normal Inatsisartut procedure takes several readings, usually spread over weeks. Nothing found suggests such a proposal is pending.
- **A Naleraq motion passes.** Naleraq would need about 4 more votes beyond its own and Siumut's, meaning coalition defections. That is implausible: the coalition just rejected even a referendum on the defence deal.
- **The §21 commission reports in early November and recommends an immediate referendum, and parliament adopts it at once.** No report has been found, and this is very unlikely to fit inside 9 days.
- **Loose resolution by the judges.** They might count a resolution on a constitution referendum, or a vague commitment, as YES. This is the most plausible "surprise" path, but I found no such pending resolution.

**Paths to NO (the default):**
- The status quo continues. The government's stated approach is gradual, it waits for the §21 commission, and it has just bound itself to the defence deal with Denmark and the US. The session agenda contains no independence-referendum item, and the week of November 9–13 is taken up by the time-zone vote, the budget, the disability law and similar business.

## 5. Signposts

- **Strong move toward YES:**
  - An independence-referendum proposal (from Naalakkersuisut or with coalition backing) placed on the Inatsisartut agenda for a vote before November 15. Check ina.gl's agenda and news pages and Sermitsiaq/KNR.
  - Publication of the §21 commission report with a recommended referendum date.
  - Demokraatit or IA leaders publicly endorsing a dated referendum.
- **Weak or irrelevant:**
  - Naleraq floating motions on its own, without coalition support.
  - Folketing debates on November 12 and 19.
  - Referendums on the anthem or the constitution, unless the judges read the prop loosely.
- **Toward NO:** The session simply continues on budget and legislation business through November 15. That is the expected path.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not read the exact prop wording or the judges' fine print, including whether an advisory or a "commitment" resolution would count.
- I could not confirm the §21 commission's current status or delivery date. Sources say "late 2026" without specifics.
- I did not see the full autumn-session agenda, so I cannot fully rule out a pending member proposal on an independence referendum. None surfaced in searches in English and Danish.
- Exact current seat counts after the 2025–26 defections, and the session end date: the reported January 29, 2027 is unusually late for Greenland.
- Whether the 56%-yes Verian poll is from January 2025 or January 2026.
