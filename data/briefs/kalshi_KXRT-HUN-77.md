# Research brief: will *The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping* have a Tomatometer above 77 on Nov 23, 2026?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** the Tomatometer (critics' score, not the audience Popcornmeter) must be strictly above 77 when checked on Monday, Nov 23, 2026 at 10:00 AM ET. In practice that means 78% or higher. A 77% resolves NO, and the rules give an example of a score equal to the strike resolving No.
- **When it is checked:** "the Monday after wide release." The US wide release is Thursday–Friday, Nov 20, 2026 (Rotten Tomatoes page lists a theatrical date of Nov 20; Deadline, Nov 2026). The score is a single snapshot at 10:00 AM ET. It is not an average and not the score at embargo lift.
- **Who decides:** Kalshi, reading the number shown on the RT page (rottentomatoes.com/m/the_hunger_games_sunrise_on_the_reaping). As of this search the page showed 0 reviews.
- **Edge cases:**
  - RT shows a whole-number percentage. A true rate of 77.6% would probably display as 78%, and the displayed number is presumably what counts.
  - The score changes live as reviews are added. Late-weekend reviews from regional and online critics can still move it on Monday morning.
  - The rules don't say what happens if the page is down, or what happens if RT changes the display (for example, the "Top Critics" toggle). The default all-critics Tomatometer is presumably the one that counts.
  - The "Fresh" and "Certified Fresh" labels don't matter.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

*Several historical scores below are from my background knowledge, not verified this session. Treat them as approximate (±2 pts).*

**A. Films in this franchise (n=5).** The scores are approximately:
- The Hunger Games (2012): 84%
- Catching Fire (2013): 90% ("The Hunger Games Movies Ranked," RT Editorial)
- Mockingjay Part 1: about 68–70%
- Mockingjay Part 2: about 70%
- The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes (2023): about 64% (Looper / Dark Horizons, 2023)

**Result: 2 of 5 (40%) are above 77.** Counting only the four films directed by Francis Lawrence (Catching Fire onward), 1 of 4 (25%) is above 77. The franchise trend has been downward since 2013. Points in this film's favour:
- Co-writer Billy Ray also co-wrote the 2012 film (84%).
- Lawrence's most recent non-franchise film, *The Long Walk* (2025), was well reviewed. From memory it scored in the high 80s; I did not verify this.
- The source novel was well reviewed (Kirkus starred review, 2025).

**B. Big-studio tentpoles with uniformly glowing social-media first reactions, 2024–25 (n≈17, approximate scores).**
- **Above 77 (8):** Wicked 88, Dune: Part Two 92, Deadpool & Wolverine 78, Superman 83, Fantastic Four: First Steps 86, Thunderbolts* 88, Mission: Impossible – The Final Reckoning ~80, Predator: Badlands ~85.
- **At or below 77 (9):** Gladiator II ~71, Moana 2 ~61, Wicked: For Good ~68–69, Captain America: Brave New World ~48, Jurassic World Rebirth ~51, Lilo & Stitch ~70, How to Train Your Dragon (2025) ~76, Avatar: Fire and Ash ~66–68, Tron: Ares ~53.

**Result: about 8 of 17 (≈45–50%) finished above 77.** Social first reactions are reliably positive whatever the film's quality, so on their own they carry little information. Reactions that specifically say "best in franchise" or "Rogue One/Andor of the franchise" tend to come at the stronger end, as with Thunderbolts*, Predator: Badlands and Fantastic Four.

**C. How much the score moves between embargo lift and the Monday after release.** The score usually drifts down by about 2–5 points as more reviews come in:
- Ballad: 67% on 46 reviews, then 64% on about 102 reviews (Looper / Dark Horizons, Nov 2023).
- Wicked: For Good: 74% on 80 reviews, then 69% on 203 reviews (GamesRadar / mxdwn, Nov 2025).

So the film probably needs to open at about 80% or higher on embargo day to be safely above 77 on Monday. One cross-check: Ballad's social reactions were also strongly positive ("Magnificent," "gritty and complex" in TheWrap's 2023 headline), yet it finished at about 64%.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (78% or higher on Monday):**
- The critical consensus agrees with the London reactions: a darker, more focused story centred on a strong lead performance, in the vein of Catching Fire. That would plausibly land in the 80s.
- The source novel is widely seen as one of Collins's stronger books, and the material (Haymitch's Quarter Quell) is lean and tragic.
- Lawrence is coming off a well-reviewed film (*The Long Walk*), and Ray's track record is strong.
- Major-outlet critics treat it as a return to form after Ballad, and "best since Catching Fire" becomes the framing.

**Paths to NO (77% or lower):**
- The 158-minute length, familiarity ("we've seen this before"), and franchise fatigue push a share of critics to mild negatives. These are the same complaints that sank Ballad (64%) and the Mockingjay films (about 70%).
- The franchise's recent baseline is about 64–70%.
- Downward drift after embargo lift: even an opening score of 79–81% could slide to 76–78% by Monday.
- RT counts each review as simply fresh or rotten, so a pile of 6/10-style "fine" reviews, split down the middle, pulls the score toward 60–75%.

**What it mostly depends on:** whether the film is "good for a franchise film" (scores in the 70s) or "good, full stop" (scores in the 80s). Fan-premiere reactions don't separate these two well.

## 5. Signposts

- **Strongest signal:** the score in the first hours after the embargo lifts (around Nov 17, 9am ET, with roughly 60–120 reviews).
  - 85% or higher: likely YES even after drift.
  - 79–84%: leaning YES but at real risk.
  - 74–78%: likely NO given typical drift.
  - Below 72%: almost certainly NO.
- **Trades and top critics:** what Variety, THR, IndieWire, NYT and the Guardian say, and the Top Critics sub-score. A lower Top Critics score often foreshadows decline.
- **Embargo timing:** an early or Tuesday lift suggests studio confidence; a lift on Wednesday or later would be a negative sign. If the Nov 17 date is confirmed, that is mildly positive.
- **Review count by Sunday:** compare the score at about 200+ reviews with the opening snapshot to measure the drift.
- **Late festival or press-screening chatter** before the embargo (e.g., "mixed" or "divisive" reports) would point toward NO.
- **CinemaScore and audience scores are irrelevant to resolution**, apart from loosely signalling the film's reception.
