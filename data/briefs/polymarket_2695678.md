# Research brief: Will Pedro Sánchez be the next Prime Minister of Spain?

*As of 11 Nov 2026. Note: WebFetch was blocked in this environment, so most facts below come from search-result summaries rather than full articles. Treat exact poll figures as approximate.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Sánchez is the next person who wins a parliamentary vote in the Congress of Deputies, is sworn in and forms a new government, by 31 Mar 2028 at 11:59 PM ET. Sánchez is the sitting PM, so being re-invested after the coming election should count as YES; the market lists him as an option.
- **What counts as NO:** anyone else invested first, such as Feijóo (PP), another PSOE figure or a compromise candidate. If nobody is invested by 31 Mar 2028, the market resolves "Other." Caretaker (*en funciones*) status does not count. Sánchez will stay on as caretaker after 29 Nov whatever happens, and that alone does not resolve YES.
- **Who decides:** official Government of Spain information, with a consensus of credible reporting as backup.
- **Edge cases:**
  - Spain's mechanism is an investiture vote under Art. 99 of the Constitution, not a "vote of confidence" in the strict sense. The rules almost certainly mean investiture.
  - A successful constructive motion of censure (Art. 113, the route Sánchez used in 2018) installs a PM without an investiture vote. It would probably count under "consensus reporting," but this is ambiguous.
- **Timeline (Real Decreto 806/2026, BOE 6 Oct 2026):**
  - Election day: Sun 29 Nov 2026.
  - Campaign: 13–27 Nov.
  - Last day polls may be published: 23 Nov.
  - New Cortes meet: 23 Dec 2026, 10:00.
  - Then the King consults the parties and the first investiture debate probably comes in January 2027.
  - If the first investiture vote fails, a two-month clock starts. When it runs out, the Cortes are dissolved and a repeat election follows about 47 days later, i.e. roughly May–June 2027.
- **"Other" is very unlikely.** Even Spain's longest deadlocks fit easily before March 2028: 2015–16 took about 10 months, and 2019 about 8 months from the April election to the Jan 2020 investiture.
- **Timing of the close:** the question closes 30 Nov 04:59 UTC, which is after the 29 Nov results will be known (polls shut 20:00 CET and the count is usually done by about midnight). The seat arithmetic will be visible before close, but who actually gets invested may not be settled until January 2027 or later.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Spanish PMs who ran again were usually re-invested.** Since 1979, incumbents who stood for re-election kept office in 9 of 10 cases (my count from memory of election history, not verified in this search):
   - Wins: Suárez 1979; González 1986, 1989, 1993; Aznar 2000; Zapatero 2008; Rajoy 2016 after the 2015 deadlock; Sánchez 2019 and 2023.
   - Loss: González 1996, when he trailed in polls and lost narrowly.
   - This is a weak prior here, because most of those incumbents were leading in polls. Sánchez is not.
2. **Spanish polls have repeatedly underestimated the PSOE and overestimated the right.**
   - In the 2023 election (23J), most polls had PP+Vox above 176 seats and the PP above 140. The result was PP 137 + Vox 33 = 170.
   - The PP was "the worst-measured party," overestimated by up to 5 points (Ara, 2023). GAD3 publicly admitted its error.
   - From memory: the final 2023 average was roughly PP 34 / PSOE 28, against an actual PP 33.1 / PSOE 31.7. That is a PSOE miss of about 3.5 points and a miss of about 10 seats on the PP+Vox total.
   - Today's averages give PP+Vox about 194–198 seats, a cushion of 18–22 seats over 176. That is roughly twice the 2023 miss.
   - Even if polls are off by as much as in 2023, the right bloc would still have about 184–188 seats. To deny it a majority, polls would need to be off by about twice as much as in 2023, although bloc-level errors and the regional seat formula (D'Hondt) can make seat swings bigger than vote swings.
3. **Incumbents trailing by about 4–6 points with three weeks to go** usually lose the plurality. Sánchez in 2023 shows that losing the plurality need not mean losing power. Here, though, one of his 2023 partners (Junts) has publicly refused to back him again.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

**Polls**

- **10 Nov – DYM for 20minutos:** PP 33.1%, PSOE 27.3%, Vox 18.6%. PP+Vox 198–205 seats. Both PP and PSOE slipped and Vox rose after the Urda rail accident and Puente's resignation (Democrata.es, 10 Nov 2026).
- **9–10 Nov – poll averages:**
  - DatosClaros: PP 30.5 / PSOE 26.9 / Vox 19.4, roughly 130 / 110 / 68 seats, PP+Vox about 198.
  - eleccionesenespana.es (15 polls): PP 130, PSOE 110, Vox 67, Sumar/Frente Amplio 11.
  - Democrata.es (14 pollsters, 9 Nov): PP 31.5 / PSOE 26.7 / Vox 19.1.
  - Mundiario (nine polls): PP+Vox about 194.
  - Right-wing parties total about 50.6% of the vote (Bilbaohiria, 10 Nov).
- **9 Nov – 40dB for El País/SER:** PP 31.9%, PSOE 29.9%, Vox 18%. Seats: 129 / 119 / 61, so PP+Vox = 190. This is the PSOE's best mainstream poll, up 1.8 points (Democrata.es, 9 Nov 2026).
- **Other recent polls:**
  - GAD3/ABC: PP 31.9, PSOE 25.9, Vox 18.6.
  - SocioMétrica/El Español: PP 135, PSOE 108, Vox about 63.
  - Sigma Dos (26 Oct): PP 125–131, PSOE 103–109, Vox 66–69.
  - SW Demoscopia/La Séptima: PSOE first at 29.8%, but PP+Vox still 179.
- **Outlier – CIS (state pollster):** the October barometer (14 Oct) put the PSOE about 11.8 points ahead; September had PSOE 31 / PP 25.5. The CIS has a long-running pro-PSOE gap against private pollsters (Democrata.es; Ara).
- **Left fragmentation:** Sumar/Frente Amplio projected at about 10–12 seats (31 in 2023), Podemos at 0–1. A joint list could take about 15 seats instead of about 7 if they run separately (DYM via El Boletín). The PSOE's "comeback" is said to be 17–24 seats short (La Voz del Sur).

**Campaign events and party positions**

- **7 Nov:** Vox is making "institutional regeneration" a precondition for backing a Feijóo investiture. The PP needs Vox for, among other things, renewing the Constitutional Court (Moncloa.com, 7 Nov 2026). Abascal has also said the election "won't be clean" and set conditions for a deal (Democrata.es).
- **4–6 Nov:**
  - Abascal promises "radical change." Feijóo keeps the red lines from his regional pacts and is distancing himself from Vox to win over centrist voters (El Independiente, 4 Nov; Moncloa.com, 6 Nov).
  - Feijóo prefers a PP government with outside Vox support but does not rule out Vox ministers (Democrata.es).
- **3–5 Nov:**
  - Transport Minister Óscar Puente resigned after the Urda (Toledo) rail accident, which killed 69 people. He also left the PSOE's list for Valladolid, and Carlos Cuerpo took over Transport.
  - The PSOE is relieved and is refocusing its campaign on public services ("we are not the same"). Internal party polls reportedly look poor after the accident (The Objective, 4 Nov; eldiario.es; CLM24, 3 Nov).
  - PSOE headquarters is also trying to use the "Trump defeat" (presumably the US midterms) for momentum (Moncloa.com, 5 Nov).
- **26 Oct:** PP and Vox agreed a Senate pact across 23 constituencies, with Vox standing down in six provinces and the PP in three (Última Hora).
- **23 Oct:** Feijóo openly aims to unseat Sánchez "even if the PSOE wins" the most votes (Mundiario).
- **7 Oct:** Junts (Turull, Nogueras) ruled out investing Sánchez again: "we came out badly burned." Before the election was called, Junts had asked the PSOE to put forward a different candidate. Junts also says PP and Vox will not govern with its votes (El Nacional; eldiario.es; ElTriangle).
- **5–6 Oct:** Sánchez called the snap election after Congress rejected his two housing decrees (PP, Vox, UPN and Junts voted against). He dissolved the Cortes about nine months early, asking voters for "a much broader majority" (Infobae; France 24, 5 Oct 2026).
- **Sánchez's own plans:** when asked whether he would quit politics if he lost, Sánchez avoided answering (Esdiario, Oct 2026).

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to NO**

- **A. Clear right-wing majority** (PP+Vox ≥176, the outcome every private poll projects). Feijóo is invested in about January 2027 with Vox votes.
  - Main risk: Vox drives a hard bargain over its regeneration demands or seats in cabinet.
  - Vox abstaining is not enough: PP's roughly 130 yes votes would lose to about 150+ no votes in the second ballot. Vox must vote yes.
  - Precedents: Vox voted for Feijóo unconditionally in 2023, and regional PP–Vox deals have eventually closed after brinkmanship.
  - A delay or even a repeat election would most likely still end with Feijóo as the next invested PM.
- **B. Hung parliament with a new PSOE candidate.** If the right falls short but Junts holds the balance and won't back Sánchez, the PSOE might put forward someone else. Junts has already hinted at this.
- **C. Feijóo replaced as the right's candidate.** An unlikely tail.

**Paths to YES**

- **D. Polls miss badly.** PP+Vox+UPN fall clearly below about 171 seats. Sánchez then needs PSOE, Sumar, Podemos, ERC, Bildu, PNV, BNG and CC to outvote the right in the second ballot, with Junts at least abstaining.
  - Because Junts refuses to vote yes, he needs yes votes to exceed no votes without them. A 2023-sized polling error is not enough; it would take an error of about twice that size.
- **E. Junts reverses** after the election (it did so in 2023 in exchange for the amnesty), in a parliament where the right is below 176.
- **F. A deadlocked right** (for example Vox refusing to back Feijóo) leads to a repeat election in 2027 in which the PSOE recovers. This is a low-probability, multi-step path.

## 5. Signposts

- **Election-night seats (29 Nov):** the decisive signal.
  - PP+Vox(+UPN) at 176 or more: Sánchez's chances fall to near zero.
  - 170–175: depends on Junts, CC and the PNV.
  - Below 170: Sánchez becomes plausible.
- **Final pre-ban polls (by 23 Nov) and debates:**
  - PSOE within about 2 points of the PP and Vox falling below 17% would raise the chance of YES.
  - Signs of lingering damage from the Urda accident would lower it.
- **Shift in the PSOE–PP vote gap:** a few points matter. In 2023 the final average gap of about 6 points became 1.4.
- **Joint Sumar–Podemos list or tactical voting on the left:** this changes the left's seat efficiency by about 5–8 seats.
- **Post-election statements:**
  - A Junts softening raises the chance of YES.
  - Sánchez hinting he'll step aside, or PSOE barons calling for a new candidate, lowers it.
  - A quick PP–Vox deal (before the 23 Dec constitutive session) lowers it.
- **Vox ultimatums or threats of a repeat election:** these would mainly delay things rather than flip the outcome.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not read the full articles because WebFetch was blocked. Some figures are inconsistent across sources; for example, one 40dB headline gives the PSOE 27.1% while the table says 29.9%.
- I found no November CIS figures.
- How large Spain's polling error will be in 2026, and its direction, is unknown. Seat translation in small provinces is volatile.
- The exact content of Vox's "three conditions" is unclear.
- Whether Sánchez would stay on as leader after a defeat is unclear.
- Junts' post-election flexibility is unknown.
- How the market would treat a PM installed by a motion of censure is ambiguous.
- My historical base rates (the incumbent record and the 2023 polling miss) partly come from memory and were only partly confirmed by search.
