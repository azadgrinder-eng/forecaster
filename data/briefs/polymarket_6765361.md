# Research brief: Will no second mandate to form a government be given after Israel's October 27, 2026 election?

*Prepared November 20, 2026. Every page fetch failed (proxy refused the connection), so this brief rests on search-result summaries of the articles, not full texts. Treat exact figures and quotes as needing confirmation.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as a second mandate:** The President of Israel (Isaac Herzog) formally tasks a **different individual** from the first mandate-holder with forming a government.
- **What does not count:**
  - Extensions of the first mandate.
  - Re-tasking the same person, including through a 61-MK petition.
  - Faction recommendations or consultations.
- **"No second mandate" resolves YES if:**
  - no different individual is tasked by Dec 31, 2027, 11:59 PM ET; or
  - the Knesset is dissolved, or new elections are called, before a second individual is tasked.
- **Who decides:** Official Government of Israel information, or a consensus of credible reporting.
- **First mandate:** Herzog tasked Yashar leader **Gadi Eisenkot** on Nov 8, 2026. Some outlets date the announcement to Saturday, Nov 7 (President's Office, Nov 8, 2026; Axios, Nov 7, 2026).
- **Legal pathway under Basic Law: The Government (2001):**
  - **§8, first mandate:** 28 days, so the deadline is about **Dec 6, 2026**. The President may extend by up to 14 days, to about **Dec 19–20, 2026**. The extension is discretionary, and Eisenkot's camp reportedly does not take it for granted (Israel365 News, Nov 2026; Times of Israel, Nov 2026).
  - **§9, the President's choice if the first candidate fails:** If the time runs out, the candidate says he can't form a government, or (reportedly) the Knesset rejects his government in a confidence vote, the President may do one of two things:
    - **(a)** Task **another MK**, which would resolve this market to that person. Translations differ on whether this MK gets 28 or 14 days. Reporting says Netanyahu would get 28 days with no extension.
    - **(b)** Tell the Knesset Speaker he sees no possibility of a government.
  - **§10, the 21-day petition route:** Within 21 days, 61 MKs may ask in writing that a specific MK be tasked. A petition naming Eisenkot again would not count; one naming anyone else (for example Netanyahu or Bennett) would count.
  - **§11, automatic dissolution:** If no petition is filed, or the petition nominee fails, the Knesset is deemed dissolved. That resolves to "No second mandate" unless a second person was already tasked earlier.
- **Timing:** Every plausible path to a second mandate runs to roughly late December 2026 or January 2027, far inside the Dec 31, 2027 deadline. The market's listed close date (Jan 1, 2027) falls before the 2027 resolution deadline.
- **Edge cases and ambiguities:**
  1. **Failed confidence vote:** Eisenkot may put a government to a confidence vote before his mandate ends. If he loses, it is not fully confirmed from primary text whether that triggers §9 (presidential choice) or goes straight to the §10 petition route. One summary of §10 lists "a rejected confidence vote" among its triggers, so check the Knesset PDF.
  2. **Later government collapse:** If Eisenkot forms a government that later falls in 2027 and someone else is tasked, it is unclear whether that would count as "following the 2026 election." The likely reading is no: the market resolves when a government is formed.
  3. **Unity-deal handoff:** In a unity deal where Netanyahu serves first, Eisenkot might return the mandate and Herzog (or a 61-MK petition) would task Netanyahu. That counts as a second mandate.
  4. **Self-dissolution:** If the Knesset passes a dissolution law before Eisenkot's mandate lapses, as in May 2019, it resolves "No second mandate."

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**RC1: All Israeli post-election formations since the 2001 return to the parliamentary system.** Ten elections: 2003, 2006, 2009, 2013, 2015, Apr 2019, Sep 2019, Mar 2020, Mar 2021, Nov 2022.
- A different second individual was tasked in **3 of 10 (30%)**:
  - Sep 2019: Gantz, after Netanyahu.
  - 2020: Netanyahu, via a 72-MK petition after Gantz failed and Rivlin referred the task to the Knesset.
  - 2021: Lapid, after Netanyahu.
- All three came during the 2019–21 deadlock (from my own knowledge and the Wikipedia article on the 2018–22 political crisis).

**RC2: First mandate-holder fails, or there is a deadlock with no 61-seat bloc.** Four cases (Apr 2019, Sep 2019, 2020, 2021).
- A second individual followed in **3 of 4**.
- The exception was Apr–May 2019: Netanyahu's camp dissolved the Knesset (74–45) before his mandate ran out, specifically to block a second mandate.
- Conditional on Eisenkot failing, history therefore points strongly to a second mandate, unless Herzog refers the task to the Knesset and no non-Eisenkot petition follows, or the Knesset dissolves itself.

**RC3: Non-incumbent opposition leaders given the first or a second mandate without a 61-seat bloc.**
- Gantz 2019 failed. Gantz 2020 failed and later joined a Netanyahu-led unity deal.
- Lapid 2021 succeeded, though Bennett became PM under the Bennett-Lapid deal, with Ra'am inside the coalition.
- That is roughly **1 of 3** successes. The 2021 success depended on Bennett, Liberman and Ra'am all joining, and today Bennett and Liberman reject Ra'am in the coalition.

**Presidential behaviour when the first mandate fails:**
- Rivlin gave a direct second mandate in **2 of 3** cases (Gantz 2019, Lapid 2021). In each case the recipient had the next-most recommendations.
- In 2020 he referred the task to the Knesset instead, and the petition route still produced a different individual.
- Herzog has never faced this situation; his only prior case was 2022, when Netanyahu formed a government.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to "No second mandate" (YES):**
1. **Eisenkot wins a confidence vote.** This could be a Haredi-backed coalition or a minority government with Ra'am votes and UTJ or Joint List abstentions. It mainly depends on:
   - Liberman (and Bennett) abstaining rather than voting no, or UTJ and Shas agreeing on draft terms;
   - a large enough number of Arab abstentions or yes votes.
   - Historical precedent: Lapid/Bennett 2021 formed with Ra'am.
2. **Unity government led by Eisenkot** with Likud under someone other than Netanyahu, or a "Netanyahu for president" deal in which Eisenkot is PM. This looks unlikely, given Netanyahu's insistence on leading.
3. **Eisenkot fails, Herzog refers to the Knesset, and either no petition succeeds or 61 MKs petition for Eisenkot again.** The 67-MK anti-Netanyahu majority could re-petition for Eisenkot; that is a plausible tactical route because it does not count. If nothing is filed, the Knesset dissolves automatically.
4. **The Knesset dissolves itself before a second mandate.** This seems less likely: the Eisenkot camp controls the Speaker, Likud lacks 61 votes, and dissolution leaves Netanyahu as caretaker, which the opposition wants to avoid.

**Paths to a second mandate (NO):**
1. **Eisenkot fails (deadline lapses or confidence vote lost) and Herzog tasks Netanyahu,** who had the next-most recommendations (46). Reporting describes this as the expected default (Israel365, Nov 2026). This is the dominant NO path. Herzog could instead refer the task to the Knesset if he judges Netanyahu also has no path. Netanyahu would need 8 or more from Bennett, Liberman, Democrats, Yashar or the Arab parties, all of whom currently refuse.
2. **A unity deal with Netanyahu leading first,** delivered through a fresh presidential tasking or a 61-MK petition.
3. **A petition for a third person,** such as Bennett or a Likud figure other than Netanyahu, backed by 61 MKs.

**Key dependency:** If Eisenkot fails, the market turns largely on Herzog's discretion under §9. Rivlin's record is 2 of 3 direct second mandates, plus 1 of 1 petitions producing a different person.

## 5. Signposts

**Would raise "No second mandate" (YES):**
- Liberman or Bennett signalling abstention, or UTJ committing to abstain, before a confidence vote.
- A draft compromise announced with UTJ or Shas.
- Ra'am agreeing to vote yes as a bloc.
- The confidence vote scheduled and passing.
- Herzog signalling he would refer the task to the Knesset rather than task Netanyahu.
- Anti-Netanyahu MKs organising a petition to re-task Eisenkot.

**Would lower "No second mandate" (toward NO):**
- A failed confidence vote, or Eisenkot returning the mandate.
- Herzog refusing an extension on about Dec 6, which brings the handoff forward.
- Reports that Herzog intends to task Netanyahu.
- A Netanyahu-first unity framework.
- A Haredi refusal (draft exemptions) combined with Liberman and Bennett voting no.

**Watch dates:** Nov 22 talks; the possible vote around Nov 28–Dec 5; Dec 6; Dec 19–20.
