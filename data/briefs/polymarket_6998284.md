# Research brief: Will Jensen Huang rejoin NVIDIA in a formal role by November 30, 2026?

**Method note:** Every attempt to open a page directly failed because the proxy refused the connection. All facts below come from search-result summaries of the cited articles, not from the full articles or filings. A forecaster should treat exact wording and small details as needing confirmation. Some reports disagree on dates, and those disagreements are flagged below.

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Between market creation and Nov 30, 2026, 11:59 PM ET, NVIDIA or Huang officially announces that Huang has accepted or begun a formal role at NVIDIA or a subsidiary. Qualifying roles are:
  - employee, executive or officer
  - director
  - formal advisor
  - a governance role, including interim or acting positions
  - membership in a body formally responsible for governing or overseeing the company

  An announcement that he has accepted is enough, even if he starts later. NVIDIA statements, SEC filings and Huang's own statements are the main sources. A consensus of credible reporting can also be used.
- **What does not count:** holding, trading or voting his shares; commercial or research partnerships; informal advice; appearing at events; honorary or emeritus titles.
- **Time left:** about 13 days from Nov 17, including the Thanksgiving week (Thanksgiving is Nov 26).
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Paid advisory or consulting agreement.** Outgoing CEOs often get a transition consulting agreement, which would count as "formal advisory." Reporting summarized on Wikipedia says the company confirmed Huang "would have no formal role at the firm going forward" (Jensen Huang – Wikipedia, accessed Nov 2026). Any 8-K/A or separation agreement that adds advisory duties would be a YES path, and a sneaky one because it would not look like a comeback.
  - **Board seat.** The board can fill a vacancy itself without a shareholder vote. Shareholders could also try to elect him, but that is not realistic in 13 days (see §3).
  - **"Market creation" date.** Not given. It is presumably on or after Nov 12, so an announcement dated earlier would not count.
  - **Wrong label in some outlets.** Some coverage loosely calls him "ex-CEO" or says he was "sacked." That reporting does not create a role.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

These counts are my own tallies of well-known cases, not systematic datasets.

**A. Prominent CEOs who left over a personal-conduct or relationship scandal, and whether they got a formal role back within about 3 weeks.**

Cases:
- Brian Krzanich (Intel, 2018)
- Steve Easterbrook (McDonald's, 2019)
- Mark Hurd (HP, 2010)
- Darren Huston (Priceline, 2016)
- Travis Kalanick (Uber, 2017). He stayed on the board but never returned as CEO.
- John Schnatter (Papa John's, 2018). He fought the board and lost.
- Dov Charney (American Apparel, 2014). His shareholder-backed attempt to return failed.
- Les Moonves (CBS, 2018)
- Steve Wynn (Wynn Resorts, 2018)
- Gary Friedman (RH, 2012)

Result: **0 of about 10 got a formal role back within 3 weeks.** The closest is Gary Friedman, who resigned in Aug 2012 over a relationship with an employee and came back as co-CEO roughly two months later, after an internal review.

**Base rate: under 5% for a return within about 3 weeks, and about 10% for any return.**

**B. Fast reversals of a CEO exit driven by employee, investor or political pressure.**

The only clear modern example is Sam Altman. OpenAI's board fired him on Nov 17, 2023, and he was reinstated about 5 days later after more than 90% of employees threatened to quit and Microsoft stepped in.

That case differs from this one in important ways:
- There was no finding of misconduct.
- There was no national-security investigation.
- Altman's backers had real leverage over a small nonprofit board.

Huang's camp does have some similar pressure: a "war room," a petition, Musk's backing, an employee rally and Trump saying he is open to a return (§3). But NVIDIA's board is facing a Wachtell probe, DOJ probes, a securities class action and attention from Congress.

**C. "Boomerang" founder-CEOs in general.**

Examples: Jobs, Dell, Schultz (twice), Iger, Dorsey and Page. Founders do come back, but the gap is usually **years**, not weeks. Among large-company CEO exits, boomerang returns are a low single-digit percentage, and almost none happen within a month.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

**Nov 17, 2026**
- NVIDIA reports Q3 FY2027 results after the close. This is Colette Kress's first call as interim CEO. Consensus was about $109.15B revenue and $2.47 EPS, against guidance of about $108B (Fool.com, Nov 15; Saxo, Nov 16; CNBC, Nov 16). I could not find the results or anything Kress said on the call.
- Business Standard (Nov 17) repeats that the board is "not considering" Huang's return.

**Nov 16, 2026**
- CNBC, citing a person familiar with the matter, says the board is focused on a national-security investigation into Huang and is "not in discussions to bring him back" (CNBC, Nov 16). Fox News, The Hill and Quartz carried similar reports.
- CBS reports that board members are not interested in reinstating Huang "as CEO or in any other role" (CBS News, Nov 2026). That covers the advisory path as well.
- A board representative says the board is choosing a search firm for a permanent CEO (Yahoo Finance / BigGo, Nov 16–17).
- Analysts quoted call a return a "long shot."

**Nov 15–16, 2026**
- The Financial Times reports that Huang has discussed a comeback as CEO with confidants. Allies are building a Silicon Valley "war room" to lobby the board and are seeking White House support. The effort reportedly has Huang's backing, but neither he nor NVIDIA has confirmed it publicly (Yahoo Finance / FT, Nov 16; Globe and Mail; Times of Israel; Digital Trends; TechTimes, Nov 16; Mediaite).
- An open letter or petition backed by Musk had more than 158,000 signatures by Sunday, Nov 15, and about 180,000 by Nov 16–17.
- President Trump says he would be open to Huang returning, while also floating Tim Cook as a possible replacement (CBS; Yahoo/BigGo).

**Nov 12, 2026 (8-K dated Nov 11)**
- NVIDIA announces that Huang has resigned as President, CEO and director, effective immediately. No reason is given and the release has no quote from Huang (NVIDIA Announces CEO Transition, Nov 12; Form 8-K, Nov 11).
- CFO Colette Kress becomes interim CEO and keeps her CFO job. Harvey C. Jones, a director since 1993, becomes NVIDIA's first-ever board chair. The board will consider internal and external candidates for CEO and gave no timeline.
- Huang has stayed largely silent in public.
- Tom's Hardware reports, citing an unverified account, that Huang was "broken and shattered" at the meeting where he resigned, and that thousands of employees posted support on an internal forum.

**Nov 12, 2026**
- House Foreign Affairs Chair Brian Mast says Huang has agreed to testify before Congress "before Thanksgiving" or within about 14 days (FourWeekMBA; TNW).
- Three House chairs (Mast, Moolenaar and Crawford) had earlier asked for a voluntary closed-door briefing on **Nov 18** about Huang's contacts with Megaspeed executive Alice Huang (TNW; Implicator).

**Nov 11, 2026**
- The change.org petition "Keep Jensen Huang as NVIDIA CEO" is created.
- Musk replies "That would be an honor" when asked to hire Huang (IBTimes).

**Nov 8–11, 2026**
- On Nov 8 the board announces an independent investigation and says it has no reason to believe national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised.
- The board retains Wachtell Lipton, led by partner and former prosecutor Joon Kim (Briefs.co; American Bazaar, Nov 11; Daily Caller, Nov 16).
- The scandal: reported extramarital affairs, including one with Alice Huang (also known as Huang Le, 63), a former Megaspeed executive whom U.S. intelligence reportedly suspects of spying for China. Beijing denies it. Attorney General Todd Blanche says the DOJ has reached no conclusion.
- There are also DOJ probes into NVIDIA's dealings with Megaspeed and Supermicro (including possible diversion of restricted chips to China) and a proposed securities class action claiming the company hid Huang's ties to Alice Huang.

**Governance facts**
- Huang owns about 870.6M shares, roughly 3.58%, which makes him the largest single holder. NVIDIA uses one share, one vote, so he has no special control (Fortune, Nov 12).
- In June 2026 shareholders re-elected all 10 directors to terms ending at the 2027 annual meeting, and approved a non-binding move to simple-majority voting.
- A 2025 proposal to remove the holding-period requirement for calling a special meeting failed.
- Upshot: there is no realistic shareholder route to force a board seat by Nov 30.

**Scheduled before Nov 30**
- Nov 17: earnings call.
- Nov 18: requested House briefing, timing unconfirmed.
- Testimony "before Thanksgiving" (Nov 26).
- Ongoing: the Wachtell probe and CEO search-firm selection. No timeline was found for either.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES**

1. **Board reverses quickly, Altman-style.** This would require:
   - a sharp stock drop or poor guidance after the earnings call
   - an employee exodus threat
   - major customers or partners pressing the board
   - White House pressure

   It would also need the Wachtell probe to clear him quickly, or the board to bring him back despite the probe. That seems unlikely given the national-security angle, the DOJ probes, and a board that is now independently chaired.
2. **Partial role.** For example, a formal "technical advisor" or "chief scientist" role, or a consulting agreement to keep continuity. Both would qualify. CBS reports the board rejects "any other role," which cuts against this.
3. **A filing reveals a transition-services or consulting agreement** that makes him a formal advisor. This is possible in principle, but reports say the company confirmed he would have no formal role.

**Paths to NO** (the default)

- The investigation runs past Nov 30. Large-firm internal probes usually take weeks to months.
- The board continues its CEO search.
- Huang's congressional testimony brings out bad facts.
- DOJ, litigation or regulatory risk makes rehiring him legally hazardous for the board, which would face fiduciary and securities exposure.
- The Thanksgiving holiday shortens the window further.

**What a YES depends on:** the probe finishing quickly with a clean result; the board being willing to accept the national-security risk; and pressure from the stock price or employees severe enough to override the board.

## 5. Signposts

**Would raise the probability of YES (large moves):**
- Wachtell or NVIDIA announces the probe has concluded and cleared Huang.
- Directors resign, or the board is reported to be "in talks" with Huang.
- A large-scale employee resignation threat, or statements from major customers or investors.
- An explicit push from Trump or the White House.
- A big post-earnings sell-off blamed on the leadership gap.
- Kress or the board describing the search as "open to all options."

**Would raise the probability of YES (smaller or technical):**
- Any 8-K/A or separation-agreement filing that mentions advisory or consulting services.

**Would lower the probability of YES:**
- Damaging testimony to Congress or leaks.
- DOJ action involving Huang personally.
- The board naming a search firm or shortlist.
- Kress's earnings-call remarks confirming the search with no role for Huang.
- Huang taking a role elsewhere (for example, with Musk).
- A formal separation agreement with no advisory terms.
