# Research Brief: Will a US-Iran deal signed by Dec 31, 2026 include an enrichment moratorium of a year or more?

*Prepared Nov 20, 2026. Every page fetch was blocked by the proxy, so all facts below come from search-result summaries and article snippets. Treat specific numbers and quotes as second-hand.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

**What YES requires:**
- **A written instrument.** The US and Iran must both sign it, or formally adopt it without signature through a joint statement, mutual confirmation, an official decision, or an exchange of notes.
- **Deadline:** Dec 31, 2026, 11:59 PM ET. That is about 41 days from today.
- **A moratorium on all Iranian enrichment for at least one year.** A permanent end qualifies. An open-ended moratorium qualifies unless it is framed as temporary or provisional.

**What does not count:**
- Limits on enrichment level or purity, such as a 3.67% cap.
- Reductions in "enrichment capacity," which is the language Vice President Vance used in October.
- Diluting, down-blending or shipping out the stockpile.
- "Maintain the status quo" wording.
- A pledge to *agree on* a moratorium in a later deal.

**Obligation test:** The commitment must be a present obligation. Implementation details can be left for later. A moratorium that depends on a future agreement or "final deal" fails. This matters here because the June 17, 2026 Islamabad MoU did exactly that: the parties "agree to discuss the issue of enrichment… based on a satisfactory framework being agreed upon in the final Deal." Language like that resolves NO.

**Other parties:** Mediators such as Qatar, Pakistan or Oman can be parties or witnesses. The US and Iran must both be parties.

**Edge cases:**
- **Text not released.** If an instrument is signed but its text is withheld and the question is genuinely ambiguous, resolution can wait up to 28 days after the deadline. After that, official announcements and a consensus of credible reporting decide.
- **Conflicting public accounts.** The two sides have already given competing accounts of the same deal (the June MoU and the Doha "what was agreed" dispute). That raises the risk of disputed interpretation.
- **Moratorium followed by low-level enrichment.** A "12–15 year moratorium, then 3.67%" structure, like the one reported in May, *would* qualify, because the moratorium itself is at least a year and covers all enrichment.
- **Later collapse doesn't matter.** Once signed and qualifying, the market resolves YES even if the deal is repudiated afterwards.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. US-Iran written instruments since 1979: 0 of 4 contained a full enrichment moratorium.**
- Algiers Accords (1981): not nuclear.
- JPOA (Nov 2013): capped enrichment at 5% and froze the stockpile. No halt.
- JCPOA (2015): a 3.67% cap. No halt.
- Islamabad MoU (Jun 17, 2026): deferred enrichment to a final deal (CNN, Jun 17, 2026; Wikisource text).

The only signed US-Iran document of the 2026 war period pushed the enrichment question into the future.

**B. Iranian enrichment suspensions in any written instrument: 2 instances, both with the EU3 and not the US.**
- Tehran Declaration (Oct 2003) and Paris Agreement (Nov 2004).
- Both were explicitly voluntary, confidence-building and temporary. Under this market's rules they would probably *not* qualify.
- Iran restarted enrichment in 2005–06.
- No Iranian government has ever signed up to an open-ended or multi-year halt. Under Mojtaba Khamenei, the current leadership calls a ban on domestic enrichment a "definite red line" (Civic Intelligence, ~May 11, 2026; Arab News op-ed, Nov 18, 2026).

**C. How fast deals close from a state of active hostilities.** This is a rough reference class, not a precise statistic. Comprehensive agreements rarely go from "far apart, trading strikes" to a signed text with detailed nuclear terms in under six weeks. JCPOA took about 20 months after JPOA. The 2026 cycle itself took about 10 weeks from the April 8 ceasefire to the June 17 MoU, and that document deliberately left out enrichment. The two-stage pattern is strong: first a ceasefire or Hormuz framework, then a nuclear deal. Under that pattern, any instrument signed before Dec 31 is likelier to be a "stage one" text without a qualifying moratorium.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (all require a sharp break from the current trajectory):**
1. **A comprehensive "grand bargain" under coercion.** Escalating US strikes, the blockade and economic collapse push Tehran to accept a package covering Hormuz, sanctions, assets and a multi-year moratorium (the May-style 12–15 years) in a single signed text before Dec 31.
   - Depends on Mojtaba Khamenei reversing the red line he has stated publicly.
   - Depends on Washington accepting a time-limited moratorium rather than "zero forever." It had already offered 20 years, so a long moratorium is acceptable to the US.
   - Depends on drafting fast enough to finish in about 6 weeks.
2. **A revived MoU-style framework that hardens enrichment into a present obligation.** For example, "Iran shall not enrich for X years, with implementation per annex." Mediators (Qatar, Pakistan) could reuse the May draft language. The risk is that wording drifts into "to be agreed in the final deal," which would resolve NO.
3. **Iran's de facto halt gets codified.** Its facilities are bombed and enrichment may already be largely halted in practice. Tehran could treat a written pause as nearly costless and trade it for sanctions relief, while framing it as "voluntary" and preserving the "right" on paper, as the Nov 18 Arab News op-ed suggests. *Risk:* "voluntary/temporary" framing may fail the "not explicitly temporary" test, unless a duration of a year or more is specified.

**Paths to NO (the default):**
- No signed instrument by Dec 31. Current conditions: active tit-for-tat strikes, Iran rejecting the US response, and leadership red lines on enrichment and stockpile export.
- A stage-one deal covering Hormuz, the blockade and assets, which defers nuclear terms like the June MoU did. This is the most likely shape of any deal that does get done, given Iran's insistence that nuclear talks come only after its conditions are met.
- A deal with capacity or purity caps, or stockpile down-blending, but no full halt. This matches Vance's October "meaningful cut in capacity" language.
- A moratorium framed as conditional on a future agreement, or shorter than a year.

## 5. Signposts

**Would raise the probability of YES (strongly):**
- Official or credible reporting of a draft text with "Iran will not enrich for N years" as an agreed term, not a "framework to discuss."
- A Mojtaba Khamenei statement or fatwa-style signal accepting a "temporary suspension" with a stated duration.
- Vance and Ghalibaf, or Witkoff and Araghchi, physically meeting in Doha or Islamabad with a signing ceremony scheduled.
- Hormuz reopening plus partial lifting of the blockade, which would show the sequencing deadlock is breaking.

**Would raise the probability of YES (moderately):**
- Leaks reviving the May 12–15 year moratorium formula.
- The US publicly dropping "zero forever" in favour of a time-limited halt.

**Would lower the probability of YES:**
- Any stage-one text that again defers enrichment.
- Iranian officials restating that the "right to enrich" is non-negotiable.
- Major new US strike campaigns, or Iranian attacks on Gulf states (the Kuwait and Bahrain pattern).
- An Iranian move toward leaving the NPT.
- Talks slipping into 2027 with no date set.
