# Research brief: Will Connor Wood finish in the Top 3 on Dancing with the Stars Season 35?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **YES** if Connor Wood (with pro partner Rylee Arnold) places 1st, 2nd or 3rd on DWTS Season 35, as ABC announces it during the live finale. **NO** if he places 4th or 5th. Five couples remain, so two of them will miss the top 3.
- **When:** The finale airs live on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, 8–11 p.m. ET (5 p.m. PT) on ABC and Disney+. It is the first finale broadcast live in every U.S. time zone at once (ABC News countdown, Nov 2026; Parade, Nov 2026). The result should be known on the night of Nov 24, more than five weeks before the Dec 31, 2026 close date.
- **Who decides:** The show's official placements. Kalshi will resolve on the announced result.
- **Format:** Each couple performs three routines: the judges' choice, an instant dance, and a freestyle. Only viewer votes cast during the live broadcast count. Voting runs from the start of the dancing until shortly after the last performance, online at dwtsvote.abc.com or by SMS. The night's votes are combined with the judges' scores (ABC News / readersfusion finale previews, Nov 2026). Semifinal votes do not appear to carry over.
- **Edge cases:**
  - DWTS normally announces 5th, then 4th, and then the top 3 in order, so the result should be clear.
  - Ties are broken in favor of the couple with more public votes (a showrunner's explanation, quoted by TV Insider, Nov 18, 2026). That rule helps a vote-heavy couple like Wood.
  - A withdrawal or injury before the finale is the only other plausible complication. If a couple withdraws, four couples remain and Wood would need to beat only one of them.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Random baseline.** Three of five finalists make the top 3, so 60%.

**B. Recent finalists who were weak with the judges but strong with voters.** This list is partly from memory; I could not verify seasons 28–32 in this search.
- S27 (2018), Bobby Bones: lowest judges' scores among the finalists; **won**, 1st of 4.
- S29 (2020), Nelly: low scorer; **3rd of 4** (from memory).
- S30 (2021), Cody Rigsby: low scorer; **4th of 4** (from memory).
- S33 (2024), Stephen Nedoroscik, also partnered with Rylee Arnold: popular, lower-scoring; **4th of 5**, ahead of Danny Amendola in 5th (AOL/TheWrap, Nov 2024).
- S34 (2025), Dylan Efron: a vote-driven finalist who survived a semifinal where high scorer Whitney Leavitt went home; **4th of 5** (AOL/GMA, Nov 2025).

Roughly 2 of 5 (40%) made the top 3. The two recent five-couple finales most like this one (S33 and S34) **both put the fan-favorite low scorer 4th**, not 5th. Note that it is not known whether Nedoroscik or Efron led the vote in their semifinals.

**C. Finalists with evidence that they led the semifinal vote.** In S27, Bobby Bones (widely understood to be the vote leader) won. I could not establish a clean count beyond that. The general pattern on DWTS is that the vote leader rarely lands at the bottom of the finale, because judges' scores in finales are compressed near the top: freestyles commonly get 30/30. That limits how much a scoring gap can hurt.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 19–22, 2026:** Fan backlash continues. Some viewers are threatening to boycott the finale over Wood advancing. Season 27 winner Bobby Bones defended him on TikTok (TV Insider, Nov 2026; Yahoo, Nov 2026). Boycott threats come up most seasons (S29, S33, S34) and have no clear history of changing outcomes.
- **Nov 18, 2026: Wood probably led the semifinal vote.** TV Insider and Yahoo report that Wood probably got the most viewer votes in the semifinal.
  - Their reasoning: in a combined judges-plus-votes ranking, Wood and Harry Shum Jr. tied for last at 7 points. Ties go to the couple with more votes, so Wood must have out-polled Shum.
  - For Shum's 6 judges' points plus his votes to be the lowest total, Wood had to rank 1st in the vote.
  - This is an inference. No official vote counts have been released. The show's vote-and-score formula may be percentage-based rather than rank-based.
- **Nov 17, 2026: semifinal results (judges' totals out of 60).** Amber Glenn/Pasha Pashkov 60; Harry Shum Jr./Jenna Johnson 60 (**eliminated**); Maura Higgins/Mark Ballas 59 (her first perfect score came on the samba); Ezra Frech/Daniella Karagach 57; Tatyana Ali/Jan Ravnik 55; **Connor Wood/Rylee Arnold 48, last by a wide margin** (TheWrap, THR, Today, Forbes, Nov 17–18, 2026).
- **Week 9 (quarterfinal), mid-Nov 2026:** Jenna Dewan and Val Chmerkovskiy were eliminated. Tatyana Ali advanced in a surprise (TVLine; CinemaBlend). Coverage says Wood had the lowest or tied-lowest judges' score in each of the last three weeks and survived each time (Yahoo/Covers roundup, Nov 2026).
- **Oct 27, 2026 (Horror Movie Night):** Wood had his best night, earning the season's first 10s from Derek Hough and Carrie Ann Inaba. Tatyana Ali was publicly placed in the bottom 3 that night. Wood was not (TV Insider, Oct–Nov 2026).
- **Early season:** Wood scored 15/30 in week 2, 20/30 in week 3 and 19/30 in week 4. He did not reach the teens until week 3. The judges have called him "most improved" (THR; Today; Fandom wiki).
- **Background:** Wood (born Connor Maisch) is a stand-up comedian and podcaster with a viral social-media following. One report says his quickstep got about 160K Instagram likes. Arnold is 21, and her 2024 partner Nedoroscik was a similar fan favorite (Fandom; Yahoo opinion piece; Wikipedia).
- **Other finalists' vote strength (inferred):**
  - Ezra Frech (Paralympian) and Amber Glenn (Olympic figure skater) are widely seen as the frontrunners.
  - Tatyana Ali has been in the publicly announced bottom 3 at least once. Her quarterfinal survival was called a surprise.
  - Coverage describes Maura Higgins as having weak support despite strong scores.
- **Scheduled before close:** Only the Nov 24 finale.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES**
- **Wood's vote lead holds.** If he again leads or comes near the top of the live vote, his judges' deficit is probably too small to drop him to 4th or 5th.
  - Rough math: if the finale is out of 90 points, Wood might score around 75–82 against 84–90 for the others. As a share of all judges' points, that is roughly 18–19% versus about 21%.
  - A vote share of 30% or more would cover that gap easily.
  - Under a rank system, a 1st-place vote rank plus a 5th-place judges' rank gives him a middling total, with ties going his way.
- **The freestyle narrows the gap.** Freestyles get generous marks, and Wood's best results have come on theme nights.
- **The live coast-to-coast window** lets West Coast fans vote live, which may help a candidate with a young, online fanbase.
- **Two other finalists have shaky vote bases** (Ali and Higgins). Wood only needs to beat two of the four others.

**Paths to NO**
- **The S33/S34 pattern repeats.** The fan favorite gets swamped as the frontrunners' fans turn out on finale night and finishes 4th.
- **Backlash** after the Shum elimination pushes casual viewers to vote for other couples.
- **One night of live-only voting** may change the electorate. Semifinal momentum may not carry over.
- **Higgins and Ali** have Mark Ballas and Jan Ravnik's fanbases plus top judges' scores. If Wood's vote share is merely good rather than dominant, his 5–10 point scoring deficit could leave him 4th.
- **A disastrous instant dance**, which is improvised with little rehearsal, could widen the scoring gap.

## 5. Signposts

All of these arrive before or during the Nov 24 broadcast.

- **Pointing toward YES:**
  - Any official or credible leak of vote data showing Wood led the semifinal vote.
  - Wood being kept on stage after the 5th and 4th-place announcements. Producers often hold back the fan favorite until late.
  - Strong freestyle scores (27 or more of 30) and a judges' gap of 8 points or less.
- **Pointing toward NO:**
  - A large finale-night scoring gap (Wood around 70/90 against others at 87 or more).
  - Coordinated vote campaigns for Ali or Higgins.
  - Evidence that the semifinal formula was percentage-based in a way that undercuts the "most votes" inference.
- **Other:** Social engagement (TikTok/Instagram) on finale promo clips, compared across couples, is a weak proxy for votes.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **The exact combining formula:** percentage-of-total or rank points. TV Insider's analysis assumed ranking points. Older DWTS rules used percentages. This affects how much a judges' deficit can cost him.
- **Official vote counts:** none have been released. The claim that Wood led the semifinal vote is an inference, not a reported figure.
- **Whether earlier vote-leaning finalists** (Nedoroscik, Efron) actually led their semifinal votes. This decides whether S33/S34 are good analogues.
- **Exact finale placements for S28–S32** and which finalist was the lowest scorer in each. These are partly from memory.
- **The size of Wood's podcast and comedy fanbase** compared with Frech's and Glenn's (Olympic/Paralympic networks) and Higgins's (UK/Love Island fans, who may not be able to vote; voting is U.S.-only).
- **Whether the boycott talk** has any measurable effect.
