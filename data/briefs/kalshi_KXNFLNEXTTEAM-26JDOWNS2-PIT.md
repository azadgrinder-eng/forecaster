# Research brief: Will Josh Downs' next team before Dec 1, 2026 be Pittsburgh?

**Prepared:** Nov 10, 2026, which is NFL trade deadline day (4:00 p.m. ET). Every page fetch was refused by the proxy, so all evidence comes from search-result summaries of the articles. I could not open any of them or check exact timestamps. **None of my searches found a completed Downs trade. I could not tell whether that is because no trade happened or because the deadline outcome hadn't been reported yet.** Forecasters should check the post-deadline transaction wire first. It will settle this question almost entirely.

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **YES** requires Downs' next team to be Pittsburgh before Dec 1, 2026 (close 04:59 UTC, which is 11:59 p.m. ET on Nov 30). Kalshi titles this "next team." In practice a trade that sends him from Indianapolis to Pittsburgh should count. The written rules mostly describe free-agent signings, which leaves a small wording ambiguity. "Remains on team at issuance" would resolve the Colts' market, not this one.
- **Early resolution** needs the transaction to be made official and reported by the NFL. A trade agreed before 4 p.m. ET but processed later still counts once the league confirms it.
- **After the deadline:** once 4 p.m. ET on Nov 10 passes, trades are impossible until the new league year in March 2027. The only remaining routes to Pittsburgh before Dec 1 would be:
  - the Colts release him and Pittsburgh claims him on waivers, or
  - he clears waivers and signs with Pittsburgh as a free agent.

  Both are extremely unlikely for a healthy 25-year-old starter who leads his team in receiving yards.
- **His contract:** he is in the final year of his 2023 rookie deal (fourth season, 2026). He becomes a free agent in March 2027, well after the close date, so free agency can't help YES.
- **Edge case:** if he goes to any other team, such as Kansas City, this market resolves NO.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **Players who publicly request a trade from a selling team in the final year of their deal, with live offers in hand by deadline week.** From memory of recent NFL deadlines (2022–2025: Chase Claypool, Kadarius Toney, Amari Cooper, Davante Adams, DeAndre Hopkins, Jakobi Meyers rumors and similar), I'd estimate roughly 65–80% of these players get moved. The main failure mode is the team holding firm on its price, as the Raiders did with Meyers in 2024. This is a rough estimate, not a counted dataset.
   - Indianapolis is clearly selling. It is 2-7 and has already traded Quenton Nelson to Seattle and DeForest Buckner to San Francisco (ESPN, late Oct 2026).
   - But the Colts had held out for a 3rd-round pick against a 4th-round offer for more than a week (NBC/PFT, Nov 7–10).
2. **When two "finalists" are named, how often does a given one win?** Typically one of the two most-reported suitors lands the player. In my judgment the leading reported team wins about 50–60% of the time, and a mystery team wins perhaps 15–25% of the time. Here, Kansas City is described as "really aggressive" (Charles Robinson, via Yahoo, ~Nov 9–10). ESPN's Fowler and The MMQB's Albert Breer both predicted Downs goes to the Chiefs. Schefter and Rapoport reported the existing 4th-round offer "probably wasn't" from Pittsburgh (Yahoo, ~Nov 9). That leaves Pittsburgh the second suitor, not the favorite.
3. **Steelers deadline behavior under GM Omar Khan.** They make modest, cheap deadline additions rather than blockbusters:
   - 2024: Mike Williams from the Jets for a 5th-round pick.
   - 2026: a veteran Patriots safety, acquired after DeShon Elliott's injury (SI, date unclear).

   A Day-3-style price for Downs fits that pattern. But Pittsburgh has **no 2027 4th-round pick** and holds two 3rd-round picks (Steelers Depot, Nov 2026), so matching a "4th" would mean overpaying with a 3rd or building a pick swap.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES**
- **Main path:** Pittsburgh outbids Kansas City before 4 p.m. ET Nov 10 with a 2027 3rd or a pick swap, and the trade is made official.
  - This depends on the Colts refusing a straight 4th from another team, and on Khan being willing to pay a 3rd for a slot WR who is a rental unless extended.
  - Downs' cold stretch lowered his price, which helps Pittsburgh.
- **Tail path:** no trade happens, the Colts later release him, and Pittsburgh claims or signs him. Probability is under 1%.

**Paths to NO**
- Kansas City (the favorite in reporting) or another team lands him: KC's 4th-round offer gets accepted, or KC raises it.
- The Colts hold out for a 3rd, no one pays, and he stays in Indianapolis through Dec 1, which resolves the Colts' market.
- Pittsburgh instead takes Brian Thomas Jr. or another WR, or Dulac's "no splash" framing holds and they pass.

## 5. Signposts
- **Decisive:** the official NFL transaction wire, or Schefter/Rapoport reports, after 4 p.m. ET Nov 10.
  - A Downs trade to Pittsburgh means near-certain YES.
  - A trade elsewhere, or no trade at all, means near-certain NO. With no trade, only a release and waiver claim could revive YES.
- **If checking before the outcome is known:**
  - Reports that Pittsburgh acquired a different WR (e.g., Thomas Jr.) point to NO.
  - Reports of Pittsburgh offering a 3rd or a pick swap point to YES.
  - Reports of KC restructuring contracts to clear cap room point to NO.
  - A Colts "Downs staying" report points to NO.

## 6. Uncertainties
- **The main one:** whether a deal was finalized on deadline day. My searches never surfaced a post-deadline report, and live trackers (ESPN, CBS, NBC) could not be fetched.
- Who made the 4th-round offer. It was reported as "probably" not Pittsburgh, but not confirmed.
- Exactly which picks Pittsburgh holds: no 2027 4th and two 3rds, per Steelers Depot. Whether it would trade a 2028 4th is unknown.
- Whether Kansas City's interest was ever confirmed. One outlet said it was unconfirmed in the original reporting.
- Whether Kalshi's settlement treats a trade as "next team." This is very likely, given the market's title.
