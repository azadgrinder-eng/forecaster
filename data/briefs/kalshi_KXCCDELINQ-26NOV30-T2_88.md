## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **Likely series:** The question doesn't name a series. The closest match is the Federal Reserve's "Charge-Off and Delinquency Rates on Loans and Leases at Commercial Banks" release, published on FRED as **DRCCLACBS**. That is the delinquency rate on credit card loans at all commercial banks, seasonally adjusted, quarterly. It measures the share of card balances at least 30 days past due. The latest value is **2.85% for Q2 2026**, so a 2.88% threshold fits this series.
- **What YES needs:** The Q3 2026 figure (dated 2026-07-01 on FRED) has to be strictly above 2.88%. Exactly 2.88% resolves NO. That means a rise of at least **+0.04 points** from Q2's 2.85%.
- **Timing:** The Fed published Q1 2026 on about May 19, 2026, and Q2 2026 around August 25, 2026, when FRED was last updated. Q3 normally comes out in the second half of November. It most likely lands about Nov 18–25, 2026, before the market closes on Nov 30. I found no sign that it was out as of today.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Seasonally adjusted vs. not.** The unadjusted series (DRCCLACBN) was 2.69% in Q2 2026 and 3.02% in Q3 2025. Unadjusted Q3 values usually run about 0.15 points above Q2: Q2 2025 was 2.87% and Q3 2025 was 3.02%. That suggests an unadjusted Q3 2026 value around 2.84%, so both versions point to a similar answer.
  - **Revisions.** Values get revised by 0.01–0.03 points. Q4 2025 went from 2.94% to 2.95%, Q1 2026 from 2.92% to 2.91%, and Q3 2024 from 3.23% to 3.20%. The first-published Q3 2026 number could sit within a few hundredths of the line.
  - **Other measures.** The NY Fed's serious-delinquency measure and the bank-issuer averages are different metrics and should not settle the question.

- **None of the last 7 quarters rose.** The average change was about −0.05 per quarter.
- A YES needs the first increase of at least +0.04 since the 2024 peak.

**B. Longer history:** Rises of at least 0.04 points in one quarter in this adjusted series have mostly come in clear deteriorating phases. Examples are 2022–2024, when it went from about 1.6% to about 3.2%, and 2007–2009. Stable or improving periods had few of them. This is my qualitative read of the series; I could not pull the full history to count exactly.

**C. Year-over-year changes at the big card issuers, Q3 2026:**

| Issuer | Q3 2025 | Q3 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital One domestic card, 30+ days | 3.89% | 3.74% | −15 bp (about −4%) |
| JPMorgan Card Services, 30+ days | 2.14% | 1.97% | −17 bp (about −8%) |
| Synchrony, September | 4.4% | 4.3% | about −10 bp |

- Citi and Bank of America also improved year over year. BofA said early- and late-stage card delinquency improved year over year for the 6th straight quarter.
- Applying these changes to Q3 2025's 2.98% gives:
  - about **2.74%–2.86%** if you use the percentage changes
  - about **2.81%–2.88%** if you use the point changes
- For calibration: Q2 2026 was down 0.19 points (about 6%) from a year earlier. Applying that to Q3 gives about 2.79–2.80%.
- These large issuers make up most of the all-banks series by balances, which is why this reference class is useful.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 10, 2026 – NY Fed Q3 2026 Household Debt and Credit report** (NY Fed press release, 2026-11-10):
  - Card balances hit a record $1.29T, up $22B.
  - The yearly rate at which card balances move into 90+ days late fell to 6.87%, from 7.05% in Q3 2025.
  - Moves into early card delinquency also "improved slightly."
  - Overall delinquency across all household debt rose 0.3 points, to 5.0%, driven by mortgages and other loans.
  - TheStreet notes TransUnion data showing the *share of card borrowers* seriously behind is still rising, but on smaller balances.
- **Oct 22, 2026 – Seeking Alpha "Credit Pulse" (via PYMNTS):** It covers seven issuers: AmEx, BofA, Bread, Capital One, Citi, JPM and Synchrony.
  - Average delinquency was 2.62% in September, up from 2.51% in August and 2.50% in July. The 3-month average was 2.55%.

  - For comparison, September 2024 was 2.99%. I could not confirm September 2025.
  - Net charge-offs fell to 2.09%, from 2.15%.

  - The Convexity write-up was titled "card borrowers fine while jobs hold."
- **Aug 25, 2026 – Fed Q2 2026 release:** The adjusted rate was 2.85%, with Q1 revised to 2.91%. The unadjusted rate was 2.69%.
- **Aug 2026 – NY Fed / Liberty Street:** The rise in the 90+ day "stock" measure is mostly old charged-off debt still on credit reports. New delinquencies have been "largely stable since 2024."
- **Philadelphia Fed large-bank data (Q2 2026):** Card delinquency and charge-off rates fell again and have been falling for more than a year. Q3 2026 large-bank data was not found; it usually comes out later.
- **Scheduled before close:** The Fed Q3 2026 charge-off and delinquency release (expected about the third or fourth week of November) and the FDIC Quarterly Banking Profile for Q3 (usually late November).

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to NO (most of the evidence):**
- The 7-quarter downward trend continues. Adjusted Q3 comes in around 2.78–2.86%.
- Every major issuer reported lower 30+ day delinquency than a year ago at Sept 30, 2026, by 10–17 bp.
- The NY Fed's measures of new delinquencies improved slightly in Q3.
- Tighter lending standards on loans made in 2023–24 are still working through the books.

**Paths to YES:**
1. **Seasonal adjustment misfit.** Q3 rose a lot before adjustment (Capital One +35 bp; issuer average 2.50% to 2.62% over July–September). If the Fed's seasonal factors take out less than that, the adjusted figure could tick up to about 2.89% or more.
2. **Smaller banks.** Banks outside the top 100 have much higher card delinquency rates. They are a small share of balances but can move the total by a few hundredths.
3. **Revision quirks.** Q2 could be revised and the seasonal factors re-estimated.
4. **Signs of slowing improvement.** The issuer average rose month to month through Q3. Seeking Alpha described "borrowers were falling behind more often." TransUnion reports more borrowers seriously behind. If a soft labor market worsened in Q3, the improvement could flatten out.
5. **Non-bank-heavy categories.** Not directly relevant, since this is a bank-only series.

**Rough read:** For YES, the adjusted series has to break a 7-quarter downtrend by at least 4 bp. That would happen in the same quarter in which the top issuers, which hold most of the balances, reported year-over-year improvement.

## 5. Signposts

- **The Fed Q3 2026 release (late November) settles the question.** Check FRED DRCCLACBS for the 2026-07-01 value.
- **FDIC Q3 2026 Quarterly Banking Profile.** If it comes out first, its card noncurrent and past-due rates are a strong cross-check. A rise in the adjusted 30–89 day rate would point toward YES.
- **Philadelphia Fed Q3 2026 large-bank card data.** It is likely to come later and so is less useful before close.
- **Small-bank delinquency (DRCCLOBS).** A sharp jump there would raise YES risk.
- **Any Q2 revision in the new release.** An upward revision of Q2 toward 2.88% would raise the odds that Q3 prints above it.
