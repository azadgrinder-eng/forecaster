# Research brief: Will Jensen Huang rank #1 on Google's 2026 Year in Search Global "People" list?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES.** Jensen Huang must be #1 on Google's official 2026 Year in Search list with location set to Global, then Trending, then People, on trends.withgoogle.com/year-in-search/. Whatever Google publishes is final.
- **What the list measures.** It ranks growth, not total searches. Google ranks "the search terms that saw the highest increase in traffic" over a fixed window compared with the same window a year earlier. The 2025 window was Jan 1 to Nov 25, 2025. The 2024 window was Jan 1 to Nov 23, 2024 (Google Year in Search data methodology; itechguides summary, Dec 2025). If 2026 follows the same pattern, the window ends around Nov 22–26, 2026. That leaves only about 1.5 more weeks of data from today (Nov 16).
- **Timing.** Google usually publishes in early to mid December: Dec 4, 2025, and about Dec 10, 2024 (TechJuice, Dec 2025; AP via ABC, Dec 10, 2024). If the list is not out by Mar 31, 2027, 11:59 PM ET, the market resolves "Other". That is very unlikely, because Google has published every year since the Zeitgeist era.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Deceased people.** In 2025 the global People top 10 did not include Charlie Kirk, even though he was the #3 global query overall and the top news story. Recent years appear to put deceased people in separate "passings"-type lists, though I could not fully confirm this for the global hub. If that holds in 2026, Ali Khamenei (killed Feb 28, 2026) would likely be off the People list.
  - **How Google labels the query.** It may be unclear whether Google counts "Jensen Huang" as a person or folds the interest into an "Nvidia" query. Google categorizes queries editorially.
  - **Format changes.** Google could drop or rename the "People" category in 2026.
  - **Google is blocked in mainland China**, so Chinese-language interest in the scandal is mostly not counted.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Who has been #1 on the Global People list.** Only 2025 is confirmed here. The rest is from memory.
- 2025: d4vd. He was little known before a September 2025 murder investigation. The global top 10 was d4vd, Kendrick Lamar, Jimmy Kimmel, Tyler Robinson, Pope Leo XIV, Vaibhav Sooryavanshi, Shedeur Sanders, Bianca Censori, Zohran Mamdani, Greta Thunberg (Search Engine Journal, Dec 2025).
- 2024: Donald Trump. Fortune and AP (Dec 2024) report Trump, Catherine Princess of Wales and Imane Khelif as the top people. That Trump was #1 globally is my recollection, not something I confirmed.
- 2023: Damar Hamlin. 2022: Johnny Depp. 2021: Alec Baldwin. 2020: Joe Biden. 2016: Donald Trump. All from memory, unverified.
- What the winners look like: almost always a scandal, crime, tragedy, election or court case. In the last ~10 years, **no business executive has been #1**. Elon Musk did not win in 2022, the year of the Twitter takeover, and as far as I recall Sam Bankman-Fried did not win in 2022 even after FTX collapsed on Nov 8–11. Base rate for a CEO: about 0 of 10.

**B. Late-breaking events.** Winners whose spike came in the last ~6 weeks of the window:
- Alec Baldwin, 2021: the Rust shooting on Oct 21, about 5 weeks before the cutoff. My recollection is he won globally.
- Biden 2020 and Trump 2016/2024: November elections, but on top of months of sustained interest.
- Counterexample: SBF, whose Nov 2022 collapse fell about 2 weeks before the cutoff and did not win (from memory).

So a spike in the final 2–3 weeks can win, but the clear one-off example (Baldwin) had about 5 weeks.

**C. People who were already famous.** The list rewards the year-over-year increase, so a high baseline hurts. Trump (2024) shows a very famous person can still win when the absolute increase is huge. Huang already had substantial 2025 interest: he made Google's 2025 business-people lists alongside Musk and Altman (WION, Dec 2025). He was also well known before 2026, while d4vd, Hamlin and Khelif went from near zero.

  - Indian figures (cricket, film) often reach the global list, e.g. Vaibhav Sooryavanshi at #6 in 2025.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES:**
1. **Scale of the spike.** A world-famous CEO of one of the most valuable companies, plus sex, espionage and a sudden fall, is the kind of story that produces enormous spikes. It has global reach in the US, Taiwan and Asia, India, and Europe.
2. **Momentum through the cutoff.** Further revelations (the NYT reporting, FBI or Congressional action, other affair partners, the lawsuit, the Nov 17 earnings) keep interest high until the window closes around Nov 25.
3. **A weak field.** If no 2026 person had a d4vd-style rise from near zero, or if the big-event people are excluded or split (Khamenei as deceased, Maduro already known, World Cup interest spread across several players), even 2–3 weeks of Huang interest could be enough.

**Paths to NO:**
1. **Short window and high baseline.** Huang gets only about 2.5 weeks of scandal inside the window, against a 2025 baseline that was already high. His growth ratio may be smaller than for people who went from obscure to famous.
2. **Strong rivals from earlier in the year:**
   - Maduro, who had months of sustained interest.
   - Mojtaba Khamenei or other Iran-war figures.
   - A World Cup breakout star.
   - A US or Indian crime or celebrity story.
   - Any person with a d4vd-type rise from zero.
3. **Split interest.** Searches may go to "Alice Huang" or "Nvidia" rather than "Jensen Huang".
4. **Category choices.** Google could place him under "news" or use another editorial split.
5. **Historical record.** CEOs have essentially never topped the global people list, and SBF's very similarly timed November 2022 collapse did not win (from memory).

## 5. Signposts

- **Google's release (early to mid Dec 2026):** this settles the question. It may come before the Dec 31 close.
- **Google Trends checks** (if a forecaster can run them): compare "Jensen Huang" worldwide, Nov 8–16, 2026, with Maduro (Jan), Mojtaba Khamenei (Mar), top World Cup players (Jun–Jul) and other 2026 spikes. If Huang's peak and the area under his 2026 curve dwarf the others, move up. If his spike is smaller than Maduro's January peak, move down.
- **Google's daily "Trending Now" lists:** if Huang topped global trending for several days, move up.
- **New revelations before ~Nov 25** (indictment, testimony, more partners, Alice Huang identified) mean more sustained interest and a move up. If the story fades after the Nov 17 earnings, move down.
- **The 2025-style cutoff:** if Google's 2026 methodology shows an earlier cutoff, move down.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not read the Google Trends hub or Wikipedia directly; my fetch tool was blocked. The 2025 global list and methodology come from secondary write-ups.
- The global #1 names for 2016–2024 are mostly from memory, as is the claim that SBF did not top the 2022 global list.
- I could not establish exactly how Google weighs ratio growth against absolute increase, or how it handles deceased people on the global People list.
- I have no quantitative Google Trends comparison of Huang's November spike against 2026 rivals such as Maduro, Mojtaba Khamenei or World Cup players.
- I have no complete list of other 2026 viral people, especially in India, Latin America, celebrity crime or entertainment. That is where many past winners came from.
- The exact 2026 cutoff date is unknown.
