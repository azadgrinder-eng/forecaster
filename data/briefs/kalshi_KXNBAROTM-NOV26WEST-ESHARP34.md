# Research brief: Will Emanuel Sharp (Kings) win the NBA's Western Conference Rookie of the Month for October/November 2026?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** Emanuel Sharp (Sacramento Kings, No. 45 pick in the 2026 draft) must be named the NBA's Western Conference Rookie of the Month for October/November of the 2026-27 season. The league treats October and November as one award period. Winners are chosen by the NBA.
- **When it's announced:** Probably in the first days of December 2026. Last season's October/November awards (Cooper Flagg in the West, Kon Knueppel in the East) were announced on Tuesday, December 2, 2025 (Hoops Rumors, Dec 2025; Bangor Daily News, Dec 3, 2025). The market closes on December 8, 2026 at 04:59 UTC, which leaves room for a similar date.
- **Shared award:** If the award is shared, YES pays $1/N. Co-winners within one conference are rare, so this hardly changes the value. Even a share would require Sharp to be named, which is very unlikely.
- **Edge cases:** The announcement could slip past the close date (unlikely given past timing). There's also no doubt about which conference Sharp is in, since Sacramento is a Western team.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
- **Who wins October/November in the West.** Recent winners were high-usage starters and lottery picks: Flagg in 2025 (16.7 points, 6.6 rebounds, 3.5 assists over 20 games; Hoops Rumors, Dec 2025), Victor Wembanyama in 2023, and Keegan Murray in 2022. The one recent exception is Jaylen Wells in 2024, a No. 39 pick. He was a starter on a winning Memphis team scoring around 10 points a game, and he was the only second-round pick among the 10 nominees (AOL/Commercial Appeal, Dec 2024). Over roughly 20+ seasons since 2001-02, close to 0% of winners averaged about 5 points a game off the bench.
- **Second-round picks in general.** They win occasionally, for example Tyler Ulis in April 2017 at 20.7 points and 6.8 assists (theScore, 2017) and Wells in October/November 2024. My rough count, which I couldn't fully check, is under 5% of West monthly awards. Every case I found involved a player with a starter's role and production. Sharp has neither.
- **Teammates.** When a teammate is the clear favorite for the award, a lower-production player from the same team effectively never wins.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)
- **Sharp, around Nov 18–20, 2026:** About 13–14 games played, roughly 4.9–5.0 points, 1.3 rebounds and 0.7 assists in about 17–18 minutes a game. He's shooting 36.8% from three. Season high is 14 points against Phoenix on Nov 18. He comes off the bench (Basketball-Reference; NBA Rookie Report; Yahoo Sports, Nov 2026). Yahoo notes he leads the Kings in net rating (-0.7) and averages 0.8 steals a game.
- **Kia Rookie Ladder, Nov 18, 2026:** No. 1 is Yaxel Lendeborg (Warriors), who has had eight 20-point games and is playing a big role while Stephen Curry is out. Cameron Boozer (Grizzlies) climbed to No. 2, helped by a 29-point game with 7 threes. Memphis was 10-4 as of Nov 19 (Basketball Network). Darius Acuff Jr. (Kings) is No. 3. Aday Mara is also mentioned. All three leaders are Western Conference players. Sharp isn't in any ladder excerpt I found.
- **Acuff Jr., Sharp's teammate:** About 26.2 points, 7.3 assists and 3.8 rebounds over 11 games (StatMuse, mid-November). His 259 points in his first 10 games were the most by a rookie since 1984. He's the first rookie since Luka Dončić with three straight games of 25 points, 5 rebounds and 5 assists (Newsweek/ClutchPoints, Nov 2026).
- **Lendeborg:** Led all rookies with 21.8 points a game as of Oct 28 (NBA.com). An early sample had him at 22.3 points and 7.3 rebounds.
- **Boozer:** About 17.7 points, 10.5 rebounds and 5.7 assists early in the season, plus a 38-point game (Tatler, NBA.com, Nov 4).
- **Kings roster:** The Kings signed Ben Simmons, which adds competition for Sharp's minutes (NBC Sports/Rotowire). Sharp has passed Clifford in the rotation at times (Roundtable, Oct–Nov 2026).
- **Schedule:** The Kings play about 5–6 more games before November 30. The award is most likely announced around December 1–3.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
- **Paths to YES (near zero):**
  - Lendeborg, Boozer and Acuff all miss most of the period through injury, *and* Sharp suddenly starts and scores 20+ a game for about 6 games. Even then, his season averages would stay far below several other West rookies.
  - A mistake or tie in how the award is reported.
- **Path to NO (almost certain):** The NBA picks one of the established leaders, most likely Lendeborg, Acuff or Boozer. Sharp's production is far below theirs, and Acuff, his own teammate, is outscoring him by about 5x.

## 5. Signposts
- An official NBA release around December 1–3 naming the West winner. This settles the question.
- Sharp moving into the starting lineup with a multi-game 20+ point run in late November. Even this would only nudge the probability up a little.
- Long injury absences for Lendeborg, Boozer or Acuff. Since there are three of them, even this barely matters.

## 6. Uncertainties
- I couldn't get the full November 18 ladder or the Basketball-Reference rookie stats page because the fetches were blocked. Exact current averages for the other West rookies (Mara, Maxime Raynaud and others) are unverified.
- I didn't confirm Sharp's result against the Lakers on November 20.
- I didn't confirm the exact 2026 announcement date. The 2025 precedent (Dec 2) suggests it will come well before the December 8 close.
- My count of second-round winners is rough, and I couldn't complete the full award history.
