# Research brief: NZ vs India, 2nd Test, Hagley Oval, Christchurch. Will it end in a draw?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **The match.** This is the 2nd and final Test of India's 2026‑27 tour of New Zealand. It is a five-day Test at Hagley Oval, Christchurch, scheduled for Nov 27 – Dec 1, 2026 local time. Play starts at 11:00 NZDT (22:00 UTC Nov 26, 5:00 PM ET Nov 26) and the toss is at 10:30 local (ICC WTC match page; esquireindia/cricketaddictor previews, Nov 2026). Day 5 should finish around 05:00–06:00 UTC on Dec 1, well before the market closes on Dec 3, 22:00 UTC.
- **YES (draw)** happens if:
  - the official result is a draw (five days completed with no winner), or
  - the match is a tie. Tests have no tiebreak, so a tie counts as a draw. Ties are extremely rare: 2 in more than 2,500 Tests.
  - The match is abandoned or cancelled with no official result, or does not start by Nov 28, 11:59 PM ET. An abandoned Test is officially recorded as a draw in any case.
- **NO** means either team wins, including by forfeit or any other declared result.
- **Who decides:** the official ICC/NZC result. If those statistics are not published within 2 hours, a consensus of credible reporting is used.
- **Edge cases:**
  - Rain shortening the match still produces a draw if there is no winner, so weather pushes only toward YES.
  - There is no DLS in Tests.
  - In New Zealand, playing conditions allow time lost to weather to be partly made up: extra time each day, and play can run up to 30 minutes past the scheduled close. This partly offsets short showers.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **All Tests, recent era.**
   - Draw rate was 5.66% in 2024 (the record low) and 7.14% in 2025 (second-lowest) (ESPNcricinfo year reviews).
   - In 2022 to 2024‑25, 11 of 120 Tests were draws, about 9% (The Quint).
   - It was about 15% in 2021–22 (7/44 in 2021; 6/41 in 2022 at the time of reporting).
   - Analysts attribute the fall to bowler-friendly pitches and WTC points incentives.
   - **Prior: about 6–10%.**
2. **Tests at Hagley Oval.**
   - ESPNcricinfo's summary for 2014–2024 shows 14 Tests for NZ there: 9 won, 4 lost, 1 drawn (the 2018 draw vs England).
   - Add the Dec 2025 Test vs West Indies, a draw. WI chased 531 and finished 457/6, with Justin Greaves 202* (Sky Sports / cricket.com.au, Dec 6, 2025).
   - That gives about 2 draws in 15–16 Tests (**about 13%**), and 1 draw in the last 4 (2024 Aus loss, 2024 Eng loss, 2025 WI draw).
   - Pace bowlers have taken about 90% of wickets at the venue, and the team bowling first has a 10‑4 win record (preview summaries, Nov 25–26, 2026).
3. **NZ home Tests and NZ‑India Tests in NZ.**
   - NZ home Tests since 2023: about 17 played, roughly 2–3 draws (my count from memory: Hagley vs WI 2025, Wellington vs India 2026). That is **about 12–15%**.
   - India Tests in NZ since 2009: 2009 (1 India win, 2 draws), 2014 (1 NZ win, 1 draw), 2020 (2 NZ wins), 2026 1st Test (draw). That is about **4 draws in 8** (from memory, not verified this session). Several of those draws came on flat Wellington and Napier pitches or in rain.
   - The 1st Test of this series was drawn on a "dead" Basin Reserve pitch. Stuff notes it was only the 2nd draw in 15 Basin Tests since 2015 (Stuff, Nov 2026).

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to NO (a result), most likely in my view:**
- Hagley's usual seam and bounce produce totals of 200–350 and a result inside 4 days, as in most Hagley Tests. That would beat the forecast rain on days 4–5.
- This is a series decider. A win is worth 12 WTC points against 4 for a draw, and India in particular need wins for the WTC. That points to aggressive declarations.
- NZ summer playing conditions allow time lost to showers to be made up.

**Paths to YES (draw):**
- A hot, dry first three days flatten a green-looking pitch, as in Wellington. High first-innings scores (500+) leave too little time.
- The front arrives on Nov 30 – Dec 1 and wipes out large parts of days 4–5. This is most dangerous if the match is still at the third-innings stage by the end of Day 3.
- A fourth-innings rearguard on a flat deck, like WI's 457/6 at Hagley in 2025 or NZ's survival in Wellington last week.
- Both attacks are weakened: NZ without Tickner and possibly Santner; India's attack is unsettled.

**What each path depends on:**
- How the pitch behaves on days 1–2, roughly the run rate and wickets per day.
- When the front arrives and how much rain it brings.
- The toss decision. Bowling first is the Hagley norm, and choosing to bat would suggest the teams think the pitch is flat.

## 5. Signposts
- **Toss and day 1 (Nov 27):** 12+ wickets on Day 1 points strongly to NO. A day like 300/2 points sharply toward YES.
- **End of Day 2:** if the first innings are still incomplete with both sides around 450+, the draw becomes much more likely, especially with rain forecast later. If the second innings is already under way, NO is likely.
- **Updated MetService forecast for Nov 30 – Dec 1:**
  - A firm, heavy-rain forecast for Monday–Tuesday raises the draw chance a lot if the game is not essentially decided by Day 3.
  - A front delayed to Dec 2 or later lowers it.
- **Day 3 heat (29°C on Sunday):** fast deterioration or cracking would favour a result. A pitch that is simply flattening would favour a draw.
- **Declaration behaviour:** early, aggressive declarations by India or NZ point to NO.
