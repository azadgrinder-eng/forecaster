# Research Brief: Will the US federal government take a stake in Lockheed Martin by December 31, 2026?

*Prepared November 27, 2026. About 34 days remain before the window closes.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** By 11:59 PM ET on December 31, 2026, the US federal government must acquire one of the following:
  - direct equity in Lockheed Martin Corporation (NYSE: LMT), voting shares, convertible rights treated as equity, or an equivalent ownership interest, or
  - the same in "a legal vehicle that primarily owns the listed company."
- **Who can hold it:** Entities fully owned or controlled by the federal government count, such as a sovereign wealth fund, a state-owned enterprise, or most likely the DoD Office of Strategic Capital (OSC). Purchases by states, pensions, index or mutual funds, consortia or private parties do not count.
- **What is enough:** An official federal announcement of either a completed acquisition or a binding agreement is sufficient. Speculation, stated plans, or "considering it" comments do not count. The August 2025 remarks by Commerce Secretary Lutnick would not have counted on their own.
- **Who decides:** Resolution relies on official US government information plus a consensus of credible reporting.
- **Edge cases and ambiguities:**
  - **Stake in a Lockheed subsidiary or business unit.** An example would be a deal like the $1B convertible preferred investment DoD made in L3Harris Missile Solutions. A subsidiary does not "primarily own the listed company," so a strict reading says NO. A loose reader might argue YES. This is the main interpretive risk.
  - **Contract terms that are not equity.** Lockheed has profit-sharing, "plowback," recovery clauses and self-funded investment commitments with the government. None of these are equity. Neither is a golden share in an unrelated firm.
  - **Warrants.** Warrants issued to the government by Lockheed itself would probably count as "convertible rights treated as equity."
  - **Letters of intent.** Many Commerce deals in 2026 were letters of intent that have not closed (CNBC/Cato, Nov 2026). A non-binding letter of intent should not count under the "binding agreement" standard, but expect arguments about it.
  - **Passive holdings.** Index or pension holdings, such as the federal Thrift Savings Plan (TSP) holding LMT through index funds, are excluded explicitly or implicitly.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Large defense primes since the August 2025 "considering stakes" remark.** Lutnick named Lockheed in August 2025. Reporting at the time also mentioned Boeing and Palantir (Computing.co.uk, Aug 2025).
- That gives about 15 months × 5–6 large primes (LMT, RTX, Boeing, GD, Northrop, plus L3Harris) with **zero parent-level stakes**.
- The only prime-related deal was the $1B DoD convertible preferred investment in **L3Harris's Missile Solutions subsidiary**. It was announced January 13, 2026 and closed April 23, 2026. It converts to common equity on a planned IPO of the unit, and L3Harris keeps about 80% (DoD release, Jan 2026; SpaceNews).
- Rough hazard: about 0 parent-level events in roughly 80 company-months. Even using a generous prior of 1 event in 80 company-months, one company over one month gives roughly ≤1–2%.

**B. The Trump administration's equity portfolio overall.**
- The government held positions in **41 companies by November 2026, up from 14 in January 2026** (Cato tracker via CNBC, Nov 11, 2026). Other counts: about 40–43 companies (24/7 Wall St., Oct 28, 2026), and about $26.7B across 30 deals (Fortune, Jul 26, 2026).
- Pace: about 2–3 new names per month in 2026.
- Almost all targets are small or mid-cap firms with capital needs in strategic sectors: chips R&D, quantum, critical minerals, rare earths, nuclear, shipbuilding. Examples include the Navy's 40% stake in an Anduril shipyard subsidiary (Oct 2026), the $389M CHIPS letters of intent (Oct 22, 2026), and stakes in MP Materials and Vulcan Elements.
- **Intel is the only mega-cap parent-level stake.** It was a distressed company converting existing CHIPS grants into equity.
- DoD itself has done about 7–10 deals (Semafor, Sep 21, 2026; Bloomberg 2026), none in a prime parent.
- Share of portfolio companies that are profitable large-cap defense primes: 0 of about 41.

**C. Large healthy companies with no capital need, where the administration floated a stake publicly.** Besides Lockheed, the administration has floated ideas such as defense firms in general and Sanders-style AI proposals (from the other side). Outside distressed or grant-conversion cases like Intel and the U.S. Steel golden share, conversion of public musings into binding deals within about 15 months appears low. My estimate is well under 20% over the full period, and much lower for any single remaining month.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 23–25, 2026:** Routine Lockheed news: JR3 test, Mizuho initiates at Outperform with a $765 target. LMT closed at about $571.67 on Nov 23, down 11.2% from its October 29 high (Defense World, Nov 25; Trefis, Nov 24). There is no reporting of stake talks. CEO Taiclet and CFO Evan Scott are scheduled to speak at the **UBS Global Industrials & Transportation Conference, early December**. That is the next scheduled forum for any comment.
- **Nov 12, 2026:** After the midterms, Democrats "emerge emboldened" and are debating adopting equity stakes themselves (Axios, Nov 12, 2026). The new Congress sits in January 2027, after the close date.
- **Nov 10–11, 2026:** Commentary covers how the Senate defense bill and a Democratic Congress could constrain or codify stakes (Cato, Nov 10; CNBC, Nov 11).
- **NDAA FY2027:** The Senate Armed Services Committee bill (S. 4784, Sec. 1051) would give OSC explicit equity authority limited to **batteries and critical minerals, materials and chemicals**. It caps OSC at 40% of an entity's equity round and $500M aggregate per entity (CRS IF13215; Cato, Jun 16, 2026; Defense One, Jun 2026). Conference status as of late November was not found. Even if enacted, these limits do not fit a roughly $130B prime.
- **Oct 22–26, 2026:** Lockheed and DoD (the "Department of War") agreed a **non-binding 10-year F-35 framework** for 850 jets (Lots 21–30, 156 per year). Lockheed will self-fund at least $7B of investment. The framework is subject to congressional authorization and appropriations, and FY27 appropriations are not yet passed. **No equity component was reported** (Lockheed Martin press release, Oct 22, 2026; Military Times, Oct 22; Air & Space Forces). Morgan Stanley upgraded the stock (CNBC, Oct 26, 2026). Separately, Lockheed won a PAC-3 MSE contract of up to $58.6B over 7 years.
- **Oct 22, 2026:** Lawmakers are scrutinizing Pentagon equity deals while DoD keeps making them, including a 35% OSC stake in a Venezuelan oil venture (American Prospect, Oct 22, 2026).
- **Sep 21, 2026:** The White House helped DoD write a legal justification for its stock deals. The legal footing is contested: OSC statute authorizes credit, not clearly equity (Semafor, Sep 21, 2026; Cato).
- **Apr 23, 2026:** On the Q1 call, Taiclet called the administration a "golden opportunity." He described recovery clauses in contracts and a Patriot/THAAD profit-sharing "plowback" arrangement with the government. This is a contractual alternative to equity (Reuters via StreetInsider; Business Chief, Apr 2026).
- **Jan 7, 2026:** An executive order restricts buybacks, dividends and executive pay at "underperforming" major defense contractors. Lockheed, RTX, Northrop and GD were the named examples. The administration is pursuing its leverage over primes through contract and payout rules rather than ownership (Morgan Lewis, Jan 2026; Cooley, Mar 5, 2026; FlightGlobal).
- **Sep 3, 2025:** Defense contractors sidestepped the ownership talk (Axios).
- **Aug 26, 2025:** Lutnick said on CNBC there was a "monstrous discussion" of stakes in defense firms and called Lockheed "basically an arm of the U.S. government." He deferred to Hegseth and Feinberg (CNBC; Forbes; Fox Business).

**Scheduled before close:** the UBS conference in early December, NDAA conference and final passage likely in December, and the FY27 appropriations/continuing resolution fight. Lockheed's Q4 earnings in late January 2027 fall after the close.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (all low-likelihood in 34 days):**
1. **Equity added to a mega-deal.** Warrants or preferred stock tied to the F-35 framework, PAC-3/THAAD capacity expansions, or "Golden Dome" work. Precedents are Intel's grant-to-equity conversion and the MP Materials preferred-plus-warrants structure. This depends on Feinberg's private-equity-style approach and Lockheed agreeing to it. Nothing reported suggests it is in train.
2. **A sudden presidential or Commerce push.** Trump or Lutnick announces a "deal" with Lockheed. This only resolves YES if it is described as completed or binding.
3. **Subsidiary deal ruled to count.** DoD invests in a Lockheed unit (for example a missile or solid rocket motor business, similar to L3Harris) and the resolver reads it loosely. This needs both the deal and a loose interpretation.

**Paths to NO (default):**
- Lockheed is well capitalized (about $130B market cap, my estimate) and has no need for capital.
- The administration's leverage over primes has run through contract terms instead: buyback and dividend limits, self-funded investment, profit-sharing.
- Equity authority for DoD is legally contested and, in the Senate NDAA, limited to minerals and batteries with a $500M cap.
- Congressional scrutiny is bipartisan.
- The time left is very short.
- No reported talks for more than 14 months.

## 5. Signposts

- **Strong move up:**
  - Any report from Bloomberg, WSJ, Reuters or Semafor that DoD or Commerce is negotiating warrants or preferred stock with Lockheed.
  - An 8-K from Lockheed disclosing securities issued to the US government.
  - Taiclet at the UBS conference refusing to rule out, or confirming, government equity.
  - Trump or Lutnick naming Lockheed as a deal "done."
- **Moderate move up:** DoD announces an investment in a Lockheed subsidiary or business unit (watch the resolution ambiguity). A final NDAA grants OSC broad equity authority without sector or size limits.
- **Move down (from an already low base):**
  - The UBS conference passes with no mention.
  - The final NDAA restricts equity to narrow sectors or caps.
  - The F-35 or munitions contracts are finalized with no equity terms.
  - December ends with no reporting of talks.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **Whether any confidential talks exist.** None were found in any 2026 reporting. Searches returned no prime-level stake news after September 2025.
- **NDAA conference outcome:** the final text and timing of the OSC equity provisions as of late November 2026 could not be confirmed.
- **Subsidiary stakes:** how the resolver would treat a stake in a Lockheed subsidiary or joint venture, such as a stake in ULA (Lockheed owns 50%) or another unit. The plain text suggests it would not count.
- **Tracker figures:** exact current portfolio counts differ (40–43). Several deals are letters of intent, and I could not read the full Cato or CNBC tracker pages.
- **Lockheed's stance:** I found no direct 2026 statement from Taiclet on government equity.
- **Market cap:** about $130B is my estimate from roughly 230M shares outstanding × about $572 per share. I did not verify the share count.
