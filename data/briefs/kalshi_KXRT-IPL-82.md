# Research brief: will I Play Rocky's Tomatometer be above 82 on Nov 23, 2026?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** the Tomatometer (critics' score, not the audience Popcornmeter) for *I Play Rocky* must be **above 82**, meaning 83% or higher, at **10:00 AM ET on Monday, Nov 23, 2026**. That is the Monday after the Nov 20 wide release. A score of exactly 82 resolves NO.
- **Who decides:** Kalshi, reading the Rotten Tomatoes page (rottentomatoes.com/m/i_play_rocky). The "Fresh" and "Certified Fresh" labels play no part.
- **Edge cases:**
  - RT shows a whole-number percentage. I could not confirm whether it rounds to the nearest whole number or truncates. If it truncates, an underlying 82.9% would show as 82 and resolve NO.
  - RT sometimes reclassifies, adds or removes reviews. Reviews filed over the release weekend can be added with a lag of a few hours to a day.
  - The release dates are now settled. The film opened in 7 theaters on Nov 6, expanded on Nov 13 and goes nationwide on Nov 20. The Monday after wide release is Nov 23, which matches the close date (TheWrap; Koimoi; Forbes, Nov 11, 2026). Some outlets wrongly list Nov 13 as the limited opening.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **How the score moves once a film has ~140 reviews.** Each new review shifts the score by less than 1 point at this point. I found no published study of score drift after 100 reviews (searched; nothing turned up). From experience, films with more than 130 reviews before wide release usually end their wide-release weekend within ±3 points of that pre-release score, and drops of 4 or more points are uncommon. Treat this as a rough prior, not a measured rate.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
- **YES (score stays at 83% or higher), the most likely path:**
  - Most of the critics who will review this film already have. At ~140 reviews, a few dozen more can only move the score modestly.
  - The film plays as a crowd-pleaser and has very strong audience scores. Regional and general-interest critics who review at wide release tend to be kinder to crowd-pleasers than festival and trade critics.
  - If new reviews come in at 75% fresh or better, the score stays at 83% or higher in nearly every case where 70 or fewer reviews are added.
- **NO (score drops to 82% or lower):**
  - The latest batch was only ~73–81% fresh. If the wide-release wave runs at ~65–70% fresh and adds 50 or more reviews, the score lands at about 82%.
  - A late wave of contrarian takes ("hollow," "Green Book-style") from alt-weeklies and online critics would push in this direction.
  - If the true current count is 119/139 rather than 120/139, or if RT truncates instead of rounding, the cushion is smaller.
  - A batch of rotten reviews being added late, or RT reclassifying reviews, could also matter.
- **Key dependencies:**
  - how many reviews are added before Monday at 10 AM (likely 30–80)
  - the fresh rate of that wave
  - the exact fresh count now

## 5. Signposts
- **The score on Nov 18–20, as wide-release reviews post (the biggest signal):**
  - 87% or higher with 150+ reviews points strongly to YES.
  - 85% suggests a modest lean toward YES.
  - 84% or lower with 160+ reviews means the market is close to a coin flip.
- **The review count growth rate:** more than 60 new reviews raises the risk of a drift down.
- **Tone of reviews from big regional papers and outlets** such as the LA Times, NYT, WaPo, Chicago Tribune, AP, Vulture and The New Yorker. More rotten reviews among Top Critics are a warning sign.
- **Opening-weekend box office and CinemaScore:** these have little direct effect on critics, but a strong reception can bring in late positive reviews.

## 6. Uncertainties
- The date of the 86%/139 snapshot is unknown. The live score as of Nov 16 is unconfirmed because I could not access RT directly.
- The exact fresh count (119 vs 120 of 139) and whether RT rounds or truncates are unknown.
- I don't know how many reviews will be added by Nov 23. Wide releases often end up with 200+ reviews eventually, but many of those arrive after the first Monday.
- The Metascore of 68 comes from one secondary source and is unverified.
- I found no systematic data on how much Tomatometer scores drift after 100+ reviews, so reference class 1 relies on judgment.
