## Research brief: Will Indiana make the 2026–27 College Football Playoff?

*Prepared 2026-11-24. Direct page fetches were blocked by the network proxy, so every 2026 fact below comes from search-result snippets and summaries. These are usually accurate on headline facts but sometimes garble details or contradict each other; conflicts are flagged.*

### 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** Indiana is one of the 12 teams named to the 2026–27 CFP bracket. The CFP selection committee decides. Final rankings and bracket come out on **Sunday, Dec 6, 2026** (NCAA.com, Nov 17, 2026). That is well before the market closes on Dec 29, so the question should be settled on Dec 6.
- **Format:** The playoff stays at 12 teams for 2026–27. Expansion talks between the SEC and Big Ten failed (NBC Sports/AP, Jan 23, 2026; ESPN, Jan 2026). Talk of a 16- or 24-team field applies to 2027 at the earliest, with a decision deadline of Dec 1, 2026, so it does not affect this season.
- **How the 12 are chosen:**
  - The five highest-ranked conference champions get automatic bids.
  - The next seven highest-ranked teams get at-large bids.
  - The top four seeds are simply the four highest-ranked teams; they don't need to be conference champions (NCAA.com, Nov 17, 2026).
- **Two ways in for Indiana:**
  - **(a) Win the Big Ten:** beat Purdue on Nov 28, get help to reach the Big Ten title game on Dec 5, then win it. A Big Ten champion would almost certainly be among the top five champions.
  - **(b) At-large bid:** finish high enough in the Dec 6 ranking. In practice the cutoff is about **No. 10–11**, depending on how many conference champions finish below No. 12.
- **Edge cases:**
  - The cutoff moves down one spot if the ACC or Big 12 champion is an upset winner ranked below No. 12. A Group of Five champion (Boise State was No. 17 on Nov 17) usually takes one bid from outside the top 12.
  - Opting out or declining a bid is not a realistic issue.
  - "Qualify" means named to the bracket, not winning a game.

### 2. Reference classes and base rates
These counts are from the analyst's memory of the 2024 and 2025 final CFP rankings. Please verify them.

1. **Lowest-ranked at-large team selected in the 12-team era.**
   - 2024: No. 10 SMU got in; No. 11 Alabama was out. Arizona State (No. 12) and Clemson (No. 16) took champion bids from lower spots.
   - 2025: No. 10 Miami got in; No. 11 Notre Dame was out. Group of Five champions ranked around No. 20 and No. 24 took bids from lower spots, and the ACC champion was unranked.
   - **Cutoff was No. 10 in both seasons (2 of 2).** If two champions again come from outside the top 12, Indiana probably needs to finish No. 10 or better.
2. **Bubble Power Four teams ranked about No. 9–13 in the second-to-last ranking.**
   - Selected: 2024 Indiana and SMU; 2025 Alabama and Miami.
   - Not selected: 2024 Alabama, Miami, Ole Miss and South Carolina; 2025 Notre Dame, BYU and Texas.
   - **About 4 of 11 (~35–40%) got in.** Two-loss teams that don't play on championship weekend went about 1 of 4 (Miami 2025 yes; Miami 2024 and Notre Dame 2025 no). The committee has been willing to let a team that won its conference title game, or lost it narrowly, pass an idle team.

   - FPI before the loss: about 85%.

### 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES:**
- **(A) Big Ten title.** Beat Purdue (~95%), get the needed Nov 28 results (rough guess 15–25%), then win the Dec 5 title game (~45–55%). That gives roughly **7–12%**.
  - Even losing the title game could help: a close loss as a 10-3 team adds a quality game. Alabama made it at 10-3 after losing the 2025 SEC title game.
- **(B) At-large at 10-2.** Indiana sits idle on Dec 5 and needs to finish around No. 10–11. This depends on:
  - (i) where tonight's ranking places it (No. 9–10 is far better than No. 13–14);
  - (ii) losses by teams ranked above it in rivalry week, such as Notre Dame, Tennessee, BYU, Wake Forest, Texas Tech and Iowa;
  - (iii) conference title games keeping the champions inside the top 12. Favorites winning helps Indiana; upsets by low-ranked teams in the ACC or Big 12 pull the cutoff down;
  - (iv) how the committee weighs the Big Ten's congestion: UCLA, Oregon, Iowa and Indiana all have about two losses, and Oregon and Iowa may have stronger résumés.

**Paths to NO:**
- Indiana loses to Purdue (~5%), which almost certainly ends it.
- Indiana is ranked No. 12 or lower tonight and the teams around it keep winning.
- Oregon and/or Iowa finish ahead of Indiana as two-loss Big Ten teams, and UCLA (even if it loses the title game) stays ahead.
- Wake Forest or BYU win their conference titles, or there is a Group of Five plus ACC/Big 12 squeeze.
- The committee holds the weak schedule against Indiana.

### 5. Signposts
- **Tonight's ranking (largest single update):**
  - No. 9–10 suggests the committee still sees Indiana as in; meaningfully above 50%.
  - No. 11–12 means a genuine coin-flip or worse.
  - No. 13 or lower, especially behind Iowa and Oregon, means the at-large path is dim; only path A remains.
- **Nov 28:**
  - Up: UCLA loses to USC; Washington beats Oregon; Nebraska beats Iowa; Minnesota beats Wisconsin; bubble teams above Indiana lose.
  - Down: Oregon wins; Iowa wins comfortably.
- **Dec 1 ranking:** where Indiana sits relative to the expected cutoff after rivalry week.
- **Dec 5:**
  - The Big Ten title game (if Indiana is in it).
  - ACC and Big 12 title games: favorites winning helps; upsets by lower-ranked teams hurt.
  - Where the Group of Five champion is ranked.
- **Injuries:** Indiana's QB situation (Wilson vs Hoover) and its kicking problems could tighten the Purdue game a little, but Indiana is still a strong favorite.
