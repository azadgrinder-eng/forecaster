# Research brief: Will the ISM Manufacturing PMI for November 2026 be between 57.0 and 57.9?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **YES condition:** The headline Manufacturing PMI for November 2026 in ISM's Report On Business must be 57.0, 57.9 or any value in between, taken at the one decimal ISM publishes. Anything 56.9 or below, or 58.0 or above, resolves NO for this bracket.
- **Timing:** The release is scheduled for Tuesday, December 1, 2026, at 10:00 AM ET (ForexFactory and FinanceCalendar calendars, accessed Nov 9, 2026). The market closes at 15:00 UTC, the same moment as the release.
- **Who decides:** ISM's published number on the release date. ISM revises seasonal factors only once a year, in January, so a later revision should not matter here.
- **Edge cases:**
  - If ISM does not publish on schedule, the market stays open until the next scheduled release (early January 2027).
  - If nothing is published by then, the market resolves on "the most recent previous month with available data." That is October's 55.5, which would resolve this bracket NO.
  - ISM is a private organisation, so a federal government shutdown does not delay it.
  - How the headline is built: it is the equal-weighted average of five seasonally adjusted subindexes (New Orders, Production, Employment, Supplier Deliveries, Inventories), rounded to one decimal.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
**A. Size of the month-to-month move.** YES needs a rise of +1.5 to +2.4 from October's 55.5.
- **2026 so far:** The path was Dec-25 47.9, Jan 52.6, Feb 52.4, Mar 52.7, Apr 52.7, May about 54.0 (implied by the June report), Jun 53.3, Jul 55.6, Aug 54.6, Sep 54.5, Oct 55.5. That gives monthly changes of +4.7, −0.2, +0.3, 0.0, +1.3, −0.7, +2.3, −1.0, −0.1, +1.0.
  - Only 1 of these 10 changes (July, +2.3) fell in the +1.5 to +2.4 window. That is about 10%.
  - Leaving out January's regime-shift jump, the changes since February have a standard deviation of about 1.0 point, so 2026 has been unusually calm.
- **Long run:** Over the long history, monthly ISM changes have a standard deviation of roughly 1.7 to 2.0 points. This is my estimate from general knowledge of the series, not something I checked this session.
  - Assuming normal changes centred near 0, the chance of landing in 56.95–57.95 is about 9% with a standard deviation of 1.2, about 11–12% with 1.5, and about 12% with 2.0.
  - A slight pull back toward the recent average (a centre of about 55.2–55.3) lowers these to roughly 8–10%.

**B. Level reference.** A reading of 57.0 or higher would be the highest since March 2022 (57.1, from memory).
- July's 55.6 was called the highest since May 2022 (TrendForce, Aug 2026), and October's production index of 59.0 was the highest since November 2021 (TheStreet, Nov 2, 2026).
- In strong expansions the ISM has often spent long stretches in the 57–61 range: 2011, Aug–Nov 2014, late 2017–2018, and Nov 2020–Mar 2022. So the level is plausible in a boom, but not routine.

**C. Consensus misses.** Recent actuals against consensus:
- October: 55.5 vs about 54.5–54.7 expected (investingLive; ForexFactory).
- August: 54.6 vs 55.2 expected (investingLive).
- July: 55.6 vs about 54 expected (TrendForce).
- Misses of about ±1 point are typical. A November consensus will probably sit near 55–56, and getting to 57+ would need a beat of about 1.5 points or more.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES (57.0–57.9), which needs about +7.4 to +12.3 points summed across the five components:**
- **Demand broadens.** New Orders jump to the high 50s (+3 to +4), production stays near 59–60, employment rises to about 53–54, and inventories keep building to about 53. This would most likely come from AI, data-centre and defence capex, plus restocking given very low customer inventories.
- **Supply stress returns.** The Iran talks collapse or strikes resume and Hormuz is disrupted again. Supplier Deliveries rise into the 60s and precautionary buying lifts inventories and orders. This raises the PMI mechanically even though it is not healthy demand. Note that in 2026 the war shocks came alongside a rising PMI.
- **Momentum.** The two main ISM components with the most momentum are production (+2.3 in October) and inventories (+2.8).

**Paths to NO, below 57.0 (most likely):**
- **Peace or easing:** A deal plus lower oil eases supply chains, so Supplier Deliveries fall from 59 and the precautionary inventory and order build reverses. That pulls the PMI back toward 54–55.
- **Demand cooling:** S&P's measure of new orders slowed, sentiment fell for three months, customers are hesitant, and high prices (75 index) erode demand.
- **Mean reversion:** Since July the index has bounced in a narrow 54.5–55.6 range.
- **Policy uncertainty after the midterms:** A newly Democratic Congress could bring tariff or fiscal shifts. This effect is ambiguous.

**Paths to NO, 58.0 or higher:** A very strong positive surprise of +2.5 or more. That is possible but less likely than 57.x, roughly 3–6%.

## 5. Signposts
- **Raise the probability:**
  - November regional Fed surveys (Empire, Philly, Richmond, KC, Dallas) show clear acceleration in new orders and shipments.
  - The S&P flash PMI for November comes in at 56 or above with faster new-order growth.
  - The Iran talks fail and Hormuz is disrupted again, so Brent rebounds above $110 and delivery times lengthen.
  - The Chicago PMI shows a large jump.
- **Lower the probability:**
  - A signed US–Iran deal and reopened Hormuz with Brent below $90 (supplier deliveries and inventories would likely ease).
  - Regional surveys flat or weaker.
  - The S&P flash at 54 or below, or slowing new orders.
  - A published consensus near 55 (forecasters' November consensus will be clearer in late November).
