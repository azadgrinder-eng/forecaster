# Research brief: Will Syria join the Abraham Accords before 2027?

*Prepared 2026-11-22. About 39 days remain before the 2026-12-31 23:59 ET deadline.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Syria must **formally sign a normalization agreement with Israel** that is (a) publicly acknowledged by **both** governments and (b) **clearly attributed to the Abraham Accords or their continuation**. The signing must happen by **Dec 31, 2026, 11:59 PM ET**.
- **Who decides:** Official government statements are the primary source. A consensus of credible reporting can also be used.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **A security or non-aggression pact is not normalization.** The talks that actually exist are about a security understanding: demilitarizing southern Syria, reviving the 1974 Disengagement Agreement and limiting Israeli forces. Signing that alone should not count unless both governments call it normalization or an Abraham Accords step. The main ambiguity is that the Trump administration might describe a security deal as "part of the Abraham Accords" while Damascus explicitly does not. Because both governments must acknowledge the attribution, that should probably still resolve NO.
  - **An announced intention is not a signing.** Kazakhstan announced in November 2025 that it would join. A September 2026 US statement still said a signing ceremony would "soon" be announced, and I found no evidence that it has happened (Fox News/CBS coverage, 2025–26). So an announcement by Syria would not satisfy "formally signs."
  - Sudan's precedent shows that signing the Abraham Accords Declaration with the US (Jan 2021) has been treated as "joining," even without a bilateral treaty with Israel. Under this question's wording, a Syria–US declaration plus Israeli acknowledgment could arguably count.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Countries joining the Abraham Accords per year:** 2020: 3 (UAE, Bahrain, Morocco). Jan 2021: Sudan. 2021–2024: 0. 2025: Kazakhstan announced, with formal signing apparently still pending as of Sept 2026. 2026 to date: 0 new signers found.
   - That is about 5 states in about 6 years, and **none of them had an active territorial dispute with Israel.** Sharaa himself makes this point (Al-Majalla, 2025).
   - The base rate for any particular candidate signing within a given ~6-week window is very low, well under 5%.
2. **Syria–Israel peace attempts:** Madrid/Washington talks (1991–96), Wye/Shepherdstown (1999–2000), Turkish-mediated indirect talks (2008) and the 2010–11 Hof channel all failed, mostly over the Golan. That is **0 of about 4 serious tracks**. The current US-mediated track has gone through 5+ formal rounds since mid-2025 and has not produced even the narrower security deal.
3. **Time from Arab–Israeli talks to signing:**
   - Egypt: Sadat's visit (Nov 1977) to treaty (Mar 1979) took about 16 months.
   - Jordan: Washington Declaration (Jul 1994) to treaty (Oct 1994) took about 3 months.
   - UAE: announcement (Aug 13, 2020) to signing (Sep 15, 2020) took about 1 month.
   - Even the fastest case needed about a month from a public breakthrough. No public normalization breakthrough exists today, and only about 5.5 weeks remain.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 20, 2026:** Israeli government formation is unresolved. President Herzog gave opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot the mandate, with sources disagreeing on whether he has 28 days or 6 weeks. Eisenkot is weighing a unity government, a minority government with Arab-party backing, or other options. If both he and Netanyahu fail, there would be another election in early 2027. Netanyahu remains caretaker PM (JTA, Nov 20, 2026; Al Jazeera, Nov 8, 2026).
- **Nov 18, 2026:** Syrian FM Asaad al-Shaibani, speaking in Brussels, said Damascus remains "committed" to a **security understanding** and to reviving the **1974 disengagement agreement**. He accused Israel of taking more territory in southern Syria and of daily incursions. He said nothing about normalization (Middle East Monitor/Anadolu, Nov 18, 2026).
- **Nov 2026:** Israeli raids are increasing in Quneitra. Syrian authorities counted 125 incursions in October. The Syrian Observatory for Human Rights counted at least 12 artillery attacks in the first half of November. There were detentions in Jbata al-Khashab (Antiwar.com, Nov 16, 2026; Anadolu, Nov 2026).

  - A Council for a Secure America report (Oct/Nov 2026) ranks Syria **3rd** among normalization prospects, behind Kuwait and Lebanon (JNS; The Media Line).

  - A RealClearWorld op-ed (Nov 5) argued that the Syria security deal "must close first."
- **Late Oct 2026:** Barrack, at the Doha Global Security Forum, said Sharaa is willing to agree terms including a **non-aggression pact**, but cautioned that this does not mean a final agreement will be reached. Barrack also urged Israel to halt its Syria operations (Shafaq News, Oct 2026).

  - FM Shaibani met an Israeli delegation in Paris.
  - Israel reportedly wants "political concessions" toward formal peace, while Syria demands withdrawal to the Dec 8, 2024 / 1974 lines (The National, Aug 25, 2026).

- **Jul 23, 2026:** Trump tied the signed Saudi nuclear deal to Saudi Arabia joining the Accords. This shows strong US appetite for expansion, but Saudi Arabia has not joined (CNN, Jul 23, 2026).
- **Background:**
  - In 2025, Sharaa repeatedly ruled out joining the Accords, both in Al-Majalla (Aug 2025) and on Fox News during his Nov 2025 White House visit, citing the Golan.
  - Trump has pressed Syria to join since May 2025.
  - In January 2026 an intelligence-sharing mechanism was agreed.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (all low-probability within 39 days):**
- **A Trump-driven surprise:** Trump pressures both sides into a package of security deal, Israeli pullback to the pre-Dec 2024 lines and Abraham Accords accession, with a White House signing in December. This would require Sharaa to reverse his repeated public position and a caretaker or new Israeli government to sign quickly. Israeli caretaker governments have signed major deals before, for example the Lebanon maritime deal in Oct 2022.
- **Relabeling:** A security or non-aggression pact gets signed, and both governments publicly frame it as an Abraham Accords step. Damascus has consistently framed it the opposite way, which makes this unlikely.

**Paths to NO (dominant):**
- The security deal itself stays stalled. The "90%" claim dates from September, and Israel has since expanded incursions.
- Even if a security deal is signed, Syria keeps it separate from normalization, as Sharaa and Shaibani have consistently done.
- The unresolved Golan and Mount Hermon positions, the Druze/Sweida issue, Turkish influence, and Syrian domestic legitimacy all weigh against normalization.
- Israeli political paralysis makes it hard to approve major concessions such as withdrawal from the buffer zone.

## 5. Signposts

- **Would raise the probability sharply:**
  - A signed Syria–Israel security agreement in late Nov or Dec.
  - Any Syrian official using "normalization," "peace" or "Abraham Accords" affirmatively.
  - An announced White House ceremony.
  - Israel announcing a withdrawal from positions taken after Dec 2024.
- **Would raise it moderately:** A new Israeli government formed quickly with a mandate for regional deals, or a Trump–Sharaa meeting.
- **Would lower it further:** Continued Quneitra incursions or escalation, renewed Sweida violence, Israel heading to new elections, or further Syrian statements limiting the talks to a "security understanding" or the 1974 framework, as on Nov 18.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **Gaps in my research:** I could not open most full articles because page fetches were blocked by the proxy. I relied on search summaries, and some of their dates were inconsistent.
- I found no reporting on Syria–Israel negotiation rounds in Oct–Nov 2026 beyond Barrack's Doha remarks and Shaibani's Nov 18 statement. Secret talks are possible; the Aug 2026 talks were only revealed later.
- I could not confirm whether Kazakhstan has formally signed. This affects how "joining" is being treated in practice.
- I could not confirm the exact Israeli coalition deadline (28 days vs. 6 weeks) or whether a caretaker government would sign a normalization deal.
- **Wording ambiguity:** If the US labels a Syria security pact an "Abraham Accords" achievement while Syria disputes that, it is unclear how the question would resolve.
