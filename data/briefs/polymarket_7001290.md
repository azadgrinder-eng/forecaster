# Research brief: Will the November 2026 CPI-U month-over-month change be 0.3%?

The latest evidence points to a November headline well below 0.3%. Gasoline prices are falling sharply and the Cleveland Fed nowcast is about 0.14%. For YES, core inflation would need to come in hot and the gasoline decline would need to be much smaller than the pump data now suggest.

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts:** The **seasonally adjusted** one-month percent change in the CPI-U (all items) for November 2026, as BLS reports it to one decimal. YES only if the printed figure is exactly **0.3%**. In practice that means an unrounded change of roughly 0.25% to 0.35%; BLS rounds the index ratio. Any other print (0.2, 0.4, 0.1, etc.) is NO.
- **When:** BLS schedules the release for **Thursday, Dec 10, 2026, 8:30 AM ET** (BLS CPI release schedule). The market closes Dec 11 at 04:59 UTC.
- **Which number:** The number first published on Dec 10 is what counts. Later revisions, including the February 2027 seasonal-factor revisions, should not matter.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Release delay:** If the report isn't out on Dec 10, the market can stay open until the next scheduled CPI release. If nothing comes out by then, it resolves to "the most recent previous month with available data." That would be October's **0.4%**, which is also NO.
  - **Shutdown risk:** Federal funding runs through **Dec 11, 2026** under H.R. 6500, signed Sep 2, 2026, so a lapse would start Dec 12 (Fox News, Sep 2026; multibagg, 2026). The release falls a day before the deadline, and November prices were collected while the government was funded, so the risk to the release is small.
  - **The 2025 data gap:** BLS never published October 2025 CPI because of the 2025 shutdown, so it printed no 12-month rate for October 2026 (Trading Economics, Nov 2026). This does **not** affect the November month-over-month figure. It may add a little noise to the seasonal factors.
- **Ambiguity:** Little. The question is about headline, seasonally adjusted, all-items CPI-U, not core and not the unadjusted figure.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. All headline month-over-month prints, 2023–2025 (as first reported).** I recalled these from memory, so they are approximate.
- 0.3% printed in about 7 of 34 published months: Dec 2023; Jan, Apr and Nov 2024; Jun, Sep and Dec 2025.
- That gives roughly **20%** unconditionally.
- 0.2% was the most common print. Tails from −0.1 to 0.6 occur often.

**B. 2026 so far (high inflation and an oil shock).** Known prints:
- Feb 0.3 (cattlerange, Mar 2026)
- May 0.5
- Jun −0.4: energy −5.7%, gasoline −9.7%, core 0.0 (Fox Business / Trading Economics, Jul 14, 2026)
- Jul 0.1 (usinflationcalculator, Aug 2026)
- Sep 0.6: energy +5.2%, gasoline about +9.1%, core 0.3 (TD Economics, Oct 2026)
- Oct 0.4 (BLS, Nov 10, 2026)

Headline has been very volatile this year because energy swung between −5.7% and +10.9% a month. The exact value 0.3 is just one bin in a wide distribution.

**C. Months where seasonally adjusted gasoline fell about 5% or more.** This is the most relevant conditional class, also from memory and approximate:

| Month | Gasoline (SA) | Core | Headline |

| Aug 2022 | −10.6% | 0.6 | 0.1 |

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Rough decomposition of headline:**

| Component | Weight | Assumption | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core | ~0.79 | — | ~0.24 pp per 0.3% of core |
| Food | ~0.135 | ~0.3% | ~0.04 pp |
| Energy | ~0.07 | gasoline −5% to −8% SA | −0.15 to −0.30 pp |

**Central path (NO):** Core 0.2–0.3, food 0.2–0.4 and gasoline −5% to −8% seasonally adjusted put headline at about **0.0–0.15**. This matches the Cleveland Fed's 0.14 and the Nov 2023 analog (core 0.3, gasoline −6%, headline 0.1).

**Path to YES (0.25 ≤ headline < 0.35):** It would need some combination of:
1. A hot core of 0.4% or more. Possible given the energy pass-through and broad gains seen in October, but core has printed 0.3 three months running and the nowcast says 0.21.
2. A much smaller seasonally adjusted gasoline decline than pump prices imply, for example from favorable seasonal factors, BLS sampling differences, or the timing of collection within the month.
3. Electricity or natural gas rising, or food above 0.5%.

If oil rebounds on renewed Iran fighting in the second half of November, the monthly average would still be well below October's because most of the drop has already happened.

**Other NO paths:**
- A big gasoline drop pushes headline to 0.0 or below, as in June 2026.
- A hot core plus a moderate gasoline drop lands at 0.2.
- Core near 0.5 with a mild energy decline lands at 0.4. This needs a large core surprise.

**Nowcast error:** Historically the Cleveland Fed's headline month-over-month nowcast misses by about 0.1–0.15 pp (my approximate recollection). Reaching 0.25 from 0.14 needs a miss of about +0.11 or more, roughly a 1-sigma upside surprise. The 0.3 bin covers roughly +0.11 to +0.21 of error.

## 5. Signposts

- **Rest-of-November gasoline (EIA weekly Nov 16/23/30, AAA daily):**
  - Further declines toward $3.75 lower the odds of YES.
  - A rebound above about $4.10 on Iran escalation raises them modestly.
- **Cleveland Fed nowcast drift:** Watch whether the November headline estimate moves toward 0.2 or more, or below 0.1. It updates daily.
- **October PCE (late November) and any core breadth signals:** A strong core PCE would raise the odds of a 0.3–0.4 core in November.
- **Economist previews (early December):** If the consensus forms at 0.1–0.2, the odds of YES fall. A consensus of 0.3 would be a major update toward YES.
- **Electricity and natural gas:** Utility price moves, including winter heating, would affect the energy component beyond gasoline.
- **Funding negotiations before the Dec 11 deadline:** Only relevant if the release date somehow moves. Under the rules a delay effectively resolves to October's 0.4, which is NO.

## 6. Uncertainties

- The 0.14% nowcast comes from a single snippet and is unconfirmed.
- I don't know the exact November seasonal factor for gasoline in 2026. Its size determines how much of the roughly 9% unadjusted drop survives seasonal adjustment.
- I don't know gasoline's current relative importance in CPI. It is likely elevated, around 3.5–4%, given $4+ prices.
- I don't have the full 2026 monthly headline series: January, March, April and August are missing.
