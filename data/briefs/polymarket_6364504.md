# Research brief: Will Trump formally declare a new national emergency under the National Emergencies Act by Nov 30, 2026 (11:59 PM ET)?

**Research limits:** Today is Nov 19, 2026. The proxy blocked every direct page fetch (whitehouse.gov, the Federal Register's executive-order list, the Brennan Center, CNN and DHS), so everything below comes from search-result summaries. Key items should be checked against the primary documents.

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** a formal presidential document issued under the National Emergencies Act (50 U.S.C. § 1621 et seq.) with explicit language that a national emergency exists. This is usually an executive order or proclamation stating "I … hereby declare a national emergency," citing the NEA and often also IEEPA.
- **Window:** from market creation to Nov 30, 2026, 11:59 PM ET. **The creation date is unknown, and this matters a lot.** A new NEA emergency was declared on Aug 26, 2026 (bulk-power system, below). If the market was created before that date, it may already resolve YES. If it was created after (more likely, since it is still being asked), only about 11 days remain from today.
- **What does not count:**
  - renewals, continuations or extensions; several routine continuation notices are due in November
  - amendments or expansions of existing emergencies, unless they formally declare a new one
  - speeches, Truth Social posts, and officials calling something an "emergency"
  - executive orders with "emergency" in the title that don't invoke the NEA, such as EO 14435 "Emergency Tax Relief on Diesel Fuel" (Oct 2026)
  - Stafford Act/FEMA emergency declarations for states, such as Hawaii on Sept 25, 2026
  - DC Home Rule Act-style "crime emergencies"
- **Who decides:** the Federal Register and White House publications first, with "consensus of credible reporting" as a fallback.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **IEEPA expansions.** Trump often "expands the scope" of an existing emergency (for example, adding Russian-oil penalties under EO 14024). These count only if the text declares a new emergency. Many IEEPA sanctions orders do declare new ones (for example, Cuba, Jan 29, 2026).
  - **Federal Register lag.** An order signed Nov 28–30 may not be published until December. Signing date should govern, using WH publication or reporting.
  - **Loose official language.** Statements like Mullin's on Nov 13 (below) could cause confusion, but under the rules they do not qualify on their own.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Trump second term (Jan 20, 2025 to now): about 12 new NEA emergencies in about 22 months, roughly 0.55 per month.**
   - The Miller Center counted 9 new emergencies in his second term's first year: border, energy, Canada, Mexico, China, ICC, reciprocal trade (EO 14257), Brazil and others (Miller Center).
   - A Substack review of the Brennan list says Trump's total across both terms reached 25 with the Aug 26, 2026 addition (unverified third-party count).
   - A Poisson rate of 0.55 per month over about 11.5 days gives about 0.21 expected events, or **P ≈ 19%**.
2. **Trump in 2026 only: about 2–3 new emergencies in about 10.6 months, roughly 0.25 per month.**
   - Cuba oil/tariffs, Jan 29 (Holland & Knight, Feb 2026)
   - Possibly Iran, EO 14382 of Feb 6 (unclear whether it was a new declaration or an expansion of the existing Iran emergency)
   - Bulk-power system, Aug 26, EO 14420/14421 (DOE; White House fact sheet; Holland & Knight, Sept 2026)
   - None found for Sept 1 to Nov 19, about 2.5 months.
   - The pace slowed after the Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs on Feb 20, 2026 (Learning Resources v. Trump; CRS LSB11398), which removed the main 2025 driver.
   - At 0.25 per month, **P ≈ 9%** over the remaining window.
3. **All presidents since the NEA took effect in 1976: about 80 declarations in about 50 years, roughly 1.5–2 per year.** The Brennan list showed 51 in effect as of Nov 5, 2026. At that rate, an 11-day window gives **P ≈ 5–6%**.

**Seasonality:** the window includes Thanksgiving (Nov 26), which usually reduces signings. On the other hand, past IEEPA declarations have sometimes landed in late November or December.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES:**
1. **ATC/telecom critical-infrastructure emergency** after the Nov 13 ground stop. Mullin's quote suggests internal framing already exists. A formal NEA order could be used to invoke emergency procurement or Defense Production Act-adjacent authorities, or to pressure Congress. It depends on whether the White House follows the rhetoric with paper. The ground stop has passed, so urgency may fade.
2. **A new IEEPA sanctions emergency** against a country or actor (Iran-conflict related, Venezuela/cartels, Nigeria, ICC-style targets, foreign adversary technology). This is the historically dominant route, and IEEPA sanctions remain legal after the tariff ruling.
3. **An election-related emergency** around certification (foreign interference or fraud claims). Trump has refused to rule it out and allies are pushing, but there was no action before the vote. Low probability, high salience.
4. **Energy or supply-chain emergencies** (critical minerals, drones, AI chips, diesel). A new NEA declaration would be needed rather than reliance on the existing 2025 energy emergency.

**Paths to NO:**
- The default is the slowed 2026 pace: none in the 85 days since Aug 26.
- The administration can usually act under existing emergencies (energy, border, Iran, Russia) rather than declaring new ones.
- The holiday week cuts the number of working days.
- Rhetorical "emergencies" without NEA paperwork do not count.

## 5. Signposts

- **Strongly toward YES:**
  - a WH "Presidential Actions" post or Federal Register public-inspection document titled "Declaring a National Emergency…" or containing "hereby declare a national emergency"
  - a WH fact sheet on an ATC emergency
  - Treasury/OFAC announcing a new sanctions program, since a new program usually means a new EO declaring a new emergency
- **Moderately toward YES:**
  - reports of a draft NEA order on ATC/telecom or elections
  - Trump stating he "will sign" an emergency declaration
  - a sharp escalation with Iran, Venezuela or another foreign actor
  - post-election disputes involving federal fraud claims
- **Toward NO:**
  - the ATC funding fight moving to ordinary appropriations
  - the White House clarifying that Mullin was speaking loosely
  - quiet news through Thanksgiving week
- **Resolution-relevant:** the market's creation date. If it predates Aug 26, 2026, check whether the bulk-power EO already settles it.

- Whether EO 14382 (Iran, Feb 6, 2026) declared a new emergency, which affects the 2026 rate.
