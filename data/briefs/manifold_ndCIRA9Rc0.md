# Research brief: Will an Irregular Opening (ECO A00) be played in at least one game of the 2026 World Chess Championship?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What resolves YES:** at least one game of the World Chess Championship 2026 must be an "Irregular Opening" with the official ECO code A00. A creator comment (2024‑12‑28) says the official A00 designation decides, not Wikipedia's wording.
- **Which first moves are A00 under standard ECO:** 1.a3, 1.a4, 1.b4 (Polish/Sokolsky), 1.c3, 1.d3, 1.e3, 1.f3, 1.g3 (Benko/Hungarian), 1.g4, 1.h3, 1.h4, 1.Nc3 (Van Geet/Dunst), 1.Na3, 1.Nh3. Wikipedia also lists 1.a3, 1.a4 and 1.b4 as A00 (Irregular chess opening, Wikipedia).
- **What does NOT count:** 1.b3 is A01, 1.f4 is A02–A03, 1.Nf3 is A04–A09 and 1.c4 is A10–A39. An odd Black reply is also not A00. For example, Spassky's 1.d4 b5 in the 1966 world championship match is A40 (Mark Rubery Chess column, 2020).
- **The match:** Gukesh D (champion) vs Javokhir Sindarov (challenger), in Cologny/Geneva. Up to 14 classical games; the first player to reach 7.5 points wins.
  - Opening ceremony Nov 22. Game 1 is Tue **Nov 24, 2026**; Game 14 is **Dec 11**; rapid/blitz tiebreaks on **Dec 12** if the score is 7–7 (ramachess schedule; worldchess.com, 2026).
  - FIDE's July release said Nov 25–Dec 15. A World Chess article reports that FIDE "quietly changed" the dates. Any version ends before the Dec 19 close date.
- **Edge cases and ambiguities:**
  1. **Transpositions are the biggest issue.** Databases (ChessBase, chessgames.com, Lichess) give a game its ECO code from the position it eventually reaches, not just the first move. So 1.g3 often ends up as A05/A07 (King's Indian Attack), A20+ (English) or E6x. 1.Nc3 d5 2.e4 often becomes a Caro/French/Scandinavian. 1.e3 or 1.d3 can become d4/e4 systems. **A game could start with an A00 move and still not be labeled A00.** Different databases may also label the same game differently.
  2. **Do tiebreak games count?** Rapid, blitz or armageddon games would most likely count as "games of the WCC 2026", but the question doesn't say. Offbeat first moves are much more common at faster time controls.
  3. **Other events with similar names:** a Women's World Championship cycle is running in 2026 (Anna Muzychuk was co-leading the Women's Candidates in April), and there is a separate "Total Chess World Championship 2026." The question most likely means the open FIDE title match, but the wording doesn't rule the others out.
  4. **Forfeits and short draws:** no special treatment. A game that starts with an A00 move and ends quickly still counts, if it is labeled A00.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Classical World Championship title matches, 1886–2024.** Roughly 50 matches and well over 1,000 games, nearly all opening 1.e4, 1.d4, 1.c4 or 1.Nf3.
- I found no documented case of a classical title-match game starting with an A00 first move. That covers: a forum list of every match's first and last moves (Chess.com forum); the Lasker–Steinitz 1894 opening lists, all 1.e4 (Chess Archaeology); chessgames.com match pages for 1886, 1927 and 1929, which show QGD, Ruy Lopez and Neo‑Grünfeld (D70); and history articles on unusual moves in title matches.
- The best-known "first move surprise" in title-match history is Black's 1...b5 (Spassky, 1966), which is not A00.
- Recent matches, from memory and coverage I could partly check:
  - 2021 Carlsen–Nepomniachtchi, 2023 Ding–Nepomniachtchi and 2024 Ding–Gukesh: none had an A00 first move.
  - In 2024 Ding's "surprises" were 1.Nf3 and the London System (ChessBase Game 4 and Game 10 reports).
- **Base rate: about 0 matches out of ~45 since 1886, so probably under 0.2% per game.** Treat "zero ever" as likely but not fully proven; I couldn't search a full database.

**B. Title-match tiebreaks (rapid/blitz).** Tiebreaks were held in 2006, 2012, 2016, 2018 and 2023 (plus 1990s–2000s FIDE knockout events, which are a different format).
- To my knowledge none of the five title-match tiebreaks used an A00 first move. That's 0 of 5, but the sample is small.
- Tiebreaks happen only if the score is 7–7. Recent matches went to tiebreaks in 5 of the 8 since 2006 (2006, 2012, 2016, 2018, 2023; not 2008, 2014, 2021). In 2024 the tiebreaks were avoided only in Game 14.

**C. Elite classical games in general (2700+ players, 2020s).**
- 1.g3 is the most common A00 move at the top, roughly 1–2% of White games. 1.Nc3, 1.e3, 1.d3, 1.a3 and 1.b4 appear mostly in rapid and blitz (Carlsen's habit).
- Sindarov's own record, from an unverified third-party stats site: of 244 White games, 199 were 1.e4, 24 were 1.d4 and 11 were 1.c4, leaving about 10 (4%) with other moves, mostly 1.Nf3 or 1.b3, which are not A00 (chessworld.net, 2026).
- Gukesh is mainly a 1.e4/1.d4 player who sometimes uses 1.Nf3/1.c4. I found no 2025–26 classical A00 games by him.
- **Rough calculation (mine):** about 7 White games each, an A00 first move at maybe 0.5–1% per game, and then roughly a 30–60% chance the game keeps its A00 label. That gives about **2–6% for the classical games**. Title-match preparation is more conservative than ordinary tournament play, which argues for the lower end.

  - This matters here: a struggling champion who falls behind might gamble on offbeat openings, though usually still within main first moves.

- **Time control:** 120 minutes for the first 40 moves, with extra time after that (zamin.uz).

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES:**
1. **A one-off surprise in classical play**, most likely 1.g3 or 1.Nc3, used as a move-order trick to avoid the opponent's preparation, ideally staying in A00 territory such as 1.g3 d5 2.Bg2 with non-transposing play.
   - Most likely from the player who is behind late in the match. Under current ratings that would probably be Gukesh.
2. **Tiebreaks.** Rapid and especially blitz games raise the chance of offbeat first moves, maybe 5–15% across a tiebreak set by my estimate. This only matters if the score is 7–7 (historically roughly 50–60% of modern matches).
3. **Loose labeling.** A database or the question creator might label a game A00 based on its first move even if it transposes, which raises YES somewhat.

**Paths to NO:**
- Both players stick to 1.e4, 1.d4, 1.c4 and 1.Nf3, as every modern title match has.
- Heavy computer preparation favours main lines.
- Someone reaches 7.5 points before Game 14, so there are fewer games and no tiebreak.
- Even if 1.g3, 1.e3 or 1.Nc3 is played, it transposes into a main-line code (A0x, A1x–A3x, D, E or B).

## 5. Signposts

- **Early-game openings (Nov 24–28):** if either player uses 1.Nf3/1.c4 move-order tricks, flexible first moves become somewhat more likely. If both play only 1.e4/1.d4, the probability drops.
- **Score:** a lopsided lead by Sindarov (say +2 after 8 games) raises Gukesh's incentive to gamble but also raises the chance of an early finish. A 7–7 or tied score near Game 12–14 raises the chance of tiebreaks, which is good for YES.
- **Any 1.g3, 1.Nc3 or 1.e3 first move:** check the official PGN/ECO label on FIDE, ChessBase and chessgames.com right away. Whether it transposes decides the outcome.
- **Creator clarification** on whether tiebreaks count, and on first-move vs final ECO code.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I couldn't access a full database of title-match games (page fetches were blocked), so "no A00 game in title-match history" comes from incomplete sources and memory.
- Sindarov's first-move statistics come from an unverified third-party site. Gukesh's 2025–26 first-move habits weren't checked against a database.
- The exact final schedule is unclear (Dec 11/12 vs Dec 13/15), as are the detailed rapid/blitz/armageddon tiebreak rules.
- How the creator will apply "official A00 designation" to games that transpose, and whether tiebreak games count.
