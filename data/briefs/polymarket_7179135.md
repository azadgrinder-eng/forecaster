*Note on method: every page fetch was blocked by the network proxy, so all facts below come from search-result summaries. They have not been checked against the live Rotten Tomatoes page. Some historical scores are marked "from memory" and should be spot-checked.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts for YES:** the "All Critics" Tomatometer shown on Rotten Tomatoes for *Jumanji: Open World* must be **65% or higher** at **10:00 AM ET on Dec 28, 2026**. The Top Critics score and the audience score (Popcornmeter) do not count.
- **Timing:** the U.S. release is Dec 25, 2026. The resolution snapshot comes about 3 days after release.
  - Sony's past pattern: reviews for *Welcome to the Jungle* were out by Dec 19, 2017, a day before its Dec 20 release, at about 80% (CinemaBlend, Dec 2017). Reviews for *The Next Level* were out about Dec 11–12, 2019, before its Dec 13 release (CNBC, Dec 12, 2019).
  - So the snapshot will probably reflect roughly 100–200 reviews, and the score should be close to its final value.
- **Who decides:** the score displayed on the Rotten Tomatoes page (rottentomatoes.com/m/jumanji_open_world). RT shows whole-number percentages, so the rounded figure is what counts: 64% is NO and 65% is YES.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Missing data:** if no score is available at the deadline, the market keeps checking until Jan 1, 2027, 11:59 PM ET, and resolves NO if there is still nothing. That would only matter if the release slipped or reviews were held back, which is unlikely since the film is already in theater listings.
  - **Release-date confusion:** the date moved from Dec 11 to Dec 25 (Wikipedia; Khaleej Times). Some listings show Dec 23 (Odeon UK, Plex) or Dec 24 (AllMovie). Australia and New Zealand open Dec 26 (Flicks; Hoyts). None of this matters much, because reviews will be out before Dec 28 either way.
  - **Score drift:** the score can still move a few points between the first wave of reviews and Dec 28. *The Next Level* opened at about 67–70% and settled at 71% (Toonado, Dec 2019; Cosmic Book News, Dec 2019). *Welcome to the Jungle* was about 80% before release and settled at 75–76% (CinemaBlend, Dec 2017; WhatCulture, 2017).

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. This franchise (same director, writers and lead cast)**
- *Jumanji* (1995): about 52–54%.
- *Welcome to the Jungle* (2017): 76%.
- *The Next Level* (2019): 71%.
- (Rotten Tomatoes page snippets; It's A Stampede, Dec 28, 2019)
- Both Kasdan-directed films cleared 65%. The trend is down about 5 points per film. Another drop of the same size would land around 66%, just above the line.

**B. Third films in a franchise**
- One analysis of the 70 highest-grossing trilogies found the third film was the lowest-rated on RT about 64–65% of the time (FlowingData, via Business Insider).
- FiveThirtyEight found sequels usually score below the films before them, and that third films beat the first film only about 20% of the time (FiveThirtyEight).
- My own rough tally (from memory, needs checking) of third films whose second film scored 60–85% shows about half reaching 65% or more:
  - Above 65: Sonic 3 (86), Paddington in Peru (93), Enola Holmes 3 (70), Madagascar 3 (79), Kung Fu Panda 3 (87), Trolls Band Together (74), Iron Man 3 (79), Star Trek Beyond (86), Deadpool & Wolverine (78).
  - Below 65: Shrek the Third (41), Despicable Me 3 (59), Spider-Man 3 (63), Pitch Perfect 3 (29), Spy Kids 3 (45), Ghostbusters: Frozen Empire (about 45), Ant-Man: Quantumania (46), X-Men: The Last Stand (57), Matrix Revolutions (34).
- The usual drop from second to third film is roughly 5–15 points. That puts this film's expected landing zone at about 56–66%, with the threshold near the top of that range.

**C. Dwayne Johnson live-action wide releases since 2017**
- Approximate scores: Welcome to the Jungle 76, Rampage about 52, Skyscraper about 48, Hobbs & Shaw about 67, The Next Level 71, Jungle Cruise 62, Red Notice about 37, Black Adam about 39, Fast X about 56, Red One about 31–33, live-action Moana (July 2026) about 31–36.
- About 3 of 11 reached 65% or more (roughly 27%), and two of those three are Jumanji films (RT editorial ranking, Oct 2025; Business Insider; Slashfilm/ComicBook, July 2026).
- *The Smashing Machine* (2025) reportedly got some of Johnson's best reviews, but I could not confirm its number.
- His recent big commercial films have reviewed badly. *Moana* (2026) is among Disney's lowest-rated live-action remakes.

**Director Jake Kasdan:** most of his comedies land between 44% and 76%. *Walk Hard* (about 74), *Welcome to the Jungle* (76) and *The Next Level* (71) cleared 65. *Bad Teacher* (about 44), *Sex Tape* (about 17) and *Orange County* (about 48) did not. (From memory.)

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES**
- The franchise formula works again. Both earlier Kasdan films were liked by critics as "fun, warm, cast-chemistry" crowd-pleasers.
- The 1995-style "real world" twist gives critics something new to praise.
- Critics grade a holiday family film gently.
- This needs a drop of no more than about 6 points from *The Next Level*.

**Paths to NO**
- Sequel fatigue and a derivative formula, which early trailer coverage already flagged.
- The negative test-screening rumor turns out to be true.
- Johnson's recent run of poorly reviewed commercial films (Red One, Black Adam, Moana).
- A long runtime (possibly over 2 hours) for a comedy.
- A typical third-film drop of more than 6 points.
- Even a "decent" reception at 60–64% resolves NO, so the threshold sits close to the middle of the plausible outcomes.

## 5. Signposts

- **First social reactions after the premiere:** these are almost always positive, so they carry little information. Muted or lukewarm reactions would be a meaningful negative sign.
- **Embargo timing:**
  - An early lift (a week or more before release) suggests the studio is confident.
  - A late lift (Dec 23–24 or later) is a negative sign.
  - No pre-release reviews at all is strongly negative.
- **Opening Tomatometer once there are 50+ reviews:** this is the single strongest signal.
  - The two previous films each lost about 1–4 points between the early score and the settled score, in different directions (*Welcome to the Jungle* fell; *The Next Level* rose).
  - An opening score of 70% or higher very likely holds above 65. An opening score of 60–68% is a toss-up.
- **Metacritic score:** previous films scored about 58 (*Welcome to the Jungle*) and about 58 (*The Next Level*) (from memory). A Metacritic score of 55 or above is consistent with RT at 65 or above. A score below 50 points to NO.
- **Reshoot or re-edit news, or another release-date move:** negative.
