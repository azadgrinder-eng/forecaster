# Research brief: Will gold be the best performer of Bitcoin, gold and the S&P 500 in 2026?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts:** The market resolves to whichever of Bitcoin, gold and the S&P 500 has the largest percentage price change in calendar 2026. Only price changes count; dividends and other total-return measures do not.
- **Gold:** Measured by the MarketWatch Gold Continuous Contract (GC00). The start point is the close on the last trading day of 2025, which was Dec 31, 2025. The end point is the GC00 close on the last trading day of 2026, which is Thursday, Dec 31, 2026.
  - On Dec 31, 2025, COMEX nearby/active gold futures settled at **$4,341.10/oz** (Barchart; CME Group Jan 2026 metals update; Yahoo Finance, Jan 2026).
  - MarketWatch's "close" can differ slightly from the CME settlement price. GC00 also rolls between contract months, so the year-end 2026 reading may come from a different delivery month and carry a small contango premium. Both effects are probably under 1% and only matter in a near-tie.
- **S&P 500:** Measured by the ^SPX close on Yahoo Finance. The 2025 close was **6,845.50** (Kiplinger; Stock Titan; dqydj; Reuters via MarketScreener, Dec 31, 2025). One low-quality article gave 6,896.24, which conflicts with the others.
- **Bitcoin:** Measured by the Binance BTC/USDT 1-minute candle close. The start is Jan 1, 2026 at 00:00 ET (05:00 UTC); the end is Dec 31, 2026 at 23:59 ET. I could not get the exact start candle. Reports put BTC at about **$87,000–88,300** around New Year's Day (Bitcoin Magazine; ts2.tech; Cryptonews, Jan 1, 2026).
- **Ties:** A tie goes to the name that comes first alphabetically: Bitcoin, then Gold, then S&P 500. Gold therefore beats the S&P 500 in an exact tie but loses to Bitcoin.
- **Who decides and when:** The market platform resolves from the named sources. The close date is Jan 1, 2027 at 04:59 UTC, which is 23:59 ET on Dec 31.
- **Edge cases:** Shortened sessions use the official close. If a day has no close, the last valid close is used. Dec 31, 2026 is a normal trading day for both NYSE and CME.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Which asset won each calendar year, 2011–2025 (unconditional base rate).** This is my tally from well-known annual price returns. Gold won 3 of 15 years: 2018 (gold about −1.6% vs S&P −6.2% and BTC −73%), 2022 (gold about −0.3% vs S&P −19%) and 2025 (gold about +64% vs S&P +16.4%). The S&P won once (2014). Bitcoin won the other 11. This prior barely matters with 5.5 weeks left, because the year is about 90% complete.

2. **Conditional reference class: how often gold beats the S&P by about 20 percentage points in roughly 6 weeks.** That is the size of reversal gold now needs (see section 3). It is rare, and it has happened mainly in crisis regimes:
   - Oct–Nov 2008: the S&P fell about 30% while gold was roughly flat.
   - Feb 19–Mar 23, 2020: the S&P fell about 34% while gold fell only a few percent.
   - January 2026: gold rose from $4,341 to a record **$5,595 on Jan 29** (+29%) while the S&P was roughly flat (goldsilver.com / forecast aggregators).

   Across the roughly 35 years of daily data, this kind of relative move shows up in perhaps 1–3% of rolling 6-week windows. That is my rough estimate, not a computed statistic. However, the current regime is unusually volatile: the S&P fell 8.6% and gold fell about 8–9% in the same week.

3. **Volatility-based reasoning (my own calculation, not a sourced figure).** Gold needs a relative log move of about 0.18–0.21 against the S&P. Assume the gold-minus-S&P spread has annualized volatility of 30–40% in the current stress regime, over about 0.105 years (27 trading days). That gives a one-standard-deviation move of roughly 10–13%. The required move is then 1.5–2 standard deviations, about a 2–7% one-tailed chance under normality, before allowing for fat tails or mean reversion.

   This year gold and stocks have sold off *together* because of forced liquidation (CNBC, Nov 20, 2026; FXStreet, Nov 18, 2026). That positive correlation lowers the spread volatility and makes a large relative move harder.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (gold finishes ahead of the S&P):**
- **AI and equity crash deepens while gold decouples (most plausible).** The S&P would fall roughly another 15–20% to about 5,600–5,800 while gold holds around $3,550–3,650. Example: gold at $3,610 (−16.8%) requires the S&P at or below about 5,693 (−16.8%), which is −17.6% from the Nov 20 close. This needs the forced-selling link between gold and stocks to break, with gold acting as a safe haven again.
- **Gold short squeeze or policy pivot with stocks flat.** Gold would need to rally about 20%, back to around $4,341+, with the S&P back near or below 6,845. A plausible trigger would be the Fed signaling it is done hiking, or a geopolitical or dollar shock. Gold did rally 29% in four weeks in January 2026, but that happened during a bull trend.
- **A mix of the two,** for example gold +10% to about $3,970 (−8.5% YTD) and the S&P −10% to about 6,260 (−8.5% YTD).

**Paths to NO:**
- **The S&P stabilizes or rebounds.** The bounce in futures, a Santa-rally seasonality, and dip-buying would keep the S&P near or above flat for the year. Gold cannot plausibly gain more than 20% in that case.
- **Correlated deleveraging.** If stocks keep falling but gold falls alongside them, as it did in Nov 2026 and in the early stages of 2008 and 2020, the gap stays wide.
- **Hawkish Fed, real yields above 5%, strong dollar.** This is a direct headwind for gold.
- **Bitcoin is very unlikely to matter.** It would need roughly a 50% rally while staying ahead of both other assets.

## 5. Signposts

**Would raise the odds of YES:**
- The S&P breaking below about 6,500, then 6,000. The gap closes about one-for-one as the S&P falls while gold holds.
- Gold rising *on* days when stocks fall, which would show the forced-selling correlation breaking and gold acting as a safe haven again.
- A dovish surprise: a soft PCE (Nov 25), a weak jobs report (Dec 4), or the Fed skipping or signaling the end of hikes on Dec 9.
- A sharp fall in the 10-year yield or the dollar.
- Credit or financial stress, such as margin-call cascades, fund failures or AI-debt defaults, that drives demand for safe havens.
- Central bank gold-buying headlines.

**Would lower the odds of YES:**
- The futures bounce holding and the S&P getting back above 7,200–7,500.
- Hot PCE or CPI readings, or a hawkish hike with guidance for more hikes.
- 10-year yields staying above 5%.
- Continued gold ETF outflows, or gold breaking below $3,500, the technical support level analysts flagged.
- Volatility compressing into the holidays. With less time left, a stable market means a sharply falling probability.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **Exact current levels.** Many sources were blogs or aggregators with slightly inconsistent figures, and I could not open the primary pages (they were blocked to me). Key examples:
  - Gold's Nov 20 close is given as $3,526, $3,535, $3,544 or $3,552, depending on spot vs. futures and the contract month.
  - BTC on Nov 21 is given as $57.8k or $61.2k. One weekly recap said BTC was above $80k, which looks stale or wrong.
- **The exact GC00 figure MarketWatch shows for Dec 31, 2025.** It may not exactly equal $4,341.10, depending on roll timing and whether it reflects settlement or the last trade.
- **The exact Binance BTC/USDT 00:00 ET Jan 1, 2026 candle.** It is estimated at about $87.4k–88.3k.
- **Monday Nov 23 cash closes.** These were not available. Only the Asia-session futures bounce was reported.
- **The current realized correlation and volatility of gold versus the S&P.** My 30–40% spread-volatility assumption is an estimate. A higher figure would materially raise the tail probability.
- **The cause of the Nvidia CEO resignation and the "Nvidia investigation."** How serious this turns out to be could determine whether the equity rout deepens.
