# Research Brief: Will Jonathan Taylor's next team be Green Bay before Dec 1, 2026?

**Bottom line:** It's November 10, 2026, and today is the NFL trade deadline (4 p.m. ET). This market comes down almost entirely to whether the Colts trade Taylor to the Packers in the next few hours. As of the latest results I found, no trade had been reported. Several insiders said the Colts were expected to keep him. Green Bay is one of roughly six interested teams.

**Research limits:** Every attempt to open a full article failed because the network proxy refused the connection. All facts below come from search-result summaries. I could not check them against the full articles or exact timestamps.

---

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Taylor's "next team" becomes the Green Bay Packers before Dec 1, 2026 (market closes 2026-12-01 04:59 UTC). The only realistic way is a Colts-to-Packers trade that the NFL makes official.
- **Fallback rules:** If Taylor is still with the team he was on when the market opened (Indianapolis) on Dec 1, the Colts outcome resolves YES. Retirement or having no team resolves "Retires / No Team." An extension or re-signing with his current team doesn't trigger early resolution.
- **Official confirmation required:** Early resolution needs the transaction to be official and reported by the NFL (the "Governing League").
- **No free-agency path:** Taylor signed a two-year, $44M extension in August 2026 ($39M guaranteed, up to $47M) and is under contract through 2028 (AP via Local10, Aug 6, 2026; NFL.com). The original three-year, $42M deal from Oct 2023, which would have ended after 2026, has been superseded.
- **The deadline is today:** The trade deadline is Tuesday, Nov 10, 2026, 4:00 p.m. ET (NFL 2026-27 important dates). After that, no trades are allowed until the new league year in March 2027. The Packers could then only get him if the Colts released him and Green Bay claimed him on waivers or signed him. With roughly $39M guaranteed, a release before Dec 1 is close to impossible.
- **Edge cases:**
  - A trade agreed just before 4 p.m. may be announced later. NFL rules require the paperwork to be filed before the deadline, and the official announcement can come that evening or the next day.
  - A three-team deal, or one where Taylor goes to Green Bay and is flipped, would follow the actual transaction record.
  - Kalshi's "next team" wording applies to a trade even though Taylor is not a free agent. The rule about free agents signing anywhere doesn't apply here.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Star RBs heavily rumored at the deadline.** These are my rough tallies from memory, not a systematic dataset:
   - Not traded: Taylor 2023, Derrick Henry 2023, Josh Jacobs 2023, Saquon Barkley 2022, Kareem Hunt 2022, Breece Hall 2025.
   - Traded: Christian McCaffrey 2022.
   - That's about 1 in 6–7, roughly 15–20%. When a team publicly asks for first-round value, the trade rate is lower still.
2. **"Team open to deal but insiders say it's likely staying" the day before the deadline.** These cases mostly end with no trade. My judgment is that 75–85% stay, but I couldn't find a systematic source.
3. **Share for one named suitor if a trade happens.** Reports name 5–6 aggressive suitors: Giants, Packers, Broncos, Seahawks, Bengals, Vikings (Jordan Schultz, via Lombardi Ave / Heavy, early Nov 2026). An even split gives the Packers about 15–20%. Adjust for:
   - **Lower:** Green Bay has no 2027 first-round pick to offer.
   - **Lower:** The Giants were the only team known to have made a concrete offer.
   - **Higher:** There is a specific, real motive (the Josh Jacobs situation, below).

  - Bill Huber (Packers On SI) said the interest is "real," on the table, and Green Bay was "prepared to make a strong offer."

  - Josh Jacobs was placed on the Commissioner's Exempt List after being charged with two misdemeanors tied to a May domestic incident. His first court date is Nov 17, and he can't play while on the list.

  - Other backs: MarShawn Lloyd, Chris Brooks, Kaleb Johnson.
- **Late Oct 2026:** ESPN (Schefter) and NFL Network (Rapoport) reported the Colts were willing to trade Taylor, with talks active. Taylor said he has not requested a trade and is "proud to be here" (ESPN).

- **2026 stats:** About 161 carries for 656 yards per the Colts' site (approximate). Taylor is 27.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (all must happen before 4 p.m. ET today, then be made official):**
- Green Bay beats the Giants' roughly 2nd + 5th offer with something like:
  - their 2027 2nd-round pick plus a player (e.g., Van Ness), or
  - a 2nd plus future picks (a 2028 1st is conceivable).
- The Colts accept under pressure to get value before an offseason market that is uncertain.
- The case depends on:
  - Green Bay's willingness to pay $22M/year through 2028 for an RB while Jacobs is still under contract (cap analysis says moving on from Jacobs would roughly offset Taylor's 2027 salary).
  - The Jacobs legal and exempt-list situation.
  - The new Colts front office deciding to sell.

**Paths to NO:**
- **(a) No trade (most likely per the latest reporting).** The Colts hold Taylor and revisit in the offseason, so the Colts outcome resolves YES on Dec 1.
- **(b) Trade to another team.** The Giants are the most concrete bidder, followed by the Vikings, Seahawks, Bengals, and Broncos.
- **(c) Packers decline.** They stick with Jacobs and Lloyd and won't meet the price.

**How it breaks down:** P(YES) ≈ P(trade today) × P(Green Bay | trade). Reporting points to P(trade) of roughly 20–40%. Green Bay's conditional share looks like roughly 15–30%. Post-deadline paths are about zero.

## 5. Signposts

- **Moves toward YES:**
  - Schefter, Rapoport, or Schultz reporting "Packers finalizing / closing in on Taylor" today.
  - Packers clearing cap space or a roster spot.
  - Jacobs released or a further league action against him.
  - The Giants re-entering talks without closing (shows the Colts are still dealing).
- **Moves toward NO:**
  - The deadline passes with no Taylor deal. If confirmed, this ends the market for practical purposes.
  - Any report of Taylor going to another team.
  - Reports that the Colts "are keeping Taylor."
  - The Packers making a cheaper RB trade (Yardbarker floated alternatives).
  - Huber or Schneidman reporting that Green Bay has backed out.
- **After the deadline:** Only a Colts release followed by Green Bay picking him up could matter, and that's extraordinarily unlikely given his guaranteed money.
