## 1. Resolution mechanics

**YES needs at least three of six conditions to occur within the window, by Dec 31, 2026 at 11:59 PM ET.** The six conditions:

1. **NVDA:** a closing price at least 50% below its all-time high close.
2. **SOXX:** a closing price at least 40% below its all-time high.
3. **OpenAI or Anthropic** declares bankruptcy.
4. **OpenAI is acquired.**
5. **H100 rental price** at or below $1.00 for five consecutive days on the SiliconData Silicon Index.
6. **"Major AI hardware supplier collapse":** TSM, ASML, AVGO, ANET or SMCI closes at least 50% below its all-time high. This is one bullet, so it should count as one condition however many names qualify.

**Resolution sources:** official company and exchange data, with "a consensus of credible reporting" as backup. The market can resolve early once three conditions are met.

**Where things stand (my reading of the evidence):**

- **Condition 6 is effectively met already.** SMCI closed at $21.85 on Nov 25, 2026 (Yahoo Finance historical data, Nov 25, 2026). Its all-time high close was about $118.8, split-adjusted, in March 2024. That figure is from my background knowledge; I could not re-verify it this session. SMCI is therefore about 82% below its peak. Its 52-week range alone is $19.48–$51.40 (Robinhood, Nov 25, 2026).
- **Condition 2 appears met as of Nov 20, 2026.**
  - SOXX closed at $123.40 on Nov 20 after a 10.55% drop (Finexus / 24/7 Wall St., Nov 20–24, 2026).
  - The split-adjusted record is about $218.3–218.65, set June 22, 2026. Unusual Whales gives a 52-week high of $218.65. Yahoo's table shows a June 22 close of $218.34. Reports of a ~$655.95 high are pre-split, and $655.95 ÷ 3 = $218.65, which points to a 3-for-1 split around early November 2026. I could not confirm the split directly.
  - On that basis SOXX was about 43.5% below its high on Nov 20 and about 41.2% below at $128.62 on Nov 24 (Unusual Whales, Nov 25, 2026).
  - The underlying SOX index was 42.5% below its June 22 record close of 14,634.72 on Nov 20 (historyofmarket.com).
  - SOXX bounced to $140.22 on Nov 23 (35.8% below the high) and traded around $119.53 intraday on Nov 25 (Finexus).
- **So YES now depends almost entirely on condition 1 (NVDA)** unless something unprecedented happens on conditions 3–5.
  - **NVDA's record close is unclear.** One report says $238.90 on Oct 5, 2026 (24/7 Wall St.). Another gives a "52-week high" of $243.37 (HeyGoTrade, Nov 26, 2026), which may be an intraday figure or a different close.
  - **The 50% threshold is therefore about $119.45 to $121.69.**
  - NVDA closed at $149.30 on Nov 25. It needs to fall roughly another 18.5–20% to a closing low.
  - Its lowest close so far was $125.53 on Nov 20, about 47.5–48.4% below the peak. It came within about 2.5 points of qualifying.

**Edge cases and ambiguities:**

- **"Within 90 days of this market's specified timeframe" is unclear.** It could mean all three conditions within a 90-day span, or within 90 days of the deadline. Either way, the Nov 20 SOXX reading and SMCI's ongoing drawdown fall inside the window.
- **SMCI's main fall happened in 2024.** A strict resolver could argue the "event" occurred outside the window. Every daily close in the window still satisfies the test, so I think this risk is low, but it is not zero.
- **Split adjustment for SOXX.** A careless resolver comparing $656 with $123 would only reach YES faster, so this cuts toward YES, not against it.
- **Data conflicts.** Sources disagree on NVDA's peak close and on some intraday-versus-close figures. Forecasters should treat the exact threshold as uncertain by about ±$2.
- **Time left:** Nov 27 (a half day) plus about 22 trading days in December (Dec 25 is a holiday), roughly 23 sessions in total.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. NVDA drawdowns of 50% or more from a peak (since 2000).** There have been about four episodes: 2000–02, 2008, 2018–19 (about −56%) and 2022 (about −66%). Two lessons follow.

- Once NVDA is about 45–50% down in a semiconductor bear market, it has usually gone on to break −50% (2002, 2008, 2022). In 2018 it went to about −56%.
- The pace was usually slower than five weeks. In 2018 the stock fell from about −35% to −56% in roughly six weeks (October to late November 2018). That is the closest precedent for the speed needed now.

**B. One-month NVDA declines of 20% or more.** Using my recollection of history, NVDA has had roughly 10–15 calendar months since 2000 with closing declines of at least 20% (for example Oct 2008, Nov 2018, several months in 2002 and 2022). That is about 4–6% of all months, but the rate is far higher in high-volatility regimes.

The current regime is extreme. Daily moves include −4.4% (Nov 12), −4.1% (Nov 16), −2.9% (Nov 18), about −14% (Nov 20 as reported, though a Motley Fool piece describes 14% as the cumulative fall since the resignation), +31.5% (Nov 23, the third-best day on record), −5.9% (Nov 24) and −3.9% (Nov 25). That implies realized volatility well above 80–100% annualized.

**C. A simple barrier model (illustrative, my own calculation).** The question is the probability that NVDA touches a level about 20% below $149.30 on a closing basis within about 23 sessions, using a reflection approximation.

| Annualized vol | Approx. probability of a touch |
|---|---|
| 60% | ~20% |
| 80% | ~33% |
| 100% | ~40% |

Closing-only observation and the half-day trim these slightly. Fat tails from news-driven jumps (Huang scandal, credit events) push them up.

**D. Semiconductor bear markets of 40% or more.** These happened in 2000–02, 2008, 2022 and now 2026. 24/7 Wall St. (Nov 17, 2026) called this the worst two months for chips since 2008. In 2008 the SOX kept falling for several more months after its worst two-month stretch. In 2022 the bottom came near −45% and stayed there for about two months.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (NVDA closes at or below ~$119.5–121.7 by Dec 31):**

- **Credit contagion.** A CoreWeave or another neocloud default, a distressed exchange or a failed financing, or Oracle's CDS widening further, would hit NVDA through the customer and backstop-financing channel. CDS markets are already pricing distress.
- **Further Huang fallout.** Possibilities include congressional testimony, espionage revelations, an SEC or DOJ probe, or the board or a regulator reversing his reinstatement. The Nov 12–20 drawdown shows this channel alone can move the stock 20% or more.
- **Weaker customer signals.** More hyperscaler capex cuts (like the Google–Broadcom report), weak Broadcom guidance on Dec 9, or bad Oracle or Micron commentary.
- **Macro.** Hot inflation leading to a hawkish Fed, tariff escalation, or China export curbs.
- **The rebound fades.** The 31% Huang-return rally has already lost about 10% in two sessions ($165.05 to $149.30).

**Paths to NO:**

- The Huang return stabilizes sentiment, and solid fundamentals take over: an earnings beat, Q4 guidance above consensus, H100 rents up about 60% year over year, and a record AI backlog at Dell.
- A December or Santa Claus rally, or a Fed cut, lifts risk assets.
- Not enough time: only about 23 sessions remain, and holiday trading is thin.
- Resolution technicalities: the NVDA peak gets defined as the higher $243.37, which lowers the threshold to ~$121.69 and actually makes a hit slightly easier; or the SMCI and SOXX conditions get challenged, which seems unlikely.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **NVDA's exact record close:** $238.90 (Oct 5) or a higher close near $243.37. This moves the threshold between about $119.45 and $121.69.
- **Nov 20 data:** whether the NVDA close of $125.53 and the SOXX close of $123.40 are exact. Sources conflict on intraday versus closing figures and on whether the ~14% NVDA fall was one day or cumulative. The arithmetic ($125.53 × 1.3148 = $165.05) supports $125.53 as a close.
- **SOXX's split-adjusted peak:** I inferred a 3-for-1 split around early November 2026 but could not confirm it. The drawdown figures are consistent across several sources, about 41–43.5% at the Nov 20 and Nov 24 closes.
- **SMCI's all-time high:** I relied on background knowledge (about $118.8 split-adjusted, March 2024) and could not re-verify it. With SMCI at about $22, the conclusion holds even if the peak were as low as $44.
- **The "within 90 days" clause** and whether a resolver treats SMCI's long-running drawdown as an event occurring inside the window.
- **NVDA implied volatility:** I did not look up option prices. The barrier estimates use realized-volatility assumptions only.
- **Timing of the December catalysts.** Only Broadcom's Dec 9 date was confirmed. The Oracle, Micron and FOMC dates and the Huang testimony date are unconfirmed.
- **The SiliconData page itself** shows inconsistent figures ($3.10 in the table versus $2.53 in its FAQ). This does not matter for resolution, since both are far above $1.
