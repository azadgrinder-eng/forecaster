# Research brief: Will Zohran Mamdani say "childcare" or "child care" at the 2026 NYT DealBook Summit (Nov 18, 2026)?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Mamdani himself must audibly say "childcare" or "child care" during his own appearance at the 2026 DealBook Summit. Because of the slash rule, either spelling or form counts. Plurals and possessives also count ("child care's", "childcares"). Words that merely contain the term count when the term appears verbatim inside them, such as "universal childcare", "childcare workers" and "Child Care Trust Fund".
- **What does not count:**
  - The words said by the moderator (Andrew Ross Sorkin or anyone else), shown on slides, or said by other speakers.
  - Synonyms and program names: "day care", "2-K", "3-K", "2-Care", "pre-K", "early childhood education".
  - Grammatical inflections other than plurals and possessives, and anything caught on a hot mic.
- **Who decides and how:** Kalshi staff watch the original NYT broadcast, stream or official video. If they can't reach consensus from video, they use official transcripts.
- **Timing:** The summit is scheduled for Wednesday, Nov 18, 2026, in New York. The market closes Dec 2, 2026, 15:00 UTC.
- **Edge cases:**
  - If Mamdani's session is cancelled, or he doesn't appear in an official capacity, "Event does not qualify" resolves YES and this market resolves NO.
  - If the event is postponed, the announcement comes by the end of the next day, and the new date is within 14 days, the market stays open.
  - It is unclear whether a separate, off-stage recorded clip released by the NYT would count. The rules exclude "recordings captured at… events wholly separate from the featured address."
  - Mamdani usually says "child care" as two words, which still counts.

   - Fox 5 "Politics Unusual" and a Fox 5 one-on-one.
   - ABC7, CBS and Brian Lehrer/WNYC interviews.

   This sample is biased toward childcare-themed pieces, so I can't give an unbiased frequency. My rough estimate is that he says the word in about 80–90%+ of general-topic interviews longer than 20 minutes. He routinely lists his agenda as "child care, housing, transit," and his administration uses the same framing: Deputy Mayor Julie Su has said "Our priorities — child care, housing, accessible public transit, and more — are economic policy."
2. **Politicians interviewed at DealBook.** Sessions typically run about 30–45 minutes with Sorkin and cover the guest's signature policies, their relationship with business and taxes, Trump, and the 2028 race. The 2025 Newsom session and earlier Adams and Hochul-style sessions are examples. A signature policy that a politician actively campaigns on almost always gets named in that format. Universal childcare is Mamdani's most-delivered signature policy.
3. **Sorkin's own prior. This is the strongest single datapoint.** In his only known prior one-on-one with Mamdani (Squawk Box, Jan 2026), Sorkin volunteered childcare as a business-retention issue. That suggests he sees it as a business topic and may raise it again (NYC.gov transcript, Jan 28, 2026).

- Sorkin asks about taxing the rich or the business climate, and Mamdani justifies the tax by citing universal childcare. This is his standard framing.
- Sorkin asks about his record or accomplishments. Childcare (2-K, 3-K, the voucher waitlist) is his headline achievement.
- Sorkin repeats his January framing about childcare and worker retention.
- Mamdani gives his usual "affordability" list ("rent, child care, buses"). He has many chances across 30 minutes or more.

**Paths to NO**
- The session is short, or dominated by 2028, Trump and the federal government, Israel/Gaza, policing, COPA and real estate, AI, or crypto and prediction markets (Polymarket's Coplan is also on the lineup).
- Mamdani names programs ("2-K," "3-K," "pre-K," "2-Care") or says "day care" or "early childhood education" without saying "child care."
