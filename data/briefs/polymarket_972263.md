# Research brief: Will Kristi Noem leave the Trump administration before 2027?

*Prepared 2026-11-21. Every web-page fetch was blocked by the proxy, so the facts below come from search-result summaries and headlines, not full articles. Treat details that rest on anonymous sources or tabloid outlets with caution.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** By Dec 31, 2026, 11:59 PM ET, either an announcement says Noem will leave the administration, or she otherwise stops being a member of it. An announced resignation or removal resolves YES immediately, even if it takes effect later.
- **Who counts as "in the administration":** People formally appointed by Trump to federal roles, including Cabinet members, EOP staff, senior advisors, ambassadors, and White House staff whose appointments were publicly announced through official channels.
- **Who decides:** Official administration information is the main source. A "consensus of credible reporting" can also be used.
- **Time left:** About 40 days (Nov 21 – Dec 31, 2026), including the holiday period.

**Edge cases and ambiguities that matter here:**

- **Moves between roles have not counted as leaving so far.** Noem was fired as DHS Secretary in March 2026 and immediately named Special Envoy for the Shield of the Americas. That envoy post expired in mid-October 2026, and she became a "senior adviser" to Deputy Secretary of State Christopher Landau. The question is still open, so the resolver apparently treated neither move as leaving. Another internal reassignment would most likely not resolve YES, while a full exit from government would.
- **Is a State Department "senior adviser" a Trump appointee?** The State Department likely appointed her to this role, not Trump directly. It was also announced through a statement that Noem posted herself on what the department says was fabricated letterhead, though spokesman Tommy Pigott did confirm the role. Someone could argue she already stopped being a "Trump-appointed" member in October. The market evidently has not taken that view, but it remains a tail risk.
- **A quiet exit might not be clearly announced.** For a low-profile adviser, departure could come through leaks ("sources say she has left"), which raises the question of whether "credible reporting" is enough. Media interest in Noem is very high (the October letterhead story was everywhere), so an exit would probably be reported quickly.
- **Possible time limit on her appointment.** Noem said the envoy job was "always a six-month appointment." If the senior adviser role is also time-limited (for example a special-government-employee or limited-term appointment), it could lapse. A second six-month term starting mid-October would end around April 2027, after the close date. I could not establish the appointment type.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Post-midterm turnover in Trump's first term (Nov 7 – Dec 31, 2018).** In roughly the same window after the 2018 midterms, these top officials announced departures:
- Attorney General Sessions (fired Nov 7)
- Chief of Staff John Kelly (departure announced Dec 8)
- Interior Secretary Zinke (Dec 15)
- Defense Secretary Mattis (Dec 20)

UN Ambassador Haley had announced her exit just before, on Oct 9. That is about 4 of roughly 24 cabinet-level officials, or about 15–17%, in about 8 weeks. Scaled to a 6-week window, that is roughly 11–13% for a typical senior official. The 2026 midterms went badly for Republicans, which fits this reshuffle pattern.

**B. Senior officials who have been sidelined or demoted.** People parked in vague roles after a public firing usually leave within months, but Noem has already lasted about 8 months (March–November 2026) across two parked roles.
- In April 2026, a State Department insider told the Daily Mail "the understanding is that she's not going to be here for much longer." She is still there seven months later.
- If you put a constant monthly exit risk of 5–10% on someone in her position, about 1.3 months remaining gives roughly 6–13%.

**C. General monthly turnover for Trump appointees.** Trump's first term had record turnover: about 65% "A-Team" turnover over 4 years per Brookings tracking (my own figure, not searched), or roughly 1.5–2% per month for a typical senior official. Noem is clearly higher-risk than average, so this is a floor of about 2–3% for the window.

Taken together, the reference classes point to roughly a 5–15% base range before case-specific adjustments.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES:**
1. **She is pushed out after the midterms or for indiscipline (most likely YES path).** Her attacks on Mullin and the letterhead episode give the White House or Rubio's State Department reasons to drop her. Dropping a parked, low-value adviser costs Trump almost nothing. This depends on whether Miller, Rubio or Trump take offense at her public criticism.
2. **She leaves voluntarily.** She already has private work (NovaRed), possible media or book deals, and a divorce underway. She might also decide to cleanly separate before 2027 politics: a 2028 race or a SD governor/House race, or positioning against John Thune. A year-end exit is a natural timing point.
3. **Investigations escalate.** If the IG report or the DOJ probe of Lewandowski expands to her or produces damaging findings, she becomes a liability.
4. **The role quietly lapses and the press reports it.** For example, a time-limited appointment ends or she is reported to have "left the department."

**Paths to NO:**
1. **Inertia.** Trump has twice chosen to keep her inside rather than cut her loose, possibly to keep her loyal and, as one report suggests, out of a Senate race. A low-cost sinecure can last indefinitely. Only about 40 days remain, many of them holidays.
2. **No clean public trigger.** Even if she has effectively disengaged, no announcement or credible reporting emerges before Dec 31.
3. **Another reassignment within the administration,** which by precedent would not count as leaving.

## 5. Signposts

**Toward YES (large moves):**
- reports that State or the White House is "reviewing" her role, or that Rubio or Landau want her out
- Trump or Miller publicly rebuking her Mullin comments
- the IG or DOJ naming her in the investigation
- a new private-sector, media or campaign role announced
- a filing to run for office
- her X bio or State Department listings removing the government title
- broader December Cabinet reshuffles, which would show turnover appetite.

**Toward NO:**
- a State Department reaffirmation of her role
- her appearing at official Shield of the Americas events in December
- reports that the adviser appointment runs into 2027
- quiet weeks with no new controversy going into the holidays.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **Appointment type and term length of the senior adviser role** (special government employee, limited-term, Schedule C, or another type). I could not establish this, and it is the biggest single unknown about whether the role could simply lapse.
- **Whether she is still in the role as of Nov 21.** The latest confirmation found is from mid-to-late October. November coverage calls her "former DHS Secretary" and does not confirm her current State Department status. One search summary said her "government role has ended," but it seemed to be confusing this with the envoy post ending, and no source backed it up.
- **Whether the resolver treats a State Department "senior adviser" as a Trump-appointed member.** If not, the market could arguably resolve YES on technical grounds, although the open market suggests not.
- **The White House reaction to her attacks on Mullin.** None was found.
- **The status and timing of the IG and DOJ investigations.**
