# Research brief: Will Ousainou Darboe win the 2026 Gambian presidential election?

*Prepared 17 Nov 2026. The election is on 5 Dec 2026 and the market closes 5 Dec 2026 at 23:59 UTC.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Ousainou Darboe of the United Democratic Party (UDP) must win the 2026 presidential election, based on a consensus of credible reporting. If that is ambiguous, the official results from the Independent Electoral Commission (IEC) decide. An interim or caretaker president does not count.
- **"Other" fallback:** The market resolves to "Other" if results are not known definitively by 31 Dec 2027.
- **Electoral system:** The winner is whoever gets the most valid votes in a single round. There is no runoff and no 50% threshold; constitutional changes made around 2003 removed the second round (Rio Times / Point coverage of the election schedule, Oct 2025–Nov 2026). This matters a lot here. With a split opposition, Barrow can win with about 35–40%. It also means Darboe could win with a similar share if opposition votes move to him.
- **Who declares:** The IEC chairman, Joseph Colley, is the returning officer and announces the result. In 2021, voting was on 4 Dec and the winner was declared on 5 Dec, so a result on 6–7 Dec 2026 is likely. Disputes go to the Supreme Court. In 2021 the UDP filed a challenge that was dismissed, so a court petition is unlikely to change the winner.
- **Edge cases:**
  - Another approved candidate, **Lamin J. Darbo**, has a very similar name. Check that any reported result refers to Ousainou Darboe of the UDP.
  - Darboe's campaign material says "GFC/UDP-Led Alliance" (Gambia First Coalition). That is the same candidacy.
  - The UDP website countdown shows 4 Dec 2026. The IEC and Reuters say 5 Dec.
  - Darboe is 78. If he died or withdrew, the answer would be NO.
  - Postponement or a constitutional crisis is a small risk (see "Other" above).

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Incumbents in Gambian presidential elections:** The incumbent won 8 of 9 elections from 1982 to 2021: Jawara in 1982, 1987 and 1992; Jammeh in 1996, 2001, 2006 and 2011; Barrow in 2021. The only loss was Jammeh in 2016, against a united opposition coalition. That puts the base rate for any challenger at about 11%, before splitting it across several challengers. (Compiled from my background knowledge of Gambian election history and the 2021 Wikipedia page.)
2. **Darboe's own record:** He has run five times and lost every time. His vote shares were about 35.8% (1996), 32.7% (2001), 26.7% (2006), 17.4% (2011) and 27.7% (2021, when Barrow got 53.2% and Mama Kandeh 12.3%) (IEC figures as reported, 2021). He did not run in 2016 because he was in jail. Of challengers who lost five times, very few win on a sixth try. Counterexamples in Africa exist: Wade (Senegal, 2000, after four losses), Hichilema (Zambia, 2021, after five), and Mahama (Ghana, 2024, a comeback). All three won when the economy was poor and opposition votes consolidated behind them.
3. **Sub-Saharan African incumbents running again:** Incumbents have historically won about 85–90% of the elections they contest (Cheeseman-type datasets, from background knowledge). Since 2020 incumbents or ruling parties have lost more often: Zambia 2021, Malawi 2020 and 2025, Senegal 2024, Botswana 2024, Mauritius 2024, Ghana 2024. A reasonable recent incumbent win rate is about 65–75%.

These point to Barrow being the favourite. Darboe is probably the most likely of the challengers, but only by a little over Bensouda.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **16 Nov 2026:** The official campaign started and runs until 3 Dec. The IEC and International IDEA held a peace-pact signing (VON; Reuters via TimesLive/US News, 16 Nov 2026).
- **14–16 Nov 2026:** The IEC approved **nine candidates**: Adama Barrow (NPP, seeking a third term, legal because the constitution has no term limits), Ousainou Darboe (UDP), Talib Ahmed Bensouda (UNITE / Coalition 2026), Isatou Touray (the only woman), Ansu Sanneh, Ebrima Tabora Manneh, Modou Lamin Sima, Lamin J. Darbo and Almameh Gibba. Reuters calls Darboe "widely expected to be Barrow's main challenger" (Reuters via US News/TimesLive, 16 Nov 2026; Voice Gambia, Nov 2026).
- **12 Nov 2026:** The IEC rejected nomination papers from Bakusa Dukureh (Gambia Labour Party) and Kebba Bojang (National Democratic Party) (Africanews, 12 Nov 2026).
- **About 9–10 Nov 2026:** Darboe filed his nomination with thousands of supporters present. He says this is his **last** presidential run (VON; WAVN; Gambia Journal, Nov 2026).
- **Early Nov 2026:** The UDP-led Gambia First Coalition launched its manifesto, "The Gambia We Want." Its four "missions" are economic, constitutional, security and civil-service reform. Promises for the first 100 days include a 20% pay cut for the president (The Point, Nov 2026).
- **2–3 Nov 2026:** The IEC reported **1,146,474 registered voters**, 53 constituencies and 1,773 polling stations. The 2026 supplementary drive added 212,095 voters. 14,334 duplicate registrations were deleted, and more than 24,000 were flagged as suspected. The UDP disputed the figures, saying the dataset it received was 32,650 names short (AllAfrica, 3 Nov 2026; The Point, 4 and 11 Nov 2026). There is no confirmed diaspora voting in 2026.
- **About 6–7 Nov 2026:** Bensouda resigned as mayor of Kanifing and filed his candidacy. Late in October the government accused him of financial impropriety, and there was speculation he would be disqualified, but he was cleared to run (Africanews, 12 Nov 2026; Guardian Nigeria, Nov 2026).
- **Oct 2026:** A large opposition rally in Fajikunda (Kanifing) was rained out before Darboe could speak (Kerr Fatou, Oct 2026). Darboe has said opposition unity is "still possible," rejected concerns about his age, and **insists the UDP must lead any coalition**. He has refused to step aside for Bensouda (Kerr Fatou; Gambia Journal, Sep–Oct 2026).
- **Sep 2026:** Long power cuts led to anti-government protests (Reuters, 16 Nov 2026). Essa Faal's All Peoples Party–Sobeyaa reached an "agreement in principle" to ally with Barrow's NPP. I could not confirm that it was finalised (The Point, Sep 2026).
- **Aug–Sep 2026:** The opposition Coalition 2026 picked Bensouda as its flag-bearer in a disputed process. Faal walked out beforehand, alleging irregularities and inducements (Standard, 2026). Bensouda says he will never rejoin the UDP but is open to cooperating with it (Kerr Fatou, 2026).
- **27 Jul–10 Aug 2026 (Gambia Participates / CepRass Round 2, n=1,556, all 53 constituencies):**
  - Vote intention: **Barrow 32%, Darboe 25%, Bensouda 19%, undecided 17%**.
  - Who has the best chance of winning: Barrow 51% (up from 31% in Round 1), Darboe 23%, Bensouda 12%.
  - Regional pattern reported: Barrow strongest in the Upper and Central River regions (Basse), Darboe in Lower River and North Bank (Mansakonko, perhaps Kerewan), Bensouda in urban Kanifing and the West Coast / Brikama.
  - Parties disputed the findings, and critics questioned the sampling (Alkamba Times; Standard; The Point; Voice Gambia, Aug 2026).
  - An earlier round found 55% opposed Barrow running for a third term (Standard, 2025).
- **Late Jul 2026:** Mama Kandeh's GDC, which won 12.3% in 2021, formed an **alliance with Barrow's NPP**. The APRC also renewed its alliance with the NPP (Wikipedia: Mama Kandeh; The Point, 2026). Former National Assembly members from several parties endorsed Darboe on 11 Aug 2026 (Jollof News, Aug 2026).
- **20 Jun 2026:** The NPP launched its nine-point manifesto (The Point / AllAfrica, Jun 2026).
- **Scheduled before the close date:** The campaign ends 3 Dec, voting is 5 Dec, and provisional or final results are likely on 6–7 Dec. That may be **after** the market close on 5 Dec at 23:59 UTC, but resolution depends on the result, not the close date. National Assembly elections follow on 10 Apr 2027.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (Darboe wins on plurality):**
- **Anti-incumbent wave:** Power cuts, cost of living and resentment of a third term (55% opposed) turn into votes. Darboe gets most of the 17% undecided and consolidates the Mandinka and Lower River / North Bank vote, getting to around 38–42%.
- **Bensouda fades:** Some Bensouda voters see him as unable to win and switch to Darboe as the strongest challenger. The Standard modelled a scenario in which a move from Bensouda to Darboe gives Darboe 42% and Barrow 39%.
- **Late deal:** Bensouda withdraws or endorses Darboe before 5 Dec. This looks unlikely given their public hostility and Darboe's insistence that the UDP lead.
- **Barrow's coalition underperforms:** The GDC, APRC and APP alliance votes do not transfer, especially in the Foni area or among Kandeh's Fula base.

**Paths to NO:**
- **Barrow wins the split field (most likely):** The Darboe and Bensouda votes divide the opposition, especially in Kanifing and West Coast, the regions with the most voters. Barrow's incumbency advantages (state resources, rural development record) and his alliances with GDC, APRC and probably APP, which together had well over 12% in 2021, carry him to roughly 40–50%.
- **Bensouda overtakes Darboe** as the main challenger with younger urban voters and wins. This is less likely, but the polls put him only 6 points behind Darboe.
- **Smaller paths:** Darboe's health at 78, postponement or disruption, or a contested result.

**Arithmetic:** In 2021 Barrow (53%) beat the combined Darboe + Kandeh share (40%), and Kandeh has since moved to Barrow's side. For Darboe to win he needs to do much better than his best result since 2001 and also benefit from opposition voters consolidating behind him.

## 5. Signposts

- **Opposition withdrawals or endorsements** (Bensouda, Touray or minor candidates) before 3 Dec. If Bensouda backed Darboe, this would move strongly toward YES.
- **Any new poll in Nov 2026.** A Darboe lead or tie would move toward YES. Bensouda overtaking Darboe would move strongly toward NO.
- **Rally turnout and defections** in Kanifing, West Coast and Brikama, the vote-rich regions. Upper River and Central River crossovers from the NPP would also matter.
- **Whether Faal's APP deal with the NPP was finalised,** and whether GDC voters (Kandeh's base in Upper River and Fula communities) follow the alliance.
- **Disputes over the voter register, IEC trust and violence,** which could signal a contested result.
- **On or after 5 Dec:** results come in by constituency. Darboe needs large margins in Lower River, North Bank and West Coast, and to be competitive in Kanifing.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not read the full poll reports or the regional tables. The poll's methodology is disputed, and Gambian polling has no established track record. I found no polling accuracy figure for 2021.
- I found no polls after August 2026, so the effect of the September protests and the nominations is unmeasured.
- I could not confirm the parties and alliances of the minor candidates, or whether Lamin J. Darbo (NUP) stayed allied with UNITE.
- I could not confirm the final status of the Faal–NPP alliance, or the details and impact of the government's accusations against Bensouda.
- I could not confirm the exact timing of results. It may be after the 5 Dec close.
- Darboe's health is not publicly documented.
- Several web pages could not be fetched directly. Some details come only from search-result summaries, and a few dates (e.g. nomination windows) were inconsistent across outlets.
