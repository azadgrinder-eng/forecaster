# Research Brief: Will the US announce the end of its Iran naval blockade by Nov 15, 2026?

**Bottom line:** As of Nov 5–6, 2026 the blockade is still in force. It ended once before, and only when a signed deal was announced. A Qatari draft that would lift it on day one is in circulation, but Iran says the two sides are "far apart." Trump says a decision between new strikes, a deal, or keeping the blockade is coming "soon." YES needs a deal or a sudden reversal within about 9–10 days.

*Method note: page fetches failed (the proxy refused every connection), so this brief relies on search-result summaries of the cited articles. Exact wording and some dates could not be checked against the full texts.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** The US government, or an authorized official, publicly and officially announces that the naval blockade on Iranian ships and ships of Iranian customers has ended, been lifted, or been suspended.
  - The announcement must fall between market creation (around July 13, 2026) and **Nov 15, 2026, 11:59 PM ET**.
  - Valid sources include the President, the Department of Defense (DoD), the Department of State, CENTCOM and official representatives.
- **Must be present and decided.** A statement that the blockade "will be lifted when the deal is signed" or "once Iran does X" is conditional and does **not** count.
  - A declared decision to lift it does count, e.g. "I have authorized the immediate removal…"
  - So does announcing a deal in which the US lifts the blockade, if the wording states a decided action.
- **Partial changes don't count.** Exemptions for specific vessels, cargoes or ports are not enough. Leftover restrictions such as Hormuz fees are fine, as long as blockading generally stops.
- **Precedent for what would qualify.** The June episode had two steps:
  - Mid-June: Trump said he authorized "the immediate removal of the United States Naval blockade" and that ships could start "heading home!" (Task & Purpose; Rudaw, June 2026).
  - About four days later, on June 18, CENTCOM said: "US forces, under the President's order, have lifted the blockade on all inbound and outbound maritime traffic to Iranian ports" (Al Jazeera, June 18, 2026; gCaptain).
  - Either statement would likely have qualified. A Truth Social post by the President is probably "official."
- **Edge cases:**
  - A two-step Qatari plan announced as "Day 1: US lifts blockade" with a future date might be read as prospective.
  - Remarks Trump makes informally in an interview may not count.
  - A YES stands even if the blockade is later reversed.
  - The resolution text gives no named arbiter. It only says resolution will be based on official US government information.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Lifting announcements per day of blockade, 2026.**
   - Blockade 1 ran from April 13 to about mid-June, roughly 62–66 days. One Wikipedia snippet instead gives April 13 to May 29; that conflicts with June 18 CENTCOM reporting.
   - Blockade 2 has run from July 14 to Nov 6, about 115 days.
   - That is one lifting announcement in about 180 blockade-days, or roughly **5% per 10-day window** (my calculation).
2. **Time from "deal is close" signals to an actual lifting.**
   - In blockade 1, Trump said the war was "very close to over" as early as April 15 (Fortune, Apr 15, 2026).
   - There were further near-misses in May: strikes were paused after Gulf states said a deal could be imminent (Axios, May 19, 2026).
   - The memorandum of understanding (MOU) and the lifting only came in mid-June, about two months later.
   - So most "close to a deal" moments did **not** produce a lifting within 10 days. Roughly 1 in 4–6 such episodes did.
3. **Trump's stated rule.** "The blockade ends as soon as the agreement is signed." He said he would not lift it before a deal (Hellenic Shipping News / Insurance Journal, April 2026).
   - In June he followed this exactly: the lifting came with the signed MOU.
   - So P(YES) ≈ P(some signed deal, even an interim confidence-building one, by Nov 15).

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 5–6:** Iran reportedly exempts ships from India, China, Iraq and Qatar from its Hormuz restrictions (Al Jazeera, Nov 6).
- **Nov 5:** Trump met his national security team for the first time since the midterms and said a decision on resuming major strikes is coming "soon" (Axios, Nov 5).
  - His options: new strikes, continued economic pressure through the blockade and sanctions, or a deal with "significant concessions."
  - CENTCOM is finalizing plans to resume major combat but has no new directive (Fox News live, Nov 5).
  - The IRGC claims two tankers and a tug caught fire from mines near Hormuz (House of Saud, Nov 5; CBS).
- **Nov 5 (internal split):** VP Vance leads the advisers pushing for a deal. CIA Director Ratcliffe and Treasury Secretary Bessent reportedly favor keeping the blockade as leverage (GlobalSecurity daily update, Nov 5; Gulf News).
  - The New York Times reports Iran is weighing specific nuclear proposals.
- **The Qatari draft:** Day 1, the US lifts the naval blockade and releases some frozen funds. In return, Iran invites IAEA inspectors to bombed sites (Axios; JPost/JFeed, early Nov).
  - Gulf News describes a three-day sequence rather than two.
  - Iran's spokesman says Iran is "not yet discussing" the conditions for inspections.
- **Nov 4:** Iran called the US reply to its Hormuz plan "unconvincing." It insists on seven conditions: end the blockade, an oil-sanctions waiver, cancel new sanctions, unfreeze funds, and others (Al Jazeera, Nov 4).
- **Nov 3:**
  - US strikes hit an anti-aircraft site on Qeshm Island, which Trump confirmed.
  - Trump told the Financial Times he had no immediate plans for new strikes (CBS).
  - A senior Iranian official said the two sides are "still far apart." Iranian negotiators returned from Doha, saying their "work is done" (Sunday Times/Reuters, Nov 3).
  - Vance said "good conversations" had been held (AP, Nov 3).
  - US midterms: Democrats reportedly won both chambers, which take their seats Jan 3, 2027 (House of Saud). Brent fell to about $93 after the vote (Gulf News).
- **Nov 2:** Trump signaled progress, and Iran pushed an accelerated two-day plan (Al Jazeera, Nov 2; Nation Thailand).
- **Oct 25–31:**
  - Vance sent a letter through Qatar offering direct talks that would include nuclear issues (Al Jazeera, Oct 30).
  - Qatari mediators held marathon talks in Tehran aiming for a deal before the midterms. That deadline was missed (Axios, Oct 31).
- **Oct 16:** CENTCOM said the blockade had halted about $25B in Iranian crude exports, and that 135 ships had been turned around.
- **July 13–14:** The 60-day ceasefire from the June MOU collapsed on July 8. Trump reinstated the blockade along with a 20% "fee" on Hormuz cargo, and CENTCOM enforcement resumed July 14 (USNI; Israel Hayom, July 13).
- **No interim lifting since July 14 was found.**
- **Scheduled before Nov 15:** No fixed meetings found. Qatar said feedback from both sides was expected "within 36 hours" and that direct talks could restart "within days." Three US carriers are in the region.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

- **YES paths:**
  - (a) Trump accepts the Qatari sequencing. Lifting the blockade is the US "Day 1" step, so a deal would bring an almost immediate announcement.
  - (b) A Trump-style sudden "deal" announcement on Truth Social, as in June.
  - These depend on:
    - Iran agreeing to the IAEA invitation or nuclear terms.
    - Vance winning out over Ratcliffe and Bessent.
    - Trump wanting a win after the midterms or lower oil prices.
    - Israel not derailing the deal; Netanyahu pressed for strikes (AP, Nov 3).
- **NO paths:**
  - (a) Resumed major strikes. CENTCOM is ready, and Trump has said he may "finish them off."
  - (b) Continued stalemate with the blockade as leverage, which Trump has called "more effective than bombing."
  - (c) A deal is reached but only announced as conditional or future-dated, or it slips past Nov 15.
- **Structural headwinds:**
  - Iran's demands are broad: sanctions, funds and Lebanon.
  - Iran calls the US offer "unconvincing."
  - The time window is short.

## 5. Signposts

- **Toward YES:**
  - Announcement of direct US–Iran talks led by Vance, or a signing venue.
  - Iran formally invites the IAEA.
  - Qatar announces both sides have accepted the draft.
  - Trump posts that he is "authorizing" an end to the blockade.
  - CENTCOM orders ships to stand down.
- **Toward NO:**
  - Resumed large-scale US strikes, which are likely imminent given CENTCOM's readiness.
  - New ship seizures.
  - Iran attacks or mines tankers.
  - Iran publicly rejects the IAEA step.
  - Trump says the blockade stays "until a deal is signed" without a timeline.

## 6. Uncertainties

- Whether anything happened on Nov 6 itself, including the outcome of Qatar's "36-hour" feedback.
- The exact content of the latest US reply and Iran's seven conditions.
- How a two-step deal announcement would be worded, which decides whether it counts as "present and decided."
- The exact start and end dates of blockade 1 (sources conflict), and whether there were any partial suspensions between July and November.
- Whether the midterm results (from a lower-reliability source) change Trump's calculation.
- How reliable the House of Saud and GlobalSecurity summaries are.
