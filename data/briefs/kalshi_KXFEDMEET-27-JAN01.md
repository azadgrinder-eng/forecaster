# Research brief: Will the FOMC hold an unscheduled ("emergency") meeting before Jan 1, 2027?

*Prepared 2026-11-27. About 34 days remain before close (2027-01-01 04:59 UTC, which is 11:59 pm ET on Dec 31, 2026). The proxy blocked direct fetches of federalreserve.gov, CNBC and Axios, so this brief relies on search-result extracts and on background knowledge of Fed history, which is flagged where used.*

---

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** the FOMC holds a meeting that is not on its published schedule, after the market was issued and before Jan 1, 2027. I don't know the issuance date. Because the market is still open on Nov 27, the forecaster should assume nothing qualifying has been recognized so far.
- **Scheduled meetings that do not count:** the only scheduled meeting left in 2026 is **Dec 8–9** (decision and press conference Dec 9; it includes a Summary of Economic Projections) (Fed calendar, "December 2026"; fedgazette.com). One outlet (Invezz, Nov 26) says "December 15–16". That is an error: the Fed's tentative 2025–26 schedule, released Aug 9, 2024, and most other sources say Dec 8–9. There is no FOMC meeting in November.
- **Who decides:** not stated. In practice the reference is likely the Fed's FOMC calendar page, which labels such events "unscheduled", "conference call" or "notation vote".
- **Edge cases and ambiguities:**
  - **Notation votes** are decisions taken without a meeting (e.g., Mar 19, 23 and 31 and Aug 27, 2020). They probably should not count as a "meeting", but a loosely worded market could be argued either way.
  - **Conference calls and videoconferences:** before 2019 the Fed called these "conference calls" (e.g., May 9, 2010; Aug 1, 2011). Since 2019 it has called them "unscheduled meetings" (Oct 4, 2019; Mar 2 and Mar 15, 2020). Either would very likely count.
  - **Delayed disclosure:** an unscheduled meeting is not always announced immediately. The Oct 4, 2019 meeting only became public with a statement on Oct 11. Anything between Oct 28 and Dec 9 would show up in the December minutes, released around Dec 30. Anything between Dec 10 and Dec 31 might not appear until the January 2027 minutes, around mid/late February 2027. Resolution could therefore come late, or a quiet meeting could be missed.
  - **Board actions are not FOMC meetings.** Discount-window changes, Board-only facilities (e.g., the BTFP during the March 2023 SVB crisis) and Board conference calls do not count. In March 2023 the Fed responded to SVB without any unscheduled FOMC meeting.
  - **Warsh's new "strategy" meetings** (announced Oct 28, 2026) take one of the eight regular 2027 meetings and turn it into a strategy-only session. They begin in 2027, they are scheduled, and they do not affect this window (Yahoo/Reuters, Oct 28, 2026; Bloomberg, Oct 28, 2026).
  - **Possible pre-window event:** the FOMC "unanimously selected" Warsh as its chair on May 22, 2026, the day he was sworn in (Fed press release, May 22, 2026). I could not establish whether this was a notation vote or an unscheduled meeting. It only matters if the market was issued before May 22, 2026. The fact that the market is still open suggests it was either not counted or happened before issuance.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Unscheduled FOMC meetings or conference calls listed on the Fed's historical calendars, 2007–2025.**
Dates come from Fed historical pages surfaced in search (2008, 2010, 2019, 2020). The 2007, 2011 and 2013 dates are from my background knowledge and need verifying.

| Year | Unscheduled meetings / calls |
|---|---|
| 2007 | Aug 10, Aug 16 |
| 2008 | Jan 9, Jan 21, Mar 10, Jul 24, Sep 29, Oct 7 |
| 2010 | May 9, Oct 15 |
| 2011 | Aug 1, Nov 28 |
| 2013 | Oct 16 |
| 2019 | Oct 4 |
| 2020 | Mar 2, Mar 15 (plus notation votes) |
| 2021–2025 | None that I know of (no search result showed any) |
| 2026 to Nov 27 | None found |

- Calendar months with at least one such meeting: about 13 of 228 months, or about 5.7% per month.
- A one-month window therefore gives roughly 5–6%. The events are heavily clustered in crisis periods (2007–08, 2020).

**B. The recent era, 2014–2025, after the Fed moved to ample reserves.**
- There were 3 unscheduled meetings in 144 months (Oct 2019; Mar 2 and Mar 15, 2020), across 2 distinct episodes.
- That is about 1.4–2% per month. The 2021–2025 stretch had none, despite real shocks: the March 2023 bank failures, the April 2025 tariff crash, and repo strains in late 2025.

**C. Equity drawdowns of 20% or more (bear markets in the Nasdaq or S&P) as a trigger.**
- Most bear markets did not produce an unscheduled FOMC meeting: 2011 did, but 2015–16, Q4 2018, 2022 and April 2025 did not. The 2008 and 2020 crashes did.
- Emergency meetings have come from systemic or funding crises (Lehman, COVID, the 2019 repo spike, euro-area dollar-funding stress in 2011), not from equity declines alone. My rough count: of about 8 major equity drawdowns since 2000, the ones with an accompanying intermeeting meeting were 2001, 2008, 2011 and 2020, roughly half. But the window for that to happen was usually many months, not five weeks.

**Rough synthesis (for the forecaster to judge):** class B gives about 2% for a ~1.1-month window, and class A about 6%. The current market stress argues for leaning somewhat above the calm-period rate. The Fed's hiking stance and the existence of standing facilities argue the other way (see sections 3–4).

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES**
1. **Financial-stability shock.** The AI/equity selloff spreads into credit or funding markets: AI-linked leasing or circular financing losses, a hedge fund or private-credit blow-up, or a dealer under stress. This is the most plausible trigger, but past equity routs without a systemic crisis have not produced emergency meetings.
2. **Funding-market or plumbing problem.** A repo spike like September 2019, especially around the year-end turn, could need FOMC action on balance-sheet or repo parameters, as the Oct 4, 2019 meeting did. The standing repo facility, now in place, reduces this need.
3. **Global dollar-funding stress** requiring swap-line changes, as in Nov 2011 or March 2020. This often involves the FOMC through a call.
4. **Something procedural or idiosyncratic** under Warsh: a reorganization, a balance-sheet policy change, or a decision that the chair wants outside the calendar. Also possible: a political shock such as attempts to remove governors or fallout from tariffs. Warsh's taste for "regime change" adds some tail risk, but his public line is to avoid reactive moves.
5. **Wording risk:** a notation vote or a Board call that the resolver mistakes for an FOMC meeting.

**Paths to NO (the default)**
- Markets stabilize. The Fed hikes, or pauses, at the scheduled Dec 8–9 meeting, which is about 11 days away and absorbs most of the need for an intermeeting decision.
- After Dec 9 there are about three weeks with holiday-thinned markets. Any crisis response could come from Board facilities, Treasury, or the scheduled January 2027 meeting.
- Inflation is high and the Fed is in a hiking cycle. That makes an emergency cut, the classic reason for these meetings, especially unlikely. Fed officials prefer to signal through speeches rather than meetings.

## 5. Signposts

**Toward YES (large moves)**
- Credit spreads blowing out, e.g., high-yield spreads widening by 150–200 bp or more in days.
- An AI-linked firm, fund or lender failing.
- SOFR spiking well above the top of the target range, or standing-repo usage jumping by tens of billions outside quarter-end.
- Cross-currency basis blowout or foreign central banks drawing on swap lines.
- Treasury market dysfunction.
- Reports (Nick Timiraos/WSJ, Bloomberg) that officials are "conferring".
- A Fed statement released outside the calendar.

**Toward YES (moderate moves)**
- The Nasdaq and S&P keep falling after Thanksgiving, for example the S&P down 15–20% from its peak.
- Brooks's predicted "meltdown" happens before Dec 9 and the hike is called into question.

**Toward NO**
- The Dec 8–9 meeting happens on schedule with an ordinary decision and orderly markets.
- The December minutes, around Dec 30, mention no unscheduled meetings.
- A calm year-end turn: SOFR close to IORB, low standing-repo usage.

- **Issuance date of the market**, and therefore whether the May 22, 2026 chair selection, whatever its format, falls inside the window.
- **What the resolver counts:** conference calls, notation votes, or briefings revealed only in minutes. Also how the resolver handles a meeting disclosed after Jan 1.
- **2021–2025 record:** I could not open the Fed's calendar pages to confirm there were zero unscheduled meetings; that relies on memory plus negative search results. The full 2007–2013 list also partly relies on memory.
