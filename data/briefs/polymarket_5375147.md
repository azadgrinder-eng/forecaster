# Research brief: Will Iran and Oman announce a Hormuz traffic agreement by November 30, 2026?

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** By 11:59 PM ET on November 30, 2026, both Iran and Oman must announce that they accept the same diplomatic agreement. The agreement must set Iranian or Omani policies, obligations or commitments meant to manage, permit, restore or increase shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Examples in the rules are vessel corridors, fee policies and other management rules.
- **Form of announcement:** A joint announcement qualifies. So do separate announcements that together clearly show both countries accepted the same agreement. Each announcement must state acceptance plainly. Language about "ongoing negotiations," "a proposed framework," "agreement in principle," or "continuing consultations" does not count. Separate national policies that are not presented as part of an agreement do not count either.
- **Who decides:** Official statements from the governments of Oman and Iran, plus a consensus of credible reporting.
- **Delay clause:** If both countries make announcements but it is unclear whether they amount to a qualifying agreement, the market stays open. It waits for clarification for up to 14 days after the last country's first potentially qualifying announcement, then resolves on the totality of information. A borderline announcement in late November could therefore be judged in mid-December.
- **Main ambiguity: the August 25, 2026 joint statement.** Iran and Oman already issued an official joint statement (Oman foreign ministry page fm.gov.om/en/53722). It described a "proposed framework" with a temporary joint navigation corridor and a joint mine-clearing project. It also said technical talks would continue toward a permanent corridor and future administration of the strait. Reporting at the time said the statement "indicated a final deal had not been concluded," and the corridor "has not yet been declared open" (IranWire, Aug 2026; Insurance Journal/Reuters, Aug 26, 2026).
  - Since this market is open with a November 30 deadline, the August statement apparently did not satisfy the rules, or was judged not to.
  - Forecasters should expect another "framework" or "consultations" statement to be treated the same way. Something new is needed: a declared or opened corridor, a signed agreement, or explicit wording that the two sides have "agreed."
- **Iranian claims alone are not enough.** Iran has repeatedly said a deal was reached:
  - Aug 5: an agreed route (Bloomberg, Aug 5, 2026).
  - Aug 7: Iranian state media said the deal bars U.S. and Israeli ships (NPR, Aug 7, 2026).
  - Aug 26: Deputy Foreign Minister Gharibabadi said a temporary route was agreed (Al Jazeera, Aug 26, 2026).
  
  Oman did not match these claims. Oman's confirmation is the binding constraint.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **This negotiation's own track record.** Iran-Oman Hormuz talks have run since at least early April 2026 (All India Radio, Apr 6, 2026). Milestones so far:
   - June 23: a pledge to work together through a joint working group (The National, Jun 23, 2026).
   - Early August: "final stages" and "agreement in principle" claims (Al Jazeera, Aug 2; i24, Aug 2026).
   - August 25: the "proposed framework" joint statement.
   - November 5: a phone call that agreed to keep consulting.
   
   That is about 7 months, or roughly nine 23-day windows, with no qualifying, mutually confirmed final agreement: **0 of ~9**. Talks have been described as "final stage" or "close" since August 2, more than three months without closure.
2. **U.S.-Iran deals during this war.** Two framework agreements in about 8 months:
   - the two-week ceasefire with safe passage (April 8, 2026);
   - an interim deal or memorandum in June that set 60 days of toll-free passage, after which "Iran and Oman would define the future administration" of the strait.
   
   The June deal lapsed or unravelled around August 17. That is roughly one U.S.-Iran deal per 4 months, or about 15–20% per 23-day window. Even a U.S.-Iran deal does not automatically produce an Iran-Oman agreement. Under the June template, Iran-Oman administration was a later step, not a simultaneous one.
3. **Iran-Oman bilateral maritime instruments in general.** The two countries do sign formal maritime agreements, for example the 1974 continental shelf/boundary agreement and a maritime boundary agreement reported by Gulf News in 2015. They are rare, roughly once a decade, and take long technical preparation. This is a weak prior, but it shows Oman signs formal instruments with Iran when conditions allow.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES**
1. **U.S.-Iran deal unlocks the Oman text (most plausible).** A Qatar-brokered U.S.-Iran deal (blockade relief and inspections in exchange for reopening) is followed within days by Iran and Oman formally declaring the temporary corridor or demining agreement they have largely drafted. This depends on:
   - a U.S.-Iran deal happening at all in the next roughly 3 weeks (talks are active but stalled, and strikes continue);
   - the U.S. accepting, or not vetoing, an Iran-Oman management role, which Trump explicitly rejected in August;
   - the sequencing. Under the June template, Iran-Oman administration came after a 60-day toll-free period, which would push it past November 30.
2. **Iran-Oman announce a narrow technical agreement without a U.S. deal.** Examples: a joint demining agreement, or a corridor declared "agreed" while awaiting implementation. Oman has avoided this so far because of explicit U.S. threats and sanctions warnings, and because it publicly ties implementation to U.S.-Iran talks. Iran also needs the Oman track as leverage, which argues against giving it away early.
3. **A multilateral deal that includes a clear Iran-Oman component.** A broad peace settlement could contain an annex in which both Iran and Oman commit to corridor management. It would qualify only if both countries clearly announce acceptance of that same instrument.

**Paths to NO**
- U.S.-Iran talks stay stuck. Iran's seven conditions are not met, U.S. strikes, mines and tanker attacks continue, and Oman keeps saying "consultations continue." This is the status quo for 3+ months.
- A U.S.-Iran deal reopens the strait under U.S.-preferred "open to all, toll-free" terms with no separate Iran-Oman instrument by November 30, with the Iran-Oman arrangement deferred.
- Iran makes unilateral claims of agreement (a recurring pattern) that Oman does not confirm. Or the announcements are ambiguous ("framework," "understandings"), which triggers the 14-day review and probably fails the "declarative acceptance" test.
- Escalation: new Iranian strikes affecting Oman, or U.S. pressure on Muscat.

## 5. Signposts

**Toward YES (large moves):**
- An announced U.S.-Iran agreement or ceasefire that mentions Iran and Oman jointly administering the corridor or demining.
- An Oman foreign ministry statement using "agreed," "signed," or "announce the opening of the temporary corridor."
- A Busaidi trip to Tehran or an Araghchi trip to Muscat with a signing ceremony.
- U.S. officials signalling tolerance of an Iran-Oman technical role, for example demining only and no tolls.
- A Notice to Mariners or UKMTO/JMIC advisory referencing an Iran-Oman corridor.

**Toward YES (smaller moves):**
- The Qatar 48-hour plan accepted by both sides.
- A sustained halt in mine and tanker attacks.
- Transit counts rising through the northern or Iranian route.

**Toward NO:**
- Further U.S. strikes on Iranian territory, with Iranian retaliation including against Gulf or Omani sites.
- Trump or Bessent repeating threats against Oman.
- Iran insisting on fees or revenue-sharing, which Washington rejects.
- Oman readouts continuing with "consultations" or "understandings" language.
- Any collapse of the Qatari channel.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **How the August 25 statement was judged.** I could not establish the exact wording of the statement or how this market series (or earlier-deadline versions) treated it. If the resolver were lenient, a statement like it could count. The evidence suggests it was treated as not qualifying.
- **When and how the "talks concluded" remark was made.** The date and exact wording of the Avapress report are unknown. It matters, because a finished text that is merely held back would make a fast announcement more plausible once U.S.-Iran terms are settled.
- **The Qatar 48-hour plan rests on one outlet.** Its content, and whether it gives Oman any role, are unverified.
- **Sequencing in any new U.S.-Iran deal.** It is unclear whether Iran-Oman administration would be simultaneous or deferred, as the June deal deferred it by 60 days.
- **Current ceasefire or hostility status and the U.S. political context after the midterms.** Whether the results change Trump's appetite for a deal is unclear.
