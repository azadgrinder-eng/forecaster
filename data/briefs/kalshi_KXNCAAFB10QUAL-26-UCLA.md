# Research brief: Will UCLA qualify for the 2026 Big Ten Championship Game?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **YES condition:** UCLA is one of the two teams in the 2026 Big Ten Championship Game. The game is scheduled for Saturday, Dec. 5, 2026 in Indianapolis (KWQC, Nov 27, 2026). The Big Ten's final standings and tiebreakers decide the field. The market's close date (Jan 10, 2027) is long after the field is set, so the answer will almost certainly be known by the night of Nov 28 or early Nov 29, 2026.
- **The deciding game is today.** UCLA (10‑1 overall, 7‑1 Big Ten) hosts USC (8‑3, 5‑3) at the Rose Bowl on Saturday, Nov. 28, 2026, at 12:30 p.m. PT / 3:30 p.m. ET on CBS. If UCLA wins, it is in outright (Big Ten clinch update, Nov 27, 2026; ESPN/Yahoo injury coverage, Nov 2026). One CBS preview gave the date as Dec. 5 and a Fox page showed an 8:30 PM kickoff. The other sources agree on Nov. 28, so those two look like errors.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Multi-team ties at 7‑2.** These go to the Big Ten tiebreaker chain. The order reported is head-to-head among the tied teams, then record against common opponents, then further steps ending in a SportSource Analytics computer ranking.
  - **Withdrawal.** In principle a qualified team could withdraw or be replaced, but nothing in the sources suggests this.
  - **Kalshi wording.** The market says "qualifies." The ordinary reading is that the conference names UCLA as a participant.
- **Status unknown.** As of my last search I found no final score for UCLA–USC. The forecaster should check ESPN gameId 401858543 before pricing.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **Ranked teams playing their final game with a "win and in" scenario.** The decisive input is one game, so the base rate is UCLA's chance of beating USC.
   - The pre-game sources I found lean toward USC. An AccuScore simulation gave USC 59% (so roughly 41% for UCLA), and an SI preview picked USC 27‑20 (AccuScore, Nov 2026; SI, Nov 2026).
   - Pro Football Network's model put UCLA's overall chance of making the title game at about 55%. That figure includes the paths where UCLA loses (PFN, ~Nov 27, 2026).
   - These are model and analyst figures, not market prices.
2. **Recent Big Ten "control your destiny" final weeks.** In 2024, Ohio State only had to beat Michigan to reach the title game and lost 13‑10. Penn State took the spot instead (CBS 2024 scenarios). This is a reminder that rivalry-week upsets happen.
3. **Recent UCLA–USC results.**
   - USC has won the last two meetings: 2025 by 29‑10 (ESPN recap, Nov 29, 2025) and 2024 by 19‑13 (my recollection; not checked this session).
   - UCLA's last win was in 2023, 38‑20 at the Coliseum (also from memory, not checked).
   - In the more general class of home teams with the better record losing to a lower-ranked rival in rivalry week, upsets happen roughly 25–35% of the time. This is my rough estimate, not a sourced count.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**A. UCLA beats USC → YES.** UCLA finishes 8‑1 and is sole first place. This is the main path.

Things that affect UCLA's chances in this game:
- whether Dieffenbach plays;
- home field at the Rose Bowl;
- a talented 8‑3 USC team with nothing left to play for in the Big Ten race, though it still has CFP and rivalry motivation.

**B. UCLA loses → finishes 7‑2 alongside Iowa, plus any of Indiana and Oregon that win.**
- **Indiana and Oregon both lose → YES.** UCLA and Iowa are the only 7‑2 teams. Reports say UCLA gets in if two of Indiana, Iowa and Oregon lose; since Iowa already won, both Indiana and Oregon must lose.
- **Indiana wins and Oregon loses (UCLA, Iowa and Indiana at 7‑2) → partial path.** Indiana gets in, and the second spot is between UCLA and Iowa.
  - CBS reports that UCLA still gets in "if either Minnesota or Ohio State wins." Otherwise the SportSource computer-ranking tiebreaker decides UCLA vs Iowa.
  - If Minnesota already lost to Wisconsin on Nov 27, this path depends on Ohio State beating Michigan or on the computer ranking.
- **Oregon wins and Indiana loses (UCLA, Iowa and Oregon at 7‑2) → probably NO.**
  - The common-opponent tiebreaker drops UCLA: Michigan is the only opponent all four contenders share, and UCLA lost to Michigan.
  - Against that, SI reported earlier in the season that UCLA holds the head-to-head tiebreaker over Oregon. How the multi-team rules apply here is unclear.
- **Indiana and Oregon both win (four-way tie) → NO.** The Michigan common-opponent step eliminates UCLA.

**How likely the loss paths are:** Indiana is likely a heavy favorite over Purdue, and Oregon is probably favored over Washington, though I did not verify either. That suggests the chance of both losing is low, perhaps 3–8%. So the probability of YES is roughly P(UCLA beats USC) plus a small extra amount.

## 5. Signposts
- **Biggest:** the final UCLA–USC score, which decides the question if UCLA wins.
- **Before or during the game:** Dieffenbach active and starting moves toward YES. Madden Iamaleava starting is roughly neutral to negative, since it is already the expected case.
- **If UCLA loses:** watch the Indiana–Purdue and Oregon–Washington results. An Indiana loss with an Oregon loss means YES; an Indiana win with an Oregon loss means a coin-flip tiebreaker (Ohio State–Michigan result and SportSource ranking). Any Oregon win is very likely NO.
- **Confirmation:** a Big Ten announcement of the title-game field on the night of Nov 28 or Nov 29.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not read the full Big Ten clinch-update text; the page fetches were blocked. The loss-path tiebreaker details come from search snippets and are partly contradictory. In particular, the role of the Minnesota and Ohio State results, the computer ranking, and the head-to-head with Oregon are unclear.
- I did not establish Wisconsin's opponent on Nov 27 or the pre-game strength of Indiana over Purdue and Oregon over Washington.
- Sources disagree on Madden Iamaleava's class (freshman vs sophomore) and on the date of the Michigan game (Nov 21 vs Nov 23).
