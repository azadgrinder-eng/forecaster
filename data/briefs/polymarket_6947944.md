# Research brief: Will Russia and Ukraine reach an energy ceasefire agreement by December 31, 2026?

*Method note: I could only read search-result summaries. Every attempt to open a full article failed because the network proxy refused the connection. Some details, especially exact quotes and dates, are therefore second-hand and should be checked against the original articles where it matters.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES.** Russia and Ukraine must mutually agree to suspend strikes on at least one defined category of the other side's energy infrastructure. Examples are refineries, power plants, the grid, pipelines, fuel storage and export terminals. A category limited to a geographic area counts. The two sides' categories do not need to match. A broader ceasefire, truce or peace framework also counts if it includes a mutual general suspension of strikes.
- **Window.** The agreement must come after the market was created and by 11:59 PM Kyiv time on December 31, 2026 (21:59 UTC). That leaves about 41 days from today, November 20. An agreement reached by then counts even if it takes effect later.
- **Who decides.** Both governments must announce the agreement officially, or a consensus of credible reporting must confirm both agreed to it. A third-party claim alone does not count, including one from Trump.
- **What does not count:**
  - informal or backchannel understandings, de-escalation without an announced agreement, or unilateral pauses
  - deals covering a single facility, such as the Zaporizhzhia nuclear plant
  - frameworks that only promise future talks
- **Main ambiguity: the "Trump request" pattern.** Past episodes did not produce one joint text. Instead, Trump announced a deal, the Kremlin said it had "accepted Trump's request," and Zelensky said Ukraine would reciprocate if Russia did. In January 2026 the Kremlin confirmed and Zelensky reciprocated, but Zelensky also said "there was no formal truce" and the two sides disagreed about how long it lasted (Reuters via The Star, Jan 30, 2026). How a resolver would treat a repeat of that pattern is the biggest rules uncertainty. A pause limited to Kyiv would probably meet the "defined geographic area" test.
- **Already-failed claim.** Trump's October 11, 2026 "energy ceasefire" did not meet the bar. The Kremlin did not confirm it, Russian strikes continued, and the Institute for the Study of War (ISW) said "no ceasefire exists" (Al Jazeera, Oct 12, 2026; Kyiv Independent, Oct 2026).

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Mutually confirmed energy-strike pauses during the full-scale war (February 2022 to November 2026, about 45 months).**

There have been roughly two qualifying or near-qualifying episodes:

1. **March 2025.** Putin agreed to a 30-day halt on energy strikes in a March 18 call with Trump. Zelensky agreed on March 19. On March 25 the White House issued statements on Black Sea and energy measures, and the Kremlin published a list of covered facilities (Al Jazeera, Mar 18 and Mar 25, 2025; Meduza, Mar 25, 2025). This would very likely have qualified.
2. **January 30, 2026.** The Kremlin confirmed it had agreed to Trump's request to pause strikes on Kyiv and/or energy targets until February 1. Zelensky said Ukraine would reciprocate (Reuters via The Star and The Moscow Times, Jan 30, 2026). Under these rules this one is borderline but plausible. Russia ended it with mass strikes on the night of February 3 (FDD, Feb 3, 2026).

That is about 2 events in 45 months, or roughly 4.4% per month. Over a 1.35-month window, that gives about 6%.

**B. The same, counting only Trump's second term (January 2025 onward, about 22 months).**

The same two events give roughly 9% per month, or about 11–12% over the window (Poisson). Both events happened in winter or early spring, during an active US-brokered push and after a personal Trump request to Putin. The current setting shares some of those features: a winter push from Europe and the US is preparing a next round of talks.

**C. Claimed or proposed energy truces in 2026.**

There were at least four proposals or claims, and about one confirmed agreement:

| Date | Proposal or claim | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Jan 2026 | Trump request to Putin | Confirmed (borderline under these rules) |
| Sep 2026 | Zelensky offered a conditional truce | Kremlin called it a "very good idea" but did not agree (Euronews, Sep 16, 2026; United24) |
| Oct 11, 2026 | Trump claimed a deal | Not confirmed |
| Nov 2026 | Ukraine raised it at the Abu Dhabi talks; European winter truce call | Rejected |

So roughly 1 in 4 to 1 in 5 such pushes became a mutually confirmed arrangement, and that one lasted about 4 days.

A full ceasefire or peace deal by December 31 is a separate and much smaller path. No general ceasefire has been agreed in 45 months. Russia keeps saying it wants "peace, not a ceasefire."

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 19–20, 2026.** Putin said Moscow would not sign ceasefire agreements on energy or the Black Sea after Ukraine struck the Crimean Bridge three times in about a week, the latest with Neptune missiles. He said "we will not negotiate" and promised a "far more painful" response (Reuters via Investing.com / Al Arabiya, Nov 19; Kyiv Independent; Japan Times, Nov 20; Pravda, Nov 19).
  - Putin said he had earlier asked his Security Council to consider a moratorium, which suggests the idea was on the table. Observers are skeptical of that claim.
  - A Kyiv Independent headline reports a mass Russian missile attack after the vow. Its targets were unconfirmed in what I could see.
- **Nov 19.** Coalition of the Willing summit in Berlin. Germany, France, the UK and Ukraine called on Russia to accept an energy ceasefire "for the duration of the winter" as a first step, plus a Black Sea ceasefire (anews, Nov 19; mlq.ai; RTÉ, Nov 19). Reports name Andy Burnham as UK prime minister.
  - Zelensky said: "Putin has always said no."
  - Putin reportedly invited the Europeans to Moscow (HNGN, Nov 19) and said for the first time that he was open to European participation in talks. Earlier, Kremlin aide Ushakov had said Russia had not agreed to widen the talks.
- **Nov 17–20.** Ukraine held talks with the US and Europe (Al Jazeera, Nov 17). Merz said a new round including Europe will come "soon," with no date fixed. The UAE has been floated as a venue. Kushner said the US is coordinating the next round with Germany, Ukraine and European partners. An Axios report of Nov 20 says Trump told skeptical Europeans "Putin wants to end the war."
- **Nov 11.** Axios reported the US asked Russia to propose a new European security arrangement as part of an end-of-war deal (Axios, Nov 11, 2026).
- **Nov 10–11.** First trilateral US–Ukraine–Russia talks in Abu Dhabi in about 9 months: Umerov for Ukraine, Medinsky for Russia, Witkoff and Kushner for the US (Kyiv Independent; PBS; Reuters via Investing.com). There was no breakthrough. The two sides agreed to prepare a prisoner exchange of about 1,000–1,500 people each. Ukraine tried to gauge Russian interest in an energy truce, and Lavrov rejected a truce as "breathing space" for Ukraine (Al Jazeera, Nov 9).
- **Early November.** Russian strikes hit the grid in four regions and cut power to hundreds of thousands. Moscow accused Kyiv of resuming refinery strikes (NBC News).
- **Nov 2.** Peskov downplayed a US-floated incentive of resuming Russian gas transit through Ukraine in exchange for a halt to grid strikes, without fully ruling it out (Moscow Times, Nov 2, 2026).
- **Late October.** Peskov said Putin was not considering an energy ceasefire and that "Russia needs peace, not a ceasefire" (Kyiv Post, post/87731). NV reported that no energy-truce negotiations were under way.
- **Oct 11–12.** Trump announced an energy ceasefire "effective immediately." The Kremlin said it had "nothing to say," then that "contacts are ongoing" while also saying "we do not want a ceasefire." Ukraine paused refinery strikes for about two days, and Russian strikes continued (Al Jazeera, Oct 12; Newsweek; NBC).
- **Sep 16, 2026.** Zelensky said Ukraine was ready for an energy ceasefire if Russia reciprocated. Russia had not agreed (Euronews).

**Scheduled or expected before December 31:**
- a next round of talks, possibly including Europe and possibly in the UAE, with no date set
- Russia's promised retaliation for the bridge strikes
- the peak winter grid-attack season
- possible holiday-period truce proposals. Orthodox Christmas (Jan 7) falls after the close date.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES**

1. **Trump-brokered winter pause, the January 2026 template.** Trump asks Putin directly, perhaps around a cold snap or the holidays. The Kremlin confirms it accepted the request and Ukraine says it will reciprocate. This depends on:
   - Putin wanting to reward or please Trump, for example while the US security-architecture track develops
   - Ukraine stopping its long-range strikes, such as on the bridge and refineries
   - the resolver accepting this kind of announcement as an "agreement"
   
   This is the most plausible path.
2. **Package deal at the next trilateral or expanded round.** An energy and Black Sea moratorium agreed as a confidence-building step, as in March 2025. This depends on the round happening before late December and on Russia dropping its "no truce before a settlement" line, which Putin explicitly took up again on Nov 19.
3. **Broader ceasefire or framework that includes a mutual halt to strikes.** Very unlikely in 6 weeks given the gaps on territory and security.

**Paths to NO**

- Putin's explicit refusal on Nov 19 plus the retaliation cycle after the bridge strikes. Retaliation is likely aimed at energy, which hardens Ukraine's position too.
- Russia's long-standing preference to use winter grid strikes as leverage.
- No date set for the next round, and the holiday season shortens the calendar.
- A Trump claim with no Kremlin confirmation, as in October.
- A short or ambiguous pause that the resolver judges unilateral or informal.

## 5. Signposts

**Would move toward YES (roughly in order of strength):**
- a Trump–Putin call, with a Kremlin readout saying Putin "accepted" a halt on energy or Kyiv strikes
- Peskov or Ushakov confirming contacts on a moratorium
- a dated next trilateral or expanded round, especially if Russia's delegation is upgraded
- Ukraine visibly pausing refinery and bridge strikes
- movement on the US–Russia security-architecture track or sanctions relief (for example on Rosselkhozbank), as in the March 2025 Black Sea deal

**Would move toward NO:**
- a large Russian retaliation against the grid
- more Ukrainian strikes on the Crimean Bridge or refineries
- Kremlin repetition of "peace, not a ceasefire"
- talks slipping into January
- Trump turning to pressure or sanctions instead of brokering

## 6. Uncertainties

- **Resolver treatment of near-misses.** It is unclear whether a Kremlin "accepted Trump's request" statement plus a conditional Ukrainian pledge to reciprocate would count. That is the key edge case.
- **Market creation date.** I could not establish it. If it was before January 30, 2026, the January episode might already be relevant to resolution, though trading presumably reflects that.
- **Real timing of the next round.** No date or venue found.
- **Nature and targets of Russia's retaliation after November 19.** Unconfirmed.
- **Whether Putin's Security Council review was real.** Unclear whether a moratorium was genuinely under consideration before the bridge strikes, or whether that was a retrospective claim.
- **Reliability of single-outlet reports.** Some sources (Daily Beirut, HNGN, mlq.ai, World Today News) are thin and only partly corroborated. I could not read any full text.
