## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Consensys must hold its first trading day by Dec 31, 2026, 11:59 PM ET. Its market cap at that day's official close must be above $1B. Market cap means shares outstanding times the first-day closing price.
- **Who decides:** Resolution uses the primary exchange's official listing page. If that page doesn't show the figure, another reliable source is used.
- **Shortened sessions:** If the first session is interrupted (for example a circuit breaker or half-day), the abbreviated session's official close is used. If no official close is published, the next day with an official close counts as the first trading day.
- **Default:** If no IPO happens by the deadline, the market resolves NO.

**Edge cases and ambiguities**

- **Which company is "Consensys"?** On Sept 9, 2026 Consensys announced a split into two companies, to be finished by the end of 2026:
  - The existing legal entity, Consensys Software Inc., is being renamed **MetaMask**, with Joe Lubin as Chairman and CEO.
  - A **newly formed company keeps the Consensys name**. It holds the protocols and institutional business (Linea, Besu, Teku), led by CEO Mike Kriak and President David Cunningham, with Lubin as Executive Chairman. (MetaMask.com announcement, Sept 9, 2026; The Block, Sept 9, 2026; Cointelegraph, Sept 2026)
  - A future IPO by "MetaMask" (the old Consensys entity) or by the "new Consensys" could each be argued as "Consensys' IPO". The question doesn't say which. This only matters if a listing happens before Dec 31, which looks very unlikely (see below).
- **Shares outstanding:** It's unclear whether this means Class A only or all classes. The question says "total number of outstanding shares", which is usually read as all classes. This matters little at a $1B threshold.
- **SPAC or direct listing:** It's not clear whether either counts as an "IPO". Both would also need public SEC filings or a shareholder vote that take far longer than the ~5 weeks left.

**Timing constraint, probably the most important fact here**

- Under SEC policy, a company that filed its draft registration confidentially must file publicly **at least 15 days before the roadshow starts**. With no roadshow, the deadline is 15 days before the registration becomes effective. (SEC Voluntary Submission of Draft Registration Statements FAQs; Goodwin DRS guide)
  - A search result showed an SEC "redline DRS FAQs" document dated 09/08/26, which may have updated this guidance. I couldn't read it because the page fetch was blocked.
- A typical roadshow then takes about 4–8 trading days before pricing.
- For trading by Dec 31, Consensys would therefore need a public S-1 by roughly the first week of December. Pricing would then fall in the last week of the year, when the IPO market is normally closed for the holidays.
- **As of Nov 24, 2026 I found no public S-1, ticker, price range or exchange listing for Consensys or MetaMask.** (Curved Trading Consensys IPO article, undated 2026; ipos.fyi tracker, updated Sept 11, 2026; Forge, accessed via search Nov 2026)

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Companies with no public S-1 five weeks before the deadline that still list before it.**
- For a standard underwritten US IPO this is close to 0%, because the 15-day public-filing rule plus the roadshow use up nearly the whole window.
- Practitioner guides typically put the time from public S-1 to pricing at about 3–6 weeks, and SEC review of a fresh filing takes longer.
- I know of no large IPO in recent years that went from no public S-1 in late November to trading in the same calendar year. That is my inference, not a counted dataset.

**B. Late-December IPO activity.**
- US IPO issuance usually all but stops after about Dec 15. Late-December pricings by large venture-backed companies are rare, a handful of small caps or SPACs at most.
- This is general market knowledge. I did not verify a 2026-specific count.

**C. Crypto companies that announced plans to go public in 2025–26 and actually listed on the targeted timeline.**
- **Completed:**
  - Circle listed in June 2025 (TechCrunch, Jun 5, 2025).
  - BitGo priced at $18, above its range, on Jan 22, 2026. It rose about 20–25% on day one to roughly a $2.6B market cap, then fell about 44–45% below its listing price by May. (TheStreet, Jan 2026; Kraken pause coverage, Mar 2026)
- **Postponed:**
  - Kraken filed confidentially in Nov 2025 and targeted Q1 2026, but paused its IPO in March 2026 "until market conditions improve". (Kitco/CoinDesk, Mar 18, 2026; PYMNTS, 2026)
  - Consensys itself targeted a draft S-1 around late Feb 2026, then delayed to "fall 2026 at the earliest". (CoinDesk, May 13, 2026)
- **Rate:** Among the large crypto names targeting 2026 (Kraken, Consensys, Ledger and others), BitGo was reportedly the only crypto-native IPO in 2026 as of March–May reporting.
- **Conclusion:** Crypto IPO plans slip often, especially in drawdowns.

**D. Conditional on an IPO happening: first-day market cap above $1B.**
- Consensys was valued at $7B in its 2022 Series D ($450M round). Press estimates for an IPO range from $7–12B. (Wikipedia/Consensys; MEXC blog, 2026; FX Leaders, May 14, 2026, "$7B IPO")
- Even after a heavy markdown, either successor company would very likely clear $1B. BitGo, a smaller business, closed day one at about $2.6B.
- Conditional probability: high, plausibly above 90%.
- **So the question reduces almost entirely to whether any listing happens by Dec 31, 2026.**

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (all low-probability)**
1. **A hidden DRS goes public very soon.** A confidential filing made earlier in 2026 becomes public in the next ~1–2 weeks, followed by a fast roadshow and a Dec 22–31 pricing.
   - This would need: SEC comments already cleared, both banks willing to market into the holidays during a crypto drawdown, and the split done or not blocking the listing.
   - Running a separation and an IPO at the same time is operationally hard, and the financials would need to reflect the new structure.
2. **An unconventional route, such as a direct listing.** This would still need a public registration and the 15-day lead time. It's not clear it would count as an "IPO".
3. **Rule or interpretive change.** If the 9/8/26 SEC FAQ update shortened the timelines, path 1 becomes slightly more feasible. This is unverified.

**Paths to NO (dominant)**
1. No public filing and no listing before Dec 31. This is the default, given:
   - management's silence;
   - the corporate split still under way;
   - BTC down sharply in November with ETF outflows;
   - press pointing to 2027;
   - the year-end IPO calendar.
2. Even with a filing in December, pricing could slip into January 2027, which also resolves NO.
3. A listing happens but closes day one below $1B. This is very unlikely given $7B+ valuations, unless it were only a small spun-out piece.
