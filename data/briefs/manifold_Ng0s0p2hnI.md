# Research Brief: Will Erling Haaland keep his clean crew cut until Christmas 2026?

**Access note:** I couldn't open any article directly because the network proxy refused every page fetch. Everything below comes from search-result summaries of the articles, not the full text. Read every figure with that in mind.

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **YES:** The first photo of Haaland published after Christmas (25 Dec 2026) shows him still wearing a "clean crew cut", meaning a buzz cut or very short back and sides kept trimmed.
- **NO:** That photo shows he has "let his hair grow", meaning visibly longer hair, a parting, or anything clearly past a crew cut.
- **Who decides:** The question creator, most likely by eye. There is no stated length threshold.
- **Timing:** The question closes 25 Dec 2026 00:00 UTC, but it resolves on a photo published after Christmas. Man City almost always plays a Premier League game on 26–28 December, so match photos will appear right away. His own Instagram is another likely source.
- **Main ambiguities:**
  - **The starting point wasn't a crew cut.** In August 2026 he had a near-shaved military buzz cut ("like I've been in the military"). Some outlets called it a shaved head; the Premier League site called it a "short back and sides".
  - **Normal regrowth.** Hair grows about 1.25 cm a month. Four months without a cut from 23 Aug leaves about 5 cm on top. That reads as a short crop, not long hair, but it is clearly no longer a buzz cut.
  - **Trimmed but longer.** If he keeps it trimmed but longer than a crew cut (for example a short side-part), the resolver has to make a judgement call. A side-parted style looks like a NO.
  - **Hats and headbands.** A beanie or headband in the first post-Christmas photo could hide his hair. Resolvers would probably fall back on match photos.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **Haaland's own hair history.** He has changed style often: a short centre parting early at Dortmund, cornrows/braids at times, then long hair and a man bun from about 2021 to 2026. In 2025 he went back to a centre parting. The 2026 cut was reported as his first proper cut since 2021 (search summaries of TMSPN/K24, Oct 2026). He changes look roughly every 1–2 years and has never kept a buzz cut long-term. His default is longer hair.
2. **Celebrities and athletes who make a one-off dramatic "big chop".** I couldn't find a rigorous dataset. Anecdotally, most people who buzz off long hair for a "fresh start" let it grow back unless the buzz is a long-term signature look (e.g. Lewandowski, Kane, Rodri). My rough prior, not sourced: fewer than 30% still have a buzz or crew cut 4 months later. This is a soft estimate.
3. **Stated intent.** He has said several times that he plans to grow it back. People who say this mostly do, at least over a season.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)
- **~11 Nov 2026 (reported "today"):** A Fox Sports piece with Alexi Lalas reportedly says Haaland's hair is "starting to grow out". I could not confirm the quote; a direct search didn't find it.

"Several inches" in two months is physically unlikely (expect about 2.5 cm), so it is probably exaggerated. Still, it points to regrowth and a styled side-part, not a kept buzz.
- **8 Oct 2026:** In a WSJ profile (via secondary reports), he said he expected the haircut to draw attention but not for "four or five days". He joked he could grow it all back before the next World Cup. Other reports quote him: "I will let it grow back over time."
- **Late Sep 2026:** Goal reported five goals in three games "rocking his new look", including a Champions League brace against Porto. He told Goal he now goes "completely unnoticed in public" without the long hair and called it "nice". That is a mild reason to keep it short.
- **23 Aug 2026:** He showed the buzz cut on Instagram ("New season, new trim" / "Short hair don't care") and in a YouTube video, hours before City beat Bournemouth 2-1.
  - In the video: "This beautiful long hair, time to let it go" and "It will grow back and be long again, hopefully."
  - He said people around him had long talked him out of it, and Zlatan Ibrahimović had told him "never cut your hair".
  - Grealish said he looked about 10 years old; Guardiola joked about it. (ESPN; ABC News; Sky Sports; Premier League; Euronews, 23–24 Aug 2026)
- **Before the cut:** He had worn his hair long through four seasons at City.
- **Coming up before close:** Premier League fixtures through December, Champions League group games, the usual Christmas-period posts, and Boxing Day fixtures right after the close.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to NO (hair grown out):**
- He keeps his stated plan to grow it back. Late-October reports already describe a side parting.
- Hair grows passively; by late December, without cuts, he would have about 5 cm, which looks clearly grown.
- A pre-World Cup grow-out goal points toward longer hair over time.

**Paths to YES (crew cut kept):**
- He enjoys being anonymous in public and keeps it short with regular trims.
- A "lucky haircut" superstition if City are scoring well.
- He re-buzzes for a new campaign or sponsorship.
- A lenient resolver who counts any short, tidy cut as a "crew cut".

## 5. Signposts
- **Toward NO:** Instagram or match photos in Nov–Dec showing a parting, fringe, or hair visibly longer than about 2 cm. Media "hair growing back" stories.
- **Toward YES:** Reports or photos of a fresh trim or re-buzz, or Haaland saying he will keep it short.
- **The resolver's own wording:** Any clarification of what counts as a "crew cut" would matter most, given how ambiguous the criteria are.

## 6. Uncertainties
- I couldn't see any photos directly. The late-October regrowth report is one secondary celebrity outlet, and the Lalas quote is unconfirmed.
- I don't know whether he has had trims since August.
- I don't know how the resolver will judge a short-but-grown style.
- Search summaries mixed up some 2025 and 2026 stories; the 2025 centre-parting article is unrelated.
