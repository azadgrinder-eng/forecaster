# Research Brief: Will *Avengers: Doomsday* open to at least $400M domestically (3-day, Dec 18–20, 2026)?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** The final domestic 3-day opening weekend gross (Fri Dec 18 – Sun Dec 20, 2026, including Thursday Dec 17 previews) must be at least $400,000,000. Studio estimates do not count.
- **Primary source:** The opening-weekend figure in The Numbers' "Weekend Box Office Performance" table. If The Numbers has no final figure, the "Weekend" figure on Box Office Mojo's "Domestic Weekend" tab is used.
- **Deadline:** January 20, 2027, 11:59 PM ET. If the film is not released by then, or no figure exists, the market resolves to the lowest bracket (NO).
- **Timing:** Studio estimates usually come out Sunday Dec 20 and actuals on Monday Dec 21, so resolution should come quickly. The close date (Dec 20, 12:00 UTC, which is Sunday morning) falls before the Sunday estimates. By then the Thursday preview and Friday grosses will be public, and Saturday may be known informally.
- **Edge cases:**
  - **Estimate vs. actual:** A Sunday estimate near $400M can move a few million when actuals arrive. *Spider-Man: Brand New Day* went from a $355M estimate to a $360M final (ABC News, Aug 2026).
  - **Exact tie:** A figure exactly on a bracket boundary resolves to the higher bracket.
  - **Source differences:** The Numbers and Box Office Mojo occasionally differ by small amounts.
  - **Date change:** The question pins the weekend to Dec 18–20. A rumored move to Dec 11 was reported, but both studios said they are staying on Dec 18 (ComicBookMovie). If the date did move, the rules would be ambiguous.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
**A. All-time domestic openings.** No film has ever opened to $400M+ domestically. Only two have passed $300M:
- *Spider-Man: Brand New Day* (Jul 31, 2026): $360M, the current record.
- *Avengers: Endgame* (Apr 2019): $357.1M.

Next come *No Way Home* ($260.1M), *Infinity War* ($257.7M) and *The Force Awakens* ($248.0M). $400M would beat the record by about 11%. Base rate for $400M+: 0 of all releases ever, so the case for YES has to rest on this film being unprecedented.

**B. Avengers team-ups against the existing record.** Four of the five Avengers films opened to records:
- *The Avengers* (2012): $207.4M, a record.
- *Age of Ultron* (2015): $191.3M, not a record and below its predecessor.
- *Infinity War* (2018): $257.7M, a record.
- *Endgame* (2019): $357.1M, a record and +39% over *Infinity War*.

Breaking the $360M record by any amount is plausible on this history. Beating it by 11% or more happened in 3 of 4 cases (Avengers +... over Deathly Hallows 2's $169M; IW +4% over TFA, which does not clear 11%; Endgame +39%). Strictly, 2 of the 4 sequels/originals beat the prior record by 11% or more.

**C. Pre-Christmas December openers.** These open softer relative to their total because many schools are still in session on the Friday, then gain on very long legs.
- *The Force Awakens* (Dec 18, 2015): $248M
- *No Way Home* (Dec 17, 2021): $260.1M
- *The Last Jedi*: $220M
- *Rogue One*: $155M
- *Avatar: The Way of Water*: $134M

No December film has opened above $260M. Dec 18, 2026 is a Friday, a full week before Christmas.

**D. Ratio of opening weekend to Thursday previews** (how much previews are multiplied by to get the weekend):
- *Brand New Day*: about 5.0x ($72M previews, $360M weekend).
- *Endgame*: about 6.0x ($60M previews).
- *No Way Home*: about 5.2x ($50M previews).
- *Infinity War*: about 6.6x ($39M previews).

To reach $400M at 5–5.5x, previews of roughly $73–80M are needed. A more front-loaded fan film at around 4.5x would need about $89M.

**E. Final tracking vs. actuals for record-type openers.** Fan-driven films usually beat their late tracking:
- *Infinity War*: tracked about $200–225M, opened $257.7M.
- *Endgame*: tracked about $260–300M, opened $357M.
- *No Way Home*: tracked low, opened $260M.
- *Deadpool & Wolverine*: tracked about $160M+, opened $211M.

These tracking figures are from memory, not re-verified in this search. Beats of 15–30% have been common.

  - Presale analysis has *Doomsday* beating *Dune 3* by about 3.2–4 to 1 that weekend. *Dune 3* is projected at $100M+ opening; *Dune: Part Two* opened at $82.5M (SlashFilm; ComicBasics, 2026).

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES (≥$400M):**
- The presale pace through early Dec stays well ahead of BND's on matched days, producing record previews of $80M+.
- Strong reviews and word of mouth: the return of Downey (as Doom) and the original Avengers drives an "event" turnout like *Endgame*.
- The *VisionQuest* crossover and post-BND MCU momentum widen the audience beyond fans.
- Premium formats and round-the-clock showtimes make up for the lost IMAX screens.
- Ticket-price inflation since 2019 helps. Endgame's $357M is roughly $440–460M in 2026 dollars.

**Paths to NO:**
- Pre-Christmas timing (schools in session on Friday) caps the weekend, as with TFA and NWH.
- No IMAX screens, with *Dune 3* taking IMAX and some premium screens and adult audiences.
- The 165-minute runtime limits showtimes.
- Presales are front-loaded by fans, so the internal multiplier stays around 4.5–5x.
- Mixed reviews, or audience fatigue with the multiverse storyline.
- Mainstream trades (about $250–300M) sit well below $400M. Even a typical 20–30% beat over $300M only gets to about $360–390M.

## 5. Signposts
- **Formal trade tracking in late Nov** (Deadline, Variety, Quorum): above $350M would be a clear upward signal; $275–325M is consistent with NO unless the usual fan-film beat is unusually large.
- **Matched-day presale comparisons with BND, Endgame and NWH** from box-office forum trackers in the final 2–3 weeks: running ahead of BND's final-week pace by 15% or more points toward YES.
- **Thursday preview gross (Dec 17):** $90M+ makes YES likely; $70–80M is borderline; below $70M makes YES unlikely.
- **Friday total including previews (Dec 18):** about $180M+ is needed for $400M at typical 2.2–2.3x weekend-to-Friday ratios.
- **Reviews and CinemaScore:** Rotten Tomatoes in the 80s/90s plus an A CinemaScore supports Saturday holds.
- **Dune 3's tracking and reviews:** strength there means more competition for screens and adults.
- **Any change to the release date or the IMAX deal.**

## 6. Uncertainties
- No verified domestic presale total after about $65M at around 50 days out. The 60K-ticket preview claim is unverified, and its meaning (cumulative vs. a single-day gain) is unclear.
- I could not access primary Deadline, Variety or Boxoffice Pro tracking pages; much of the evidence comes from secondary aggregators with inconsistent dating.
- The "$150M presales" figure for BND has unclear scope (domestic vs. worldwide), and comparisons are distorted because *Doomsday*'s sale window is much longer.
- How much of the lost IMAX revenue premium formats will recover.
- The final runtime, review embargo date, and the share of schools still in session on Dec 18.
- How the pre-Christmas holiday calendar will shape the Fri/Sat/Sun pattern compared with BND's summer opening.
