# Research brief: Will a storm named Fay be classified as an Atlantic hurricane between May 15 and December 1, 2026?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What YES requires:** a storm named Fay must be classified as a hurricane in the Atlantic between May 15 and December 1, 2026. Close is 2026-12-01 15:00 UTC. A hurricane means 1-minute sustained winds of at least 64 kt (74 mph).
- **Who decides:** the rules name no source. In practice that means the National Hurricane Center (NHC) advisories, its later best-track data and Tropical Cyclone Reports (TCRs), and probably Wikipedia as a summary.
- **The name is used up:** Fay is the 6th name on the 2026 Atlantic list. It went to Tropical Storm Fay (AL062026), which formed on about September 19–20, 2026 and had its last NHC advisory on September 29. Names are not reused within a season, so no new "Fay" can form in 2026.
- **Only path to YES:** Fay was never a hurricane in NHC's live advisories. YES would need one of two things before December 1:
  - NHC's post-season TCR or best track raises Fay to at least 64 kt and is published in time, or
  - the resolver misreads or misapplies the rules.
- **Wording ambiguity:** "Is a storm categorized as a hurricane" could arguably be read as covering a later reclassification. But a TCR published after December 1 almost certainly falls outside the window.
- **Things to ignore:** a fan wiki (hypotheticalhurricanes.fandom.com) describes made-up 2026 "Fay" storms, including one that hit Puerto Rico. It is fiction and has no bearing on resolution.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **Tropical storms upgraded to hurricane in post-season analysis.** This is rare. Well-known Atlantic cases:
   - Cindy 2005, upgraded to Category 1 in a reanalysis announced around January 2006 (Insurance Journal, Jan 27, 2006).
   - Gamma 2020, raised from 69 mph to 75 mph in a TCR released April 2021 (weather.com / News4Jax, Apr 20, 2021).

   Roughly, out of about 15 Atlantic tropical storms a year over 20+ years (around 300 storms), only a handful were upgraded to hurricane after the fact. That is about 1–2% per storm. The odds are higher for storms that peaked near the threshold. Fay's operational peak was 60 kt, which is 5 kt short. Upgrades usually depend on aircraft, buoy or ground data found later. Fay was a mid-Atlantic storm near the Azores, so recon data are probably thin, though a SAR satellite pass on September 25 did measure about 55 kt.
2. **How fast TCRs are published.** Gamma's report came out about 6 months after the storm, and the Cindy reanalysis about 6 months after. NHC usually releases TCRs between December and the following spring. Short reports for storms that never threatened land sometimes come out earlier, but a release within about 2 months (late September to late November) is unusual. Even if Fay were upgraded, the chance the report appears before December 1 looks well under 50%.
3. **The combined chance** (upgrade × published in time) is plausibly well below 1%. The main remaining risk is a resolver error, not the weather.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)
- **Nov 20, 2026:** NHC's mobile page said there were no tropical cyclones in the Atlantic. The 2026 advisory archive lists Arthur through Isaias, plus Subtropical Storm Josephine. Of those, only Isaias is a hurricane (NHC mobile / 2026 advisory archive, Nov 2026).
- **Early November 2026:** the season had 10 named storms and 1 hurricane (Isaias). AccuWeather said the US threat was over (AccuWeather, Nov 2026; Ecoticias, Nov 2026).
- **Oct 7–8, 2026:** Isaias became the first hurricane of the season, the latest first hurricane on record (beating or tying 1905). It made landfall near Destin, Florida as a Category 2 on October 9 (KBTX, Oct 8, 2026; ITV, Oct 8, 2026). This confirms Fay, which came earlier, was never a hurricane in live advisories.
- **Sep 28–29, 2026:** NHC briefly raised Fay back to a 35-kt tropical storm, then went to post-tropical advisories. The final advisory (#39) on September 29 gave 30 mph and 1011 mb (NHC advisories, Sep 28–29, 2026).
- **Sep 25, 2026:** NHC's discussion cited a SAR pass showing about 55 kt and set the intensity at 50 kt (NHC discussion, Sep 25, 2026).
- **Sep 24, 2026:** "Fay regains tropical storm status," described as a "zombie storm" (NHC; AccuWeather, Sep 2026).
- **Sep 21–23, 2026:** Fay peaked at 60 kt (about 65–70 mph, minimum pressure about 997 mb) near the Azores. It "nearly ends record hurricane drought," then wind shear knocked it down to about 40 kt and it briefly became a remnant low (Local10, Sep 21, 2026; Nature World News, Sep 23, 2026; NHC advisory 007/008).
- **Sep 20, 2026:** Tropical Depression Six became Fay at 45 mph and 1004 mb. Forecasters expected shear and dry air to stop it becoming a hurricane (Fox13; WUSF, Sep 20, 2026).
- **Scheduled before close:** the season officially ends November 30. NHC has no fixed date for the Fay TCR. I could not find any published 2026 Atlantic TCRs.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
- **NO (dominant):** Fay stays a tropical storm (peak around 60 kt) in all official records through December 1. Either no TCR comes out before then, or it keeps the peak below 64 kt.
- **YES path A:** NHC publishes Fay's TCR before December 1 with a peak of at least 65 kt. This would need new data, such as scatterometer, SAR or ship reports, or a re-weighting of satellite estimates at the September 20–21 peak. Both the timing and the size of the change make this unlikely.
- **YES path B:** the resolver misreads something, for example Wikipedia vandalism, the fan wiki, or confusion with earlier hurricanes named Fay (2014 was a Category 1). This is a low but non-zero risk.

## 5. Signposts
- **Toward YES (strongly):** the Fay 2026 TCR appearing on nhc.noaa.gov/data/tcr with a peak of at least 65 kt, or the Wikipedia season page or infobox calling Fay a hurricane with an NHC citation.
- **Toward NO:** a TCR published with a peak at or below 60 kt, or nothing published by December 1.
- **Other:** any clarification from the market operator about which source it will use.

## 6. Uncertainties
- I could not reach NHC's TCR index or the Wikipedia season page directly (the proxy blocked them). So I could not confirm whether a Fay TCR exists or what its final best-track peak is.
- Sources disagree slightly on the peak: NHC said 60 kt, and media reported "65–70 mph."
- Sources also disagree on formation dates (September 17 vs 19–20) and on whether Fay was the "longest-lived" storm. Neither affects resolution.
- The resolution source is unspecified, so I could not judge how the resolver would treat a post-season reclassification.
