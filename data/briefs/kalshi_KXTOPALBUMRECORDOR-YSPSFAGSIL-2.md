# Research brief: Will Olivia Rodrigo's *you seem pretty sad for a girl so in love* hold #1 on the Billboard 200 for more than 2 straight weeks?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts:** The question is about the **album** *you seem pretty sad for a girl so in love* (Geffen/Interscope, released June 12, 2026) and its position on the **Billboard 200**. It is not about a song or the Hot 100.
- **YES condition:** The album must be #1 for **more than 2 consecutive weeks**, which means at least 3 chart weeks in a row.
- **The current streak:** It returned to #1 on the chart **dated Nov. 14, 2026**, after My Chemical Romance's *The Black Parade* was #1 on the Nov. 7 chart (Billboard, ~Nov. 8, 2026; Gold Derby 2026 list). The current streak is therefore 1 week. To resolve YES it must also be #1 on the charts dated **Nov. 21** and **Nov. 28**.
- **Timing:**
  - The Nov. 21 chart covers tracking week Nov. 6–12. Billboard usually announces the top of the chart on Sunday, Nov. 15.
  - The Nov. 28 chart covers Nov. 13–19 and should be revealed around Nov. 22.
  - Per the rules, "if this event occurs, or the streak ends, the market will close the following 10am ET." If the album misses #1 on Nov. 21, the market should close on NO around Nov. 16.
- **Who decides:** Kalshi, based on Billboard's published chart (Luminate data).
- **Edge cases:**
  - The album's first run (charts dated June 27 and July 4) lasted exactly 2 weeks. That does not meet "more than 2," so it cannot produce YES even if Kalshi counted it.
  - If the streak breaks on Nov. 21, a later return to #1 should not count, because the market closes when the streak ends.
  - A tie or a late chart correction is very unlikely.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
- **Midweek HITS projections that name a different #1.** HITS Daily Double's Tuesday preview has Lil Baby's *FRESH* ahead by about 11% (64.0k vs 57.2k units). In my experience, HITS midweek calls for #1 are usually right when the gap is above roughly 5–10%, and leader flips mostly happen in races within a few thousand units. I estimate the projected leader wins about 80–90% of races with a gap this size. **I did not verify this rate systematically.**
- **HITS tracks Billboard closely for this album.** HITS had the album at 56,655 units for the week ending Nov. 5, and Billboard reported 57,000 (HITS Top 50, Nov. 5, 2026; Billboard, Nov. 8, 2026). That gap is under 1%.
- **Late-run catalog albums with low unit totals.** The album has bounced in and out of #1 in its later run:
  - October 31 chart: #1 for a third, nonconsecutive week, helped by the "expectations" remix with Chappell Roan. HITS had projected 78k that week.
  - November 7 chart: lost #1 to the MCR reissue.
  - November 14 chart: back at #1 with only 57k units, down 10% week over week.
  
  An album at this level (about 50–60k a week) loses #1 to almost any big-name new release. Since October it has held #1 for only 1 week at a time.
- **Debut weeks with streaming-heavy rap albums.** Lil Baby's last two albums both opened at #1: *WHAM* with 140k and *It's Only Me* with 216k. HITS calls *FRESH* his potential "sixth straight #1." A projection of 60–64k is weak for him, which is why this is a contest at all.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
- **Path to NO (main scenario):** *FRESH* debuts at #1 on Nov. 21 as HITS projects, and the streak ends at 1 week. This only requires Lil Baby's lead of about 7k to hold over the last days of tracking. Two things make it more likely:
  - Any bulk sales or bundles would add to his lead.
  - Billboard may weight streams slightly differently from HITS.
- **Path to YES:** Both of these must happen.
  1. Rodrigo beats *FRESH* on Nov. 21. That could happen if *FRESH* fades faster than projected late in the week (rap debuts are front-loaded), or if Billboard's final figures are about 10% below HITS's for *FRESH* and/or above them for Rodrigo. Rodrigo's 8k in pure sales are stable, while *FRESH* depends heavily on streams.
  2. She then holds #1 on Nov. 28 against the Nov. 13 releases. This looks likely if step 1 happens, because no major release was found. Risks:
     - the *Hunger Games* soundtrack (depending on its artists)
     - Wallen or Langley
     - holiday catalog albums rising (Thanksgiving week is the Nov. 20–26 tracking week, so mostly after Nov. 28)
     - her own decline continuing at about 10% a week, putting her near 50k
- **Structure:** P(YES) ≈ P(#1 on Nov. 21) × P(#1 on Nov. 28 given #1 on Nov. 21). The first factor is the binding constraint.

## 5. Signposts
- **Sunday, Nov. 15:** Billboard's announcement of the Nov. 21 #1 decides almost everything. If *FRESH* is #1, the outcome is effectively NO. If Rodrigo is #1, YES becomes likely.
- **Before that:** Updated HITS figures (a final-week "Top 50" post or Talk of the Charts tweets around Nov. 13–15) that narrow or widen the 64k vs 57k gap. Final *FRESH* units below about 58k would favor YES. Final units of 70k or more, or confirmed bulk sales, would favor NO.
- **If Rodrigo wins Nov. 21:** The HITS preview around Nov. 17 for the Nov. 28 chart. Watch for a surprise release on Nov. 13 or a soundtrack surge.
- **Any announced Rodrigo deluxe edition, vinyl restock or new remix** during Nov. 13–19 would favor YES. None was found.

## 6. Uncertainties
- I could not open the Billboard, HITS or Wikipedia pages directly (the fetches were refused). Figures come from search-result summaries of those pages and could contain small errors.
- I did not find a later HITS update or final number after the Nov. 10 preview. Projections can move by more than 5–10% between Tuesday and the final count.
- How Billboard's stream weighting compares with HITS's for a streaming-heavy debut is unverified, though Rodrigo's figures matched almost exactly last week.
- The full Nov. 13 release slate may include albums missing from the lists I found, such as surprise drops or K-pop releases with heavy physical sales.
- How Kalshi handles edge cases (for example, counting nonconsecutive weeks) is not spelled out beyond "consecutive." The plain reading is that the current streak must reach 3 weeks.
