## Research brief: Will Waymo operate in 12–15 cities on December 31, 2026?

**Summary:** Credible reporting puts Waymo at **15 publicly available markets** since September 14, 2026. That is the top of the 12–15 bucket. The question resolves YES unless a 16th city opens publicly in the next ~5 weeks, or a strict reading of "invite-only" drops the count below 12. The strict reading looks very unlikely to go that low.

Note on sourcing: WebFetch was blocked in this environment, so every fact below comes from search-result summaries of the cited articles, not from reading the pages directly. That matters most for the city-by-city "open to everyone" status.

---

### 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts:** distinct cities or regions where the general public can book a driverless Waymo ride, through the Waymo One app or a partner such as Uber, at 11:59 PM ET on **December 31, 2026**. YES needs a count of **12, 13, 14 or 15**.
- **Excluded:** limited pilots, employee-only rides, and invite-only service. Membership or other financial restrictions do not disqualify a city.
- **Regions:** if Waymo describes a broader area as one service area (e.g. "San Francisco Bay Area" or "Los Angeles"), it counts once. Silicon Valley and San Jose would normally fold into the Bay Area.
- **Source:** official Waymo information first, then a consensus of credible reporting.
- **Edge cases and ambiguities:**
  - **Invite-code rollouts.** Waymo has opened several cities to "public riders" while admitting people from the waitlist gradually. Las Vegas launched on September 14, 2026 with invite codes (Las Vegas Sun, Sep 14 2026). Denver's status as of October/November is reported inconsistently: some trackers say invite-only, one October list says open to everyone. A strict resolver could exclude these cities. That would lower the count, but probably to 13–14, still inside the bucket.
  - **Lyft in Nashville.** The rules name Waymo One and Uber, but also say "any taxi service available to the general public… will count." Nashville is also bookable in the Waymo app, so this likely doesn't matter.
  - **Waymo's own counts.** Media counts ("14 cities" on September 1, "15th market" on September 14) generally match the resolution definition. Aggregator lists differ: one August tracker cited Waymo's service-area page as listing only 9 directly operated areas, which looks like a narrower definition.
  - **Freeway suspension.** The November 2026 recall and freeway pause do not remove any city, because surface-street service continues.

### 2. Reference classes and base rates
- **Waymo's announced-for-2026 launches, completed versus slipped.** The 2025–26 pipeline included Miami, Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, Orlando, Nashville, Denver, San Diego, Tampa, Las Vegas, Detroit, Washington DC and London.
  - Launched publicly so far: about 10 of 13 (Miami, Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, Orlando, Nashville, Denver, San Diego, Tampa, Las Vegas).
  - Slipped to 2027 or blocked: Detroit (Axios Detroit, Nov 20 2026) and DC (council committee voted the bill down on Nov 12, 2026).
  - Still pending: London.
  - Roughly 75–80% on-time delivery. Misses are concentrated where regulation is uncertain (DC, UK) or the climate is hard (Detroit winter).
- **Pace of launches in 2026.** Public launches came in batches:
  - Miami: January (from my background knowledge, not confirmed in this search)
  - Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, Orlando: February 24, initially "select riders" (CNBC, Feb 24 2026)
  - Nashville: sometime in 2026 (TheStreet; exact date not established)
  - Denver, San Diego, Tampa: September 1 (Electrek, Sep 1 2026)
  - Las Vegas: September 14

  That is about 5 launch events in roughly 11 months, about one every two months. There has been no new public city in the ~10 weeks since September 14. A naive Poisson rate (~0.45 launch events per month) gives roughly a 40–50% chance of *some* launch event in a 5-week window. This overstates the real chance, because almost all near-term candidates have been ruled out or are unconfirmed (see section 3).
- **Big-company "by end of year" targets for regulator-dependent launches in new countries.** These often slip. London already moved from a "September 2026" commercial target to "by the end of the year." A FutureSearch analysis rated a late-2026 London launch as marginal and late 2027 as more realistic (FutureSearch, undated). Rough base rate for hitting a once-slipped year-end target that needs a new permitting regime (the UK's APS permit): perhaps 20–35%.

### 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)
- **Nov 24, 2026:** Waymo recalled about 4,500 robotaxis after 11 incidents of entering closed freeway lanes since October 1. Commercial freeway rides are suspended with no restoration timeline. This follows a June 2026 recall of about 3,900 vehicles (GV Wire / Reuters via US News, Nov 24 2026). The California DMV also issued an order tied to construction-zone incidents. Nothing reported suggests any city is being withdrawn.
- **Nov 19–20, 2026:** Waymo told Axios Detroit that public rides in Detroit will start in **2027**. Detroit has had employee-only fully driverless operation since about October 6, 2026 (Axios Detroit, Nov 20 2026; Detroit News, Oct 6 2026).
- **Nov 12, 2026:** the DC Council transportation committee voted down the AV deployment bill. Waymo can only test in DC, and one advocate did not expect a new vote before about September 2027 (Washington Sun, Nov 2026).
- **November 2026:** Waymo is described as "gearing up" for Toronto, with no launch date (The Eyeopener, Nov 2026).
- **Oct 14, 2026:** the Waymo Help Center announced that Tampa, San Diego and San Antonio are open to everyone with no invite code (Waymo Help announcements, Oct 14 2026).
- **October 2026:** trackers count **15 cities** and name Chicago, Charlotte and London as next (driverlessrider.com, Oct 2026; Fifth Level Consulting, as of Oct 21 2026).
- **Sep 14, 2026:** Las Vegas opened to public riders as the **15th market**. More than 100,000 people signed up and are being admitted in batches via invite codes (Las Vegas Sun, Sep 14 2026; News3LV).
- **Sep 1, 2026:** Denver, San Diego and Tampa opened to public riders, for **14 cities** in total. Waymo said "tens of thousands" had signed up and it would "steadily offer more and more riders" access (Electrek, Sep 1 2026; Repairer Driven News, Sep 4 2026).
- **Aug 4 and Aug 20, 2026:** Dallas, then Houston, opened to everyone (TechCrunch, Aug 4 2026; Waymo Help).
- **Aug 18, 2026 (latest London status found):** no commercial London rides yet. About 100 Jaguars were testing with specialists on board, Waymo was working toward an APS permit, and a spokesperson said the launch is targeted "by the end of the year" (Carcody / Cloudspress, Aug 2026; Waymo UK page).
- **Seattle:** one low-quality source (af.net, undated and with garbled content) claims Seattle is the 16th city. No credible outlet corroborates it, and October lists still show 15. Treat as unverified and probably false.
- **Current list of 15:** Phoenix, SF Bay Area, Los Angeles, Austin (Uber), Atlanta (Uber), Miami, Dallas, Houston, San Antonio, Orlando, Nashville, Denver, San Diego, Tampa, Las Vegas.
- **Scheduled before December 31:** no announced launch date for any new city. London is the only market with a public "2026" commitment still open.

### 4. Drivers and scenarios
- **YES, status quo at 15 (most likely path).** No new city goes public before New Year's Eve. London slips to 2027 and Toronto, Chicago, Charlotte and Seattle aren't ready. This depends on Waymo's usual pattern of employee-only operation, then invite rollout, then opening to all, which takes months. None of the pipeline cities is known to be in employee-only driverless operation except Detroit, which is explicitly 2027.
- **YES, strict reading of invite-only.** If Las Vegas, Denver or others are still gated on December 31 and the resolver excludes them, the count is about 13–14. That is still YES. Falling below 12 would require four or more of the 15 to be excluded, which looks implausible given the Help Center "open to everyone" notices for Dallas, Houston, Tampa, San Diego and San Antonio, plus the older markets.
- **NO, a 16th city launches.** The most plausible candidate is London, launched "by end of year" to meet the 2026 pledge. That needs the APS permit and local consent, and the first riders could still be invite-only. An invite-only London launch might itself not count under the rules, which further lowers this risk. Less plausible candidates: a surprise Seattle, Chicago, Charlotte or Toronto launch, or reclassifying a sub-region (e.g. a separately named Silicon Valley or Orange County area) as a new market.
- **NO, the count drops below 12.** This would need a mass suspension, such as regulators halting service in several states. The November recall is freeway-specific and probably a software fix, so this risk looks very small.

### 5. Signposts
- **Toward NO (strongly):**
  - A UK APS permit announcement, or Waymo or TfL announcing London public rides in December.
  - Any Waymo blog post or Help Center notice of a new city "welcoming public riders."
  - Credible confirmation of Seattle or any 16th-market launch.
- **Toward NO (mildly):** Waymo or the media reclassifying service areas into more distinct "cities."
- **Toward YES:**
  - Waymo or UK reports saying London rides start in 2027.
  - Waymo's year-end recap listing 15 markets.
  - The freeway recall dragging into December. That suggests caution about expansion, but doesn't remove cities.
- **Ambiguity watch:** whether Waymo or major outlets still call Las Vegas or Denver "invite-only" in December. This only matters for a strict count, which stays at 12 or more.

### 6. Uncertainties
- I could not open any page directly. Exact statuses (especially Denver, Las Vegas, Orlando, Nashville and Miami being open to everyone versus invite-only) come from search summaries, and some aggregator sources conflict.
- London's status after mid-August 2026 is unknown. No October or November reporting turned up, and the APS permit status is unconfirmed.
- The Seattle "16th city" claim is unverified, and I could not rule it out definitively.
- Nashville's launch date and whether it is fully open are not established.
- It's unclear how the resolver will treat a launch with invite-code access, and whether Waymo's own count will include sub-regions as separate markets.
