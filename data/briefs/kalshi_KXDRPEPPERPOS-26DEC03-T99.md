## Research brief: Will Carbon Arc's Dr Pepper Point of Sale Growth index for November 2026 be above 99?

### 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What counts as YES:** Carbon Arc's Dr Pepper Point of Sale Growth index for November 2026 must be strictly above 99. The index is year-over-year (YoY) spend, where 100 means no change from November 2025. So YES means Dr Pepper spend in the panel fell by less than about 1%, or grew. A reading of exactly 99.0 resolves NO.
- **Who decides and when:** Kalshi resolves on the "finalized Carbon Arc values" recorded at 11:59 p.m. ET on December 2, 2026. The contract closes December 3, 2026 at 04:59 UTC. A related Carbon Arc contract states that later corrections or revisions do not change the recorded value (Public.com nicotine array listing, Nov 2026). Presumably the same rule applies here.
- **Data source:** Carbon Arc's receipt panel, covering in-store and online purchases captured through receipt-scanning and rewards apps and matched to the same period a year earlier. The panel updates daily.
- **Edge cases and ambiguities:**
  - **Late receipts:** The value is fixed only two days after month-end. Receipts for the last days of November (Thanksgiving week, Black Friday) may not all be in. If the November 2025 base is fully filled in and November 2026 is not, that could pull the reading down. I could not confirm whether Carbon Arc normalizes for this lag.
  - **Brand scope:** It is not defined whether "Dr Pepper" includes Zero Sugar and the flavor line extensions (Strawberries & Cream, Blackberry, and so on). It probably includes them, but this is not confirmed.
  - **Panel changes:** The panel's makeup (which apps, which users, how many) may shift from year to year.
  - **Track record:** These contracts are new. Kalshi announced the Carbon Arc sector KPI markets around late October 2026 (Kalshi announcement; Markets Media, ~Oct 26, 2026). There is little or no public history of how this index behaves or how it compares with company-reported sales.

### 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **KDP's U.S. Refreshment Beverages net sales growth.** Management called Q3 2026 the segment's "eighth consecutive quarter of double-digit top line growth" (KDP Q3 2026 call, Nov 3, 2026). So the segment has been above "index 100" in 8 of the last 8 quarters, and by a wide margin. Dr Pepper itself had double-digit net sales growth in Q3 2026. It has gained U.S. soda share steadily since about 2020 and passed Pepsi as the #2 soda in 2024 (Fortune, Jun 4, 2024). For the brand at the quarterly, company-reported level, the base rate of YoY decline worse than −1% in recent years is close to 0 out of ~12 quarters.
2. **U.S. carbonated soft drink (CSD) dollar sales.** Circana grocery CSD dollar sales were up 4.9% YoY in Q3 2026 (Abasto/Circana, Nov 25, 2026). CSD dollar sales grew 6.2% in the 52 weeks to April 2025. Category dollar sales have grown YoY in almost every quarter since 2021, helped by price increases.
3. **Single-brand monthly readings from a receipt panel.** This is the most relevant class, but I found no historical data. Brand-level monthly YoY readings from consumer panels are much noisier than company sales or scanner data. A spread of roughly ±3–6 index points around the true trend is plausible but not verified. Even with a true trend of +5–10%, a single month from a panel can land below 99.

### 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)
- **Nov 25, 2026:** Circana Q3 2026 grocery data shows carbonated beverages at $9.83B, up $461.5M or 4.9% YoY. They were the second-largest dollar gainer after energy drinks, which grew 17.4% (Abasto).
- **Nov 3, 2026, KDP Q3 results:**
  - U.S. Refreshment Beverages net sales rose 10.9% to $3.03B: volume/mix +7.2%, price +3.7%, raw volume +3.2%.
  - Carbonated soft drinks grew, led by Dr Pepper, Dr Pepper Zero Sugar and Canada Dry. The CEO said Dr Pepper led with double-digit net sales growth and continued share gains.
  - Excluding the JDE Peet's acquisition, total company sales rose 8.6%. 2026 guidance was reaffirmed. U.S. Coffee is the weak spot.
  - Sources: KDP 8-K and press release; 10-Q; Fool and Benzinga transcripts; Zacks.
  - One source attributes "Dr Pepper Zero retail sales +25%, >$1B annualized" to Q3 2026, but this may be a 2025 quote. Treat it with caution.
- **Nov 3, 2026:** State waivers barring soda purchases with SNAP benefits expanded to four more states. Earlier research found:
  - Q1 2026 soda spend per SNAP household in waiver states fell 8%.
  - NBER estimates show a 12–13% drop in restricted-drink purchases by SNAP households.
  - An August 2026 NBER paper counts about 23 waiver states.
  - KDP's CEO says the effect is "limited" and signals are "mixed."
  - Sources: FoodNavigator, Nov 3, 2026; NBER w35613; Food Dive.
- **Aug 2026:** Grocery volume declines deepened as SNAP eligibility cuts under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA) changed shopper behavior (FoodNavigator, Aug 21, 2026). This is a headwind for lower-income households.
- **Oct 8, 2026:** PepsiCo announced single-digit price increases on some sodas, citing aluminum, fuel and commodity costs (Fortune). KDP's CSD pricing was +3.7% in Q3, supporting dollar-based YoY.
- **Q1 2026:** U.S. Refreshment Beverages net sales +11.9% (volume/mix +7.2%, price +4.7%) (Fortune, Apr 23, 2026).
- **November 2025 base month (easier comparison):**
  - The federal shutdown (Oct 1 to ~Nov 12, 2025) left November 1, 2025 SNAP benefits unpaid or delayed.
  - Numerator found SNAP-household grocery spend down about 10%, with beverages among the categories hit hardest.
  - Some convenience stores saw sales fall about 10% after Nov 1.
  - Sources: Numerator; AOL/Reuters, Nov 2025.
  - This depressed base should lift November 2026 YoY, especially if the receipt panel over-represents value-seeking or lower-income shoppers.
- **Calendar:**
  - November 2025 had 5 Saturdays and 5 Sundays. November 2026 has 5 Sundays and 5 Mondays but only 4 Saturdays, which is a small negative.
  - Thanksgiving (Nov 27, 2025 vs Nov 26, 2026) and Black Friday fall inside the month both years.
- **Scheduled before close:** There is no KDP earnings report before December 3. Q4 results are expected around late February 2027. The only remaining "event" is the rest of November's data coming in, including Thanksgiving-week purchases.

### 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES (index above 99):**
- Dr Pepper's underlying momentum shows up in the panel: double-digit net sales, share gains, Zero Sugar growth, and about 3–4% price increases.
- The weak November 2025 base caused by the SNAP lapse adds a few points.
- Category CSD dollar growth of about 5% offers a cushion even if Dr Pepper only matches the category.
- If the panel tracks retail dollars reasonably well, the expected reading is roughly 103–110.

**Paths to NO (index at or below 99):**
- **Panel effects:** Receipt-app panels can drift from actual sales through changes in who is in the panel, the retailer mix, or how receipts are captured. Single-brand monthly readings are noisy.
- **SNAP headwinds:** Soda restrictions now cover roughly 20+ states, and OBBBA eligibility cuts weigh on lower-income buyers. Receipt-app users may be over-weighted toward these shoppers, making the hit larger in the panel than in total sales.
- **Late receipts:** If the value is locked on December 2 with late-November receipts missing and no normalization, the reading could be pulled down.
- **Comparison quirks:** A strong promotion or limited-time flavor in November 2025 (for example, a holiday seasonal item) could make the comparison harder. One fewer Saturday is a small extra negative.
- **Shift in the sales mix:** KDP's growth is partly fountain/foodservice and partly energy drinks, not in-store Dr Pepper. Company net sales may overstate what shows up in retail receipts. Shipment timing also differs from consumer takeaway.

### 5. Signposts
- **Carbon Arc's running reading for November 2026,** if a forecaster can legitimately see it (not market prices). This is by far the strongest signal, since the panel updates daily.
- **Earlier monthly readings of the same index** (for example, September or October 2026, if they were published or settled). These show whether the panel tracks KDP's double-digit growth or comes in much lower. A history of readings near 100 would point clearly toward NO risk.
- **Late-November Circana or Nielsen CSD and Dr Pepper takeaway data.** Dollar growth of +5% or more supports YES; flat or negative weakens it.
- **Signs that November 2025 data captured the SNAP lapse.** A depressed base supports YES.
- **News of a big November 2025 Dr Pepper promotion, or retailer or distribution disruptions in November 2026.** Either would point toward NO.

### 6. Uncertainties
- I could not find any published historical values of the Carbon Arc Dr Pepper index, nor any check of how well it tracks Nielsen, Circana or KDP figures. This is the key unknown.
- I could not confirm how Carbon Arc handles late receipts, changes in panel size, or brand definitions such as including Zero Sugar and flavors.
- I found no Dr Pepper-specific retail takeaway data for October or November 2026, only quarterly company results and category data.
- The size of the SNAP-restriction effect on Dr Pepper specifically, and how heavily the panel weights SNAP households, is unknown.
- I could not open several primary sources (the KDP press release, the call transcripts and the Kalshi announcement) because fetches were refused. The figures above come from search-result extracts of those documents.
