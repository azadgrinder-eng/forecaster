# Research Brief: Will any part of the US federal government take a stake above 0% in TSMC before Jan 1, 2027?

**Bottom line:** As of Nov 19, 2026, no source reports a US government stake in TSMC, a plan for one, or talks about one. The White House said in August 2025 that it was not seeking equity in TSMC, and nothing found since contradicts that. About six weeks remain. The main risk to a NO resolution is not a new deal. It is the possibility that the resolver counts index-fund holdings of TSMC by federal entities such as the TSP or PBGC (Section 1).

**Research limitation:** Every WebFetch call failed with "proxy refused the connection." Everything below comes from search-result summaries, not from reading the full articles or primary documents.

## 1. Resolution mechanics

**What counts as YES**
- Any part of the US federal government has taken, or announced it is taking, an equity stake above 0% in TSMC before Jan 1, 2027 (close is 2027-01-01 04:59 UTC).
- An announcement before the deadline counts even if the deal closes later.
- Qualifying forms: direct equity, voting shares, equivalent ownership interests, convertible rights treated as equity, and indirect ownership through wholly controlled investment vehicles.
- Pre-existing stakes count. Crossing the threshold at any point counts. Bonds and loans do not.
- Stakes held by sovereign wealth funds or state-owned enterprises attributed to the federal government count.

**Who decides:** The rules don't say. Presumably the market's resolver, based on public reporting.

**Edge cases and ambiguities**
- **Passive holdings by federal entities (the main ambiguity).**
  - The TSP I Fund, run by the Federal Retirement Thrift Investment Board (an independent federal agency), has tracked the MSCI ACWI IMI ex USA ex China ex Hong Kong index since 2024. That index includes Taiwan, and TSMC is reportedly its top holding (TSPTalk forum, undated; FedSmith, Mar 29, 2024).
  - PBGC, a federal corporation, invests its trust fund mainly in equities through outside managers (PBGC Investment Policy). I could not confirm that it holds TSMC.
  - Other federal trusts, such as TVA's, may also hold TSMC through index funds.
  - Arguments that these shouldn't count: the assets are held in trust for beneficiaries, mostly through commingled funds rather than wholly controlled vehicles, and nobody has "announced" taking a stake.
  - The question's wording ("take a stake"), the template, and the 2025 news context all suggest it is about a policy stake like the one in Intel. A literal-minded resolver could still treat "pre-existing stakes count" as covering these holdings. This is the largest non-news path to YES.
- **Indirect stakes through other companies.** The US owns about 9.9% of Intel. I found no evidence that Intel owns TSMC shares. A revived Intel–TSMC joint venture would give the government a stake in the venture, not in TSMC.
- **Subsidiaries.** "Stake in TSMC" presumably means TSMC the listed parent (TWSE 2330, NYSE TSM). A US stake in TSMC Arizona would be ambiguous. Nothing like that has been proposed.
- **Credit support.** The Taiwan credit guarantees under the US–Taiwan trade deal are debt-style support, not equity, so they don't qualify.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **CHIPS Act grant recipients the administration said it was considering for equity (Aug 2025: Intel, Samsung, TSMC, Micron).**
   - 1 of 4 (Intel) gave the government equity, converting $8.9B of grants into a 9.9% stake in August 2025.
   - 0 of 3 foreign or "investment-boosting" firms have done so in roughly 15 months.
   - Micron's FY2026 10-K says its funding of up to $6.1B is unchanged (Micron 10-K, Sept 2026). I found no Samsung deal.
2. **The administration's equity deals overall.**
   - Count: 14 companies in January 2026 rose to 41 by November 2026 (Cato count via search summaries, Oct–Nov 2026). One tracker counts 43 deals totalling about $30B.
   - Pace: about 2–3 new names per month. Almost all are small or mid-size US strategic firms (critical minerals, quantum, chip R&D startups, defense units), not mega-caps.
   - Foreign firms are rare: about 1 of roughly 41. The exception is Korea Zinc, where a joint venture controlled by the Pentagon and US investors bought about 10% (Al Jazeera, Dec 15, 2025; Skillings, 2026). Commentators called a US stake in a foreign company "rare."
   - No foreign mega-cap (TSMC's market value is over $1T) has received a US stake.
3. **Big foreign investment pledges under Trump trade deals (Japan, Korea, Taiwan).**
   - These have used investment funds, credit guarantees, and tariff relief, not US equity in the national champion companies.
   - In the Taiwan deal (Jan 2026), the 0 of 1 relevant cases involved equity in TSMC.

**Implied base rate:** For a specific foreign mega-cap the government has explicitly exempted, the chance of a new announced stake in a roughly six-week window is very low, well under 5% on frequencies alone.

  - The WSJ reports TSMC executives discussed returning their subsidies rather than give up equity.

**Scheduled before close:** TSMC's November revenue release (around Dec 10), TSMC's January 2027 earnings (after close), the defense bill (NDAA) conference and passage in December, and possible personnel changes at Commerce. I found no scheduled event aimed at TSMC equity.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES**
1. **Literal-reading resolution (most material).** The resolver counts existing passive holdings by the TSP I Fund, PBGC, or another federal trust. This depends entirely on how the resolver reads the rules; nothing has to happen in the world. It is a meaningful tail risk, but most resolvers of this kind of template look for a news-reported policy stake.
2. **Renewed CHIPS grant-for-equity push.** Commerce demands equity for TSMC's remaining $6.6B grant. This contradicts the stated exemption and TSMC's preference to return the money. Taiwan's government and TSMC's 2,000+ foreign institutional shareholders would resist. It is very unlikely in six weeks.
3. **A Taiwan-security or "golden share" style arrangement.** For example, national-security leverage tied to chip export controls or the Nvidia scandal. This is speculative, and no reporting supports it.
4. **The US buying TSMC shares on the market** through a strategic fund or a proposed US sovereign wealth fund. No such vehicle is active for foreign mega-caps.

**Paths to NO (the default)**
- The status quo holds. The administration gets leverage over TSMC through tariffs and investment pledges ($265B), not equity.
- TSMC and Taiwan have firmly opposed equity in the past.
- Commerce leadership is distracted by the scandal, and political pushback on state stakes is growing after the midterms.
- Only about six weeks remain, including the holidays.

## 5. Signposts

- **Toward YES (large):**
  - Any report from Reuters, Bloomberg, or WSJ that Commerce or the White House is seeking TSMC equity, warrants, or a golden share.
  - Taiwan's Executive Yuan or Ministry of Economic Affairs commenting on US equity talks.
  - A TSMC 6-K disclosing a US holder or a share issuance to a US entity.
- **Toward YES (resolution risk):** Any sign the resolver accepts index-fund or pension holdings, such as similar template questions on other companies resolving YES on that basis.
- **Toward NO:**
  - TSMC receiving CHIPS grant payments without equity.
  - Lutnick leaving with a successor who is cooler on stakes.
  - Congress restricting equity deals.
  - Year-end arriving with no news.
- **Moderate toward YES:** A revived Intel–TSMC tie-up that includes TSMC equity going to a government-controlled vehicle.

## 6. Uncertainties

One low-quality tracker (themoneygps) reportedly lists TSMC because of the $6.6B grant. That appears to confuse a grant with equity.
- I could not confirm whether the TSP I Fund holds TSMC directly or through a BlackRock commingled fund, or whether PBGC or TVA trusts hold TSMC.
- I don't know the 2026 midterm results or the exact status of the Senate NDAA provisions.
- I couldn't confirm whether TSMC's $6.6B CHIPS grant has been fully paid out or renegotiated.
- I don't know how the resolver will apply "pre-existing stakes count" to passive holdings by federal trusts.
- The reported Nvidia CEO resignation and the Lutnick scandal are only partly verified.
