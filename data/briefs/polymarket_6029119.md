## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **Contest:** Burgos is one of Spain's Congress constituencies and elects **4 deputies** by D'Hondt (3% threshold per constituency). That is the same number as in 2023 (Test Electoral, Nov 2026). Nine lists are running. The main lead candidates are Ángel Mariano Ibáñez (PP), Esther Peña (PSOE, an incumbent deputy) and Eugenia Landaluce (Vox) (Test Electoral, Nov 2026).
- **Date:** The election is confirmed. Pedro Sánchez announced on 5 Oct 2026 that the Cortes would be dissolved and an election held on **Sun 29 Nov 2026**. The legal basis is Real Decreto 806/2026, published in the BOE on 6 Oct (La Moncloa, 5 Oct 2026; eldiario.es, 5 Oct 2026). He called it early after two housing decrees were defeated in Congress.
- **How it resolves YES:** The PP list has to rank first in Burgos.
  - It wins outright with more seats than any other list.
  - It also wins if it ties on seats and has more valid votes. With 4 seats, the realistic tie is 2–2 with PSOE, so in that case the higher vote total wins.
  - In practice, PP resolves YES whenever it is the most-voted list in Burgos. It also resolves YES if it is narrowly second in votes but still converts that into more seats, which is rare with 4 seats.
  - It resolves NO only if PSOE (or, implausibly, Vox) gets more seats, or ties on seats and has more votes.
- **Who decides:** A consensus of credible reporting. If that is ambiguous, the Interior Ministry's infoelectoral site decides.
  - Provisional results come out on election night (29 Nov).
  - The Junta Electoral Provincial then runs the official count and proclaims the winners, usually within about 2 weeks. The full official results appear in the BOE weeks later.
  - The "Other" fallback only applies if results are not known by 30 Nov 2027, so it is practically irrelevant.
- **Edge cases:**
  - PP runs under its own name in Castilla y León, so there is no coalition or attribution issue.
  - The market's close date (30 Nov 04:59 UTC) is just after election night. Provisional results will be known by then. Reversing a clear provisional winner in a 4-seat constituency through the official count has essentially never happened.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Most-voted party in Burgos at Congress elections (recent era)**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2011 | 54.2% | — | — | PP 3 |
| 2015 | 38.0% | — | — | PP 2 |
| 2016 | 42.9% | — | — | PP 2 |
| Apr 2019 | — | ≈29.3% | — | PSOE 2, PP 1, Cs 1 |
| Nov 2019 | ≈30.8% | 32.3% (64,182 votes) | Vox ≈ high teens | PSOE 2, PP 1, Vox 1 |
| 2023 | 40.6% (82,329 votes) | 34.5% (69,775 votes) | Vox ≈14.9%, no seat | PP 2, PSOE 2 |

- PP finished first in 2011, 2015, 2016 and 2023. In 2023 it ranked first on the vote tie-break.
- PSOE led in both 2019 elections, and possibly in April 2019 as well; PP's April 2019 share was not confirmed.
- Sources: Wikipedia constituency page via search snippets; Última Hora 23J results; The Objective, 23 Jul 2023. Snippets disagreed slightly: Última Hora gives PP 40.72% and 81,626 votes for 2023.
- **Rough base rate:** PP or its predecessors topped Burgos in most elections since the late 1980s. The only clear recent exceptions are the two 2019 elections, when PP had collapsed nationally to 16.7–20.8%. I could not verify 1977–1986 (UCD-era and 1982/86 PSOE landslides). Estimate: PP first in about 9 of the last 11 elections (≈80%). Both misses came when PP was at historic national lows.

**B. Burgos's lean relative to the national PP–PSOE gap**

| Election | National gap | Burgos gap | Burgos lean |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | PP +1.4 (33.1 vs 31.7) | PP +6.2 | ≈5 pts more PP |
| Nov 2019 | PSOE +7.9 | PSOE +1.5 | ≈6 pts more PP |

- Rule of thumb: PSOE needs to beat PP nationally by roughly 5–6 points to win Burgos on votes.

**C. Recent elections in Castilla y León**

- At the regional election of 15 Mar 2026, PP won Burgos province with 35.12% and 5 of 11 seats, against PSOE 4 and Vox 2 (RTVC, 15 Mar 2026; eldiario.es, 15 Mar 2026).
- RTVC called it an "ajustada victoria" (a narrow win). The PSOE share for Burgos was not found, and the wording implies a gap of only a few points.
- Region-wide: PP 35.45%, 33 seats; PSOE 30; Vox 14.
- So even in an election where PSOE "resisted", PP kept first place in Burgos.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**YES paths (dominant):**
1. **PP 2, PSOE 1, Vox 1 (the consensus).** PP wins outright on seats. For this, Vox needs to beat half of PSOE's vote, which happens once Vox exceeds about 15–16% when PSOE is near 30%.
2. **PP 2, PSOE 2 (a repeat of 2023).** PP still wins if it outpolls PSOE in Burgos, which it did by 6 points in 2023 and is projected to by 6–11 now.
3. **PP 2, Vox 1, PSOE 1 with PP below 40%.** Still YES.

Note that PP keeps 2 seats unless its vote falls below about twice the third-largest quotient. That would require PP to collapse to the low 30s while PSOE and Vox both run high.

**NO paths (all need a large polling miss or a late shift):**
1. **PSOE beats PP on votes in Burgos with a 2–2 split, or wins 2 seats to PP's 1.** This needs PSOE to lead PP nationally by about 5–6 points. Current polls show PSOE trailing by 2–8, so that is a swing of roughly 7–14 points from today's polls.
   - For scale: in 2023 polls understated PSOE versus PP by about 4–5 points on the gap. A miss that size would still leave PP ahead in Burgos.
2. **Vox outpolls PP in Burgos.** That needs Vox at about 30% or more and PP below it. No poll suggests this, so it is implausible.
3. **Collapse of PP into Vox (PP 1, Vox 2, or Vox first).** Not supported by any data.
4. **Administrative "Other" (results not known by Nov 2027).** Negligible.

## 5. Signposts

- **Toward NO:**
  - Any Burgos-specific poll (Diario de Burgos, El Correo de Burgos, CIS provincial breakdowns) with a PP–PSOE gap under 3 points.
  - National polls before the 24 Nov blackout showing PSOE level with or ahead of PP. The 40dB trend is the one to watch.
  - A big late campaign event that damages PP, such as a scandal involving Feijóo or the PP, or a PP–Vox pact controversy that pushes moderate voters to PSOE.
  - Vox surging well above 22% at PP's expense.
- **Toward YES (confirming):**
  - The CIS pre-election macro-survey, if it gives provincial seat allocations.
  - Further GAD3, Sigma Dos or 40dB provincial maps showing Burgos at PP 2, PSOE 1, Vox 1.
- **Election night:**
  - The provisional count in Burgos is usually clear by about 22:00 CET on 29 Nov.
  - Burgos city counts early. Its results lean somewhat more PSOE than the rural province (PSOE topped the city in 2019), so an early city lead for PSOE would not settle the province.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not retrieve the vote shares from the GAD3 Burgos or University of Burgos polls; my tools blocked fetching those pages. So I don't know the projected PP–PSOE margin in Burgos, only the seat split.
- Provincial PSOE and Vox shares from the March 2026 regional election in Burgos are unknown. "Ajustada victoria" suggests PP led by only a few points there, although regional elections differ: the PSOE candidate was not Sánchez, and turnout was different.
- Historical Burgos winners before 2011 (1977–2008), and PP's share in April 2019, could not be verified from primary tables. Search snippets also gave conflicting 2023 figures (PP 40.64% vs 40.72%), and one snippet mislabelled Nov 2019 numbers as 2023.
- The effect of the Urda crash and Puente's resignation on PSOE is still unsettled: pollsters disagree on whether PSOE is falling or holding steady.
- Provincial polling in 4-seat constituencies has small samples and large error margins, often ±5 points per party.
