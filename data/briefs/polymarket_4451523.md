# Research brief: Will Houthi forces enter Aden by December 31, 2026?

*Prepared November 24, 2026. The web page reader was blocked in this environment, so every article below was read only through search-result summaries, not in full. Dates and figures come from those summaries. Where sources disagree, the brief says so.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Houthi military forces, or any armed force acting under their direction or on their behalf, deliberately operate inside any part of seven Aden districts. The districts are Crater (Sirah), Tawahi, Mualla, Khor Maksar, Sheikh Othman, Dar Saad and Mansura. Even a brief entry counts. This must happen by 11:59 PM ET on December 31, 2026, which is 37 days from today.
- **Excluded:**
  - Al-Buraiqeh (Little Aden) on its own. An approach along the coast from the west would reach Buraiqeh first, so the line between Buraiqeh and Mansura/Sheikh Othman matters.
  - Missile, drone or artillery strikes on the city. Aden is already being hit, so this exclusion matters right now.
  - Sabotage, naval presence offshore, and temporary disruption.
  - Claims or threats of entry without confirmation.
  - Emissaries, negotiators or commercial delegations.
  - Prisoners of war.
- **Districts most exposed to a land advance:** Dar Saad and Sheikh Othman in the north border Lahj, so an approach from the north would probably reach them first. In 2015 the Houthis also entered through the isthmus and airport area of Khor Maksar.
- **Who decides:** "A consensus of credible reporting." If entry is genuinely ambiguous, the market can stay open for three more days, to January 3, 2027. If entry is not established by then, it resolves NO.
- **Edge cases:**
  - Infiltration by sleeper cells, a commando raid, or an assassination team inside the city. These might count as "armed force operating on its behalf" and "deliberately operate," but sabotage alone does not count. This is the most likely source of disputes.
  - A local pro-Houthi militia rising up inside the city.
  - Clashes right on the edge of Dar Saad or Sheikh Othman, where reporting may only say "the outskirts of Aden."
  - Reports from partisan outlets only. Houthi outlets such as Al-Masirah and government or southern outlets routinely make claims the other side denies.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Houthi pushes on Aden.** There is one earlier case, in 2015.
   - The Houthis and Saleh-aligned units moved south after taking Taiz around March 22, 2015.
   - They captured al-Anad air base on about March 25 and reached Aden's airport and Khor Maksar within days.
   - They were in Crater by early April and held parts of the city until July 2015 (Battle of Aden (2015), Wikipedia).
   - The jump from about 60 km away to inside the city took roughly one to two weeks.
   - In 2015 the defenders were weak, Saudi air power had only just started, and there were no organised southern brigades. Today the Giants Brigades and the Saudi-backed Nation's Shield (Homeland Shield) forces are defending, and coalition air strikes are under way.
2. **Houthi offensives on defended major cities since 2016.** These include Marib (2020–22), Taiz (2015 onward), and the government-side push on Hodeidah in 2018.
   - The attacker reached the outskirts, about 10–60 km out, in about 3 of these 3–4 sieges, but broke into the city centre in roughly 0 of 3 within a year.
   - At Marib the Houthis sat within roughly 10–20 km for well over a year and never took the city.
   - Taiz has been divided or besieged since 2015, and the Houthis encircled it again in October 2026 without taking it.
   - This class suggests a low chance of a defended city falling within about five weeks of the attacker coming within 60 km.
3. **The 2026 campaign so far, which shows how fast Houthi forces are moving now.**
   - They took Mokha on September 10, 2026 and Dhubab and the Bab al-Mandeb islands within days (Al Jazeera, Sep 10, 2026; The National, Sep 11, 2026).
   - They encircled Taiz in early October.
   - They took the Kharaz camp and the al-Maqatirah and al-Madaribah districts in Lahj by November 15.
   - Over about 10 weeks they advanced 100+ km along the coast, but those were mostly lightly held areas or areas the government gave up. The government had not yet chosen to make a stand at any of them, unlike Aden, which it now calls its priority.

**Rough synthesis:** Historically, when Houthi forces were within about 60–110 km of a big defended city, they broke in within about 5 weeks in about 1 of 4 to 5 cases. The one case was Aden in 2015, under far weaker defence.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

**November 22–23, 2026: Houthis reinforcing toward Abyan**
- Military sources say the Houthis are sending reinforcements toward Abyan, Aden's eastern gateway, possibly along an al-Bayda–Abyan axis to encircle Aden from the east.
- Southern forces have also massed troops and strengthened positions in Abyan.
- No completed encirclement has been reported (The New Arab, around Nov 22, 2026).
- Analysts note that the corridor through Abyan and Shabwah is Aden's main remaining overland link to the rest of government territory.

**November 20–22: Reuters says Aden's defenders are divided**
- Feuds among rival factions and their Gulf backers have weakened the city's defence. There are checkpoints and trenches in Aden.
- Reuters puts the Houthis about **80 km** from Aden on the northern highland front and about **110 km** to the west (Reuters via US News, Nov 20, 2026; Sight Magazine repost, Nov 22).
- This conflicts with the earlier AFP figure of **60 km**, probably because the distances were measured differently.

**About November 18–19**
- Two Western officials say Saudi Arabia is preparing for a war that could last into 2027, possibly six more months, and is holding off on negotiations until government forces make gains (Times of Israel, Nov 18, 2026).
- A bne IntelliNews explainer cites anonymous Xinhua sources saying the Giants Brigades "collapsed faster than expected" and that Saudi–southern (STC) tensions kept many southern fighters out of the fight. This is unverified, and other outlets say the Giants are leading the fightback.

**November 17–18**
- The coalition says it struck 60 Houthi targets in Sanaa, Hodeidah, Amran and Saada, and that fighting in Taiz is fierce (Al Jazeera, Nov 18, 2026).
- Yemen's army reports 661 strikes on the Houthis (Al Jazeera, Nov 17).
- Yemen's parliament passed a law designating the Houthis as terrorists.
- The Houthis freed 600 detainees, as confirmed by the ICRC.

**November 16**
- Saudi Arabia removed Lt. Gen. Fahd bin Hamad al-Salman as coalition commander and replaced him with Chief of Staff Gen. Fayyad al-Ruwaili. This came a day after a forced withdrawal (France 24, Nov 16, 2026).
- No Saudi ground troops have been reported deploying to Aden.

**November 15–16**
- Government forces pulled out of the Bab al-Mandeb area to defend Aden and Lahj.
- The Houthis took the Kharaz base and the al-Maqatirah and al-Madaribah districts, about 60 km from Aden (AFP via France 24, Nov 15; Xinhua, Nov 15; Al Jazeera, Nov 16).
- Sandbag lines and tunnels are being built around Aden.
- A Houthi missile hit a checkpoint in Lahj and killed 5 to 8 people.

**Late October to early November**
- Government forces, the Giants Brigades and Nation's Shield reported a counter-offensive in al-Maqatirah and along the Tur al-Bahah axis that stopped a three-pronged Houthi push (Asharq Al-Awsat; exact dates unclear).
- Steptoe judged that a limited ceasefire within months was possible (Nov 5).
- Israel Hayom reported, without confirmation, that Saudi Arabia offered the Houthis $1 billion in a deal brokered by Iran. Riyadh officially rejects any ceasefire before the Houthis give back the territory they took (Oct 26).
- Responsible Statecraft reported that Saudi Arabia and the UAE are returning to the fight with different goals (Nov 2).

**October 2026**
- The UAE re-engaged by restarting payments to anti-Houthi forces in the south (SBS, Oct 18; i24).
- Houthi missiles and drones hit Aden airport and the Maashiq presidential palace.
- Aden residents are preparing for war (Al Jazeera, Oct 30).
- The Houthis encircled Taiz.

**September 2026**
- The offensive started on September 3. The Houthis took Mokha on September 10 and then Dhubab and the Perim and Zuqar islands.
- They also launched strikes on Saudi Arabia.
- The IOM reports more than 340,000 people displaced since August (Anadolu).

**Background**
- In January 2026 the STC lost Aden to Saudi-backed forces and was dissolved.
- The UAE withdrew its forces in early January 2026.
- This left Aden's security in the hands of a mixed, partly resentful set of southern and Saudi-backed forces.

**Scheduled events before December 31:** I found nothing scheduled. No talks have been announced. The Saudi timeline points to continued fighting.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES**
1. **Defences collapse.** Divided southern units, or STC loyalists who won't fight for a Saudi-run setup, give way the way the Red Sea coast did, and Houthi columns push from Lahj (al-Hawtah) into Dar Saad and Sheikh Othman. This depends on whether the Giants Brigades keep fighting, and on how far coalition air strikes can slow armoured columns.
2. **A raid or infiltration.** A Houthi strike team or proxy cell operates inside the city. This is a lower bar, but it may not meet "deliberately operate" or may be read as sabotage.
3. **Encirclement from the east plus a breakthrough from the north,** if Abyan falls quickly.

**Paths to NO**
1. **Defensive lines hold.** Aden is the government's declared top priority, the southern forces have a strong motive to defend their home city, and the Saudis and the UAE are back in with air strikes and money. On this path the front settles around 30–80 km out, as at Marib.
2. **The Houthis choose not to attack.** They consolidate the coast and the Bab al-Mandeb, keep pressure on Taiz, and use missile strikes as leverage. Some coverage judged an imminent attack "unlikely" (Red Sea Brief #23, as summarised).
3. **A ceasefire or deal brokered with Saudi Arabia or Iran** freezes the lines.
4. **Time runs out.** Even a successful drive covering 60–80 km of defended ground could take more than 37 days.

## 5. Signposts

**Would raise the probability of YES:**
- The Houthis take al-Anad air base, al-Hawtah (Lahj's capital) or Tur al-Bahah.
- Credible reports of fighting at the Ras Imran or Buraiqeh line, or in Sabr on Aden's northern edge.
- Abyan or Zinjibar falls, or the Aden–Abyan road is cut.
- Giants or southern units defect or refuse to fight.
- Government leaders or the presidential council (PLC) leave Aden.
- Embassies or the UN evacuate the city.

**Would lower the probability:**
- A verified government counter-offensive retakes the Kharaz base or al-Madaribah.
- Saudi or UAE ground or special forces deploy to Aden.
- A Houthi statement that Aden is not their target.
- Ceasefire talks are announced, for example in Muscat or Riyadh, or through Iranian mediation.
- The fronts stay static for 2–3 weeks.

**Watch also:** whether reporting starts saying "within 20 km," and whether neutral wires (Reuters, AFP or AP) repeat any Houthi claim of entry.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **Exact front lines and distances.** The figures conflict: 60 km (AFP), versus 80 km north and 110 km west (Reuters). Government claims of retaking ground in al-Maqatirah are unverified.
- **How cohesive and loyal the Aden garrison is.** Reports range from "Giants collapsed" (Xinhua, anonymous) to "Giants lead fightback."
- **Houthi intentions,** that is, whether Aden is their real objective or a source of leverage. I found no recent statement from Abdul-Malik al-Houthi about Aden.
- **No reporting after about November 23,** and I could not read any article in full because the reader was blocked.
- **Whether any Saudi–Houthi channel is active.** The $1 billion report is unconfirmed.
- **How the resolver would treat a commando raid or sleeper-cell operation.**
