## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **YES:** Amanda Nunes wins the Kayla Harrison vs. Amanda Nunes fight. It is the co-main event of UFC 334 at Madison Square Garden, New York, on Saturday Nov 14, 2026. It is a five-round bout for Harrison's UFC women's bantamweight (135 lb) title (UFC 334 Card Announced, Forbes, Sep 19 2026; UFC 334: 14 Bouts, Forbes, Oct 30 2026). Any winning method counts: KO/TKO, submission, decision, or a disqualification announced on the night.
- **Draw or no contest:** pays $0.50.
- **Who decides:** the official result on the night. Any change after the contract expires (an overturned result, a later DQ, a failed drug test) is ignored.
- **Timing:** if the fight is delayed but starts within 48 hours of its scheduled start, the official result counts. If it is cancelled, or doesn't start within 48 hours, the market settles at a "fair price" rather than NO. A withdrawal by either fighter therefore does not resolve NO.
- **Backup fighter:** Joselyne Edwards (18-6), a ranked bantamweight contender, will weigh in as the official backup (UFC 334 Backup Fighter Revealed, SI, ~Oct 29 2026; Cagesidepress, Oct 29 2026). If she replaces either fighter, this matchup is off and the fair-price rule should apply. If she replaces Harrison and Nunes then beats her, there is some ambiguity, but the wording ("the Kayla Harrison vs Amanda Nunes fight") points to fair price, not YES.
- **Weight:** if Harrison misses weight the fight would probably still go ahead with changed title stakes. That has no effect on resolution.
- **Schedule:** weigh-ins Nov 13. Main card 9 pm ET Nov 14, so the co-main is expected around 11:30 pm–12:30 am ET, roughly 04:30–05:30 UTC Nov 15. That is well before the Nov 17 03:00 UTC close. Some non-US previews list Nov 15 because of time zones, and one Boxing News piece says Nov 12, which looks like an error.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to YES**
- **Early knockout:** Nunes's main edge is punching power and striking skill; she has KO'd Rousey, Cyborg and Holm. Harrison's stand-up is less refined, and her takedown entries leave her open on the way in.
- **Damaged or rusty Harrison:** a repaired neck could affect her clinch and scrambles, or make her hesitant. Recovery was long and the camp short.
- **Stopping the takedowns:** Nunes is very strong for the division and has handled grapplers before. If she blocks the takedowns early, Harrison has to strike with her.
- **Decision:** possible but less likely. It would need Nunes to win the striking over 25 minutes while not getting controlled on the ground.

**Paths to NO**
- **Ground control:** Harrison's chain wrestling and judo lead to top control and a submission (she finished Holm and Peña). Peña's 2021 submission of Nunes shows this kind of sustained grappling pressure can beat her.
- **Ring rust and age:** 3.4 years out at 38. Long layoffs often hurt cardio and timing, which matters in five rounds; Nunes's cardio was already a weak point late in fights.
- **Decision:** Harrison controls the fight on points.
- **Cancellation (fair price, not NO):** either fighter pulls out or there is a weight-cut problem. Harrison's injury history slightly raises this risk; Edwards is standing by.

## 5. Signposts
- **Fight-week health or withdrawal news** (biggest swing for whether the fight happens; it goes to fair price, not NO).
- **Weigh-ins (Nov 13):** Harrison has struggled at 135. A hard cut or a miss would point toward YES. A smooth cut, or Nunes looking soft or depleted, points the other way.
- **Media week:** signs of how well Harrison's neck has recovered (e.g., her limiting grappling in camp) would point toward YES. Reports of Nunes having camp problems would point toward NO.
- **During the fight:** an early successful takedown by Harrison is strongly NO. Nunes landing clean in round 1 and stuffing takedowns is strongly YES. Late rounds probably favour Harrison.

## 6. Uncertainties
- How healthy Harrison really is, and how long her camp was. One report said only about 3 weeks of full training as of mid-October.
- Nunes's actual fitness after 3.4 years away, and what her camp looked like this time. Pacheco's involvement is unconfirmed for this camp.
- The returner base rate is a small, mixed sample (n≈9) and partly from memory. The champion-retention rate (~55–65%) was not checked this session.
- The Edwards "emergency plan" detail comes from one outlet, and none of the primary pages could be opened.
