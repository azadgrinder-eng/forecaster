# Research brief: West Palm Beach (CLIDJT) November 2026 precipitation strictly above 21.00 inches

*Prepared Nov 22, 2026. WebFetch was refused on every page I tried (weather.gov, IEM, X, Yahoo, WFLX, weather.com), so the facts below come from search-result snippets and I could not read the articles in full. I did not look up any market prices.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **YES condition:** The November 2026 monthly precipitation total in the NWS Daily Climate Report (CLI) for West Palm Beach must be **strictly greater than 21.00 inches**. Exactly 21.00 resolves NO.
- **Station:** "CLIDJT" is the CLI product for the main airport, Palm Beach International (PBI). The airport has been renamed President Donald J. Trump International (DJT) (Nearly 8 inches of rain recorded at DJT airport, Nov 3, 2026). That means only the official airport gauge counts. City-wide, ZIP-code, radar-estimated and CoCoRaHS totals do not, and those are the sources quoting 20–27+ inches.
- **Source and timing:** The deciding report is the first CLI that covers all calendar days of the month, normally issued early on Dec 1 local time. The close is 2026-12-01 04:59 UTC, which is 11:59 pm EST on Nov 30. Rain falling through Nov 30 local standard time counts.
- **Edge cases:** The material-error and revised-data provisions could matter if a gauge malfunctioned during the extreme Nov 2 and Nov 19 rains. Station-ID or product-name confusion after the airport rename is a minor administrative risk.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **Climatology for a whole November:** Normal November rain at PBI is roughly 3–4 inches. The city called the 11.67-inch Nov 1–2 total "more than three times" a typical November (City of West Palm Beach, Nov 3, 2026). A whole November above 21 inches has never happened at this station: 18.25 inches was reported as a new monthly record (weather.com, Nov 21, 2026). Base rate for the full month is effectively 0 in about 135 years (records from 1888). This class is only background now, because most of the month is already observed.
2. **The conditional question that matters:** Will Nov 20/21–30 add about 2.75 inches or more? That is the gap from 18.25 to just over 21.00. Normal rain for the last ~10 days of November is roughly 0.7–1.0 inch (my estimate from a ~3–4 inch monthly normal, falling seasonally). Getting about 3 times normal in that window usually needs a nor'easter, stalled front or late tropical system. The Nov 21–24, 1984 nor'easter is the clearest past example: it dropped 7.41 inches in one day, the November daily record (WFLX/WPTV, Nov 3, 2026), and up to 11.29 inches over several days (Wikipedia, Late November 1984 nor'easter). My rough judgment is that 2.75 inches or more in a late-November 9–10 day window happens in about 5–10% of years. I could not compute this from data. A dry near-term forecast should push it lower.
3. **Persistence:** This November has been extremely wet: two separate multi-day events (Nov 1–2 and Nov 17–20), and an NWS dry-season outlook of "Wetter and Stormier" (NWS outlook snippet, undated). That argues for somewhat fatter tails than pure climatology.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)
- **Nov 21–22:** The NWS point forecast for Palm Beach (updated Nov 21) has Thanksgiving (Nov 26) mostly sunny with a 20% chance of showers, and a 30% chance on Tuesday, Nov 24. CBS12 says rain chances fall off Sunday and Monday, then build again Tuesday and Wednesday (Nov 24–25). AccuWeather says rain "does not look to be a big concern for Thanksgiving." A low tracking up the East Coast this weekend is mainly a Mid-Atlantic and Northeast event (weather.com, Nov 21, 2026).
- **Nov 21 (Sat):** WFLX forecast a sunny start, "a few afternoon storms" and low flooding risk, then a sunny Sunday (WFLX, Nov 20, 2026). Another outlook put Saturday rain chances at 20–30% (AOL/Palm Beach Post). I found no observed total for Nov 21.
- **Nov 21 (weather.com article):** "West Palm Beach (18.25 inches) also smashed their record wettest November." The surrounding text suggests data through about Nov 19, but the snippet does not name the station or the exact end date. Fort Lauderdale is listed at 15.49 inches and Vero Beach at 12.32 inches, both apparently official airport sites, so 18.25 is very likely the official PBI/DJT figure.
- **~Nov 20 (social media):** Meteorologist Jeff Berardelli posted on X that West Palm Beach had about 27 inches "with 10 days left." The gauge is unnamed. His other figures (40 inches in Grant, 30 inches at Cape Canaveral) look like maximum or CoCoRaHS gauges, so this is probably not the official CLI number. If the airport really were at 27 inches, YES would already be locked in, which is why this point matters.
- **Nov 20 (Fri):** Palm Beach County schools were closed. One forecast expected "1 to 2 more inches" in the county Friday, with improvement later in the day (Nacionale/Palm Beach Post, ~Nov 19). The official Nov 20 airport amount is unknown, and this is the biggest gap in the brief.
- **Nov 20:** CBS12 storm totals since Tuesday (Nov 17) were 12.92 inches in The Acreage, 7.91 in Royal Palm Beach and 7.30 in Haverhill. These are not airport figures.
- **Nov 19:** The official daily record for the date was 3.08 inches at West Palm Beach, beating 2.99 inches from 2021 (WPBF). A Palm Beach flood watch ran until 7 pm on Nov 19, and through Friday on the Treasure Coast.
- **Nov 17–19:** Flood watch across Miami-Dade, Broward and Palm Beach, with forecasts of 4–6 inches and isolated 8–12 inches (NBC Miami, Nov 18; WPTV).
- **Nov 1–2:** Official 7.39 inches on Nov 2, the second-wettest November day on record behind 7.41 inches on Nov 22, 1984. The official two-day total was 11.67 inches (City of WPB, Nov 3; WFLX/WPTV, Nov 3). Radar and local estimates were much higher (20+ inches; "1,000-year" event).
- **Reconciliation:** 11.67 (Nov 1–2) + 3.08 (Nov 19) = 14.75 inches, leaving about 3.5 inches spread over other days by the time of the 18.25 figure. That is consistent with 18.25 covering through about Nov 19, or possibly Nov 20.
- **Other gauges, not official:** Precip.ai ZIP 33404 showed 25.17 inches month-to-date around Nov 19, and RainDrop ZIP 33412 showed 16.26 inches as of Nov 19. These show how much totals vary across the city.
- **Remaining scheduled period:** Nov 22–30. No named tropical system or organized heavy-rain event was in the forecasts I found.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Key number: the official month-to-date total as of Nov 21.** Rain needed by Nov 30:
- If the total is still about 18.25 (Nov 20–21 light): more than 2.75 inches needed.
- If Nov 20–21 added about 1 inch (total ~19.3): about 1.75 inches needed.
- If Nov 20–21 added about 2 inches (total ~20.3): about 0.7 inch needed.

**Paths to YES:**
- **A.** Nov 20 delivered the forecast 1–2 inches at the airport. Then the Nov 24–25 showers, or the front after Thanksgiving, only need to add a modest 0.7–1.7 inches. This is plausible in an anomalously wet regime.
- **B.** An unforecast coastal trough or stalled front brings onshore convergence showers. Narrow coastal bands can drop 2–4 inches on one gauge, as Nov 19 did. East Palm Beach County is prone to these in late fall with easterly flow.
- **C.** The 27-inch figure turns out to be the official one. This is unlikely.

**Paths to NO:**
- **A.** The month-to-date total stays near 18.25–19 inches, and the drier pattern now forecast (Sun–Mon dry, 20–30% showers Tue–Thu) holds through Nov 30. This is the most consistent with the evidence I found.
- **B.** Late-month showers happen but stay light (under 1 inch total), which is typical for 20–30% shower days.

## 5. Signposts
- **Biggest swing:** The official CLIDJT month-to-date for Nov 20 and Nov 21, from the daily CLI or NWS Miami. Above 20 inches pushes strongly toward YES. Still under about 18.8 inches pushes strongly toward NO.
- NWS Miami forecast discussions for Nov 24–25 and Nov 27–30. Watch for a coastal trough, a stalled front, a 50%+ PoP, QPF above 1 inch, or a flood watch, any of which moves toward YES. Continued 20% PoPs move toward NO.
- WPC 7-day QPF for SE Florida: a coastal Palm Beach bullseye of 1.5 inches or more raises YES odds.
- Any NWS statement correcting the record or a gauge reading for Nov 2 or Nov 19, through the material-error provisions.

## 6. Uncertainties
- **Most important:** The exact official total through Nov 21. Neither Nov 20 nor Nov 21 airport amounts could be confirmed, and the end date of the 18.25-inch figure is not certain.
- Which gauge Berardelli's 27 inches comes from. It is probably not the airport.
- The exact 1991–2020 PBI November normal and the prior monthly record. One aggregator listed 1992 at about 9.8 inches, which seems unreliable. If that or similar were the old record, it would underline how anomalous this month is.
- Forecasts beyond about Nov 26 have low skill. Late-November events like 1984 can appear on short notice.
