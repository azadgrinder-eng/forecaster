# Research brief: Will Vox win 56–60 seats in Spain's general election on 29 November 2026?

*Prepared 12 Nov 2026. The tool could not open any page directly; every request was refused by the network proxy. All facts below come from search-result summaries of the cited articles, so treat individual figures as moderately reliable and check them where possible.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** Vox lists must win **56, 57, 58, 59 or 60 seats** in the 350-seat Congress of Deputies at the 29 November 2026 general election. Seats count by the electoral list they were won on. Vox runs alone everywhere, so how lists are attributed should not matter for Vox.
- **The election is confirmed.** Sánchez announced the snap election on 5 Oct 2026 after Congress rejected his housing decrees, with Junts voting against. The decree dissolving parliament was published in the BOE (the official gazette) on 6 Oct. It sets election day for 29 Nov and the new chambers meet on 23 Dec (Atalayar, 5 Oct 2026; IMI Daily, Oct 2026).
- **Who decides:** a consensus of credible reporting. If that is ambiguous, the Ministry of the Interior's results site (infoelectoral.interior.gob.es) decides. The provisional count on election night normally settles the seat total within hours. The official count by the electoral boards and the formal declaration of results follow over the next 1–3 weeks. A seat can occasionally change between the two, usually the last seat in a province decided by a few hundred votes. That matters most if Vox lands on 55/56 or 60/61.
- **Fallback:** if results are not definitively known by 30 Nov 2027, the market resolves to the lowest bracket. That is practically irrelevant.
- **Close date:** the market closes on 30 Nov 2026 at 04:59 UTC. That is the night of the election, while counting is finishing.
- **Edge cases:**
  - Recounts or challenges in close provinces.
  - A difference between the provisional count and the final official count at the bracket edges.
  - A postponement of the election, which is very unlikely now that parliament is dissolved.

- Coverage of the 2023 election says the big miss was over-estimating the PP. Vox came in **above** election-day projections: the CIS had at most 29 seats, GAD3 had 31, one poll had 24–27 (Diari de Tarragona, Jul 2023; Ara, Jul 2023).
- **Takeaway:** the miss on Vox's seat total has typically been **about 3–12 seats in either direction**, with no consistent bias. Today's consensus is about 63–67 seats, so landing in 56–60 needs roughly a 5–9 seat underperformance. That happened once in three cases (April 2019).

**C. Rough link between national vote share and Vox seats.** This comes from past results and current poll seat projections:
- 12.4% gave 33 seats; 15.1% gave 52.
- In current polls: 17.5–18% gives about 58–64 seats; 18.6–18.8% gives 63–66; 19.3–19.9% gives 65–72.
- So **56–60 seats corresponds to roughly 16.5–17.8% of the vote**, assuming the PP gets about 31–33%. A weaker PP or a stronger PSOE can shift this by a seat or two.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (56–60 seats):**
- **Tactical vote to the PP.** Right-leaning voters consolidate behind Feijóo to secure a PP-led government or to reduce dependence on Vox, as in 2023, when Vox lost 19 seats. A drop of 1.5–2.5 points from about 18.7% to about 16.5–17.5% lands Vox in the bracket.
- **The 40dB or CIS reading is right.** If 40dB's downward trend (18.9% to 18.0% or 17.5%) reflects reality and continues, 58–60 seats is plausible.
- **PSOE recovers in campaign.** A PSOE rebound concentrated in a few provinces, or a strong PSOE debate, would push Vox to the 17% range. This would come with higher turnout on the left.
- **Seat-allocation quirks.** Many of Vox's seats are the last seat in medium-sized provinces. A small loss of vote share can cost several such seats at once, so seat counts move non-linearly.

**Paths to NO, above 60 (the most likely direction given the polls):**
- Polls are roughly right: about 18.5–20% and 62–70 seats.
- Anger over Urda and Puente keeps pushing anti-government voters to Vox.
- Vox has matched or beaten its polls in the 2026 regional elections (Aragón, Andalucía).
- Vox under-performed its polls in 2023 but beat them in November 2019 (51/52 seats against polls of about 40–46).

**Paths to NO, below 56:**
- A 2023-style collapse, with Vox falling below about 16.5%. This would need a big swing to the PP late in the campaign, or a Vox scandal or gaffe. It is possible but would mean Vox under-performing its polls by more than 2–3 points.

## 5. Signposts

**Would move toward YES (56–60):**
- The final polls before the ban (around 23 Nov) show Vox at 17–18%. 40dB, GAD3 and Sigma Dos are the key ones.
- The CIS pre-election survey gives Vox 17% or less.
- Polls show the PP gaining at Vox's expense, PP above 33% with Vox falling, or the "useful vote" (voting PP to avoid wasting a right-wing vote) dominating campaign coverage.
- A weak Abascal debate performance or a Vox controversy.

**Would move away from YES:**
- *Upward:* several pollsters at 19.5% or more, or seat ranges with a floor above 62 (Target Point, DATA10 and GESOP already sit there).
- *Upward:* the Urda story stays dominant and Vox rallies keep drawing large crowds.
- *Downward (below 56):* evidence of a 2023-style late collapse to 15–16%.

**On election night:**
- The share of the count at which the provisional total stabilises.
- Any province where Vox wins or loses the last seat by a margin under 0.5%. This is especially important if the total is near 55/56 or 60/61.
