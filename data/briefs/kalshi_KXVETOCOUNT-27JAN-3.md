# Research brief: Will Trump veto at least 3 bills or resolutions before Jan 1, 2027?

*Prepared Nov 25, 2026. I could not open full pages (WebFetch was blocked by the proxy), so the facts below come from search-result summaries plus background knowledge, flagged where relevant.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** President Trump vetoes 3 or more bills or joint resolutions before 00:00 Jan 1, 2027. Pocket vetoes count.
- **Current count:** Trump has issued **2 vetoes in his second term**. Both came on Dec 29, 2025: H.R. 131 (Finish the Arkansas Valley Conduit Act) and H.R. 504 (Miccosukee Reserved Area Amendments Act). This is the figure on the Senate's official list (Senate.gov "Vetoes by President Donald J. Trump", 2nd admin) and from Ballotpedia. A trumpinsight.com page dated October 2026 also shows 2. If those two count, **one more veto in about 5 weeks resolves YES.**
- **Ambiguity 1, what the count covers:** The rules don't say whether the count runs from the start of the second term, from when the market opened, or across his whole career. He issued 10 vetoes in his first term, so a whole-career count would already be YES, which is very unlikely to be the intent. If the market only counts vetoes after it opened, YES would need 3 new vetoes. Forecasters should check when the market was created and how the platform has handled this. Most likely reading: the 2 second-term vetoes count and 1 more is needed.
- **Ambiguity 2, timing of pocket vetoes:** A bill is pocket-vetoed only when the 10-day window (Sundays excepted) runs out while Congress is adjourned. The House reportedly sits only until Dec 17 (Fox News/Just the News, Nov 2026). A bill presented around Dec 17–19 would hit its 10-day deadline around Dec 30–31. A bill presented after about Dec 20 would hit it after Jan 1, so a pocket veto would come too late. Which date the resolver uses (the end of the 10 days, or an announcement / memorandum of disapproval) could matter.
- **What does not count:** Concurrent resolutions, such as the war powers measures H.Con.Res. 93 and Massie's new one, never go to the president and cannot be vetoed (ASIL, June 2026). Veto threats and Statements of Administration Policy (SAPs) don't count either. The 119th Congress ends Jan 3, 2027. Vetoes of bills from the new Democratic 120th Congress would come after the deadline.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **Trump's own second-term rate.** 2 vetoes in about 22 months (Jan 2025 to Nov 2026), roughly 0.09 per month. A plain Poisson model for about 5 weeks gives P(≥1) ≈ 10%. But vetoes bunch at the end of a session: both came on Dec 29, 2025, after Congress cleared a batch of minor bills by voice vote or unanimous consent. Measured per end-of-session period instead, the record is 1 of 1 Decembers with a veto (2 vetoes). This is the strongest argument for a higher number than the plain rate.
2. **Same-party lame ducks after the president's party loses a chamber or Congress.** Clinton in Dec 1994, Bush in Dec 2006, Obama in Dec 2010 and Trump in Dec 2018 all issued 0 vetoes during the lame duck, as far as I know (from memory of Senate veto lists; not checked this session). That is **0 of 4**. Obama's Dec 2009 pocket veto attempt and Oct 2010 veto came outside these windows. Since 1981, about 98% of vetoes have come under divided government (USAFacts/CBS). Congress stays Republican until Jan 3, 2027, and House Appropriations Chair Tom Cole has said a Republican Congress "is not going to send bills to a Republican president that he's going to veto" (via search summary, Nov 2026).
3. **December vetoes in general.** Trump in Dec 2020 (divided government) issued 1 December veto, the FY2021 NDAA on Dec 23, which was overridden. Trump in Dec 2025 issued 2. Obama in Dec 2016 issued, I believe, about 1–2 (not verified). One December veto happens fairly often. Several in one December, under unified government, is rare outside 2025.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 24, 2026:** Polling shows Democrats want the Iran war ended and some would accept a shutdown. If Congress can't agree, or if Trump vetoes the agreed spending bill, it would be the third shutdown since he returned to office (The National, Nov 24, 2026).
- **Nov 23, 2026:** A single left-leaning outlet reports that Democratic leaders backed off demanding Iran-war funding limits in the stopgap. I could not confirm this elsewhere (WSWS, Nov 23, 2026).
- **Nov 21, 2026:** Punchbowl reports Johnson and Thune won't accept Iran-war limits in a stopgap, and Jeffries and Schumer can't agree without them. Thune called such limits "not a realistic ask." Congress returns Nov 30. Funding expires Dec 11. There is talk of sending the House home early after a stopgap (Punchbowl, ~Nov 2026). The House is reportedly in session only until Dec 17.
- **Nov 20, 2026:** Trump says he will veto Democratic bills once Democrats control Congress in 2027, calls the veto a "starting point," and is open to deals on health care and film production (Roll Call, Washington Times, US News, Nov 20, 2026). He posted a red "VETO" hat and has called himself "Mr. Veto" (Benzinga, Newsweek, Nov 2026). On Nov 3 he told CBS, "I have the ultimate power — it's called 'veto'" (Fox News).
- **Nov 19, 2026:** The Senate rejected the Iran war powers measure H.Con.Res. 93, 48–51, which the House had passed 220–204 on Sep 15. A White House SAP the same day opposed it. Being a concurrent resolution, it could not have been vetoed anyway (Roll Call, CBS, Nov 19, 2026; Inside Defense).
- **Nov 10, 2026:** Massie filed a new concurrent resolution under War Powers Resolution §5(c). He can force a House vote 15 days after filing. No vote is scheduled, and it could not be vetoed either (Military.com, Benzinga, Nov 2026).
- **Nov 3, 2026, midterms:** Democrats won both chambers effective Jan 3, 2027, well short of the two-thirds needed to override (KCRA, Al Jazeera Nov 6, 2026).
- **Tariffs:** The House passed a resolution ending the Canada tariff emergency, 219–211, on Feb 11, 2026. The Senate passed S.J.Res. 88 on global tariffs, 51–47, on Oct 30, 2025, but the House held it at the desk. I found no joint tariff resolution that cleared both chambers or reached Trump (CFR, CBC, Congress.gov).
- **FY2027 NDAA:** The House passed H.R. 8800 on Jul 22, 2026 by a near party-line vote (219–206 or 216–212 depending on the source). The bill is stalled in the Senate, with no conference as of the July CRS update. A secondary source says the SAP on H.R. 8800 contains no veto threat (Breaking Defense, Air & Space Forces, CRS IN12704, Jul 2026).
- **Jan 8, 2026:** The House failed to override both December vetoes. The Arkansas Valley Conduit vote was 248–177 (35 Republicans voted to override). The Miccosukee vote was 236–188 (24 Republicans) (CNBC, NBC, CBS, The Hill, Jan 8, 2026).
- **Dec 29, 2025:** First two vetoes. Both bills had passed unanimously or by voice vote. The vetoes were widely seen as political: the Conduit bill was sponsored by Boebert, and Trump said the Miccosukee tribe had "obstructed" immigration policy, apparently referring to the Everglades detention site (CBS, NBC, Dec 2025).

**Scheduled before the deadline:** Congress returns Nov 30. The stopgap expires Dec 11 (shutdown from Dec 12 if it lapses). The House is expected out around Dec 17. The usual end-of-Congress rush of minor bills, private bills and suspension bills follows, and the 119th Congress ends sine die by Jan 3, 2027.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES (one more veto, or three if only new vetoes count):**
- **Political vetoes of minor bills, as in Dec 2025.** End-of-Congress cleanup sends dozens of low-profile bills to the president. Trump has shown he will veto noncontroversial bills tied to lawmakers or groups he is feuding with; Boebert had broken with him over the Epstein files. After the midterm losses, more Republicans are openly defying him (Massie, members pushing war powers votes), which raises the odds of retaliation. Each such veto is a separate count, so one batch could produce 1–3 vetoes, as Dec 2025 produced 2.
- **A stopgap or omnibus with Iran-war limits that Trump vetoes.** This is unlikely: GOP leaders control the floors and refuse such limits, and Trump would more likely sign a clean stopgap. Possible if a bipartisan deal includes a constraint he rejects.
- **Pocket vetoes** of bills presented around mid-to-late December, if Congress adjourns sine die. They count only if the window ends before Jan 1.
- **A joint resolution (tariff or CRA disapproval) that clears both chambers.** Unlikely: the Senate GOP has 53 seats, and these lacked the votes to reach his desk all year.

**Paths to NO:**
- Republican leaders filter what reaches his desk (the Cole doctrine). Trump keeps his veto as a threat against the 2027 Democratic Congress rather than using it now.
- Congress passes a clean stopgap and leaves early. Few enrolled bills are presented in time, and bills presented late have deadlines after Jan 1.
- Under the "new vetoes only" reading, three vetoes in 5 weeks under unified government would be historically unusual.

## 5. Signposts

- **Toward YES:** Trump publicly attacking a specific bill or sponsor in the cleanup batch. A SAP with an explicit veto threat on a bill that has passed one chamber. Large suspension or unanimous-consent packages passing Dec 1–17 that include bills from his critics (Massie, Boebert, tribes, blue-state projects). Any veto at all before about Dec 20: under the "existing 2 count" reading, one more resolves YES. A stopgap fight ending with a bill sent to him over his objection.
- **Toward NO:** A clean stopgap signed by Dec 11 and the House going home early. Few bills presented after early December. Trump signing year-end packages without comment.
- **Timing:** Watch when bills are presented, not just when they pass. In 2025, bills passed in early-to-mid December were vetoed on Dec 29.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **Count baseline:** whether the 2 Dec 2025 vetoes count toward the 3. This is the biggest swing factor.
- I could not open primary pages (Senate.gov, Ballotpedia, Congress.gov) to confirm no vetoes occurred between Jan and Nov 2026. Several secondary sources (Ballotpedia snippet, trumpinsight Oct 2026, news coverage in November 2026) consistently say "two vetoes so far," so I'm fairly confident.
- Exact lame-duck adjournment dates, including the Senate's and whether a sine die adjournment allows pocket vetoes.
- Which minor bills are queued for year-end passage and whether any involve Trump adversaries.
- Lame-duck veto counts for past presidents (reference class 2) are from memory, not checked this session.
