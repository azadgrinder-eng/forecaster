# Research brief: Will Donald Tusk be Poland's Prime Minister at 11:59pm CET on 30 November 2026?

*Prepared 10 Nov 2026. The proxy refused every attempt to open a full article, so all facts below come from search-result snippets. Treat exact vote counts and dates as needing confirmation.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **YES** if Tusk holds the office of Prime Minister of Poland at 23:59 CET on 30 Nov 2026. That is about 20 days from today. **NO** otherwise.
- The rules name no resolution source. The likely sources are the official record (gov.pl/premier, Monitor Polski) and major media.
- **How Tusk could stop being PM within the window:**
  - **A constructive vote of no confidence (Constitution Art. 158).** At least 46 MPs must file a motion that names a replacement PM. The vote can happen no sooner than 7 days after filing. It needs an absolute majority of 231 out of 460. If it passes, the President must accept the government's resignation and appoint the PM the Sejm chose. That can happen within days (Polskie Radio Jedynka / Interia procedural summaries, Nov 2026).
  - **Voluntary resignation or replacement by his own party.** Tusk leads the Civic Coalition (KO), so his party is very unlikely to replace him.
  - **Death or incapacity.**
  - **Sejm self-dissolution.** This needs 307 votes and would not remove the PM before 30 Nov in any case.
- **Edge case:** if the government resigned, Tusk would probably stay in office as caretaker until a successor is appointed (Art. 162(3)). Whether a caretaker PM counts as "the Prime Minister" for this market is unclear, but such a caretaker would most likely still be described as PM. No resignation is currently in sight.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **Constructive no-confidence motions in Poland since 1997.** None has succeeded (search summary citing Polish procedure coverage, Nov 2026). Known failures include motions against Tusk in 2013 (Gliński as the alternative PM) and against Szydło in 2017 (Schetyna). That is about 0 for roughly 5 or more against PMs. Motions against individual ministers in 2026 also all failed:
   - Hennig-Kloska, 30 Apr
   - Sobierańska-Grenda, 238 MPs voted to reject
   - Kierwiński: 10 Jun, and again on 6 Nov by 212 for vs 235 against (Polsat News, 6 Nov 2026)
2. **Polish PMs leaving office mid-term, 1997–2026.** There were 5 such exits: Miller (May 2004), Marcinkiewicz (Jul 2006), Tusk (Sep 2014, moved to the EU), Szydło (Dec 2017), and Morawiecki's failed confidence vote (Dec 2023, which followed an election). Over about 29 years that is roughly 0.15–0.2 exits per year. Scaled to a 20-day window, the chance is about 1% or less. Almost all of those exits were replacements by the PM's own party, which does not fit Tusk because he leads his party.
3. **Death or incapacity.** Tusk is 69. Actuarial annual mortality is about 1.5–2%, so roughly 0.1% over 20 days.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)
- **7–9 Nov 2026:** A terrorist shooting in Kraków killed 6 people. Tusk led the briefings. He and Nawrocki jointly declared two days of national mourning (Notes from Poland, 7–9 Nov 2026). This is a rally-type event and lowers the odds of a political rupture in the short term.
- **6 Nov:** The Sejm rejected a PiS no-confidence motion against Interior Minister Kierwiński by **212 to 235**. This is the most recent test of the coalition's majority. Rozwój Plus MP Horała used the debate to call for the whole government to go (Polsat News / Bankier, 6 Nov 2026). Earlier, PiS and Rozwój Plus argued over whose Kierwiński motion would go forward (WP, Niezalezna, early Nov).
- **6 Nov:** RMF FM reported, unofficially, that Tusk and Poland 2050 had agreed a small reshuffle: Osmalak to become deputy infrastructure minister and Romowicz to go to the Ministry of Funds (Ground News/RMF, 6 Nov 2026). This suggests Poland 2050 is staying in the coalition.
- **3 Nov:** Rozwój Plus MP Szczucki said the party would file a constructive no-confidence motion "at the next Sejm sitting" (WNET, 3 Nov 2026). Tusk and Nawrocki met for the first time since the court crisis began. Nawrocki blamed Tusk (Notes from Poland, 3 Nov).
- **2 Nov poll:** 28.7% said Nawrocki was right in the court dispute, 26.6% said Tusk, and 38.6% said neither (Wikipedia, opinion polling on the Tusk government).
- **31 Oct:** Morawiecki, who was expelled from PiS in August and now leads Rozwój Plus with about 40 MPs, announced a constructive motion. It would install a "technical government" under Marek Jurek until the 2027 election. Jurek openly invited PSL leader Kosiniak-Kamysz to leave Tusk and keep his deputy-PM post. One report targeted a vote on 18–19 Nov. Morawiecki has claimed only 10–11 more MPs are needed. Another outlet puts the gap at 15 votes (bne IntelliNews; AA; Salon24, ~31 Oct–early Nov).
  - **Not confirmed:** I found no report that the motion has been formally filed. Rozwój Plus alone is short of the 46 signatures needed, and it refuses to cooperate with Braun's Korona party.
- **30–31 Oct:** The Constitutional Tribunal crisis began. Judge Korwin-Piotrowska suspended the Tribunal's president, Święczkowski. Police were deployed. Nawrocki turned his plane around and clashed physically with judges at the court. Tusk said "no compromise" (Notes from Poland, 31 Oct; Euronews, 31 Oct; PAP).
  - The coalition is split on how to respond. The Left boycotted Nawrocki's consultations. PSL's Kosiniak-Kamysz attended and called for a "constitutional reset" (AA; CNN, ~31 Oct–4 Nov).
- **22–23 Oct:** Pełczyńska-Nałęcz resigned as minister and as Poland 2050 leader. Hołownia took back control and the party expelled her. Poland 2050 remains in the coalition (Notes from Poland, 23 Oct 2026; Brussels Signal, Oct 2026).
- **Early Oct:** Polish media reported, citing anonymous sources, that Trump urged Kosiniak-Kamysz in March to leave the coalition. Kosiniak-Kamysz and PSL denied it and reaffirmed loyalty to the coalition (Notes from Poland, 5 Oct 2026; Brussels Signal, Oct 2026).
- **Feb 2026:** Poland 2050 split. Hennig-Kloska's 15 MPs formed "Centrum", which stays in the coalition. The coalition had 240 seats before the split (Notes from Poland, 18 Feb 2026).
- **Polls:** Government approval is negative: CBOS −12, OGB −26.9 (Oct 2026). PSL polls about 3% and Poland 2050 about 1%. Both are below the 5% threshold, which gives them a strong reason to avoid an early election, though it could also make them want to switch sides.
- **Scheduled before 30 Nov:** Sejm sittings in the second half of November (exact dates not established). A possible constructive-motion vote around 18–19 Nov or later. A next regular parliamentary election in autumn 2027.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
**Paths to NO (all low probability):**
- **A) The constructive motion passes.** This needs the motion filed by roughly 20–23 Nov because of the 7-day wait. It also needs all of PiS, Rozwój Plus and Konfederacja (and probably Korona) to unite behind Jurek, plus about 15–19 coalition MPs, most plausibly PSL's roughly 28–32. Obstacles:
  - PiS and Morawiecki are feuding (PiS expelled him; they fought over the Kierwiński motion).
  - PSL has publicly denied any plan to defect.
  - The coalition held 235 votes on 6 Nov.
  - The post-Kraków mourning climate works against a coup-like switch.
- **B) Tusk resigns voluntarily**, for example to break the deadlock or for a European post. There is no sign of this. He is defiant.
- **C) Death or serious incapacity.** About 0.1%.

**Path to YES:** the status quo. The coalition's majority (about 235) holds, any motion fails or is never filed or voted on before 30 Nov, and Tusk stays in office.

## 5. Signposts
- **Formal filing** of the constructive motion with 46+ signatures, and the vote date set by the Sejm Marshal. This moves the probability slightly toward NO. The direction depends on who signs.
- **Any PSL statement** on Jurek's offer, any PSL MP signing the motion, or Kosiniak-Kamysz leaving the government. This would be a strong move toward NO.
- **A joint PiS–Rozwój Plus–Konfederacja whip** in favour of Jurek. A moderate move toward NO.
- **Further escalation in the court crisis**, for example prosecutions, Nawrocki moves, or EU/US intervention. Watch whether this pushes PSL or Poland 2050 away from the coalition.
- **Coalition vote margins** on other November votes. A drop below about 231 would be a warning sign.
- **Tusk's health, or talk of his successor.**
- **Moves toward YES:** the motion not being filed, or failing (expected margin about 210–220 for vs 235–240 against), and the agreed Poland 2050 reshuffle being completed.
