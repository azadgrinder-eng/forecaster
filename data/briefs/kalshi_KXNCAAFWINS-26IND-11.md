# Research brief: Will Indiana win at least 11 regular-season games in 2026?

## 1. Resolution mechanics
- **What YES requires:** Indiana must have at least 11 official regular-season wins in 2026. Bowl games, the Big Ten Championship Game (Dec 5) and College Football Playoff games do not count. Ties do not count as wins, though college football overtime makes ties practically impossible.
- **Indiana needs to win all three remaining games.** Indiana is 8-1 with three regular-season games left (2026 Indiana Hoosiers football team, Wikipedia; Sports-Reference 2026 schedule). That is a 12-game season, so 11 wins is the most Indiana can reach. One loss makes the market NO. Because of this, YES is just the three individual win chances multiplied together.
- **Remaining schedule** (CBS Sports schedule; NBC Sports schedule article):
  1. Sat **Nov 14** vs USC in Bloomington, 3:30 pm ET
  2. Sat **Nov 21** at Washington, Husky Stadium, Seattle
  3. Sat **Nov 28** vs Purdue in Bloomington (Old Oaken Bucket)
- **Timing:** The regular season ends Nov 28. The question closes Dec 20, 2026, so the outcome should be known well before close. Kalshi decides resolution.
- **Edge cases:**
  - If any remaining game is cancelled or not made up (weather, force majeure), the most Indiana could reach is 10 wins, which is effectively NO.
  - A postponed game played before Dec 20 would presumably still count, but the rules don't spell out how a game rescheduled after close would be handled.
  - A vacated or forfeited win could raise questions about which count is "official." This is very unlikely to matter.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates
1. **Cignetti-era Indiana in regular-season games (2024 to now):** 11-1 in 2024 (only loss at Ohio State), 12-0 in 2025, and 8-1 so far in 2026. That is about 31-2, or 94%, in regular-season games. Indiana also won the 2025 national title. For three straight wins this rate implies roughly 0.94³ ≈ 83%. That is an upper-anchor estimate, because the 2026 team is clearly weaker than the 2025 team (see section 3). Source: my background knowledge plus the 2026 Wikipedia and Sports-Reference pages.
2. **Teams that are 8-1 or better with three games left needing to win out:** Even very good teams lose about 1 in 4 to 1 in 3 of these runs. Each game being 80 to 95% likely compounds quickly: 0.85³ ≈ 61% and 0.80³ ≈ 51%. These are illustrative calculations, not sourced counts.
3. **Single-game win rates by point spread (general CFB rule of thumb):**
   - A home favorite of about 7 to 7.5 points wins outright roughly 72 to 76% of the time.
   - A favorite of 10 to 14 points wins about 80 to 88%.
   - A favorite of 20 points or more wins about 95% or more.
   - Indiana is about a 7.5-point home favorite against USC, according to sportsbook previews on Covers and BetMGM (Nov 10-11). That fits the models below.
4. **Purdue rivalry:** Indiana beat Purdue 66-0 in 2024 and 56-3 in 2025 (background knowledge). Purdue under Barry Odom went 2-10 (0-9 Big Ten) in 2025 (Barry Odom, Wikipedia).

## 4. Drivers and scenarios
- **Paths to YES (about 3 wins needed; each estimate is a rough analyst estimate):**
  - **Beat USC (about 72-78%).** Key factors are Hoover's health, Indiana's elite defense (held Minnesota to 215 yards), and home field. Indiana is favored by about a touchdown.
  - **Win at Washington (about 78-85%, my estimate; no line or FPI number found).** Washington is 5-4, has struggled at home, and may be demoralized or out of contention. Risks are the cross-country trip, a possible letdown after USC, and a bowl-eligibility push if Washington lost to Michigan State.
  - **Beat Purdue at home (about 93-97%).** This is a heavy mismatch based on recent scores and records.
  - **Combined:** roughly 0.75 × 0.82 × 0.95 ≈ **0.58**, with a plausible range of about 0.48 to 0.68.
- **Paths to NO:**
  - A loss to USC is the single biggest risk, especially if Wilson starts or Hoover is limited.
  - An upset at Washington, made more likely by a weak offense.
  - Turnover- or kicking-driven randomness: Indiana's recent games have been one-score games.
  - Low-probability cancellation of a game.
- **Correlation:** If Hoover's arm stays bad, every remaining game gets harder (except perhaps Purdue). So the true chance may sit below a simple product of independent game odds, though if the USC game is passed, the remaining odds become fairly high.
