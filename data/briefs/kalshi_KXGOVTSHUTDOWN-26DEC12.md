# Research brief: Will the US government be shut down at 10:00 AM ET on Saturday, Dec 12, 2026?

*Prepared Nov 12, 2026. Every page fetch was blocked by the network proxy, so all facts below come from search-result summaries rather than full articles. Treat details, especially quotes, with some caution.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** At 10:00 AM ET on Dec 12, 2026, the federal government must be at least partly shut down because appropriations have lapsed. Two kinds of evidence would show this:
  - an OMB directive telling agencies to "execute plans for an orderly shutdown" that is in effect at that time, or
  - an OPM operating-status notice that operations "vary by agency" because of a lapse.
- **Deadline:** Current FY2027 funding is the Continuing Appropriations Act, 2027 (Division A of P.L. 119-103, from H.R. 9770). It covers all 12 regular appropriations bills through **Friday, Dec 11, 2026**. With no new law, funding lapses at 12:01 AM on **Saturday, Dec 12**, about 10 hours before the resolution time (CRS R49353; gofedpath, 2026).
  - No full-year FY2027 bills have been enacted, so a lapse would be close to a government-wide shutdown, not a narrow one.
  - One commentary piece gives the deadline as Dec 13 (Off Message, Nov 2026). Another low-quality site claims a stopgap was signed Nov 5 (govtschemes). CRS, CNBC and the Washington Post all say Dec 11.
- **Edge cases:**
  1. **Technical lapse:** If Congress clears a bill late Friday or early Saturday and signing is imminent, OMB has in the past told agencies not to shut down (March 2024, Dec 2024). That resolves NO under the rules. A signature before 10 AM Saturday would also end any lapse.
  2. **Weekend timing:** Because Dec 12 is a Saturday, few workers are affected and OPM may not post a status. Resolution would then likely rest on whether OMB has issued a shutdown directive. In January 2018 and on Jan 31, 2026, Saturday lapses did trigger formal shutdowns.
  3. **Short CR passed before Dec 11:** A one-week or few-day extension resolves NO, even if a later lapse follows.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

- **A. Lame-duck December deadlines, 2010–2024: about 1 in 8 ended in a shutdown.**
  - 2010, 2012, 2014, 2016, 2020, 2022 and 2024 all ended without a lapse. 2016 had a Democratic threat over miners' benefits. In 2024 the deal was killed and revived, then passed just after midnight with no OMB shutdown.
  - **2018** did shut down. Republicans had just lost the House, Trump demanded wall money, and the lapse began Dec 22 and lasted 35 days.
  - 2018 is the closest analog: a lame duck after a heavy GOP loss, with a policy demand tied to the funding bill. This time, though, the demand comes from the incoming majority, not the president.
- **B. Deadlines under Trump's second term: 3 of about 5 lapsed.**
  - Mar 14, 2025: no lapse.
  - Sept 30, 2025: 43-day shutdown.
  - Jan 30, 2026: partial shutdown from Jan 31 to Feb 3.
  - Feb 13, 2026: DHS shutdown from Feb 14 to about Apr 30/May 1, a record 75–76 days (NPR/OPB, Apr 30, 2026; Fox, Feb 2026).
  - Sept 30, 2026: no lapse. The CR cleared Sept 1, a month early, by 90-6 in the Senate and 370-48 in the House (CRFB; SpacePolicyOnline).
  - The recent rate is high and shows both parties now accept shutdowns as a tactic.
- **C. All funding deadlines since FY1998: roughly 5–8%.**
  - About 7 lapses (2013, Jan 2018, Feb 2018 overnight, Dec 2018, Oct 2025, Jan 2026, Feb 2026 DHS) across more than 100 CR or regular-bill deadlines. This is my rough count.
  - Most deadlines are resolved with a short-term extension, often in the last 24–48 hours.

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 11:** The Washington Post reported Congress is "far from a deal."
  - Top House and Senate appropriators met Nov 9–10 without agreement.
  - Democrats want a short-term CR at current levels into 2027, when they take control.
  - Republicans want to use their last weeks in power to fund the Iran war.
  - House Appropriations Chair Tom Cole proposed passing several packages of full-year bills ("minibuses"). Senate Republicans released Agriculture and MilCon-VA text on Nov 10.
  - One House Republican expects a CR running to February or March (WaPo; Kensington tracker).
- **Nov 9–11: Democrats are split over using the deadline to restrict the Iran war.**
  - **For using it:** Schumer has threatened to use "the power of the purse" and urged Congress to "end this war now." Sen. Michael Bennet urged Democrats to refuse cloture on any package with "another cent for the war." Sen. Van Hollen and Rep. Khanna are pushing restriction language. DeLauro said Democrats will "absolutely not fund" the war.
  - **Against risking a shutdown:** Coons warned against it and suggested Democrats instead reject the supplemental request and the reconciliation package. Murray's line, "We are not going to accept their supplemental funding," targets war money, not the CR itself.
  - **Jeffries:** He would not say whether he would hold up the funding bill and pointed to war powers resolutions instead.
  - Moderates are reluctant to risk a shutdown "weeks before Democrats take control" (Washington Sun / Political Wire, Nov 11; yournews, Nov 11; CBS).
  - Punchbowl, as quoted by Political Wire on Nov 9: "the odds of a government shutdown have increased dramatically since Democrats' big win on Tuesday." Bloomberg (Nov 9): "Shutdown risks emerge as Democrats consider ways to end Iran war."
- **Senate vote math:** Republicans hold the Senate until Jan 3 and need at least 7 Democratic votes for cloture (yournews, Nov 11). In Nov 2025, 8 Democratic-caucus members broke ranks to end that shutdown.
- **Republican position:**
  - Cole opposes Iran restrictions in appropriations bills.
  - Senate appropriators would like to attach an emergency war supplemental to the CR. The White House requested $87.6B in June, including about $67B for the Pentagon; another estimate puts the war money at about $200B.
  - A third reconciliation package (about $95B, including about $73B for the Pentagon) is pushed by Johnson and conservatives. Thune doubts he has 50 votes, and aides say it won't happen before January (The Hill; Breaking Defense, Nov 2026).
  - Senate Appropriations Chair Collins lost re-election (CNBC, Nov 9).
- **Nov 9:** Congress returned for the lame duck with funding, the debt limit, a Hegseth impeachment push and leadership races on the agenda (CNBC; Fox; ABC11).
- **Nov 3–4 midterms:** Democrats won both chambers: about 228+ House seats and about 54 Senate seats, flipping NC, SC, OH, TX, KS, IA and ME. Jeffries becomes Speaker and Schumer Majority Leader on Jan 3, 2027 (NPR/OPB, Nov 4).
- **Sept 1–2:** The current CR was enacted after the House dropped Hegseth's $37.5B Iran request. 109 Republicans opposed the House bill and it passed on Democratic votes. OMB's Statement of Administration Policy said Trump would sign it "to prevent a shutdown" despite objecting to its contents (HNGN, Sept 1; CRFB).
- **No direct Trump or OMB (Vought) statement on the December bill was found.**
- **Scheduled:** About 4 legislative weeks remain before Dec 11. Thanksgiving recess is likely the week of Nov 23. GOP leadership elections are happening now. The deadline is Friday, Dec 11, at midnight.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to NO (most likely first):**
1. **Clean CR into early 2027** (January to March), which both sides tolerate. Democrats get to write FY2027 bills from the majority. Republicans avoid a post-election shutdown that would be blamed on them. This depends on Democrats dropping or softening the Iran-restriction demand, as Coons and Jeffries lean, and on Trump signing, as he did in September.
2. **Cole's minibuses plus a CR** for the remaining bills.
3. **Last-minute deal or a short bridge CR** passed Dec 10–11. Alternatively, final passage slips just past midnight but OMB treats it as a technical lapse, or the president signs before 10 AM Saturday.

**Paths to YES:**
1. **Senate Democrats deny cloture** on any CR without Iran-war restrictions, or on any CR carrying war money. Republicans or Trump refuse the restrictions. This depends on Schumer, who faces progressive pressure and calls for his ouster, holding at least 41 Democrats together, when only about 7 defections are needed to pass a bill.
2. **Republicans insist on attaching the war supplemental**, Democrats refuse, and the deadline passes.
3. **Trump vetoes or refuses to sign** a CR containing an Iran funding ban; a veto was floated as a possibility.
4. **Procedural failure:** GOP leadership turmoil, a lame-duck House Republican majority unable to pass a rule, or 109-style conservative revolts. The House would then need Democratic votes, which raises Democrats' leverage.

**Structural asymmetry:** Democrats gain power on Jan 3. A shutdown starting Dec 12 would run into a Democratic Congress facing a Trump veto, which could make it long. Moderates fear owning that. Republicans, after a "blowout" loss, have little appetite for a shutdown fight they would be blamed for, but they also cannot accept war restrictions without Trump's approval.

## 5. Signposts

- **Toward YES:**
  - Schumer formally conditions cloture on Iran language, with a caucus or whip count.
  - A failed cloture vote on a CR during Dec 7–11.
  - A White House or OMB veto threat, or a demand to include war money.
  - OMB contingency guidance or agency shutdown-plan updates late the week of Dec 7.
  - A major Iran escalation that hardens positions.
- **Toward NO:**
  - Bipartisan agreement on a CR end date in January–March.
  - Jeffries or moderate senators publicly rejecting the shutdown strategy.
  - Iran supplemental money moved to a separate track (reconciliation or 2027).
  - Early House passage, before Dec 5, of a clean CR.
  - A war powers resolution vote that gives Democrats a "message" outlet.
  - A ceasefire or de-escalation in Iran.
- **Timing signals:** If a bill is clearing Friday evening, check whether OMB issues a "hold" (resolves NO) or an execute-shutdown memo (resolves YES).

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not read primary articles (WaPo, Punchbowl, Bloomberg, CBS); everything is from search summaries.
- Trump's and Vought's current stance on a clean CR versus a CR with restrictions is unknown.
- How many Senate Democrats would actually vote against cloture is unknown. No whip counts were found.
- The Iran war's status and trajectory over the next month are unclear.
- Republican leadership elections may shift the House and Senate approach.
- The CR's exact signing date varies by source; Dec 11 is consistent across authoritative ones. The Dec 13 and "Nov 5" claims are unverified outliers.
- It is unclear how the market resolver would treat an OMB shutdown memo during a weekend lapse that is resolved before Monday.
