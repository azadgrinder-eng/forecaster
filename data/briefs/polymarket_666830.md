# Research brief: Will a U.S.–UK free trade agreement become U.S. law by December 31, 2026?

*Prepared November 26, 2026. All page fetches were blocked by the proxy, so the facts below come from search-result summaries. Where those summaries disagree, I say so.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** A free trade agreement between the U.S. and the UK must *become law in the United States* by 11:59 PM ET on December 31, 2026. There are two ways that can happen:
  - The Senate ratifies it as a treaty (two-thirds vote) and the President approves it.
  - Congress passes it as a Congressional-Executive Agreement (implementing legislation through both chambers) and the President signs it.
- **Who decides:** "A consensus of credible reporting."
- **What does not count:**
  - **The May 8, 2025 Economic Prosperity Deal (EPD).** It is not new. It is also explicitly "not legally binding," and Congress "did not have a formal role" in approving it (CRS IF11123 / R49037, July 2026).
  - **Agreements implemented only by the executive.** Presidential proclamations, Section 232 tariff adjustments, or Trump-style "Agreements on Reciprocal Trade" (ARTs), which a CFR tracker says "exclude any role for Congress," would not satisfy "becomes law" under the rules as written.
- **Ambiguities:**
  - The title says "agrees to a new trade deal," while the resolution criteria require enactment into law. A careless resolver could count a headline announcement, such as an expanded EPD chapter. The written criteria are clearly stricter.
  - "Free trade agreement" is not defined. A narrow sectoral deal (steel, pharmaceuticals, digital) would arguably not qualify even if it somehow passed Congress.
- **Time left:** About 35 days, including the Thanksgiving and Christmas recesses of a lame-duck Congress.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

1. **How long FTAs take to become U.S. law, start to finish.** The U.S. has 14 FTAs in force with 20 countries. Every one went through a long sequence: negotiation, signing, an ITC study, implementing legislation, and votes in both chambers.
   - Korea: negotiations launched 2006, signed 2007, enacted 2011.
   - Colombia: signed 2006, enacted 2011.
   - Panama: signed 2007, enacted 2011.
   - USMCA: signed November 2018, enacted January 29, 2020.
   - Typical gap from signing to enactment is 1 to 5 years. I know of **no case** where a U.S. FTA went from "no signed text" to "law" in under about 2 months. Frequency in that situation: 0 out of roughly 14.
2. **U.S. FTAs enacted since Trade Promotion Authority (TPA) lapsed on July 1, 2021.** Zero. Neither the Biden nor the Trump administration has sought to renew TPA (CRS R47679; policyrisk wiki). The only trade-powers bill I found in 2026 is Sen. Wyden's Congressional Trade Powers Reform Act of July 22, 2026. It aims to rein in presidential tariff authority. It is not a TPA renewal (Senate Finance, July 22, 2026).
3. **Trump second-term trade deals submitted to Congress.** Roughly 0 out of 15+. The deals in this group include the UK EPD (May 2025) and signed ARTs with Taiwan, Indonesia (February 19, 2026), Jordan (July 21, 2026), El Salvador, Argentina, Ecuador, Guatemala and others, as listed by USTR and USDA. I found no hearing, markup or floor vote on implementing legislation for any of them (CFR tracker; USTR fact sheets). Taiwan's ART goes to *Taiwan's* legislature, not to Congress.
4. **The U.S.–UK FTA specifically.** Negotiations ran from 2020 to early 2021, went through five rounds, and were never concluded. They were shelved under Biden. The 2025 EPD was presented as "not intended to be a comprehensive FTA" but as a "platform for phased delivery" (BABCC one-year review).

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **November 25, 2026.** The UK and EU announced a deal package (Baker McKenzie Global Import Blog, November 25, 2026). It includes:
  - a Sanitary and Phytosanitary (SPS) agreement on food and agriculture, targeted to start March 1, 2028, with dynamic alignment to EU food rules;
  - linking the UK and EU emissions trading schemes, from January 1, 2028.
- **November 20–22, 2026.** PM Andy Burnham held a UK–EU reset summit in Brussels, and Downing Street said "the deal is done" (Institute of Export, ~November 20, 2026). According to WSWS (November 22, 2026; partisan source):
  - Greer accused London of "choosing Brussels over America."
  - U.S. Ambassador Warren Stephens warned that closer EU ties would not be viewed favourably in the White House.
  - Kemi Badenoch and Nigel Farage have pledged to reverse the EU deal.
- **~Early September 2026 (exact date uncertain).** USTR Jamieson Greer told the FT that the UK's alignment with EU rules is "a problem" for *expanding* the 2025 deal. He urged the UK to accept goods and farm products made to U.S. standards (Investing.com; Inside U.S. Trade; ripr/Euronews).
- **September 2026.** Burnham and Trump met at the UN General Assembly and discussed trade, energy and immigration. Trump said the relationship was "more up" with Burnham than with Starmer. They still disagreed on AI regulation and Chagos (Yahoo/DPA, September 2026). I found no reporting on formal FTA negotiations.
- **July 24, 2026.**
  - The Section 122 global 10% tariff hit its 150-day limit and expired.
  - It was replaced by Section 301 duties of 10% or 12.5% on 60 trading partners (Morgan Lewis, July 2026; ABA, Fall 2026).
  - The UK says it stays at 10%, that its "preferential access…remains in place," and that it faced no additional tariffs (HuffPost UK / Yahoo).
  - CRS updated its U.S.–UK reports on July 17 and July 24, 2026. They describe the EPD as non-binding, mention no FTA negotiations, and frame Congress's role as oversight only.
- **July 20, 2026.** Burnham succeeded Keir Starmer as PM (Wikipedia).
- **February 20, 2026 (approx.).** The Supreme Court ruled that the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) does not authorize tariffs. That ruling undercut the legal basis for the UK's 10% baseline tariff and led to the Section 122 tariff taking effect February 24, 2026 (Thomson Reuters, February 23, 2026; White & Case).
- **December 2025.** According to CRS, the U.S. suspended the Technology Prosperity Deal signed in September 2025. A US–UK pharmaceutical arrangement gives zero U.S. tariffs on UK pharma from January 1, 2026 to January 19, 2029 (House of Commons Library CBP-10850).
- **A claim to discount.** GTsetu.org says a "US–UK FTA" was signed on February 6, 2026. No credible source corroborates it, and the July 2026 CRS reports contradict it. It looks spurious.
- **Scheduled before the close date:** The lame-duck session of the 119th Congress runs through December, mostly on appropriations and must-pass items. I found no U.S.–UK trade event scheduled before December 31, and no UK–US summit or negotiating round.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to YES** (all extremely unlikely in about 5 weeks):
- An existing FTA text is quickly signed, submitted, and passed by both chambers in the lame duck. There is no evidence any such text exists. Without TPA there would be no fast-track deadlines, and the Senate filibuster would apply.
- A loose resolution in which the resolver counts a new executive announcement, for example a deal to expand the EPD, as "agreeing to a trade deal." This goes against the written criteria, and the U.S.–EU alignment dispute makes even such an announcement unlikely before year-end.

**Paths to NO** (the default):
- No negotiated FTA text exists.
- The administration's model is executive-only deals.
- TPA has lapsed.
- U.S.–UK relations are strained over the UK's EU SPS alignment, the main obstacle to U.S. farm access.
- The calendar leaves almost no time.

## 5. Signposts

- **Would move toward YES (none observed):**
  - A USTR notice to Congress of intent to sign a U.S.–UK FTA.
  - Implementing legislation introduced in the House Ways and Means or Senate Finance committees.
  - A scheduled floor vote.
  - A TPA renewal bill moving.
  - A Burnham–Trump summit announcing a "comprehensive FTA."
- **Would confirm NO:**
  - Congress adjourns sine die without trade legislation.
  - Further U.S. criticism of the UK–EU SPS deal.
  - New U.S. Section 301 or 232 actions against the UK.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not open the primary documents (CRS, USTR, gov.uk) because fetches were blocked. Some dates come only from search summaries, including the exact date of Greer's FT remarks.
- I did not verify the results of the November 3, 2026 U.S. midterms or the lame-duck agenda.
- I cannot fully rule out an unreported U.S.–UK announcement in the last few days. Even if one happened, it could not plausibly become law by December 31.
- The main residual risk is how the resolver reads "agrees to" against "becomes law."
