# Research Brief: Will Situational Awareness announce a fund wind-down by Dec 31, 2026?

*Prepared Nov 26, 2026. My fetches of primary article pages were blocked, so this brief relies on search-result summaries of the WSJ, FT, Bloomberg, CNBC, Reuters and others. Details that come only from small aggregators are marked as weak.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts as YES:** By Dec 31, 2026, 11:59 PM ET, Situational Awareness (SA), the AI-focused hedge fund run by Leopold Aschenbrenner, must announce one of three things:
  - it will cease operations;
  - Situational Awareness LP will be wound down;
  - all outside investor capital in SA LP will be returned.
- **A credible-reporting consensus is enough.** If credible outlets agree that one of those things has happened, that also resolves YES. This matters because SA usually talks through private investor letters that leak to the WSJ, FT or Bloomberg. A leaked letter saying "we are winding down," widely reported, would probably be enough.
- **Edge cases that count as YES:**
  - Converting to a family office or proprietary vehicle, or distributing assets in kind, but only if *all* outside capital is returned or announced to be returned.
  - Moving all assets into a liquidating trust or SPV that is announced to accept no new outside capital.
- **Edge cases that count as NO:**
  - Closing to new investors.
  - Redemption gates or suspensions.
  - Redemptions under the existing lockup terms, or partial redemptions.
  - Selling off positions or a strategy without winding down the fund. The July 2026 fire sale to Citadel is exactly this type.
  - A renamed, successor, continuation or rollover vehicle that still accepts outside capital.
- **The main ambiguity is "Situational Awareness 2.0."** If Aschenbrenner launches a new vehicle and closes the old LP, the successor/continuation exclusion would most likely make it NO. A YES would require all outside capital in SA LP to be returned and the new vehicle not to be a rollover of it. Expect a dispute if SA LP is closed and its investors are offered a new fund.
- **SEC filing:** A withdrawal of the adviser's SEC registration (Form ADV-W; SEC file 801-132039, CRD 333011) is named as a resolution source.
- **Once YES, always YES:** If a qualifying announcement happens, a later reversal does not change the outcome.
- **Time left:** About 5 weeks.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Large hedge funds after a catastrophic drawdown (more than 50% in weeks): eventual closure.** From well-known cases I recall (approximate):
- Closed quickly, within weeks: Amaranth (Sept 2006, about $6B loss, wind-down announced in about 2 weeks), Peloton Partners (2008), Malachite (March 2020), IPM (2020), Tetras Capital (crypto, 75% loss).
- Survived the immediate crisis, then closed later: LTCM (recapitalized Sept 1998, wound down about 15 months later) and Melvin Capital (lost about 53% in Jan 2021, wind-down announced May 2022, about 16 months later) (Fortune, May 18, 2022).
- Survived indefinitely: Tiger Global after its roughly 56% loss in 2022, Pershing Square after Valeant, Greenlight.
- Rough result: about half of famous blowups eventually close. **Funds that get past the first ~2 months usually take 12 months or more to close, if they close at all.** SA has already survived about 4 months since July.

**B. Hazard rate for a specific 5-week window.** If a post-crisis survivor has roughly a 30–50% chance of closing within about 18 months, and the risk is spread evenly, the chance in any given 5-week window is about 2–4%. Year-end, when investors redeem and managers decide whether to carry on, may raise that somewhat.

**C. Hedge fund attrition in general.**
- About 1,000 funds closed per year in 2015–16 (HFR, via Fortune, 2016).
- Typical fund lifespan is described as about 3 years (Fortune, May 20, 2022).
- 2026 examples: AlphaQuest closed after a 3-year losing streak (Bloomberg, Feb 18, 2026). QVR announced a wind-down after a 30% loss plus redemptions (Bloomberg/Hedgeweek, May 13, 2026).
- Closures usually follow redemptions and the manager losing the will to continue. SA's manager is publicly signalling the opposite (see section 3).

## 3. Current state of the world (most recent first)

- **Nov 25–26, 2026 (WSJ, via Reuters/Bloomberg): Q3 investor letter.**
  - SA lost **65% after fees in Q3** but was still **up 88.5% after fees for the year** through Sept 30.
  - The portfolio has been restructured, risk-taking is more restrained, and the book is about 70% public stocks and 30% private companies after Anthropic's IPO. Limited public equity trading has resumed.
  - The letter reportedly calls current opportunities "the highest potential return we have seen in the lifetime of the fund." Reuters said it had not confirmed the letter.
  - One outlet notes the selloff got worse in October, after the period the letter covers, so **Q4 performance is unknown** (Global Banking & Finance/Reuters, Nov 25; Bloomberg, Nov 26; BigGo Finance, Nov 2026).
- **~Nov 18–19, 2026 (FT, via Hedgeweek, Benzinga, Seeking Alpha, Winbuzzer): relaunch plans.**
  - Aschenbrenner is exploring a relaunch called **"Situational Awareness 2.0,"** including a return to leveraged trading and a new outside capital raise possibly in **early 2027**. Plans are not final.
  - He is in talks with **Jefferies** about prime brokerage. Jefferies turned SA away earlier in 2026 over hedging concerns.
  - SA is also working with **Clear Street** and is looking to **hire a chief risk officer**.
  - The fund still manages **more than $10B**, mostly private holdings, including a stake in **Anthropic worth about $5B**. SA and Jefferies declined to comment.
- **Nov 17–19, 2026: Anthropic IPO.** Anthropic's S-1/A names SA LP among significant Series H investors. The IPO priced at $83 a share, raising about $81B (Axios, Nov 19; FourWeekMBA). Insiders and pre-IPO holders usually face a lockup of about 180 days. That would make SA's largest asset hard to sell before about May 2027, which argues against a quick liquidation (this is my inference).
- **Mid-Nov 2026: Q3 13F filing.** Reported 13F holdings fell from about **$20.24B (June 30) to $1.58B (Sept 30)**. The IREN stake was cut 94%, from 9.47M to 0.53M shares. SA exited Core Scientific, Riot and Hive. The largest remaining positions are SharonAI (about $441M) and call options (about $1.04B) (Crypto Briefing; AlphaMaven). A fund that is still filing 13Fs and holding new positions is still operating.
- **~Sept 13, 2026 (FT): JPMorgan ended its prime brokerage lending to SA.** Goldman, Citi and Bank of America reportedly still provide services (Hedgeweek; Investing.com).
- **Aug 24, 2026: SEC inquiry.** The SEC subpoenaed several Wall Street banks over SA's leverage and margin calls. The inquiry is early-stage and SA is not accused of wrongdoing (Fortune; Bloomberg Government). Separately, the NYT reported that SA had no risk team (AI Weekly, citing the NYT).
- **Aug 21, 2026: Citadel unwind.** Ken Griffin said Citadel had unwound more than 80% of the risk from the SA portfolio it bought (CNBC).
- **Late July to August 2026: survival statements.**
  - Aschenbrenner told investors SA would **keep investing in public equities rather than wind down**, and would stop using bank borrowing (FT, via Hedgeweek).
  - A second letter reportedly said SA "was not liquidated, and the fund would not shut down."
  - An appeal for fresh capital starting Aug 1 drew less than he had hoped (Bloomberg, per secondary sources).
  - A $400M private investment was reportedly made after the collapse (The Next Web).
- **July 24–31, 2026: the collapse.**
  - SA had grown to about $45B (gross) in early July.
  - Losses on AI longs and a short on software stocks hit at the same time, so the hedges did not protect the fund. Margin calls followed.
  - Citadel bought most of the public book. SA was down about 67% for July (CNBC, July 30–31; WSJ).
- **Fund terms:** $25M minimum, **two-year lockup, then quarterly redemptions spread over two more years**, 2-and-20 fees (Stafforini notes; Beyond Beta). The fund launched around late 2024 (Form D filed 2024), so most investors are probably still locked up or limited to small quarterly redemptions. Redemptions under these terms do not count toward YES.
- **Scheduled before Dec 31:** Dec 31 quarter-end redemption date (partial redemptions do not count). Possible Q4 or year-end investor letter in early January, probably after the deadline. Possible chief risk officer hire and prime brokerage announcements. Possible further FT/WSJ/Bloomberg reporting on 2.0.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**Paths to NO (dominant):**
- Status quo. The manager is clearly committed: relaunch planning, chief risk officer search, prime brokerage talks, a bullish Q3 letter, and plans to raise capital in early 2027.
- Long lockups protect SA from a run on the fund.
- The large private and Anthropic stakes are hard to sell quickly, which makes liquidating before Jan 1 impractical.
- Even if Aschenbrenner restructures, the likely form (a 2.0 successor vehicle) is explicitly excluded.

**Paths to YES (low probability within 5 weeks):**
1. **A new shock.** A severe Q4 or October loss, possibly worse than reported, triggers lender or regulator pressure and an abrupt decision to return capital.
2. **A clean break.** Aschenbrenner closes SA LP, returns or distributes all outside capital (possibly in kind, as Anthropic shares), and launches 2.0 as a genuinely new fund. Ambiguous under the successor exclusion; it would need all SA LP outside capital returned.
3. **Conversion to a family office** with all outside capital returned. Unlikely given his public fundraising plans.
4. **SEC escalation into enforcement, or a key-person or legal event** that forces closure. The SEC inquiry is at subpoena stage only, and these usually take many months.

## 5. Signposts

- **Toward YES (strong):**
  - Reports that the 2.0 relaunch has been abandoned.
  - Reports of large redemption requests or investors pushing to liquidate.
  - Q4 losses reported as very large.
  - Loss of the remaining prime brokers (Goldman, Citi, BofA).
  - A Form ADV-W filing.
  - Reports of in-kind distributions of Anthropic shares to all LPs.
  - SEC enforcement action against SA or Aschenbrenner.
  - Key staff leaving.
- **Toward NO:**
  - A chief risk officer hired.
  - Jefferies agreeing to prime broker.
  - Marketing materials for the 2.0 raise.
  - A new 13F or Form D filing.
  - Anthropic shares trading well, which lifts SA's net asset value.
- **Ambiguity signpost:** Any announcement that SA LP will be "closed" and its investors offered a new vehicle. Read the rollover terms closely.

## 6. Uncertainties

- **October and November performance:** the size of the October drawdown, and whether SA reused leverage before it.
- **Current AUM and assets:** the "more than $10B" figure combines private marks and may be stale. The Q3 letter's 70/30 public/private split looks inconsistent with a $1.58B 13F book plus a $5B Anthropic stake, unless the Anthropic shares are counted as public after the IPO.
- **Fund terms:** exact launch date, lockup expiry dates, and whether any side pockets or gates exist.
- **SEC inquiry:** whether it has moved beyond bank subpoenas.
- **2.0 structure:** whether 2.0 would be a new legal entity replacing SA LP, which matters for the successor exclusion.
- **Thin sourcing:** much of the detail comes from secondary aggregators. I could not read the WSJ, FT or Bloomberg originals, and some figures conflict (for example the $45B gross vs. about $20B AUM numbers for July).
