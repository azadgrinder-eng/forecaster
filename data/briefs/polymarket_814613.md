# Research brief: Will the DPP win the most mayor and magistrate races in Taiwan's 2026 local elections (vote on Nov 28, 2026)?

*Prepared Nov 13, 2026. The page-reading tool failed on every URL, so all facts below come from search-result summaries and snippets. Figures are reported as published, but I could not check them against the original articles, so check key numbers before relying on them.*

## 1. Resolution mechanics

- **What counts:** 22 races for mayor or magistrate: Taipei, New Taipei, Taoyuan, Taichung, Tainan, Kaohsiung, Keelung, Hsinchu City, Chiayi City, Yilan, Hsinchu County, Miaoli, Changhua, Nantou, Yunlin, Chiayi County, Pingtung, Taitung, Hualien, Penghu, Lienchiang and Kinmen. Polls close Nov 28, 2026. Results normally come in the same night, and the market closes Nov 29, 04:59 UTC.
- **Who counts as a party's candidate:** the person must be nominated by the party and registered under its label. Independents count for nobody, even if a party backs them. This matters a lot this cycle:
  - **Chiayi City:** the joint KMT–TPP candidate, Chang Chi-kai (張啓楷), is a TPP member. A win for him counts for the TPP, not the KMT (UDN; PTS).
  - **Chiayi County:** the KMT is backing independent Wu Pin-jui (吳品叡) (Newtalk, Jul 22, 2026).
  - **Hsinchu City:** the KMT is backing incumbent Kao Hung-an (高虹安), who is listed as an independent (Newtalk, Jul 22, 2026; Templeman wrap-up).
  - **Result:** the KMT has its own registered candidate in at most about 19 of the 22 races. The DPP appears to have nominees in all or nearly all of them. I could not confirm the official list from the Central Election Commission (CEC), especially for the offshore islands and Miaoli.
- **Ties:** a tie goes to the party whose English name comes first alphabetically *as listed in the market group*. If the KMT is listed as "Kuomintang", the DPP ("Democratic…") wins ties. If it is listed as "Chinese Nationalist Party", the KMT wins ties. Forecasters should check the listing. In practice YES means **DPP seats ≥ KMT seats**, assuming the "Kuomintang" listing.
- **Final arbiter:** consensus of credible reporting, with the CEC (db.cec.gov.tw) as backup if anything is ambiguous. Possible edge cases:
  - A candidate dies, which in Taiwan can postpone that race. This happened in Chiayi City in 2022, when the vote moved to Dec 18.
  - Recounts in very close races.
  - Any race still unresolved is judged on "available results" by June 30, 2027.
- **Polling blackout:** Taiwanese law bans publishing polls from Nov 18, 2026 until voting ends, so very little new polling will appear in the last 10 days.

## 2. Reference classes and base rates

**A. Which party won the most county and city executive races, 1997–2022.** From my own memory of past results; please verify.

| Year | Result | Who won most |
|---|---|---|
| 1997 | DPP 12, KMT 8 (of 23) | DPP |
| 2001 | DPP 9, KMT 9 | Tie |
| 2005 | KMT 14, DPP 6 | KMT |
| 2009 + 2010 combined | KMT about 15, DPP about 6 | KMT |
| 2014 | DPP 13, KMT 6, independents 3 | DPP |
| 2018 | KMT 15, DPP 6, independent 1 | KMT |
| 2022 | KMT 14, DPP 5, TPP 1, independents 2 | KMT |

The DPP reached at least a tie with the KMT in **3 of 7 cycles (about 43%)**, but in **only 1 of the last 4**.

**B. Midterm local elections for the party holding the presidency.** The DPP is the national ruling party now, under President Lai, and this is his first midterm.
- When the DPP held the presidency (2001, 2005, 2018, 2022), it won the most or tied in 1 of 4. The only case was the 2001 tie.
- The presidential party lost ground in 1997, 2005, 2014, 2018 and 2022. That is a fairly consistent penalty for the party in power at midterm.

**C. The starting position.**
- The KMT holds 16 of 22 seats, the DPP 5 (Tainan, Kaohsiung, Chiayi County, Pingtung, Penghu), and there is one independent (Kao in Hsinchu City).
- The KMT is defending **10 open seats** (Templeman, "Preview (5) – Summing Up", fall 2026).
- In 2014, the DPP's best recent year, it gained 7 seats net. It needs a swing of roughly that size now.

## 4. Drivers and scenarios

**The basic arithmetic.**
- Assume a DPP base of 8: Tainan, Kaohsiung, Chiayi County, Pingtung, Penghu, Changhua, Yilan and Chiayi City.
- Assume a KMT base of 6: Nantou, Kinmen, Lienchiang, Miaoli, Yunlin and Hualien. Hsinchu City is assumed to go to Kao, an independent.
- Seven races are then genuine DPP-vs-KMT battlegrounds: New Taipei, Keelung, Hsinchu County, Taichung, Taipei, Taoyuan and Taitung.
- **The DPP gets YES if it holds its base and wins at least 3 of those 7** (11 vs 10). A Hsinchu City win by the DPP's Chuang Ching-cheng (莊競程) would be a bonus.
- Each base seat the DPP loses (Penghu, Chiayi City, Yilan and Changhua look most exposed) raises the number of battlegrounds it needs by about one. Losing a seat to the KMT costs two seats of margin; losing one to the TPP or an independent costs one.

**Paths to YES:**
- A national swing toward the DPP, helped by the KMT's poor favorability under Cheng Li-wun.
- KMT–TPP vote splitting, as in Hsinchu County and Yilan.
- New Taipei, which currently leans DPP, plus two of Keelung, Hsinchu County, Taichung or Taitung.
- The races are correlated: if the DPP-leaning polls are right, several close races flip together.

**Paths to NO:**
- The usual midterm penalty for the ruling party (pattern B in section 2).
- KMT advantages from incumbency and local machines.
- TPP voters consolidating behind KMT candidates, as in Kaohsiung and Taipei.
- Many polls favoring the DPP were commissioned by DPP-aligned groups (Hsinmin, Strategy and Public Research Institute, campaign-commissioned Dadi). Taiwanese polls have often misjudged undecided and "hidden" KMT voters.
- Losing Penghu or Chiayi City would make the DPP need 4 or more of the 7 battlegrounds.

## 5. Signposts

- **Last polls before the Nov 18 blackout**, especially independent pollsters (TVBS, UDN, Formosa) in Taichung, Taipei, Keelung, Hsinchu County and Taoyuan.
  - Polls showing the DPP leading or tied in 4 or more battlegrounds point toward YES.
  - Polls showing the KMT ahead in Taichung, Taoyuan and Taipei plus one of Keelung or Hsinchu County point toward NO.
- **Signs of KMT–TPP coordination or splits:** TPP chair Huang Kuo-chang (黃國昌) or Ko Wen-je (柯文哲) campaigning for KMT candidates would favor NO. Open TPP defections, as in Hsinchu County, would favor YES.
- **Late shocks:** scandals, cross-strait military pressure, or candidate legal trouble. On average, cross-strait tension has tended to help the DPP.
- **On election night:** early results from the north, especially Keelung and New Taipei, will show the direction of the swing.
- **Official CEC candidate list:** confirm the party labels of the candidates in Penghu, Kinmen, Miaoli, Hualien and Hsinchu City.

## 6. Uncertainties

- I could not open any article. Every number comes from search summaries, and some showed internal mix-ups; for example, one source confused the Taoyuan and Tainan candidates.
- I could not confirm:
  - the official CEC registration list or every candidate's party label (especially the KMT's Penghu nominee, Hualien and Miaoli);
  - Kao Hung-an's legal and party status;
  - whether the date of Templeman's ratings came before the recent polling;
  - the wording of the market group's party names, which decides ties.
- The historical seat counts in section 2 come from memory and should be checked.
- I found no recent polls for Hualien, Miaoli, Nantou, Pingtung, Chiayi County or Kinmen.
- I am not sure how big the "KMT–TPP cooperation collapse" really is outside Hsinchu County.
